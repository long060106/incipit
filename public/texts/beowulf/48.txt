<p>Beowulf’s pyre.</p>
<p>The folk of the Geatmen got him then ready</p>
<p>A pile on the earth strong for the burning,</p>
<p>Behung with helmets, hero-knights’ targets,</p>
<p>And bright-shining burnies, as he begged they should have them;</p>
<p>5</p>
<p>Then wailing war-heroes their world-famous chieftain,</p>
<p>Their liegelord beloved, laid in the middle.</p>
<p>The funeral-flame.</p>
<p>Soldiers began then to make on the barrow</p>
<p>The largest of dead-fires: dark o’er the vapor</p>
<p>The smoke-cloud ascended, the sad-roaring fire,</p>
<p>10</p>
<p>Mingled with weeping (the wind-roar subsided)</p>
<p>Till the building of bone it had broken to pieces,</p>
<p>Hot in the heart. Heavy in spirit</p>
<p>They mood-sad lamented the men-leader’s ruin;</p>
<p>And mournful measures the much-grieving widow</p>

<p>The Weders carry out their lord’s last request.</p>
<p>The men of the Weders made accordingly</p>
<p>A hill on the height, high and extensive,</p>
<p>Of sea-going sailors to be seen from a distance,</p>
<p>And the brave one’s beacon built where the fire was,</p>
<p>25</p>
<p>In ten-days’ space, with a wall surrounded it,</p>
<p>As wisest of world-folk could most worthily plan it.</p>
<p>They placed in the barrow rings and jewels,</p>
<p>Rings and gems are laid in the barrow.</p>
<p>All such ornaments as erst in the treasure</p>
<p>War-mooded men had won in possession:</p>
<p>30</p>
<p>The earnings of earlmen to earth they entrusted,</p>
<p>The gold to the dust, where yet it remaineth</p>
<p>As useless to mortals as in foregoing eras.</p>
<p>’Round the dead-mound rode then the doughty-in-battle,</p>
<p>Bairns of all twelve of the chiefs of the people,</p>
<p>They mourn for their lord, and sing his praises. 35</p>
<p>More would they mourn, lament for their ruler,</p>
<p>Speak in measure, mention him with pleasure,</p>
<p>Weighed his worth, and his warlike achievements</p>
<p>Mightily commended, as ’tis meet one praise his</p>
<p>Liegelord in words and love him in spirit,</p>
<p>40</p>
<p>When forth from his body he fares to destruction.</p>
<p>So lamented mourning the men of the Geats,</p>
<p>Fond-loving vassals, the fall of their lord,</p>
<p>An ideal king.</p>
<p>Said he was kindest of kings under heaven,</p>
<p>Gentlest of men, most winning of manner,</p>
<p>45</p>
<p>Friendliest to folk-troops and fondest of honor.</p>
