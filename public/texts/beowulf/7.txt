<p>Beowulf succeeds his father Scyld</p>
<p>In the boroughs then Beowulf, bairn of the Scyldings,</p>
<p>Belovèd land-prince, for long-lasting season</p>
<p>Was famed mid the folk (his father departed,</p>
<p>The prince from his dwelling), till afterward sprang</p>
<p>5</p>
<p>Great-minded Healfdene; the Danes in his lifetime</p>
<p>He graciously governed, grim-mooded, agèd.</p>
<p>Healfdene’s birth.</p>
<p>Four bairns of his body born in succession</p>
<p>Woke in the world, war-troopers’ leader</p>
<p>Heorogar, Hrothgar, and Halga the good;</p>
<p>10</p>
<p>Heard I that Elan was Ongentheow’s consort,</p>
<p>He has three sons—one of them, Hrothgar—and a daughter named Elan. Hrothgar becomes a mighty king.</p>
<p>The well-beloved bedmate of the War-Scylfing leader.</p>
<p>Then glory in battle to Hrothgar was given,</p>
<p>Waxing of war-fame, that willingly kinsmen</p>
<p>Obeyed his bidding, till the boys grew to manhood,</p>
<p>15</p>
<p>A numerous band. It burned in his spirit</p>
<p>To urge his folk to found a great building,</p>
<p>A mead-hall grander than men of the era</p>
<p>He is eager to build a great hall in which he may feast his retainers</p>
<p>Ever had heard of, and in it to share</p>
<p>With young and old all of the blessings</p>
<p>20</p>
<p>The Lord had allowed him, save life and retainers.</p>
<p>Then the work I find afar was assigned</p>
<p>To many races in middle-earth’s regions,</p>
<p>To adorn the great folk-hall. In due time it happened</p>
<p>Early ’mong men, that ’twas finished entirely,</p>
<p>25</p>
<p>The greatest of hall-buildings; Heorot he named it</p>
<p>The hall is completed, and is called Heort, or Heorot.</p>
<p>Who wide-reaching word-sway wielded ’mong earlmen.</p>
<p>His promise he brake not, rings he lavished,</p>
<p>Treasure at banquet. Towered the hall up</p>
<p>High and horn-crested, huge between antlers:</p>
<p>30</p>
<p>It battle-waves bided, the blasting fire-demon;</p>
<p>Ere long then from hottest hatred must sword-wrath</p>
<p>Arise for a woman’s husband and father.</p>
<p>Then the mighty war-spirit<sup>1</sup> endured for a season,</p>
<p>The Monster Grendel is madly envious of the Danemen’s joy.</p>
<p>Bore it bitterly, he who bided in darkness,</p>
<p>35</p>
<p>That light-hearted laughter loud in the building</p>
<p>Greeted him daily; there was dulcet harp-music,</p>
<p>Clear song of the singer. He said that was able</p>
<p>[The course of the story is interrupted by a short reference to some old account of the creation.]</p>
<p>To tell from of old earthmen’s beginnings,</p>
<p>That Father Almighty earth had created,</p>
<p>40</p>
<p>The winsome wold that the water encircleth,</p>
<p>Set exultingly the sun’s and the moon’s beams</p>
<p>To lavish their lustre on land-folk and races,</p>
<p>And earth He embellished in all her regions</p>
<p>With limbs and leaves; life He bestowed too</p>
<p>45</p>
<p>On all the kindreds that live under heaven.</p>
<p>The glee of the warriors is overcast by a horrible dread.</p>
<p>So blessed with abundance, brimming with joyance,</p>
<p>The warriors abided, till a certain one gan to</p>
<p>Dog them with deeds of direfullest malice,</p>
<p>A foe in the hall-building: this horrible stranger<sup>2</sup></p>
<p>50</p>
<p>Was Grendel entitled, the march-stepper famous</p>
<p>Who<sup>3</sup> dwelt in the moor-fens, the marsh and the fastness;</p>
<p>The wan-mooded being abode for a season</p>
<p>In the land of the giants, when the Lord and Creator</p>
<p>Had banned him and branded. For that bitter murder,</p>
<p>55</p>
<p>The killing of Abel, all-ruling Father</p>
<p>Cain is referred to as a progenitor of Grendel, and of monsters in general.</p>
<p>The kindred of Cain crushed with His vengeance;</p>
<p>In the feud He rejoiced not, but far away drove him</p>
<p>From kindred and kind, that crime to atone for,</p>
<p>Meter of Justice. Thence ill-favored creatures,</p>
<p>60</p>
<p>Elves and giants, monsters of ocean,</p>
<p>Came into being, and the giants that longtime</p>
<p>Grappled with God; He gave them requital.</p>
<p>[1] R. and t. B. prefer ‘ellor-gæst’ to ‘ellen-gæst’ (86): <i>Then the stranger from afar endured, etc.</i></p>
<p>[2] Some authorities would translate ‘<i>demon</i>’ instead of ‘<i>stranger</i>.’</p>
<p>[3] Some authorities arrange differently, and render: <i>Who dwelt in the moor-fens, the marsh and the fastness, the land of the giant-race.</i></p>
