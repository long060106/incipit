<p>Wiglaf is sorely grieved to see his lord look so un-warlike.</p>
<p>It had wofully chanced then the youthful retainer</p>
<p>To behold on earth the most ardent-belovèd</p>
<p>At his life-days’ limit, lying there helpless.</p>
<p>The slayer too lay there, of life all bereavèd,</p>
<p>5</p>
<p>Horrible earth-drake, harassed with sorrow:</p>
<p>The dragon has plundered his last hoard.</p>
<p>The round-twisted monster was permitted no longer</p>
<p>To govern the ring-hoards, but edges of war-swords</p>
<p>Mightily seized him, battle-sharp, sturdy</p>
<p>Leavings of hammers, that still from his wounds</p>
<p>10</p>
<p>The flier-from-farland fell to the earth</p>
<p>Hard by his hoard-house, hopped he at midnight</p>
<p>Not e’er through the air, nor exulting in jewels</p>
<p>Suffered them to see him: but he sank then to earthward</p>
<p>Through the hero-chief’s handwork. I heard sure it throve then</p>
<p>Few warriors dared to face the monster. 15</p>
<p>But few in the land of liegemen of valor,</p>
<p>Though of every achievement bold he had proved him,</p>
<p>To run ’gainst the breath of the venomous scather,</p>
<p>Or the hall of the treasure to trouble with hand-blows,</p>
<p>If he watching had found the ward of the hoard-hall</p>
<p>20</p>
<p>On the barrow abiding. Beowulf’s part of</p>
<p>The treasure of jewels was paid for with death;</p>
<p>Each of the twain had attained to the end of</p>
<p>Life so unlasting. Not long was the time till</p>
<p>The cowardly thanes come out of the thicket.</p>
<p>The tardy-at-battle returned from the thicket,</p>
<p>25</p>
<p>The timid truce-breakers ten all together,</p>
<p>Who durst not before play with the lances</p>
<p>In the prince of the people’s pressing emergency;</p>
<p>They are ashamed of their desertion.</p>
<p>But blushing with shame, with shields they betook them,</p>
<p>With arms and armor where the old one was lying:</p>
<p>30</p>
<p>They gazed upon Wiglaf. He was sitting exhausted,</p>
<p>Foot-going fighter, not far from the shoulders</p>
<p>Of the lord of the people, would rouse him with water;</p>
<p>No whit did it help him; though he hoped for it keenly,</p>
<p>He was able on earth not at all in the leader</p>
<p>35</p>
<p>Life to retain, and nowise to alter</p>
<p>The will of the Wielder; the World-Ruler’s power<sup>1</sup></p>
<p>Would govern the actions of each one of heroes,</p>
<p>Wiglaf is ready to excoriate them.</p>
<p>As yet He is doing. From the young one forthwith then</p>
<p>Could grim-worded greeting be got for him quickly</p>
<p>40</p>
<p>Whose courage had failed him. Wiglaf discoursed then,</p>
<p>Weohstan his son, sad-mooded hero,</p>
<p>He begins to taunt them.</p>
<p>Looked on the hated: “He who soothness will utter</p>
<p>Can say that the liegelord who gave you the jewels,</p>
<p>The ornament-armor wherein ye are standing,</p>
<p>45</p>
<p>When on ale-bench often he offered to hall-men</p>
<p>Helmet and burnie, the prince to his liegemen,</p>
<p>As best upon earth he was able to find him,—</p>
<p>Surely our lord wasted his armor on poltroons.</p>
<p>That he wildly wasted his war-gear undoubtedly</p>
<p>When battle o’ertook him.<sup>2</sup> The troop-king no need had</p>
<p>50</p>
<p>To glory in comrades; yet God permitted him,</p>
<p>He, however, got along without you</p>
<p>Victory-Wielder, with weapon unaided</p>
<p>Himself to avenge, when vigor was needed.</p>
<p>I life-protection but little was able</p>
<p>To give him in battle, and I ’gan, notwithstanding,</p>
<p>With some aid, I could have saved our liegelord 55</p>
<p>Helping my kinsman (my strength overtaxing):</p>
<p>He waxed the weaker when with weapon I smote on</p>
<p>My mortal opponent, the fire less strongly</p>
<p>Flamed from his bosom. Too few of protectors</p>
<p>Came round the king at the critical moment.</p>
<p>Gift-giving is over with your people: the ring-lord is dead. 60</p>
<p>Now must ornament-taking and weapon-bestowing,</p>
<p>Home-joyance all, cease for your kindred,</p>
<p>Food for the people; each of your warriors</p>
<p>Must needs be bereavèd of rights that he holdeth</p>
<p>In landed possessions, when faraway nobles</p>
<p>65</p>
<p>Shall learn of your leaving your lord so basely,</p>
<p>What is life without honor?</p>
<p>The dastardly deed. Death is more pleasant</p>
<p>To every earlman than infamous life is!”</p>
