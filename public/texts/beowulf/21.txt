<p>Heorot is adorned with hands.</p>
<p>Then straight was ordered that Heorot inside<sup>1</sup></p>
<p>With hands be embellished: a host of them gathered,</p>
<p>Of men and women, who the wassailing-building</p>
<p>The guest-hall begeared. Gold-flashing sparkled</p>
<p>5</p>
<p>Webs on the walls then, of wonders a many</p>
<p>To each of the heroes that look on such objects.</p>
<p>The hall is defaced, however.</p>
<p>The beautiful building was broken to pieces</p>
<p>Which all within with irons was fastened,</p>
<p>Its hinges torn off: only the roof was</p>
<p>10</p>
<p>Whole and uninjured when the horrible creature</p>
<p>Outlawed for evil off had betaken him,</p>
<p>Hopeless of living. ’Tis hard to avoid it</p>
<p>[A vague passage of five verses.]</p>
<p>(Whoever will do it!); but he doubtless must come to<sup>2</sup></p>
<p>The place awaiting, as Wyrd hath appointed,</p>
<p>15</p>
<p>Soul-bearers, earth-dwellers, earls under heaven,</p>
<p>Where bound on its bed his body shall slumber</p>
<p>Hrothgar goes to the banquet.</p>
<p>When feasting is finished. Full was the time then</p>
<p>That the son of Healfdene went to the building;</p>
<p>The excellent atheling would eat of the banquet.</p>
<p>20</p>
<p>Ne’er heard I that people with hero-band larger</p>
<p>Bare them better tow’rds their bracelet-bestower.</p>
<p>The laden-with-glory stooped to the bench then</p>
<p>(Their kinsmen-companions in plenty were joyful,</p>
<p>Many a cupful quaffing complaisantly),</p>
<p>25</p>
<p>Doughty of spirit in the high-tow’ring palace,</p>
<p>Hrothgar’s nephew, Hrothulf, is present.</p>
<p>Hrothgar and Hrothulf. Heorot then inside</p>
<p>Was filled with friendly ones; falsehood and treachery</p>
<p>The Folk-Scyldings now nowise did practise.</p>
<p>Hrothgar lavishes gifts upon Beowulf.</p>
<p>Then the offspring of Healfdene offered to Beowulf</p>
<p>30</p>
<p>A golden standard, as reward for the victory,</p>
<p>A banner embossed, burnie and helmet;</p>
<p>Many men saw then a song-famous weapon</p>
<p>Borne ’fore the hero. Beowulf drank of</p>
<p>The cup in the building; that treasure-bestowing</p>
<p>35</p>
<p>He needed not blush for in battle-men’s presence.</p>
<p>Four handsomer gifts were never presented.</p>
<p>Ne’er heard I that many men on the ale-bench</p>
<p>In friendlier fashion to their fellows presented</p>
<p>Four bright jewels with gold-work embellished.</p>
<p>’Round the roof of the helmet a head-guarder outside</p>
<p>40</p>
<p>Braided with wires, with bosses was furnished,</p>
<p>That swords-for-the-battle fight-hardened might fail</p>
<p>Boldly to harm him, when the hero proceeded</p>
<p>Hrothgar commands that eight finely caparisoned steeds be brought to Beowulf.</p>
<p>Forth against foemen. The defender of earls then</p>
<p>Commanded that eight steeds with bridles</p>
<p>45</p>
<p>Gold-plated, gleaming, be guided to hallward,</p>
<p>Inside the building; on one of them stood then</p>
<p>An art-broidered saddle embellished with jewels;</p>
<p>’Twas the sovereign’s seat, when the son of King Healfdene</p>
<p>Was pleased to take part in the play of the edges;</p>
<p>50</p>
<p>The famous one’s valor ne’er failed at the front when</p>
<p>Slain ones were bowing. And to Beowulf granted</p>
<p>The prince of the Ingwins, power over both,</p>
<p>O’er war-steeds and weapons; bade him well to enjoy them.</p>
<p>In so manly a manner the mighty-famed chieftain,</p>
<p>55</p>
<p>Hoard-ward of heroes, with horses and jewels</p>
<p>War-storms requited, that none e’er condemneth</p>
<p>Who willeth to tell truth with full justice.</p>
<p>[1] Kl. suggests ‘hroden’ for ‘háten,’ and renders: <i>Then quickly was Heorot adorned within, with hands bedecked</i>.—B. suggests ‘gefrætwon’ instead of ‘gefrætwod,’ and renders: <i>Then was it commanded to adorn Heorot within quickly with hands</i>.—The former has the advantage of affording a parallel to ‘gefrætwod’: both have the disadvantage of altering the text.</p>
<p>[2] The passage 1005-1009 seems to be hopeless. One difficult point is to find a subject for ‘gesacan.’ Some say ‘he’; others supply ‘each,’ <i>i.e., every soul-bearer … must gain the inevitable place</i>. The genitives in this case are partitive.—If ‘he’ be subj., the genitives are dependent on ‘gearwe’ (= prepared).—The ‘he’ itself is disputed, some referring it to Grendel; but B. takes it as involved in the parenthesis.</p>
