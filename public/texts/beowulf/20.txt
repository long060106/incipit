<p>Hrothgar discoursed (to the hall-building went he,</p>
<p>He stood by the pillar,<sup>1</sup> saw the steep-rising hall-roof</p>
<p>Gleaming with gold-gems, and Grendel his hand there):</p>
<p>Hrothgar gives thanks for the overthrow of the monster.</p>
<p>“For the sight we behold now, thanks to the Wielder</p>
<p>5</p>
<p>Early be offered! Much evil I bided,</p>
<p>Snaring from Grendel:<sup>2</sup> God can e’er ’complish</p>
<p>Wonder on wonder, Wielder of Glory!</p>
<p>I had given up all hope, when this brave liegeman came to our aid.</p>
<p>But lately I reckoned ne’er under heaven</p>
<p>Comfort to gain me for any of sorrows,</p>
<p>10</p>
<p>While the handsomest of houses horrid with bloodstain</p>
<p>Gory uptowered; grief had offfrightened<sup>3</sup></p>
<p>Each of the wise ones who weened not that ever</p>
<p>The folk-troop’s defences ’gainst foes they should strengthen,</p>
<p>’Gainst sprites and monsters. Through the might of the Wielder</p>
<p>15</p>
<p>A doughty retainer hath a deed now accomplished</p>
<p>Which erstwhile we all with our excellent wisdom</p>
<p>If his mother yet liveth, well may she thank God for this son.</p>
<p>Failed to perform. May affirm very truly</p>
<p>What woman soever in all of the nations</p>
<p>Gave birth to the child, if yet she surviveth,</p>
<p>20</p>
<p>That the long-ruling Lord was lavish to herward</p>
<p>In the birth of the bairn. Now, Beowulf dear,</p>
<p>Hereafter, Beowulf, thou shalt be my son.</p>
<p>Most excellent hero, I’ll love thee in spirit</p>
<p>As bairn of my body; bear well henceforward</p>
<p>The relationship new. No lack shall befall thee</p>
<p>25</p>
<p>Of earth-joys any I ever can give thee.</p>
<p>Full often for lesser service I’ve given</p>
<p>Hero less hardy hoard-treasure precious,</p>
<p>Thou hast won immortal distinction.</p>
<p>To a weaker in war-strife. By works of distinction</p>
<p>Thou hast gained for thyself now that thy glory shall flourish</p>
<p>30</p>
<p>Forever and ever. The All-Ruler quite thee</p>
<p>With good from His hand as He hitherto did thee!”</p>
<p>Beowulf replies: I was most happy to render thee this service.</p>
<p>Beowulf answered, Ecgtheow’s offspring:</p>
<p>“That labor of glory most gladly achieved we,</p>
<p>The combat accomplished, unquailing we ventured</p>
<p>35</p>
<p>The enemy’s grapple; I would grant it much rather</p>
<p>Thou wert able to look at the creature in person,</p>
<p>Faint unto falling, the foe in his trappings!</p>
<p>On murder-bed quickly I minded to bind him,</p>
<p>With firm-holding fetters, that forced by my grapple</p>
<p>40</p>
<p>Low he should lie in life-and-death struggle</p>
<p>’Less his body escape; I was wholly unable,</p>
<p>I could not keep the monster from escaping, as God did not will that I should.</p>
<p>Since God did not will it, to keep him from going,</p>
<p>Not held him that firmly, hated opposer;</p>
<p>Too swift was the foeman. Yet safety regarding</p>
<p>45</p>
<p>He suffered his hand behind him to linger,</p>
<p>His arm and shoulder, to act as watcher;</p>
<p>He left his hand and arm behind.</p>
<p>No shadow of solace the woe-begone creature</p>
<p>Found him there nathless: the hated destroyer</p>
<p>Liveth no longer, lashed for his evils,</p>
<p>50</p>
<p>But sorrow hath seized him, in snare-meshes hath him</p>
<p>Close in its clutches, keepeth him writhing</p>
<p>In baleful bonds: there banished for evil</p>
<p>The man shall wait for the mighty tribunal,</p>
<p>God will give him his deserts.</p>
<p>How the God of glory shall give him his earnings.”</p>
<p>55</p>
<p>Then the soldier kept silent, son of old Ecglaf,</p>
<p>Unferth has nothing more to say, for Beowulf’s actions speak louder than words.</p>
<p>From boasting and bragging of battle-achievements,</p>
<p>Since the princes beheld there the hand that depended</p>
<p>’Neath the lofty hall-timbers by the might of the nobleman,</p>
<p>Each one before him, the enemy’s fingers;</p>
<p>60</p>
<p>Each finger-nail strong steel most resembled,</p>
<p>The heathen one’s hand-spur, the hero-in-battle’s</p>
<p>Claw most uncanny; quoth they agreeing,</p>
<p>No sword will harm the monster.</p>
<p>That not any excellent edges of brave ones</p>
<p>Was willing to touch him, the terrible creature’s</p>
<p>65</p>
<p>Battle-hand bloody to bear away from him.</p>
<p>[1] B. and t.B. read ‘staþole,’ and translate <i>stood on the floor</i>.</p>
<p>[2] For ‘snaring from Grendel,’ ‘sorrows at Grendel’s hands’ has been suggested. This gives a parallel to ‘láðes.’ ‘Grynna’ may well be gen. pl. of ‘gyrn,’ by a scribal slip.</p>
<p>[3] The H.-So punctuation has been followed; but B. has been followed in understanding ‘gehwylcne’ as object of ‘wíd-scofen (hæfde).’ Gr. construes ‘wéa’ as nom abs.</p>
