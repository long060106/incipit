<p class="lede">The furies⁠—The angel⁠—The city of Dis⁠—The Sixth Circle⁠—Heresiarchs.</p>
<p class="verse">That hue which cowardice brought out on me,<sup>124</sup> <br> Beholding my Conductor backward turn, <br> Sooner repressed within him his new color. <br> He stopped attentive, like a man who listens, <br> Because the eye could not conduct him far <br> Through the black air, and through the heavy fog. <br> “Still it behoveth us to win the fight,” <br> Began he; “Else⁠ ⁠… Such offered us herself⁠ ⁠…<sup>125</sup> <br> O how I long that someone here arrive!”<sup>126</sup> <br> Well I perceived, as soon as the beginning <br> He covered up with what came afterward, <br> That they were words quite different from the first; <br> But none the less his saying gave me fear, <br> Because I carried out the broken phrase, <br> Perhaps to a worse meaning than he had. <br> “Into this bottom of the doleful conch<sup>127</sup> <br> Doth any e’er descend from the first grade, <br> Which for its pain has only hope cut off?” <br> This question put I; and he answered me: <br> “Seldom it comes to pass that one of us <br> Maketh the journey upon which I go. <br> True is it, once before I here below <br> Was conjured by that pitiless Erictho, <br> Who summoned back the shades unto their bodies. <br> Naked of me short while the flesh had been, <br> Before within that wall she made me enter, <br> To bring a spirit from the circle of Judas; <br> That is the lowest region and the darkest, <br> And farthest from the heaven which circles all. <br> Well know I the way; therefore be reassured. <br> This fen, which a prodigious stench exhales, <br> Encompasses about the city dolent, <br> Where now we cannot enter without anger.” <br> And more he said, but not in mind I have it; <br> Because mine eye had altogether drawn me <br> Tow’rds the high tower with the red-flaming summit, <br> Where in a moment saw I swift uprisen <br> The three infernal Furies stained with blood, <br> Who had the limbs of women and their mien, <br> And with the greenest hydras were begirt; <br> Small serpents and cerastes were their tresses, <br> Wherewith their horrid temples were entwined. <br> And he who well the handmaids of the Queen <br> Of everlasting lamentation knew, <br> Said unto me: “Behold the fierce Erinnys. <br> This is Megaera, on the left-hand side; <br> She who is weeping on the right, Alecto; <br> Tisiphone is between”; and then was silent. <br> Each one her breast was rending with her nails; <br> They beat them with their palms, and cried so loud, <br> That I for dread pressed close unto the Poet. <br> “Medusa come, so we to stone will change him!”<sup>128</sup> <br> All shouted looking down; “in evil hour <br> Avenged we not on Theseus his assault!”<sup>129</sup> <br> “Turn thyself round, and keep thine eyes close shut, <br> For if the Gorgon appear, and thou shouldst see it, <br> No more returning upward would there be.” <br> Thus said the Master; and he turned me round <br> Himself, and trusted not unto my hands <br> So far as not to blind me with his own. <br> O ye who have undistempered intellects, <br> Observe the doctrine that conceals itself<sup>130</sup> <br> Beneath the veil of the mysterious verses! <br> And now there came across the turbid waves <br> The clangor of a sound with terror fraught, <br> Because of which both of the margins trembled; <br> Not otherwise it was than of a wind <br> Impetuous on account of adverse heats, <br> That smites the forest, and, without restraint, <br> The branches rends, beats down, and bears away; <br> Right onward, laden with dust, it goes superb, <br> And puts to flight the wild beasts and the shepherds. <br> Mine eyes he loosed, and said: “Direct the nerve <br> Of vision now along that ancient foam, <br> There yonder where that smoke is most intense.” <br> Even as the frogs before the hostile serpent <br> Across the water scatter all abroad, <br> Until each one is huddled in the earth, <br> More than a thousand ruined souls I saw, <br> Thus fleeing from before one who on foot <br> Was passing o’er the Styx with soles unwet. <br> From off his face he fanned that unctuous air, <br> Waving his left hand oft in front of him, <br> And only with that anguish seemed he weary. <br> Well I perceived one sent from Heaven was he, <br> And to the Master turned; and he made sign <br> That I should quiet stand, and bow before him. <br> Ah! how disdainful he appeared to me! <br> He reached the gate, and with a little rod <br> He opened it, for there was no resistance. <br> “O banished out of Heaven, people despised!” <br> Thus he began upon the horrid threshold; <br> “Whence is this arrogance within you couched? <br> Wherefore recalcitrate against that will, <br> From which the end can never be cut off, <br> And which has many times increased your pain? <br> What helpeth it to butt against the fates? <br> Your Cerberus, if you remember well, <br> For that still bears his chin and gullet peeled.” <br> Then he returned along the miry road, <br> And spake no word to us, but had the look <br> Of one whom other care constrains and goads <br> Than that of him who in his presence is; <br> And we our feet directed tow’rds the city, <br> After those holy words all confident. <br> Within we entered without any contest; <br> And I, who inclination had to see <br> What the condition such a fortress holds, <br> Soon as I was within, cast round mine eye, <br> And see on every hand an ample plain, <br> Full of distress and torment terrible. <br> Even as at Arles, where stagnant grows the Rhone,<sup>131</sup> <br> Even as at Pola near to the Quarnaro,<sup>132</sup> <br> That shuts in Italy and bathes its borders, <br> The sepulchres make all the place uneven; <br> So likewise did they there on every side, <br> Saving that there the manner was more bitter; <br> For flames between the sepulchres were scattered, <br> By which they so intensely heated were, <br> That iron more so asks not any art. <br> All of their coverings uplifted were, <br> And from them issued forth such dire laments, <br> Sooth seemed they of the wretched and tormented. <br> And I: “My Master, what are all those people <br> Who, having sepulture within those tombs, <br> Make themselves audible by doleful sighs?” <br> And he to me: “Here are the Heresiarchs, <br> With their disciples of all sects, and much <br> More than thou thinkest laden are the tombs. <br> Here like together with its like is buried; <br> And more and less the monuments are heated.” <br> And when he to the right had turned, we passed <br> Between the torments and high parapets.</p>
