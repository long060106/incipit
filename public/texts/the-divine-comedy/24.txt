<p class="lede">The Sixth Bolgia: hypocrites⁠—Catalano and Loderingo⁠—Caiaphas.</p>
<p class="verse">Silent, alone, and without company<sup>327</sup> <br> We went, the one in front, the other after, <br> As go the Minor Friars along their way. <br> Upon the fable of Aesop was directed<sup>328</sup> <br> My thought, by reason of the present quarrel, <br> Where he has spoken of the frog and mouse; <br> For <i>mo</i> and <i>issa</i> are not more alike<sup>329</sup> <br> Than this one is to that, if well we couple <br> End and beginning with a steadfast mind. <br> And even as one thought from another springs, <br> So afterward from that was born another, <br> Which the first fear within me double made. <br> Thus did I ponder: “These on our account <br> Are laughed to scorn, with injury and scoff <br> So great, that much I think it must annoy them. <br> If anger be engrafted on ill-will, <br> They will come after us more merciless <br> Than dog upon the leveret which he seizes,” <br> I felt my hair stand all on end already <br> With terror, and stood backwardly intent, <br> When said I: “Master, if thou hidest not <br> Thyself and me forthwith, of Malebranche <br> I am in dread; we have them now behind us; <br> I so imagine them, I already feel them.” <br> And he: “If I were made of leaded glass, <br> Thine outward image I should not attract <br> Sooner to me than I imprint the inner. <br> Just now thy thoughts came in among my own, <br> With similar attitude and similar face, <br> So that of both one counsel sole I made. <br> If peradventure the right bank so slope <br> That we to the next Bolgia can descend, <br> We shall escape from the imagined chase.” <br> Not yet he finished rendering such opinion, <br> When I beheld them come with outstretched wings, <br> Not far remote, with will to seize upon us. <br> My Leader on a sudden seized me up,<sup>330</sup> <br> Even as a mother who by noise is wakened, <br> And close beside her sees the enkindled flames, <br> Who takes her son, and flies, and does not stop, <br> Having more care of him than of herself, <br> So that she clothes her only with a shift; <br> And downward from the top of the hard bank <br> Supine he gave him to the pendent rock, <br> That one side of the other Bolgia walls. <br> Ne’er ran so swiftly water through a sluice <br> To turn the wheel of any land-built mill, <br> When nearest to the paddles it approaches, <br> As did my Master down along that border, <br> Bearing me with him on his breast away, <br> As his own son, and not as a companion. <br> Hardly the bed of the ravine below <br> His feet had reached, ere they had reached the hill <br> Right over us; but he was not afraid; <br> For the high Providence, which had ordained <br> To place them ministers of the fifth moat, <br> The power of thence departing took from all. <br> A painted people there below we found, <br> Who went about with footsteps very slow, <br> Weeping and in their semblance tired and vanquished. <br> They had on mantles with the hoods low down <br> Before their eyes, and fashioned of the cut <br> That in Cologne they for the monks are made.<sup>331</sup> <br> Without, they gilded are so that it dazzles; <br> But inwardly all leaden and so heavy <br> That Frederick used to put them on of straw.<sup>332</sup> <br> O everlastingly fatiguing mantle!<sup>333</sup> <br> Again we turned us, still to the left hand <br> Along with them, intent on their sad plaint; <br> But owing to the weight, that weary folk <br> Came on so tardily, that we were new <br> In company at each motion of the haunch. <br> Whence I unto my Leader: “See thou find <br> Someone who may by deed or name be known, <br> And thus in going move thine eye about.” <br> And one, who understood the Tuscan speech, <br> Cried to us from behind: “Stay ye your feet, <br> Ye, who so run athwart the dusky air! <br> Perhaps thou’lt have from me what thou demandest.” <br> Whereat the Leader turned him, and said: “Wait, <br> And then according to his pace proceed.” <br> I stopped, and two beheld I show great haste <br> Of spirit, in their faces, to be with me; <br> But the burden and the narrow way delayed them. <br> When they came up, long with an eye askance <br> They scanned me without uttering a word. <br> Then to each other turned, and said together: <br> “He by the action of his throat seems living; <br> And if they dead are, by what privilege <br> Go they uncovered by the heavy stole?” <br> Then said to me: “Tuscan, who to the college<sup>334</sup> <br> Of miserable hypocrites art come, <br> Do not disdain to tell us who thou art.” <br> And I to them: “Born was I, and grew up <br> In the great town on the fair river of Arno,<sup>335</sup> <br> And with the body am I’ve always had. <br> But who are ye, in whom there trickles down <br> Along your cheeks such grief as I behold? <br> And what pain is upon you, that so sparkles?” <br> And one replied to me: “These orange cloaks <br> Are made of lead so heavy, that the weights <br> Cause in this way their balances to creak. <br> Frati Gaudenti were we, and Bolognese;<sup>336</sup> <br> I Catalano, and he Loderingo <br> Named, and together taken by thy city, <br> As the wont is to take one man alone, <br> For maintenance of its peace; and we were such <br> That still it is apparent round Gardingo.”<sup>337</sup> <br> “O Friars,” began I, “your iniquitous⁠ ⁠…” <br> But said no more; for to mine eyes there rushed <br> One crucified with three stakes on the ground. <br> When me he saw, he writhed himself all over, <br> Blowing into his beard with suspirations;<sup>338</sup> <br> And the Friar Catalan, who noticed this, <br> Said to me: “This transfixed one, whom thou seest,<sup>339</sup> <br> Counselled the Pharisees that it was meet <br> To put one man to torture for the people. <br> Crosswise and naked is he on the path, <br> As thou perceivest; and he needs must feel, <br> Whoever passes, first how much he weighs; <br> And in like mode his father-in-law is punished<sup>340</sup> <br> Within this moat, and the others of the council, <br> Which for the Jews was a malignant seed.” <br> And thereupon I saw Virgilius marvel <br> O’er him who was extended on the cross <br> So vilely in eternal banishment. <br> Then he directed to the Friar this voice: <br> “Be not displeased, if granted thee, to tell us <br> If to the right hand any pass slope down <br> By which we two may issue forth from here, <br> Without constraining some of the black angels <br> To come and extricate us from this deep.” <br> Then he made answer: “Nearer than thou hopest <br> There is a rock, that forth from the great circle<sup>341</sup> <br> Proceeds, and crosses all the cruel valleys, <br> Save that at this ’tis broken, and does not bridge it; <br> You will be able to mount up the ruin, <br> That sidelong slopes and at the bottom rises.” <br> The Leader stood awhile with head bowed down; <br> Then said: “The business badly he recounted <br> Who grapples with his hook the sinners yonder.” <br> And the Friar: “Many of the Devil’s vices<sup>342</sup> <br> Once heard I at Bologna, and among them, <br> That he’s a liar and the father of lies.” <br> Thereat my Leader with great strides went on, <br> Somewhat disturbed with anger in his looks; <br> Whence from the heavy-laden I departed <br> After the prints of his beloved feet.</p>
