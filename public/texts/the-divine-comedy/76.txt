<p class="lede">Justinian⁠—The Roman eagle⁠—Romeo.</p>
<p class="verse">“After that Constantine the eagle turned<sup>1282</sup> <br> Against the course of heaven, which it had followed <br> Behind the ancient who Lavinia took, <br> Two hundred years and more the bird of God<sup>1283</sup> <br> In the extreme of Europe held itself,<sup>1284</sup> <br> Near to the mountains whence it issued first; <br> And under shadow of the sacred plumes <br> It governed there the world from hand to hand, <br> And, changing thus, upon mine own alighted. <br> Caesar I was, and am Justinian,<sup>1285</sup> <br> Who, by the will of primal Love I feel, <br> Took from the laws the useless and redundant;<sup>1286</sup> <br> And ere unto the work I was attent, <br> One nature to exist in Christ, not more,<sup>1287</sup> <br> Believed, and with such faith was I contented. <br> But blessed Agapetus, he who was<sup>1288</sup> <br> The supreme pastor, to the faith sincere <br> Pointed me out the way by words of his. <br> Him I believed, and what was his assertion <br> I now see clearly, even as thou seest <br> Each contradiction to be false and true. <br> As soon as with the Church I moved my feet, <br> God in his grace it pleased with this high task <br> To inspire me, and I gave me wholly to it, <br> And to my Belisarius I commended<sup>1289</sup> <br> The arms, to which was heaven’s right hand so joined <br> It was a signal that I should repose. <br> Now here to the first question terminates <br> My answer; but the character thereof <br> Constrains me to continue with a sequel, <br> In order that thou see with how great reason <br> Men move against the standard sacrosanct, <br> Both who appropriate and who oppose it. <br> Behold how great a power has made it worthy <br> Of reverence, beginning from the hour <br> When Pallas died to give it sovereignty.<sup>1290</sup> <br> Thou knowest it made in Alba its abode<sup>1291</sup> <br> Three hundred years and upward, till at last <br> The three to three fought for it yet again.<sup>1292</sup> <br> Thou knowest what it achieved from Sabine wrong<sup>1293</sup> <br> Down to Lucretia’s sorrow, in seven kings <br> O’ercoming round about the neighboring nations; <br> Thou knowest what it achieved, borne by the Romans <br> Illustrious against Brennus, against Pyrrhus,<sup>1294</sup> <br> Against the other princes and confederates. <br> Torquatus thence and Quinctius, who from locks<sup>1295</sup> <br> Unkempt was named, Decii and Fabii,<sup>1296</sup> <br> Received the fame I willingly embalm; <br> It struck to earth the pride of the Arabians, <br> Who, following Hannibal, had passed across <br> The Alpine ridges, Po, from which thou glidest; <br> Beneath it triumphed while they yet were young <br> Pompey and Scipio, and to the hill<sup>1297</sup> <br> Beneath which thou wast born it bitter seemed; <br> Then, near unto the time when heaven had willed<sup>1298</sup> <br> To bring the whole world to its mood serene, <br> Did Caesar by the will of Rome assume it. <br> What it achieved from Var unto the Rhine, <br> Isere beheld and Saone, beheld the Seine, <br> And every valley whence the Rhone is filled; <br> What it achieved when it had left Ravenna, <br> And leaped the Rubicon, was such a flight <br> That neither tongue nor pen could follow it. <br> Round towards Spain it wheeled its legions; then <br> Towards Durazzo, and Pharsalia smote<sup>1299</sup> <br> That to the calid Nile was felt the pain.<sup>1300</sup> <br> Antandros and the Simois, whence it started,<sup>1301</sup> <br> It saw again, and there where Hector lies, <br> And ill for Ptolemy then roused itself.<sup>1302</sup> <br> From thence it came like lightning upon Juba;<sup>1303</sup> <br> Then wheeled itself again into your West,<sup>1304</sup> <br> Where the Pompeian clarion it heard. <br> From what it wrought with the next standard-bearer<sup>1305</sup> <br> Brutus and Cassius howl in Hell together, <br> And Modena and Perugia dolent were;<sup>1306</sup> <br> Still doth the mournful Cleopatra weep <br> Because thereof, who, fleeing from before it, <br> Took from the adder sudden and black death. <br> With him it ran even to the Red Sea shore; <br> With him it placed the world in so great peace, <br> That unto Janus was his temple closed.<sup>1307</sup> <br> But what the standard that has made me speak <br> Achieved before, and after should achieve <br> Throughout the mortal realm that lies beneath it, <br> Becometh in appearance mean and dim, <br> If in the hand of the third Caesar seen<sup>1308</sup> <br> With eye unclouded and affection pure, <br> Because the living Justice that inspires me <br> Granted it, in the hand of him I speak of, <br> The glory of doing vengeance for its wrath.<sup>1309</sup> <br> Now here attend to what I answer thee; <br> Later it ran with Titus to do vengeance<sup>1310</sup> <br> Upon the vengeance of the ancient sin. <br> And when the tooth of Lombardy had bitten<sup>1311</sup> <br> The Holy Church, then underneath its wings <br> Did Charlemagne victorious succor her. <br> Now hast thou power to judge of such as those <br> Whom I accused above, and of their crimes,<sup>1312</sup> <br> Which are the cause of all your miseries. <br> To the public standard one the yellow lilies<sup>1313</sup> <br> Opposes, the other claims it for a party, <br> So that ’tis hard to see which sins the most. <br> Let, let the Ghibellines ply their handicraft <br> Beneath some other standard; for this ever <br> Ill follows he who it and justice parts. <br> And let not this new Charles e’er strike it down,<sup>1314</sup> <br> He and his Guelfs, but let him fear the talons <br> That from a nobler lion stripped the fell. <br> Already oftentimes the sons have wept <br> The father’s crime; and let him not believe <br> That God will change His scutcheon for the lilies.<sup>1315</sup> <br> This little planet doth adorn itself<sup>1316</sup> <br> With the good spirits that have active been, <br> That fame and honor might come after them;<sup>1317</sup> <br> And whensoever the desires mount thither, <br> Thus deviating, must perforce the rays <br> Of the true love less vividly mount upward. <br> But in commensuration of our wages <br> With our desert is portion of our joy, <br> Because we see them neither less nor greater. <br> Herein doth living Justice sweeten so<sup>1318</sup> <br> Affection in us, that for evermore <br> It cannot warp to any iniquity. <br> Voices diverse make up sweet melodies; <br> So in this life of ours the seats diverse <br> Render sweet harmony among these spheres; <br> And in the compass of this present pearl <br> Shineth the sheen of Romeo, of whom<sup>1319</sup> <br> The grand and beauteous work was ill rewarded. <br> But the Provençals who against him wrought, <br> They have not laughed, and therefore ill goes he <br> Who makes his hurt of the good deeds of others. <br> Four daughters, and each one of them a queen, <br> Had Raymond Berenger, and this for him <br> Did Romeo, a poor man and a pilgrim; <br> And then malicious words incited him <br> To summon to a reckoning this just man, <br> Who rendered to him seven and five for ten. <br> Then he departed poor and stricken in years, <br> And if the world could know the heart he had, <br> In begging bit by bit his livelihood, <br> Though much it laud him, it would laud him more.”<sup>1320</sup></p>
