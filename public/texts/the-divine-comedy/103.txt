<p class="lede">Prayer to the Virgin⁠—The Threefold Circle of the Trinity⁠—Mystery of the divine and human nature.</p>
<p class="verse">“Thou Virgin Mother, daughter of thy Son,<sup>2087</sup> <br> Humble and high beyond all other creature, <br> The limit fixed of the eternal counsel, <br> Thou art the one who such nobility <br> To human nature gave, that its Creator <br> Did not disdain to make himself its creature. <br> Within thy womb rekindled was the love, <br> By heat of which in the eternal peace <br> After such wise this flower has germinated. <br> Here unto us thou art a noonday torch <br> Of charity, and below there among mortals <br> Thou art the living fountainhead of hope. <br> Lady, thou art so great, and so prevailing, <br> That he who wishes grace, nor runs to thee, <br> His aspirations without wings would fly. <br> Not only thy benignity gives succor <br> To him who asketh it, but oftentimes <br> Forerunneth of its own accord the asking. <br> In thee compassion is, in thee is pity, <br> In thee magnificence; in thee unites <br> Whate’er of goodness is in any creature. <br> Now doth this man, who from the lowest depth <br> Of the universe as far as here has seen <br> One after one the spiritual lives, <br> Supplicate thee through grace for so much power <br> That with his eyes he may uplift himself <br> Higher towards the uttermost salvation. <br> And I, who never burned for my own seeing <br> More than I do for his, all of my prayers <br> Proffer to thee, and pray they come not short, <br> That thou wouldst scatter from him every cloud <br> Of his mortality so with thy prayers, <br> That the Chief Pleasure be to him displayed. <br> Still farther do I pray thee, Queen, who canst <br> Whate’er thou wilt, that sound thou mayst preserve <br> After so great a vision his affections.<sup>2088</sup> <br> Let thy protection conquer human movements; <br> See Beatrice and all the blessed ones <br> My prayers to second clasp their hands to thee!” <br> The eyes beloved and revered of God, <br> Fastened upon the speaker, showed to us <br> How grateful unto her are prayers devout; <br> Then unto the Eternal Light they turned, <br> On which it is not credible could be <br> By any creature bent an eye so clear. <br> And I, who to the end of all desires <br> Was now approaching, even as I ought <br> The ardor of desire within me ended.<sup>2089</sup> <br> Bernard was beckoning unto me, and smiling, <br> That I should upward look; but I already <br> Was of my own accord such as he wished; <br> Because my sight, becoming purified, <br> Was entering more and more into the ray <br> Of the High Light which of itself is true. <br> From that time forward what I saw was greater <br> Than our discourse, that to such vision yields, <br> And yields the memory unto such excess. <br> Even as he is who seeth in a dream, <br> And after dreaming the imprinted passion <br> Remains, and to his mind the rest returns not, <br> Even such am I, for almost utterly <br> Ceases my vision, and distilleth yet <br> Within my heart the sweetness born of it; <br> Even thus the snow is in the sun unsealed, <br> Even thus upon the wind in the light leaves <br> Were the soothsayings of the Sibyl lost.<sup>2090</sup> <br> O Light Supreme, that dost so far uplift thee <br> From the conceits of mortals, to my mind <br> Of what thou didst appear re-lend a little, <br> And make my tongue of so great puissance, <br> That but a single sparkle of thy glory <br> It may bequeath unto the future people; <br> For by returning to my memory somewhat, <br> And by a little sounding in these verses, <br> More of thy victory shall be conceived! <br> I think the keenness of the living ray <br> Which I endured would have bewildered me, <br> If but mine eyes had been averted from it;<sup>2091</sup> <br> And I remember that I was more bold <br> On this account to bear, so that I joined <br> My aspect with the Glory Infinite. <br> O grace abundant, by which I presumed <br> To fix my sight upon the Light Eternal, <br> So that the seeing I consumed therein! <br> I saw that in its depth far down is lying <br> Bound up with love together in one volume,<sup>2092</sup> <br> What through the universe in leaves is scattered; <br> Substance, and accident, and their operations, <br> All interfused together in such wise <br> That what I speak of is one simple light.<sup>2093</sup> <br> The universal fashion of this knot <br> Methinks I saw, since more abundantly <br> In saying this I feel that I rejoice. <br> One moment is more lethargy to me,<sup>2094</sup> <br> Than five and twenty centuries to the emprise <br> That startled Neptune with the shade of Argo! <br> My mind in this wise wholly in suspense, <br> Steadfast, immovable, attentive gazed, <br> And evermore with gazing grew enkindled. <br> In presence of that light one such becomes, <br> That to withdraw therefrom for other prospect <br> It is impossible he e’er consent; <br> Because the good, which object is of will,<sup>2095</sup> <br> Is gathered all in this, and out of it <br> That is defective which is perfect there. <br> Shorter henceforward will my language fall <br> Of what I yet remember, than an infant’s <br> Who still his tongue doth moisten at the breast. <br> Not because more than one unmingled semblance <br> Was in the living light on which I looked, <br> For it is always what it was before; <br> But through the sight, that fortified itself <br> In me by looking, one appearance only <br> To me was ever changing as I changed.<sup>2096</sup> <br> Within the deep and luminous subsistence<sup>2097</sup> <br> Of the High Light appeared to me three circles,<sup>2098</sup> <br> Of threefold color and of one dimension, <br> And by the second seemed the first reflected <br> As Iris is by Iris, and the third <br> Seemed fire that equally from both is breathed. <br> O how all speech is feeble and falls short <br> Of my conceit, and this to what I saw <br> Is such, ’tis not enough to call it little! <br> O Light Eterne, sole in thyself that dwellest, <br> Sole knowest thyself, and, known unto thyself <br> And knowing, lovest and smilest on thyself! <br> That circulation, which being thus conceived <br> Appeared in thee as a reflected light,<sup>2099</sup> <br> When somewhat contemplated by mine eyes, <br> Within itself, of its own very color <br> Seemed to me painted with our effigy,<sup>2100</sup> <br> Wherefore my sight was all absorbed therein. <br> As the geometrician, who endeavors <br> To square the circle, and discovers not, <br> By taking thought, the principle he wants, <br> Even such was I at that new apparition; <br> I wished to see how the image to the circle <br> Conformed itself, and how it there finds place; <br> But my own wings were not enough for this, <br> Had it not been that then my mind there smote <br> A flash of lightning, wherein came its wish.<sup>2101</sup> <br> Here vigor failed the lofty fantasy: <br> But now was turning my desire and will, <br> Even as a wheel that equally is moved,<sup>2102</sup> <br> The Love which moves the sun and the other stars.<sup>2103</sup></p>
