<p class="lede">Guido Guinicelli and Arnaldo Daniello.</p>
<p class="verse">While on the brink thus one before the other<sup>1037</sup> <br> We went upon our way, oft the good Master <br> Said: “Take thou heed! suffice it that I warn thee.” <br> On the right shoulder smote me now the sun, <br> That, raying out, already the whole west<sup>1038</sup> <br> Changed from its azure aspect into white. <br> And with my shadow did I make the flame <br> Appear more red; and even to such a sign <br> Shades saw I many, as they went, give heed. <br> This was the cause that gave them a beginning <br> To speak of me; and to themselves began they <br> To say: “That seems not a factitious body!”<sup>1039</sup> <br> Then towards me, as far as they could come, <br> Came certain of them, always with regard <br> Not to step forth where they would not be burned. <br> “O thou who goest, not from being slower <br> But reverent perhaps, behind the others, <br> Answer me, who in thirst and fire am burning. <br> Nor to me only is thine answer needful; <br> For all of these have greater thirst for it <br> Than for cold water Ethiop or Indian. <br> Tell us how is it that thou makest thyself <br> A wall unto the sun, as if thou hadst not <br> Entered as yet into the net of death.” <br> Thus one of them addressed me, and I straight <br> Should have revealed myself, were I not bent <br> On other novelty that then appeared. <br> For through the middle of the burning road <br> There came a people face to face with these, <br> Which held me in suspense with gazing at them. <br> There see I hastening upon either side <br> Each of the shades, and kissing one another <br> Without a pause, content with brief salute. <br> Thus in the middle of their brown battalions <br> Muzzle to muzzle one ant meets another <br> Perchance to spy their journey or their fortune. <br> No sooner is the friendly greeting ended, <br> Or ever the first footstep passes onward, <br> Each one endeavors to outcry the other; <br> The new-come people: “Sodom and Gomorrah!” <br> The rest: “Into the cow Pasiphae enters,<sup>1040</sup> <br> So that the bull unto her lust may run!” <br> Then as the cranes, that to Riphaean mountains<sup>1041</sup> <br> Might fly in part, and part towards the sands, <br> These of the frost, those of the sun avoidant, <br> One folk is going, and the other coming, <br> And weeping they return to their first songs, <br> And to the cry that most befitteth them; <br> And close to me approached, even as before, <br> The very same who had entreated me, <br> Attent to listen in their countenance. <br> I, who their inclination twice had seen, <br> Began: “O souls secure in the possession, <br> Whene’er it may be, of a state of peace, <br> Neither unripe nor ripened have remained <br> My members upon earth, but here are with me <br> With their own blood and their articulations. <br> I go up here to be no longer blind; <br> A Lady is above, who wins this grace,<sup>1042</sup> <br> Whereby the mortal through your world I bring. <br> But as your greatest longing satisfied <br> May soon become, so that the Heaven may house you<sup>1043</sup> <br> Which full of love is, and most amply spreads, <br> Tell me, that I again in books may write it, <br> Who are you, and what is that multitude <br> Which goes upon its way behind your backs?” <br> Not otherwise with wonder is bewildered <br> The mountaineer, and staring round is dumb, <br> When rough and rustic to the town he goes, <br> Than every shade became in its appearance; <br> But when they of their stupor were disburdened, <br> Which in high hearts is quickly quieted, <br> “Blessed be thou, who of our border-lands,” <br> He recommenced who first had questioned us, <br> “Experience freightest for a better life. <br> The folk that comes not with us have offended <br> In that for which once Caesar, triumphing, <br> Heard himself called in contumely, ‘Queen.’<sup>1044</sup> <br> Therefore they separate, exclaiming, ‘Sodom!’ <br> Themselves reproving, even as thou hast heard, <br> And add unto their burning by their shame. <br> Our own transgression was hermaphrodite; <br> But because we observed not human law, <br> Following like unto beasts our appetite, <br> In our opprobrium by us is read, <br> When we part company, the name of her <br> Who bestialized herself in bestial wood.<sup>1045</sup> <br> Now knowest thou our acts, and what our crime was; <br> Wouldst thou perchance by name know who we are, <br> There is not time to tell, nor could I do it. <br> Thy wish to know me shall in sooth be granted; <br> I’m Guido Guinicelli, and now purge me,<sup>1046</sup> <br> Having repented ere the hour extreme.” <br> The same that in the sadness of Lycurgus<sup>1047</sup> <br> Two sons became, their mother re-beholding, <br> Such I became, but rise not to such height, <br> The moment I heard name himself the father <br> Of me and of my betters, who had ever <br> Practised the sweet and gracious rhymes of love; <br> And without speech and hearing thoughtfully <br> For a long time I went, beholding him, <br> Nor for the fire did I approach him nearer. <br> When I was fed with looking, utterly <br> Myself I offered ready for his service, <br> With affirmation that compels belief. <br> And he to me: “Thou leavest footprints such <br> In me, from what I hear, and so distinct, <br> Lethe cannot efface them, nor make dim. <br> But if thy words just now the truth have sworn, <br> Tell me what is the cause why thou displayest <br> In word and look that dear thou holdest me?” <br> And I to him: “Those dulcet lays of yours <br> Which, long as shall endure our modern fashion, <br> Shall make forever dear their very ink!” <br> “O brother,” said he, “he whom I point out,” <br> And here he pointed at a spirit in front, <br> “Was of the mother tongue a better smith. <br> Verses of love and proses of romance,<sup>1048</sup> <br> He mastered all; and let the idiots talk, <br> Who think the Lemosin surpasses him.<sup>1049</sup> <br> To clamor more than truth they turn their faces, <br> And in this way establish their opinion, <br> Ere art or reason has by them been heard. <br> Thus many ancients with Guittone did,<sup>1050</sup> <br> From cry to cry still giving him applause, <br> Until the truth has conquered with most persons. <br> Now, if thou hast such ample privilege <br> ’Tis granted thee to go unto the cloister <br> Wherein is Christ the abbot of the college, <br> To him repeat for me a Paternoster, <br> So far as needful to us of this world, <br> Where power of sinning is no longer ours.” <br> Then, to give place perchance to one behind, <br> Whom he had near, he vanished in the fire <br> As fish in water going to the bottom. <br> I moved a little tow’rds him pointed out, <br> And said that to his name my own desire<sup>1051</sup> <br> An honorable place was making ready. <br> He of his own free will began to say: <br>  <i>Tan m’ abellis vostre cortes deman,</i>  <br>  <i>Que jeu nom’ puesc ni vueill a vos cobrire;</i>  <br>  <i>Jeu sui Arnaut, que plor e vai chantan;</i><sup>1052</sup>  <br>  <i>Consiros vei la passada folor,</i>  <br>  <i>E vei jauzen lo jorn qu’ esper denan.</i>  <br>  <i>Ara vus prec per aquella valor,</i>  <br>  <i>Que vus condus al som de la scalina,</i>  <br>  <i>Sovenga vus a temprar ma dolor.</i><sup>1053</sup>  <br> Then hid him in the fire that purifies them.</p>
