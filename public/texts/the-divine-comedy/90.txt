<p class="lede">The eagle praises the righteous kings of old.</p>
<p class="verse">When he who all the world illuminates<sup>1746</sup> <br> Out of our hemisphere so far descends <br> That on all sides the daylight is consumed,<sup>1747</sup> <br> The heaven, that erst by him alone was kindled, <br> Doth suddenly reveal itself again<sup>1748</sup> <br> By many lights, wherein is one resplendent. <br> And came into my mind this act of heaven, <br> When the ensign of the world and of its leaders <br> Had silent in the blessed beak become; <br> Because those living luminaries all, <br> By far more luminous, did songs begin <br> Lapsing and falling from my memory. <br> O gentle Love, that with a smile dost cloak thee, <br> How ardent in those sparks didst thou appear, <br> That had the breath alone of holy thoughts! <br> After the precious and pellucid crystals, <br> With which begemmed the sixth light I beheld, <br> Silence imposed on the angelic bells, <br> I seemed to hear the murmuring of a river <br> That clear descendeth down from rock to rock, <br> Showing the affluence of its mountaintop. <br> And as the sound upon the cithern’s neck <br> Taketh its form, and as upon the vent <br> Of rustic pipe the wind that enters it, <br> Even thus, relieved from the delay of waiting, <br> That murmuring of the eagle mounted up <br> Along its neck, as if it had been hollow. <br> There it became a voice, and issued thence <br> From out its beak, in such a form of words <br> As the heart waited for wherein I wrote them. <br> “The part in me which sees and bears the sun <br> In mortal eagles,” it began to me, <br> “Now fixedly must needs be looked upon; <br> For of the fires of which I make my figure, <br> Those whence the eye doth sparkle in my head <br> Of all their orders the supremest are. <br> He who is shining in the midst as pupil<sup>1749</sup> <br> Was once the singer of the Holy Spirit, <br> Who bore the ark from city unto city; <br> Now knoweth he the merit of his song, <br> In so far as effect of his own counsel,<sup>1750</sup> <br> By the reward which is commensurate. <br> Of five, that make a circle for my brow, <br> He that approacheth nearest to my beak<sup>1751</sup> <br> Did the poor widow for her son console; <br> Now knoweth he how dearly it doth cost <br> Not following Christ, by the experience <br> Of this sweet life and of its opposite. <br> He who comes next in the circumference<sup>1752</sup> <br> Of which I speak, upon its highest arc, <br> Did death postpone by penitence sincere;<sup>1753</sup> <br> Now knoweth he that the eternal judgment <br> Suffers no change, albeit worthy prayer <br> Maketh below tomorrow of today. <br> The next who follows, with the laws and me,<sup>1754</sup> <br> Under the good intent that bore bad fruit<sup>1755</sup> <br> Became a Greek by ceding to the pastor; <br> Now knoweth he how all the ill deduced <br> From his good action is not harmful to him, <br> Although the world thereby may be destroyed. <br> And he, whom in the downward arc thou seest, <br> Guglielmo was, whom the same land deplores<sup>1756</sup> <br> That weepeth Charles and Frederick yet alive; <br> Now knoweth he how heaven enamoured is <br> With a just king; and in the outward show <br> Of his effulgence he reveals it still. <br> Who would believe, down in the errant world, <br> That e’er the Trojan Ripheus in this round<sup>1757</sup> <br> Could be the fifth one of the holy lights? <br> Now knoweth he enough of what the world <br> Has not the power to see of grace divine, <br> Although his sight may not discern the bottom.” <br> Like as a lark that in the air expatiates,<sup>1758</sup> <br> First singing and then silent with content <br> Of the last sweetness that doth satisfy her, <br> Such seemed to me the image of the imprint <br> Of the eternal pleasure, by whose will <br> Doth everything become the thing it is. <br> And notwithstanding to my doubt I was <br> As glass is to the color that invests it, <br> To wait the time in silence it endured not, <br> But forth from out my mouth, “What things are these?” <br> Extorted with the force of its own weight; <br> Whereat I saw great joy of coruscation. <br> Thereafterward with eye still more enkindled <br> The blessed standard made to me reply, <br> To keep me not in wonderment suspended: <br> “I see that thou believest in these things <br> Because I say them, but thou seest not how; <br> So that, although believed in, they are hidden. <br> Thou doest as he doth who a thing by name <br> Well apprehendeth, but its quiddity<sup>1759</sup> <br> Cannot perceive, unless another show it. <br> <i>Regnum coelorum</i> suffereth violence<sup>1760</sup> <br> From fervent love, and from that living hope <br> That overcometh the Divine volition; <br> Not in the guise that man o’ercometh man, <br> But conquers it because it will be conquered, <br> And conquered conquers by benignity. <br> The first life of the eyebrow and the fifth<sup>1761</sup> <br> Cause thee astonishment, because with them <br> Thou seest the region of the angels painted. <br> They passed not from their bodies, as thou thinkest, <br> Gentiles, but Christians in the steadfast faith <br> Of feet that were to suffer and had suffered.<sup>1762</sup> <br> For one from Hell, where no one e’er turns back<sup>1763</sup> <br> Unto good will, returned unto his bones, <br> And that of living hope was the reward⁠— <br> Of living hope, that placed its efficacy <br> In prayers to God made to resuscitate him, <br> So that ’twere possible to move his will.<sup>1764</sup> <br> The glorious soul concerning which I speak,<sup>1765</sup> <br> Returning to the flesh, where brief its stay, <br> Believed in Him who had the power to aid it; <br> And, in believing, kindled to such fire <br> Of genuine love, that at the second death <br> Worthy it was to come unto this joy. <br> The other one, through grace, that from so deep<sup>1766</sup> <br> A fountain wells that never hath the eye <br> Of any creature reached its primal wave, <br> Set all his love below on righteousness; <br> Wherefore from grace to grace did God unclose <br> His eye to our redemption yet to be, <br> Whence he believed therein, and suffered not <br> From that day forth the stench of paganism, <br> And he reproved therefor the folk perverse. <br> Those Maidens three, whom at the right-hand wheel<sup>1767</sup> <br> Thou didst behold, were unto him for baptism <br> More than a thousand years before baptizing. <br> O thou predestination, how remote<sup>1768</sup> <br> Thy root is from the aspect of all those <br> Who the First Cause do not behold entire! <br> And you, O mortals! hold yourselves restrained <br> In judging; for ourselves, who look on God, <br> We do not know as yet all the elect; <br> And sweet to us is such a deprivation, <br> Because our good in this good is made perfect, <br> That whatsoe’er God wills, we also will.” <br> After this manner by that shape divine, <br> To make clear in me my shortsightedness, <br> Was given to me a pleasant medicine; <br> And as good singer a good lutanist <br> Accompanies with vibrations of the chords, <br> Whereby more pleasantness the song acquires, <br> So, while it spake, do I remember me <br> That I beheld both of those blessed lights, <br> Even as the winking of the eyes concords, <br> Moving unto the words their little flames.</p>
