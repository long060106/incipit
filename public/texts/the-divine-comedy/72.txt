<p class="lede">The First Heaven, or that of the moon, in which are seen the spirits of those who, having taken monastic vows, were forced to violate them.</p>
<p class="verse">O Ye, who in some pretty little boat,<sup>1219</sup> <br> Eager to listen, have been following <br> Behind my ship, that singing sails along, <br> Turn back to look again upon your shores; <br> Do not put out to sea, lest peradventure, <br> In losing me, you might yourselves be lost. <br> The sea I sail has never yet been passed; <br> Minerva breathes, and pilots me Apollo,<sup>1220</sup> <br> And Muses nine point out to me the Bears. <br> Ye other few who have the neck uplifted <br> Betimes to th’ bread of Angels upon which<sup>1221</sup> <br> One liveth here and grows not sated by it, <br> Well may you launch upon the deep salt-sea <br> Your vessel, keeping still my wake before you <br> Upon the water that grows smooth again. <br> Those glorious ones who unto Colchos passed<sup>1222</sup> <br> Were not so wonder-struck as you shall be, <br> When Jason they beheld a ploughman made! <br> The con-created and perpetual thirst<sup>1223</sup> <br> For the realm deiform did bear us on, <br> As swift almost as ye the heavens behold. <br> Upward gazed Beatrice, and I at her; <br> And in such space perchance as strikes a bolt<sup>1224</sup> <br> And flies, and from the notch unlocks itself, <br> Arrived I saw me where a wondrous thing <br> Drew to itself my sight; and therefore she <br> From whom no care of mine could be concealed, <br> Towards me turning, blithe as beautiful, <br> Said unto me: “Fix gratefully thy mind <br> On God, who unto the first star has brought us.” <br> It seemed to me a cloud encompassed us, <br> Luminous, dense, consolidate and bright <br> As adamant on which the sun is striking. <br> Into itself did the eternal pearl <br> Receive us, even as water doth receive <br> A ray of light, remaining still unbroken. <br> If I was body, (and we here conceive not <br> How one dimension tolerates another, <br> Which needs must be if body enter body,) <br> More the desire should be enkindled in us <br> That essence to behold, wherein is seen <br> How God and our own nature were united. <br> There will be seen what we receive by faith, <br> Not demonstrated, but self-evident <br> In guise of the first truth that man believes. <br> I made reply: “Madonna, as devoutly <br> As most I can do I give thanks to Him <br> Who has removed me from the mortal world. <br> But tell me what the dusky spots may be <br> Upon this body, which below on earth <br> Make people tell that fabulous tale of Cain?”<sup>1225</sup> <br> Somewhat she smiled; and then, “If the opinion <br> Of mortals be erroneous,” she said, <br> “Where’er the key of sense doth not unlock, <br> Certes, the shafts of wonder should not pierce thee <br> Now, forasmuch as, following the senses, <br> Thou seest that the reason has short wings. <br> But tell me what thou think’st of it thyself.” <br> And I: “What seems to us up here diverse,<sup>1226</sup> <br> Is caused, I think, by bodies rare and dense.” <br> And she: “Right truly shalt thou see immersed <br> In error thy belief, if well thou hearest <br> The argument that I shall make against it. <br> Lights many the eighth sphere displays to you<sup>1227</sup> <br> Which in their quality and quantity <br> May noted be of aspects different. <br> If this were caused by rare and dense alone, <br> One only virtue would there be in all <br> Or more or less diffused, or equally. <br> Virtues diverse must be perforce the fruits <br> Of formal principles; and these, save one, <br> Of course would by thy reasoning be destroyed. <br> Besides, if rarity were of this dimness<sup>1228</sup> <br> The cause thou askest, either through and through <br> This planet thus attenuate were of matter, <br> Or else, as in a body is apportioned <br> The fat and lean, so in like manner this <br> Would in its volume interchange the leaves. <br> Were it the former, in the sun’s eclipse <br> It would be manifest by the shining through <br> Of light, as through aught tenuous interfused. <br> This is not so; hence we must scan the other, <br> And if it chance the other I demolish, <br> Then falsified will thy opinion be. <br> But if this rarity go not through and through, <br> There needs must be a limit, beyond which <br> Its contrary prevents the further passing, <br> And thence the foreign radiance is reflected, <br> Even as a color cometh back from glass, <br> The which behind itself concealeth lead.<sup>1229</sup> <br> Now thou wilt say the sunbeam shows itself <br> More dimly there than in the other parts, <br> By being there reflected farther back. <br> From this reply experiment will free thee <br> If e’er thou try it, which is wont to be <br> The fountain to the rivers of your arts. <br> Three mirrors shalt thou take, and two remove <br> Alike from thee, the other more remote <br> Between the former two shall meet thine eyes. <br> Turned towards these, cause that behind thy back <br> Be placed a light, illuming the three mirrors <br> And coming back to thee by all reflected. <br> Though in its quantity be not so ample <br> The image most remote, there shalt thou see <br> How it perforce is equally resplendent. <br> Now, as beneath the touches of warm rays <br> Naked the subject of the snow remains<sup>1230</sup> <br> Both of its former color and its cold, <br> Thee thus remaining in thy intellect, <br> Will I inform with such a living light, <br> That it shall tremble in its aspect to thee.<sup>1231</sup> <br> Within the heaven of the divine repose<sup>1232</sup> <br> Revolves a body, in whose virtue lies <br> The being of whatever it contains. <br> The following heaven, that has so many eyes,<sup>1233</sup> <br> Divides this being by essences diverse, <br> Distinguished from it, and by it contained. <br> The other spheres, by various differences, <br> All the distinctions which they have within them <br> Dispose unto their ends and their effects. <br> Thus do these organs of the world proceed, <br> As thou perceivest now, from grade to grade; <br> Since from above they take, and act beneath. <br> Observe me well, how through this place I come <br> Unto the truth thou wishest, that hereafter <br> Thou mayst alone know how to keep the ford. <br> The power and motion of the holy spheres, <br> As from the artisan the hammer’s craft, <br> Forth from the blessed motors must proceed. <br> The heaven, which lights so manifold make fair, <br> From the Intelligence profound, which turns it,<sup>1234</sup> <br> The image takes, and makes of it a seal. <br> And even as the soul within your dust <br> Through members different and accommodated <br> To faculties diverse expands itself, <br> So likewise this Intelligence diffuses <br> Its virtue multiplied among the stars, <br> Itself revolving on its unity. <br> Virtue diverse doth a diverse alloyage <br> Make with the precious body that it quickens, <br> In which, as life in you, it is combined. <br> From the glad nature whence it is derived, <br> The mingled virtue through the body shines, <br> Even as gladness through the living pupil. <br> From this proceeds whate’er from light to light <br> Appeareth different, not from dense and rare: <br> This is the formal principle that produces,<sup>1235</sup> <br> According to its goodness, dark and bright.”</p>
