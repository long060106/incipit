<p class="lede">Those who died by violence, but repentant⁠—Buonconte di Montefeltro⁠—La Pia.</p>
<p class="verse">I had already from those shades departed,<sup>585</sup> <br> And followed in the footsteps of my Guide, <br> When from behind, pointing his finger at me, <br> One shouted: “See, it seems as if shone not <br> The sunshine on the left of him below, <br> And like one living seems he to conduct him!” <br> Mine eyes I turned at utterance of these words, <br> And saw them watching with astonishment <br> But me, but me, and the light which was broken! <br> “Why doth thy mind so occupy itself,” <br> The Master said, “that thou thy pace dost slacken? <br> What matters it to thee what here is whispered? <br> Come after me, and let the people talk; <br> Stand like a steadfast tower, that never wags <br> Its top for all the blowing of the winds; <br> For evermore the man in whom is springing <br> Thought upon thought, removes from him the mark, <br> Because the force of one the other weakens.” <br> What could I say in answer but “I come”? <br> I said it somewhat with that color tinged <br> Which makes a man of pardon sometimes worthy. <br> Meanwhile along the mountain-side across <br> Came people in advance of us a little, <br> Singing the Miserere verse by verse. <br> When they became aware I gave no place <br> For passage of the sunshine through my body, <br> They changed their song into a long, hoarse “Oh!” <br> And two of them, in form of messengers, <br> Ran forth to meet us, and demanded of us, <br> “Of your condition make us cognizant.” <br> And said my Master: “Ye can go your way <br> And carry back again to those who sent you, <br> That this one’s body is of very flesh. <br> If they stood still because they saw his shadow, <br> As I suppose, enough is answered them; <br> Him let them honor, it may profit them.” <br> Vapors enkindled saw I ne’er so swiftly <br> At early nightfall cleave the air serene,<sup>586</sup> <br> Nor, at the set of sun, the clouds of August, <br> But upward they returned in briefer time, <br> And, on arriving, with the others wheeled <br> Tow’rds us, like troops that run without a rein. <br> “This folk that presses unto us is great, <br> And cometh to implore thee,” said the Poet; <br> “So still go onward, and in going listen.” <br> “O soul that goest to beatitude <br> With the same members wherewith thou wast born,” <br> Shouting they came, “a little stay thy steps, <br> Look, if thou e’er hast any of us seen, <br> So that o’er yonder thou bear news of him; <br> Ah, why dost thou go on? Ah, why not stay? <br> Long since we all were slain by violence, <br> And sinners even to the latest hour; <br> Then did a light from heaven admonish us, <br> So that, both penitent and pardoning, forth <br> From life we issued reconciled to God, <br> Who with desire to see Him stirs our hearts.” <br> And I: “Although I gaze into your faces, <br> No one I recognize; but if may please you <br> Aught I have power to do, ye well-born spirits, <br> Speak ye, and I will do it, by that peace <br> Which, following the feet of such a Guide, <br> From world to world makes itself sought by me.” <br> And one began: “Each one has confidence <br> In thy good offices without an oath, <br> Unless the I cannot cut off the I will;<sup>587</sup> <br> Whence I, who speak alone before the others,<sup>588</sup> <br> Pray thee, if ever thou dost see the land <br> That ’twixt Romagna lies and that of Charles, <br> Thou be so courteous to me of thy prayers <br> In Fano, that they pray for me devoutly, <br> That I may purge away my grave offences. <br> From thence was I; but the deep wounds, through which <br> Issued the blood wherein I had my seat,<sup>589</sup> <br> Were dealt me in bosom of the Antenori,<sup>590</sup> <br> There where I thought to be the most secure; <br> ’Twas he of Este had it done, who held me <br> In hatred far beyond what justice willed. <br> But if towards the Mira I had fled,<sup>591</sup> <br> When I was overtaken at Oriaco, <br> I still should be o’er yonder where men breathe. <br> I ran to the lagoon, and reeds and mire <br> Did so entangle me I fell, and saw there <br> A lake made from my veins upon the ground.” <br> Then said another: “Ah, be that desire <br> Fulfilled that draws thee to the lofty mountain, <br> As thou with pious pity aidest mine. <br> I was of Montefeltro, and am Buonconte;<sup>592</sup> <br> Giovanna, nor none other cares for me;<sup>593</sup> <br> Hence among these I go with downcast front.” <br> And I to him: “What violence or what chance <br> Led thee astray so far from Campaldino,<sup>594</sup> <br> That never has thy sepulture been known?” <br> “Oh,” he replied, “at Casentino’s foot <br> A river crosses named Archiano, born <br> Above the Hermitage in Apennine.<sup>595</sup> <br> There where the name thereof becometh void<sup>596</sup> <br> Did I arrive, pierced through and through the throat, <br> Fleeing on foot, and bloodying the plain; <br> There my sight lost I, and my utterance <br> Ceased in the name of Mary, and thereat <br> I fell, and tenantless my flesh remained. <br> Truth will I speak, repeat it to the living; <br> God’s Angel took me up, and he of hell<sup>597</sup> <br> Shouted: ‘O thou from heaven, why dost thou rob me? <br> Thou bearest away the eternal part of him, <br> For one poor little tear, that takes him from me; <br> But with the rest I’ll deal in other fashion!’ <br> Well knowest thou how in the air is gathered<sup>598</sup> <br> That humid vapor which to water turns, <br> Soon as it rises where the cold doth grasp it. <br> He joined that evil will, which aye seeks evil,<sup>599</sup> <br> To intellect, and moved the mist and wind <br> By means of power, which his own nature gave; <br> Thereafter, when the day was spent, the valley <br> From Pratomagno to the great yoke covered<sup>600</sup> <br> With fog, and made the heaven above intent, <br> So that the pregnant air to water changed;<sup>601</sup> <br> Down fell the rain, and to the gullies came <br> Whate’er of it earth tolerated not; <br> And as it mingled with the mighty torrents, <br> Towards the royal river with such speed <br> It headlong rushed, that nothing held it back. <br> My frozen body near unto its outlet <br> The robust Archian found, and into Arno <br> Thrust it, and loosened from my breast the cross<sup>602</sup> <br> I made of me, when agony o’ercame me; <br> It rolled me on the banks and on the bottom, <br> Then with its booty covered and begirt me.” <br> “Ah, when thou hast returned unto the world, <br> And rested thee from thy long journeying,” <br> After the second followed the third spirit, <br> “Do thou remember me who am the Pia; <br> Siena made me, unmade me Maremma;<sup>603</sup> <br> He knoweth it, who had encircled first, <br> Espousing me, my finger with his gem.”</p>
