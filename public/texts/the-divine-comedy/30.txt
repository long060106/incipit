<p class="lede">The Tenth Bolgia: alchemists⁠—Griffolino d’ Arezzo and Capocchio.</p>
<p class="verse">The many people and the divers wounds<sup>435</sup> <br> These eyes of mine had so inebriated, <br> That they were wishful to stand still and weep; <br> But said Virgilius: “What dost thou still gaze at? <br> Why is thy sight still riveted down there <br> Among the mournful, mutilated shades? <br> Thou hast not done so at the other Bolge; <br> Consider, if to count them thou believest, <br> That two-and-twenty miles the valley winds, <br> And now the moon is underneath our feet; <br> Henceforth the time allotted us is brief, <br> And more is to be seen than what thou seest.” <br> “If thou hadst,” I made answer thereupon, <br> “Attended to the cause for which I looked, <br> Perhaps a longer stay thou wouldst have pardoned.” <br> Meanwhile my Guide departed, and behind him <br> I went, already making my reply, <br> And superadding: “In that cavern where <br> I held mine eyes with such attention fixed, <br> I think a spirit of my blood laments <br> The sin which down below there costs so much.” <br> Then said the Master: “Be no longer broken <br> Thy thought from this time forward upon him; <br> Attend elsewhere, and there let him remain; <br> For him I saw below the little bridge, <br> Pointing at thee, and threatening with his finger <br> Fiercely, and heard him called Geri del Bello.<sup>436</sup> <br> So wholly at that time wast thou impeded <br> By him who formerly held Altaforte,<sup>437</sup> <br> Thou didst not look that way; so he departed.” <br> “O my Conductor, his own violent death, <br> Which is not yet avenged for him,” I said, <br> “By any who is sharer in the shame, <br> Made him disdainful; whence he went away, <br> As I imagine, without speaking to me,<sup>438</sup> <br> And thereby made me pity him the more.”<sup>439</sup> <br> Thus did we speak as far as the first place <br> Upon the crag, which the next valley shows <br> Down to the bottom, if there were more light. <br> When we were now right over the last cloister <br> Of Malebolge, so that its lay-brothers <br> Could manifest themselves unto our sight, <br> Divers lamentings pierced me through and through, <br> Which with compassion had their arrows barbed, <br> Whereat mine ears I covered with my hands. <br> What pain would be, if from the hospitals<sup>440</sup> <br> Of Valdichiana, ’twixt July and September, <br> And of Maremma and Sardinia <br> All the diseases in one moat were gathered, <br> Such was it here, and such a stench came from it <br> As from putrescent limbs is wont to issue. <br> We had descended on the furthest bank <br> From the long crag, upon the left hand still, <br> And then more vivid was my power of sight <br> Down tow’rds the bottom, where the ministress <br> Of the high Lord, Justice infallible, <br> Punishes forgers, which she here records.<sup>441</sup> <br> I do not think a sadder sight to see <br> Was in Aegina the whole people sick,<sup>442</sup> <br> (When was the air so full of pestilence, <br> The animals, down to the little worm, <br> All fell, and afterwards the ancient people, <br> According as the poets have affirmed, <br> Were from the seed of ants restored again,) <br> Than was it to behold through that dark valley <br> The spirits languishing in divers heaps. <br> This on the belly, that upon the back <br> One of the other lay, and others crawling <br> Shifted themselves along the dismal road. <br> We step by step went onward without speech, <br> Gazing upon and listening to the sick <br> Who had not strength enough to lift their bodies. <br> I saw two sitting leaned against each other, <br> As leans in heating platter against platter, <br> From head to foot bespotted o’er with scabs; <br> And never saw I plied a currycomb <br> By stable-boy for whom his master waits, <br> Or him who keeps awake unwillingly, <br> As everyone was plying fast the bite <br> Of nails upon himself, for the great rage <br> Of itching which no other succor had. <br> And the nails downward with them dragged the scab, <br> In fashion as a knife the scales of bream, <br> Or any other fish that has them largest. <br> “O thou, that with thy fingers dost dismail thee,” <br> Began my Leader unto one of them, <br> “And makest of them pincers now and then, <br> Tell me if any Latian is with those<sup>443</sup> <br> Who are herein; so may thy nails suffice thee <br> To all eternity unto this work.” <br> “Latians are we, whom thou so wasted seest, <br> Both of us here,” one weeping made reply; <br> “But who art thou, that questionest about us?” <br> And said the Guide: “One am I who descends <br> Down with this living man from cliff to cliff, <br> And I intend to show Hell unto him.” <br> Then broken was their mutual support, <br> And trembling each one turned himself to me, <br> With others who had heard him by rebound. <br> Wholly to me did the good Master gather, <br> Saying: “Say unto them whate’er thou wishest.” <br> And I began, since he would have it so: <br> “So may your memory not steal away <br> In the first world from out the minds of men, <br> But so may it survive ’neath many suns, <br> Say to me who ye are, and of what people; <br> Let not your foul and loathsome punishment <br> Make you afraid to show yourselves to me.” <br> “I of Arezzo was,” one made reply,<sup>444</sup> <br> “And Albert of Siena had me burned; <br> But what I died for does not bring me here. <br> ’Tis true I said to him, speaking in jest, <br> That I could rise by flight into the air, <br> And he who had conceit, but little wit, <br> Would have me show to him the art; and only <br> Because no Daedalus I made him, made me<sup>445</sup> <br> Be burned by one who held him as his son. <br> But unto the last Bolgia of the ten, <br> For alchemy, which in the world I practised, <br> Minos, who cannot err, has me condemned.” <br> And to the Poet said I: “Now was ever <br> So vain a people as the Sienese?<sup>446</sup> <br> Not for a certainty the French by far.” <br> Whereat the other leper, who had heard me, <br> Replied unto my speech: “Taking out Stricca,<sup>447</sup> <br> Who knew the art of moderate expenses, <br> And Niccolò, who the luxurious use <br> Of cloves discovered earliest of all <br> Within that garden where such seed takes root; <br> And taking out the band, among whom squandered <br> Caccia d’ Ascian his vineyards and vast woods, <br> And where his wit the Abbagliato proffered! <br> But, that thou know who thus doth second thee <br> Against the Sienese, make sharp thine eye <br> Tow’rds me, so that my face well answer thee, <br> And thou shalt see I am Capocchio’s shade,<sup>448</sup> <br> Who metals falsified by alchemy; <br> Thou must remember, if I well descry thee, <br> How I a skilful ape of nature was.”</p>
