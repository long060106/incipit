<p class="lede">Forese.</p>
<p class="verse">The while among the verdant leaves mine eyes<sup>983</sup> <br> I riveted, as he is wont to do <br> Who wastes his life pursuing little birds,<sup>984</sup> <br> My more than Father said unto me: “Son, <br> Come now; because the time that is ordained us <br> More usefully should be apportioned out.” <br> I turned my face and no less soon my steps <br> Unto the Sages, who were speaking so <br> They made the going of no cost to me; <br> And lo! were heard a song and a lament, <br> “<i>Labia mea, Domine</i>,” in fashion<sup>985</sup> <br> Such that delight and dolence it brought forth. <br> “O my sweet Father, what is this I hear?” <br> Began I; and he answered: “Shades that go <br> Perhaps the knot unloosing of their debt.” <br> In the same way that thoughtful pilgrims do, <br> Who, unknown people on the road o’ertaking, <br> Turn themselves round to them, and do not stop, <br> Even thus, behind us with a swifter motion <br> Coming and passing onward, gazed upon us <br> A crowd of spirits silent and devout. <br> Each in his eyes was dark and cavernous, <br> Pallid in face, and so emaciate <br> That from the bones the skin did shape itself. <br> I do not think that so to merest rind <br> Could Erisichthon have been withered up<sup>986</sup> <br> By famine, when most fear he had of it. <br> Thinking within myself I said: “Behold, <br> This is the folk who lost Jerusalem, <br> When Mary made a prey of her own son.”<sup>987</sup> <br> Their sockets were like rings without the gems;<sup>988</sup> <br> Whoever in the face of men reads <em>omo</em><sup>989</sup> <br> Might well in these have recognized the <em>m</em>. <br> Who would believe the odor of an apple, <br> Begetting longing, could consume them so, <br> And that of water, without knowing how? <br> I still was wondering what so famished them, <br> For the occasion not yet manifest <br> Of their emaciation and sad squalor; <br> And lo! from out the hollow of his head <br> His eyes a shade turned on me, and looked keenly; <br> Then cried aloud: “What grace to me is this?” <br> Never should I have known him by his look; <br> But in his voice was evident to me <br> That which his aspect had suppressed within it. <br> This spark within me wholly re-enkindled <br> My recognition of his altered face, <br> And I recalled the features of Forese.<sup>990</sup> <br> “Ah, do not look at this dry leprosy,” <br> Entreated he, “which doth my skin discolor, <br> Nor at default of flesh that I may have; <br> But tell me truth of thee, and who are those <br> Two souls, that yonder make for thee an escort; <br> Do not delay in speaking unto me.” <br> “That face of thine, which dead I once bewept, <br> Gives me for weeping now no lesser grief,” <br> I answered him, “beholding it so changed! <br> But tell me, for God’s sake, what thus denudes you? <br> Make me not speak while I am marvelling, <br> For ill speaks he who’s full of other longings.” <br> And he to me: “From the eternal council <br> Falls power into the water and the tree <br> Behind us left, whereby I grow so thin. <br> All of this people who lamenting sing, <br> For following beyond measure appetite <br> In hunger and thirst are here re-sanctified. <br> Desire to eat and drink enkindles in us <br> The scent that issues from the apple-tree, <br> And from the spray that sprinkles o’er the verdure; <br> And not a single time alone, this ground <br> Encircling, is renewed our pain⁠— <br> I say our pain, and ought to say our solace⁠— <br> For the same wish doth lead us to the tree <br> Which led the Christ rejoicing to say <i>Eli</i>,<sup>991</sup> <br> When with his veins he liberated us.”<sup>992</sup> <br> And I to him: “Forese, from that day <br> When for a better life thou changedst worlds, <br> Up to this time five years have not rolled round. <br> If sooner were the power exhausted in thee <br> Of sinning more, than thee the hour surprised <br> Of that good sorrow which to God reweds us, <br> How hast thou come up hitherward already? <br> I thought to find thee down there underneath,<sup>993</sup> <br> Where time for time doth restitution make.” <br> And he to me: “Thus speedily has led me <br> To drink of the sweet wormwood of these torments, <br> My Nella with her overflowing tears;<sup>994</sup> <br> She with her prayers devout and with her sighs <br> Has drawn me from the coast where one awaits, <br> And from the other circles set me free. <br> So much more dear and pleasing is to God <br> My little widow, whom so much I loved, <br> As in good works she is the more alone; <br> For the Barbagia of Sardinia<sup>995</sup> <br> By far more modest in its women is <br> Than the Barbagia I have left her in. <br> O brother sweet, what wilt thou have me say? <br> A future time is in my sight already, <br> To which this hour will not be very old, <br> When from the pulpit shall be interdicted <br> To the unblushing womankind of Florence <br> To go about displaying breast and paps.<sup>996</sup> <br> What savages were e’er, what Saracens, <br> Who stood in need, to make them covered go, <br> Of spiritual or other discipline? <br> But if the shameless women were assured <br> Of what swift Heaven prepares for them, already <br> Wide open would they have their mouths to howl; <br> For if my foresight here deceive me not, <br> They shall be sad ere he has bearded cheeks <br> Who now is hushed to sleep with lullaby. <br> O brother, now no longer hide thee from me; <br> See that not only I, but all these people <br> Are gazing there, where thou dost veil the sun.” <br> Whence I to him: “If thou bring back to mind <br> What thou with me hast been and I with thee, <br> The present memory will be grievous still. <br> Out of that life he turned me back who goes <br> In front of me, two days agone when round <br> The sister of him yonder showed herself,” <br> And to the sun I pointed. “Through the deep <br> Night of the truly dead has this one led me, <br> With this true flesh, that follows after him. <br> Thence his encouragements have led me up, <br> Ascending and still circling round the mount <br> That you doth straighten, whom the world made crooked. <br> He says that he will bear me company, <br> Till I shall be where Beatrice will be; <br> There it behoves me to remain without him. <br> This is Virgilius, who thus says to me,” <br> And him I pointed at; “the other is <br> That shade for whom just now shook every slope<sup>997</sup> <br> Your realm, that from itself discharges him.”</p>
