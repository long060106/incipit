<p class="lede">Ciampolo, Friar Gomita, and Michael Zanche.</p>
<p class="verse">I have erewhile seen horsemen moving camp,<sup>318</sup> <br> Begin the storming, and their muster make, <br> And sometimes starting off for their escape; <br> Vaunt-couriers have I seen upon your land, <br> O Aretines, and foragers go forth,<sup>319</sup> <br> Tournaments stricken, and the joustings run, <br> Sometimes with trumpets and sometimes with bells,<sup>320</sup> <br> With kettle-drums, and signals of the castles, <br> And with our own, and with outlandish things, <br> But never yet with bagpipe so uncouth <br> Did I see horsemen move, nor infantry, <br> Nor ship by any sign of land or star. <br> We went upon our way with the ten demons; <br> Ah, savage company! but in the church <br> With saints, and in the tavern with the gluttons!<sup>321</sup> <br> Ever upon the pitch was my intent, <br> To see the whole condition of that Bolgia, <br> And of the people who therein were burned. <br> Even as the dolphins, when they make a sign <br> To mariners by arching of the back, <br> That they should counsel take to save their vessel, <br> Thus sometimes, to alleviate his pain, <br> One of the sinners would display his back, <br> And in less time conceal it than it lightens. <br> As on the brink of water in a ditch <br> The frogs stand only with their muzzles out, <br> So that they hide their feet and other bulk, <br> So upon every side the sinners stood; <br> But ever as Barbariccia near them came, <br> Thus underneath the boiling they withdrew. <br> I saw, and still my heart doth shudder at it, <br> One waiting thus, even as it comes to pass <br> One frog remains, and down another dives; <br> And Graffiacan, who most confronted him, <br> Grappled him by his tresses smeared with pitch, <br> And drew him up, so that he seemed an otter. <br> I knew, before, the names of all of them, <br> So had I noted them when they were chosen, <br> And when they called each other, listened how. <br> “O Rubicante, see that thou do lay <br> Thy claws upon him, so that thou mayst flay him,” <br> Cried all together the accursed ones. <br> And I: “My Master, see to it, if thou canst, <br> That thou mayst know who is the luckless wight, <br> Thus come into his adversaries’ hands.” <br> Near to the side of him my Leader drew, <br> Asked of him whence he was; and he replied: <br> “I in the kingdom of Navarre was born;<sup>322</sup> <br> My mother placed me servant to a lord, <br> For she had borne me to a ribald knave, <br> Destroyer of himself and of his things. <br> Then I domestic was of good King Thibault;<sup>323</sup> <br> I set me there to practise barratry, <br> For which I pay the reckoning in this heat.” <br> And Ciriatto, from whose mouth projected, <br> On either side, a tusk, as in a boar, <br> Caused him to feel how one of them could rip. <br> Among malicious cats the mouse had come; <br> But Barbariccia clasped him in his arms, <br> And said: “Stand ye aside, while I enfork him.” <br> And to my Master he turned round his head; <br> “Ask him again,” he said, “if more thou wish <br> To know from him, before someone destroy him.” <br> The Guide: “Now tell then of the other culprits; <br> Knowest thou any one who is a Latian,<sup>324</sup> <br> Under the pitch?” And he: “I separated <br> Lately from one who was a neighbor to it; <br> Would that I still were covered up with him, <br> For I should fear not either claw nor hook!” <br> And Libicocco: “We have borne too much”; <br> And with his grapnel seized him by the arm, <br> So that, by rending, he tore off a tendon. <br> Eke Draghignazzo wished to pounce upon him <br> Down at the legs; whence their Decurion <br> Turned round and round about with evil look. <br> When they again somewhat were pacified, <br> Of him, who still was looking at his wound, <br> Demanded my Conductor without stay: <br> “Who was that one, from whom a luckless parting <br> Thou sayest thou hast made, to come ashore?” <br> And he replied: “It was the Friar Gomita, <br> He of Gallura, vessel of all fraud,<sup>325</sup> <br> Who had the enemies of his Lord in hand, <br> And dealt so with them each exults thereat; <br> Money he took, and let them smoothly off, <br> As he says; and in other offices <br> A barrator was he, not mean but sovereign. <br> Foregathers with him one Don Michael Zanche<sup>326</sup> <br> Of Logodoro; and of Sardinia <br> To gossip never do their tongues feel tired. <br> O me! see that one, how he grinds his teeth; <br> Still farther would I speak, but am afraid <br> Lest he to scratch my itch be making ready.” <br> And the grand Provost, turned to Farfarello, <br> Who rolled his eyes about as if to strike, <br> Said: “Stand aside there, thou malicious bird.” <br> “If you desire either to see or hear,” <br> The terror-stricken recommenced thereon, <br> “Tuscans or Lombards, I will make them come. <br> But let the Malebranche cease a little, <br> So that these may not their revenges fear, <br> And I, down sitting in this very place, <br> For one that I am will make seven come, <br> When I shall whistle, as our custom is <br> To do whenever one of us comes out.” <br> Cagnazzo at these words his muzzle lifted, <br> Shaking his head, and said: “Just hear the trick <br> Which he has thought of, down to throw himself!” <br> Whence he, who snares in great abundance had, <br> Responded: “I by far too cunning am, <br> When I procure for mine a greater sadness.” <br> Alichin held not in, but running counter <br> Unto the rest, said to him: “If thou dive, <br> I will not follow thee upon the gallop, <br> But I will beat my wings above the pitch; <br> The height be left, and be the bank a shield, <br> To see if thou alone dost countervail us.” <br> O thou who readest, thou shalt hear new sport! <br> Each to the other side his eyes averted; <br> He first, who most reluctant was to do it. <br> The Navarrese selected well his time; <br> Planted his feet on land, and in a moment <br> Leaped, and released himself from their design. <br> Whereat each one was suddenly stung with shame, <br> But he most who was cause of the defeat; <br> Therefore he moved, and cried: “Thou art o’ertakern.” <br> But little it availed, for wings could not <br> Outstrip the fear; the other one went under, <br> And, flying, upward he his breast directed. <br> Not otherwise the duck upon a sudden <br> Dives under, when the falcon is approaching, <br> And upward he returneth cross and weary. <br> Infuriate at the mockery, Calcabrina <br> Flying behind him followed close, desirous <br> The other should escape, to have a quarrel. <br> And when the barrator had disappeared, <br> He turned his talons upon his companion, <br> And grappled with him right above the moat. <br> But sooth the other was a doughty sparhawk <br> To clapperclaw him well; and both of them <br> Fell in the middle of the boiling pond. <br> A sudden intercessor was the heat; <br> But ne’ertheless of rising there was naught, <br> To such degree they had their wings belimed. <br> Lamenting with the others, Barbariccia <br> Made four of them fly to the other side <br> With all their gaffs, and very speedily <br> This side and that they to their posts descended; <br> They stretched their hooks towards the pitch-ensnared, <br> Who were already baked within the crust, <br> And in this manner busied did we leave them.</p>
