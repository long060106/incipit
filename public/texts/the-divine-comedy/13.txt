<p class="lede">The Minotaur⁠—The Seventh Circle⁠—The violent⁠—Phlegethon⁠—The violent against their neighbors⁠—The centaurs⁠—Tyrants.</p>
<p class="verse">The place where to descend the bank we came<sup>159</sup> <br> Was alpine, and from what was there, moreover,<sup>160</sup> <br> Of such a kind that every eye would shun it. <br> Such as that ruin is which in the flank <br> Smote, on this side of Trent, the Adige,<sup>161</sup> <br> Either by earthquake or by failing stay, <br> For from the mountain’s top, from which it moved, <br> Unto the plain the cliff is shattered so, <br> Some path ’twould give to him who was above; <br> Even such was the descent of that ravine, <br> And on the border of the broken chasm <br> The infamy of Crete was stretched along,<sup>162</sup> <br> Who was conceived in the fictitious cow; <br> And when he us beheld, he bit himself, <br> Even as one whom anger racks within. <br> My Sage towards him shouted: “Peradventure <br> Thou think’st that here may be the Duke of Athens, <br> Who in the world above brought death to thee?<sup>163</sup> <br> Get thee gone, beast, for this one cometh not <br> Instructed by thy sister, but he comes<sup>164</sup> <br> In order to behold your punishments.” <br> As is that bull who breaks loose at the moment <br> In which he has received the mortal blow, <br> Who cannot walk, but staggers here and there, <br> The Minotaur beheld I do the like; <br> And he, the wary, cried: “Run to the passage; <br> While he wroth, ’tis well thou shouldst descend.” <br> Thus down we took our way o’er that discharge <br> Of stones, which oftentimes did move themselves <br> Beneath my feet, from the unwonted burden. <br> Thoughtful I went; and he said: “Thou art thinking <br> Perhaps upon this ruin, which is guarded <br> By that brute anger which just now I quenched. <br> Now will I have thee know, the other time <br> I here descended to the nether Hell, <br> This precipice had not yet fallen down. <br> But truly, if I well discern, a little <br> Before His coming who the mighty spoil <br> Bore off from Dis, in the supernal circle,<sup>165</sup> <br> Upon all sides the deep and loathsome valley <br> Trembled so, that I thought the Universe <br> Was thrilled with love, by which there are who think<sup>166</sup> <br> The world ofttimes converted into chaos; <br> And at that moment this primeval crag <br> Both here and elsewhere made such overthrow. <br> But fix thine eyes below; for draweth near <br> The river of blood, within which boiling is <br> Whoe’er by violence doth injure others.” <br> O blind cupidity, O wrath insane, <br> That spurs us onward so in our short life, <br> And in the eternal then so badly steeps us! <br> I saw an ample moat bent like a bow, <br> As one which all the plain encompasses, <br> Conformable to what my Guide had said. <br> And between this and the embankment’s foot <br> Centaurs in file were running, armed with arrows,<sup>167</sup> <br> As in the world they used the chase to follow. <br> Beholding us descend, each one stood still, <br> And from the squadron three detached themselves, <br> With bows and arrows in advance selected; <br> And from afar one cried: “Unto what torment <br> Come ye, who down the hillside are descending? <br> Tell us from there; if not, I draw the bow.” <br> My Master said: “Our answer will we make <br> To Chiron, near you there; in evil hour, <br> That will of thine was evermore so hasty.” <br> Then touched he me, and said: “This one is Nessus, <br> Who perished for the lovely Dejanira,<sup>168</sup> <br> And for himself, himself did vengeance take. <br> And he in the midst, who at his breast is gazing, <br> Is the great Chiron, who brought up Achilles;<sup>169</sup> <br> That other Pholus is, who was so wrathful. <br> Thousands and thousands go about the moat <br> Shooting with shafts whatever soul emerges <br> Out of the blood, more than his crime allots.” <br> Near we approached unto those monsters fleet; <br> Chiron an arrow took, and with the notch<sup>170</sup> <br> Backward upon his jaws he put his beard. <br> After he had uncovered his great mouth, <br> He said to his companions: “Are you ware <br> That he behind moveth whate’er he touches? <br> Thus are not wont to do the feet of dead men.” <br> And my good Guide, who now was at his breast, <br> Where the two natures are together joined, <br> Replied: “Indeed he lives, and thus alone <br> Me it behoves to show him the dark valley; <br> Necessity, and not delight, impels us. <br> Someone withdrew from singing Halleluja, <br> Who unto me committed this new office; <br> No thief is he, nor I a thievish spirit. <br> But by that virtue through which I am moving <br> My steps along this savage thoroughfare, <br> Give us someone of thine, to be with us, <br> And who may show us where to pass the ford, <br> And who may carry this one on his back; <br> For ’tis no spirit that can walk the air.” <br> Upon his right breast Chiron wheeled about, <br> And said to Nessus: “Turn and do thou guide them, <br> And warn aside, if other band may meet you.” <br> We with our faithful escort onward moved, <br> Along the brink of the vermilion boiling, <br> Wherein the boiled were uttering loud laments. <br> People I saw within up to the eyebrows, <br> And the great Centaur said: “Tyrants are these, <br> Who dealt in bloodshed and in pillaging. <br> Here they lament their pitiless mischiefs; here <br> Is Alexander, and fierce Dionysius<sup>171</sup> <br> Who upon Sicily brought dolorous years. <br> That forehead there which has the hair so black <br> Is Azzolin; and the other who is blond,<sup>172</sup> <br> Obizzo is of Esti, who, in truth,<sup>173</sup> <br> Up in the world was by his stepson slain.” <br> Then turned I to the Poet; and he said, <br> “Now he be first to thee, and second I.” <br> A little farther on the Centaur stopped <br> Above a folk, who far down as the throat <br> Seemed from that boiling stream to issue forth. <br> A shade he showed us on one side alone, <br> Saying: “He cleft asunder in God’s bosom<sup>174</sup> <br> The heart that still upon the Thames is honored.” <br> Then people saw I, who from out the river <br> Lifted their heads and also all the chest; <br> And many among these I recognized.<sup>175</sup> <br> Thus ever more and more grew shallower <br> That blood, so that the feet alone it covered; <br> And there across the moat our passage was. <br> “Even as thou here upon this side beholdest <br> The boiling stream, that aye diminishes,” <br> The Centaur said, “I wish thee to believe <br> That on this other more and more declines <br> Its bed, until it reunites itself <br> Where it behoveth tyranny to groan. <br> Justice divine, upon this side, is goading <br> That Attila, who was a scourge on earth,<sup>176</sup> <br> And Pyrrhus, and Sextus; and forever milks<sup>177</sup> <br> The tears which with the boiling it unseals <br> In Rinier da Corneto and Rinier Pazzo,<sup>178</sup> <br> Who made upon the highways so much war.” <br> Then back he turned, and passed again the ford.</p>
