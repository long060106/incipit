<p class="lede">Marco Lombardo.</p>
<p class="verse">Darkness of hell, and of a night deprived<sup>828</sup> <br> Of every planet under a poor sky,<sup>829</sup> <br> As much as may be tenebrous with cloud, <br> Ne’er made unto my sight so thick a veil, <br> As did that smoke which there enveloped us, <br> Nor to the feeling of so rough a texture; <br> For not an eye it suffered to stay open; <br> Whereat mine escort, faithful and sagacious, <br> Drew near to me and offered me his shoulder. <br> E’en as a blind man goes behind his guide, <br> Lest he should wander, or should strike against <br> Aught that may harm or peradventure kill him, <br> So went I through the bitter and foul air, <br> Listening unto my Leader, who said only, <br> “Look that from me thou be not separated.” <br> Voices I heard, and everyone appeared <br> To supplicate for peace and misericord <br> The Lamb of God who takes away our sins. <br> Still “<i>Agnus Dei</i>” their exordium was;<sup>830</sup> <br> One word there was in all, and metre one, <br> So that all harmony appeared among them. <br> “Master,” I said, “are spirits those I hear?” <br> And he to me: “Thou apprehendest truly, <br> And they the knot of anger go unloosing.” <br> “Now who art thou, that cleavest through our smoke, <br> And art discoursing of us even as though <br> Thou didst by calends still divide the time?”<sup>831</sup> <br> After this manner by a voice was spoken; <br> Whereon my Master said: “Do thou reply, <br> And ask if on this side the way go upward.” <br> And I: “O creature that dost cleanse thyself <br> To return beautiful to Him who made thee, <br> Thou shalt hear marvels if thou follow me.” <br> “Thee will I follow far as is allowed me,” <br> He answered; “and if smoke prevent our seeing, <br> Hearing shall keep us joined instead thereof.” <br> Thereon began I: “With that swathing band <br> Which death unwindeth am I going upward, <br> And hither came I through the infernal anguish. <br> And if God in his grace has me infolded, <br> So that he wills that I behold his court <br> By method wholly out of modern usage, <br> Conceal not from me who ere death thou wast, <br> But tell it me, and tell me if I go <br> Right for the pass, and be thy words our escort.” <br> “Lombard was I, and I was Marco called;<sup>832</sup> <br> The world I knew, and loved that excellence, <br> At which has each one now unbent his bow. <br> For mounting upward, thou art going right.” <br> Thus he made answer, and subjoined: “I pray thee <br> To pray for me when thou shalt be above.” <br> And I to him: “My faith I pledge to thee <br> To do what thou dost ask me; but am bursting <br> Inly with doubt, unless I rid me of it. <br> First it was simple, and is now made double <br> By thy opinion, which makes certain to me, <br> Here and elsewhere, that which I couple with it.<sup>833</sup> <br> The world forsooth is utterly deserted <br> By every virtue, as thou tellest me, <br> And with iniquity is big and covered; <br> But I beseech thee point me out the cause, <br> That I may see it, and to others show it; <br> For one in the heavens, and here below one puts it.” <br> A sigh profound, that grief forced into Ai!<sup>834</sup> <br> He first sent forth, and then began he: “Brother, <br> The world is blind, and sooth thou comest from it! <br> Ye who are living every cause refer<sup>835</sup> <br> Still upward to the heavens, as if all things <br> They of necessity moved with themselves.<sup>836</sup> <br> If this were so, in you would be destroyed<sup>837</sup> <br> Free will, nor any justice would there be <br> In having joy for good, or grief for evil. <br> The heavens your movements do initiate, <br> I say not all; but granting that I say it, <br> Light has been given you for good and evil, <br> And free volition; which, if some fatigue <br> In the first battles with the heavens it suffers, <br> Afterwards conquers all, if well ’tis nurtured.<sup>838</sup> <br> To greater force and to a better nature,<sup>839</sup> <br> Though free, ye subject are, and that creates <br> The mind in you the heavens have not in charge. <br> Hence, if the present world doth go astray, <br> In you the cause is, be it sought in you; <br> And I therein will now be thy true spy.<sup>840</sup> <br> Forth from the hand of Him, who fondles it <br> Before it is, like to a little girl <br> Weeping and laughing in her childish sport, <br> Issues the simple soul, that nothing knows, <br> Save that, proceeding from a joyous Maker, <br> Gladly it turns to that which gives it pleasure. <br> Of trivial good at first it tastes the savor; <br> Is cheated by it, and runs after it,<sup>841</sup> <br> If guide or rein turn not aside its love. <br> Hence it behoved laws for a rein to place, <br> Behoved a king to have, who at the least <br> Of the true city should discern the tower.<sup>842</sup> <br> The laws exist, but who sets hand to them? <br> No one; because the shepherd who precedes <br> Can ruminate, but cleaveth not the hoof;<sup>843</sup> <br> Wherefore the people that perceives its guide <br> Strike only at the good for which it hankers,<sup>844</sup> <br> Feeds upon that, and farther seeketh not. <br> Clearly canst thou perceive that evil guidance <br> The cause is that has made the world depraved, <br> And not that nature is corrupt in you. <br> Rome, that reformed the world, accustomed was <br> Two suns to have, which one road and the other,<sup>845</sup> <br> Of God and of the world, made manifest. <br> One has the other quenched, and to the crosier <br> The sword is joined, and ill beseemeth it <br> That by main force one with the other go, <br> Because, being joined, one feareth not the other; <br> If thou believe not, think upon the grain, <br> For by its seed each herb is recognized. <br> In the land laved by Po and Adige,<sup>846</sup> <br> Valor and courtesy used to be found, <br> Before that Frederick had his controversy;<sup>847</sup> <br> Now in security can pass that way <br> Whoever will abstain, through sense of shame, <br> From speaking with the good, or drawing near them. <br> True, three old men are left, in whom upbraids <br> The ancient age the new, and late they deem it <br> That God restore them to the better life: <br> Currado da Palazzo, and good Gherardo,<sup>848</sup> <br> And Guido da Castel, who better named is, <br> In fashion of the French, the simple Lombard: <br> Say thou henceforward that the Church of Rome, <br> Confounding in itself two governments, <br> Falls in the mire, and soils itself and burden.” <br> “O Marco mine,” I said, “thou reasonest well; <br> And now discern I why the sons of Levi <br> Have been excluded from the heritage.<sup>849</sup> <br> But what Gherardo is it, who, as sample <br> Of a lost race, thou sayest has remained <br> In reprobation of the barbarous age?” <br> “Either thy speech deceives me, or it tempts me,” <br> He answered me; “for speaking Tuscan to me, <br> It seems of good Gherardo naught thou knowest. <br> By other surname do I know him not, <br> Unless I take it from his daughter Gaia.<sup>850</sup> <br> May God be with you, for I come no farther. <br> Behold the dawn, that through the smoke rays out, <br> Already whitening; and I must depart⁠— <br> Yonder the Angel is⁠—e’er he appear.” <br> Thus did he speak, and would no farther hear me.</p>
