<p class="lede">The gate of Hell⁠—The inefficient or indifferent⁠—Pope Celestine V⁠—The shores of Acheron⁠—Charon⁠—The earthquake and the swoon.</p>
<p class="verse">“Through me the way is to the city dolent;<sup>43</sup> <br> Through me the way is to eternal dole; <br> Through me the way among the people lost. <br> Justice incited my sublime Creator; <br> Created me divine Omnipotence, <br> The highest Wisdom and the primal Love. <br> Before me there were no created things, <br> Only eterne, and I eternal last. <br> All hope abandon, ye who enter in!” <br> These words in sombre color I beheld <br> Written upon the summit of a gate; <br> Whence I: “Their sense is, Master, hard to me!” <br> And he to me, as one experienced: <br> “Here all suspicion needs must be abandoned, <br> All cowardice must needs be here extinct. <br> We to the place have come, where I have told thee <br> Thou shalt behold the people dolorous <br> Who have foregone the good of intellect.”<sup>44</sup> <br> And after he had laid his hand on mine <br> With joyful mien, whence I was comforted, <br> He led me in among the secret things. <br> There sighs, complaints, and ululations loud<sup>45</sup> <br> Resounded through the air without a star, <br> Whence I, at the beginning, wept thereat. <br> Languages diverse, horrible dialects, <br> Accents of anger, words of agony, <br> And voices high and hoarse, with sound of hands, <br> Made up a tumult that goes whirling on <br> Forever in that air forever black, <br> Even as the sand doth, when the whirlwind breathes. <br> And I, who had my head with horror bound, <br> Said: “Master, what is this which now I hear? <br> What folk is this, which seems by pain so vanquished?” <br> And he to me: “This miserable mode <br> Maintain the melancholy souls of those <br> Who lived withouten infamy or praise.<sup>46</sup> <br> Commingled are they with that caitiff choir <br> Of Angels, who have not rebellious been, <br> Nor faithful were to God, but were for self. <br> The heavens expelled them, not to be less fair; <br> Nor them the nethermore abyss receives, <br> For glory none the damned would have from them.”<sup>47</sup> <br> And I: “O Master, what so grievous is <br> To these, that maketh them lament so sore?” <br> He answered: “I will tell thee very briefly. <br> These have no longer any hope of death; <br> And this blind life of theirs is so debased, <br> They envious are of every other fate. <br> No fame of them the world permits to be; <br> Misericord and Justice both disdain them. <br> Let us not speak of them, but look, and pass.” <br> And I, who looked again, beheld a banner,<sup>48</sup> <br> Which, whirling round, ran on so rapidly, <br> That of all pause it seemed to me indignant; <br> And after it there came so long a train <br> Of people, that I ne’er would have believed <br> That ever Death so many had undone. <br> When some among them I had recognized, <br> I looked, and I beheld the shade of him<sup>49</sup> <br> Who made through cowardice the great refusal. <br> Forthwith I comprehended, and was certain, <br> That this the sect was of the caitiff wretches <br> Hateful to God and to his enemies. <br> These miscreants, who never were alive, <br> Were naked, and were stung exceedingly <br> By gadflies and by hornets that were there. <br> These did their faces irrigate with blood, <br> Which, with their tears commingled, at their feet <br> By the disgusting worms was gathered up. <br> And when to gazing farther I betook me, <br> People I saw on a great river’s bank; <br> Whence said I: “Master, now vouchsafe to me, <br> That I may know who these are, and what law <br> Makes them appear so ready to pass over, <br> As I discern athwart the dusky light.”<sup>50</sup> <br> And he to me: “These things shall all be known <br> To thee, as soon as we our footsteps stay <br> Upon the dismal shore of Acheron.” <br> Then with mine eyes ashamed and downward cast, <br> Fearing my words might irksome be to him, <br> From speech refrained I till we reached the river. <br> And lo! towards us coming in a boat<sup>51</sup> <br> An old man, hoary with the hair of eld, <br> Crying: “Woe unto you, ye souls depraved! <br> Hope nevermore to look upon the heavens; <br> I come to lead you to the other shore, <br> To the eternal shades in heat and frost.<sup>52</sup> <br> And thou, that yonder standest, living soul, <br> Withdraw thee from these people, who are dead!”<sup>53</sup> <br> But when he saw that I did not withdraw, <br> He said: “By other ways, by other ports <br> Thou to the shore shalt come, not here, for passage; <br> A lighter vessel needs must carry thee.”<sup>54</sup> <br> And unto him the Guide: “Vex thee not, Charon;<sup>55</sup> <br> It is so willed there where is power to do <br> That which is willed; and farther question not.” <br> Thereat were quieted the fleecy cheeks <br> Of him the ferryman of the livid fen, <br> Who round about his eyes had wheels of flame. <br> But all those souls who weary were and naked <br> Their color changed and gnashed their teeth together, <br> As soon as they had heard those cruel words. <br> God they blasphemed and their progenitors, <br> The human race, the place, the time, the seed <br> Of their engendering and of their birth! <br> Thereafter all together they drew back, <br> Bitterly weeping, to the accursed shore, <br> Which waiteth every man who fears not God. <br> Charon the demon, with the eyes of glede,<sup>56</sup> <br> Beckoning to them, collects them all together, <br> Beats with his oar whoever lags behind. <br> As in the autumn-time the leaves fall off,<sup>57</sup> <br> First one and then another, till the branch <br> Unto the earth surrenders all its spoils; <br> In similar wise the evil seed of Adam <br> Throw themselves from that margin one by one, <br> At signals, as a bird unto its lure. <br> So they depart across the dusky wave, <br> And ere upon the other side they land, <br> Again on this side a new troop assembles. <br> “My son,” the courteous Master said to me, <br> “All those who perish in the wrath of God <br> Here meet together out of every land; <br> And ready are they to pass o’er the river, <br> Because celestial Justice spurs them on, <br> So that their fear is turned into desire. <br> This way there never passes a good soul; <br> And hence if Charon doth complain of thee, <br> Well mayst thou know now what his speech imports.” <br> This being finished, all the dusk champaign <br> Trembled so violently, that of that terror <br> The recollection bathes me still with sweat. <br> The land of tears gave forth a blast of wind, <br> And fulminated a vermilion light, <br> Which overmastered in me every sense, <br> And as a man whom sleep hath seized I fell.</p>
