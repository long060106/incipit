<p class="lede">Piccarda and Constance.</p>
<p class="verse">That Sun, which erst with love my bosom warmed,<sup>1236</sup> <br> Of beauteous truth had unto me discovered, <br> By proving and reproving, the sweet aspect. <br> And, that I might confess myself convinced <br> And confident, so far as was befitting, <br> I lifted more erect my head to speak. <br> But there appeared a vision, which withdrew me <br> So close to it, in order to be seen, <br> That my confession I remembered not. <br> Such as through polished and transparent glass, <br> Or waters crystalline and undisturbed, <br> But not so deep as that their bed be lost, <br> Come back again the outlines of our faces <br> So feeble, that a pearl on forehead white <br> Comes not less speedily unto our eyes; <br> Such saw I many faces prompt to speak, <br> So that I ran in error opposite <br> To that which kindled love ’twixt man and fountain.<sup>1237</sup> <br> As soon as I became aware of them, <br> Esteeming them as mirrored semblances, <br> To see of whom they were, mine eyes I turned, <br> And nothing saw, and once more turned them forward <br> Direct into the light of my sweet Guide, <br> Who smiling kindled in her holy eyes. <br> “Marvel thou not,” she said to me, “because <br> I smile at this thy puerile conceit, <br> Since on the truth it trusts not yet its foot, <br> But turns thee, as ’tis wont, on emptiness. <br> True substances are these which thou beholdest, <br> Here relegate for breaking of some vow. <br> Therefore speak with them, listen and believe; <br> For the true light, which giveth peace to them, <br> Permits them not to turn from it their feet.” <br> And I unto the shade that seemed most wishful <br> To speak directed me, and I began, <br> As one whom too great eagerness bewilders: <br> “O well-created spirit, who in the rays <br> Of life eternal dost the sweetness taste <br> Which being untasted ne’er is comprehended, <br> Grateful ’twill be to me, if thou content me <br> Both with thy name and with your destiny.”<sup>1238</sup> <br> Whereat she promptly and with laughing eyes: <br> “Our charity doth never shut the doors <br> Against a just desire, except as one<sup>1239</sup> <br> Who wills that all her court be like herself. <br> I was a virgin sister in the world; <br> And if thy mind doth contemplate me well, <br> The being more fair will not conceal me from thee, <br> But thou shalt recognise I am Piccarda, <br> Who, stationed here among these other blessed, <br> Myself am blessed in the slowest sphere. <br> All our affections, that alone inflamed <br> Are in the pleasure of the Holy Ghost, <br> Rejoice at being of his order formed; <br> And this allotment, which appears so low, <br> Therefore is given us, because our vows <br> Have been neglected and in some part void.” <br> Whence I to her: “In your miraculous aspects <br> There shines I know not what of the divine, <br> Which doth transform you from our first conceptions. <br> Therefore I was not swift in my remembrance; <br> But what thou tellest me now aids me so, <br> That the refiguring is easier to me. <br> But tell me, ye who in this place are happy, <br> Are you desirous of a higher place, <br> To see more or to make yourselves more friends?” <br> First with those other shades she smiled a little; <br> Thereafter answered me so full of gladness, <br> She seemed to burn in the first fire of love: <br> “Brother, our will is quieted by virtue<sup>1240</sup> <br> Of charity, that makes us wish alone <br> For what we have, nor gives us thirst for more. <br> If to be more exalted we aspired, <br> Discordant would our aspirations be <br> Unto the will of Him who here secludes us; <br> Which thou shalt see finds no place in these circles, <br> If being in charity is needful here, <br> And if thou lookest well into its nature; <br> Nay, ’tis essential to this blest existence <br> To keep itself within the will divine, <br> Whereby our very wishes are made one; <br> So that, as we are station above station <br> Throughout this realm, to all the realm ’tis pleasing, <br> As to the King, who makes his will our will. <br> And his will is our peace; this is the sea <br> To which is moving onward whatsoever <br> It doth create, and all that nature makes.” <br> Then it was clear to me how everywhere <br> In heaven is Paradise, although the grace <br> Of good supreme there rain not in one measure. <br> But as it comes to pass, if one food sates, <br> And for another still remains the longing, <br> We ask for this, and that decline with thanks, <br> E’en thus did I; with gesture and with word, <br> To learn from her what was the web wherein <br> She did not ply the shuttle to the end. <br> “A perfect life and merit high in-heaven <br> A lady o’er us,” said she, “by whose rule <br> Down in your world they vest and veil themselves, <br> That until death they may both watch and sleep <br> Beside that Spouse who every vow accepts <br> Which charity conformeth to his pleasure. <br> To follow her, in girlhood from the world <br> I fled, and in her habit shut myself, <br> And pledged me to the pathway of her sect. <br> Then men accustomed unto evil more <br> Than unto good, from the sweet cloister tore me; <br> God knows what afterward my life became. <br> This other splendor, which to thee reveals <br> Itself on my right side, and is enkindled <br> With all the illumination of our sphere, <br> What of myself I say applies to her; <br> A nun was she, and likewise from her head <br> Was ta’en the shadow of the sacred wimple. <br> But when she too was to the world returned <br> Against her wishes and against good usage, <br> Of the heart’s veil she never was divested. <br> Of great Costanza this is the effulgence,<sup>1241</sup> <br> Who from the second wind of Suabia <br> Brought forth the third and latest puissance.” <br> Thus unto me she spake, and then began <br> “Ave Maria” singing, and in singing <br> Vanished, as through deep water something heavy. <br> My sight, that followed her as long a time <br> As it was possible, when it had lost her <br> Turned round unto the mark of more desire, <br> And wholly unto Beatrice reverted; <br> But she such lightnings flashed into mine eyes, <br> That at the first my sight endured it not; <br> And this in questioning more backward made me.</p>
