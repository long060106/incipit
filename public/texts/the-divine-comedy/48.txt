<p class="lede">The sculptures on the pavement⁠—Ascent to the Second Circle.</p>
<p class="verse">Abreast, like oxen going in a yoke,<sup>734</sup> <br> I with that heavy-laden soul went on, <br> As long as the sweet pedagogue permitted;<sup>735</sup> <br> But when he said, “Leave him, and onward pass, <br> For here ’tis good that with the sail and oars, <br> As much as may be, each push on his barque”; <br> Upright, as walking wills it, I redressed <br> My person, notwithstanding that my thoughts <br> Remained within me downcast and abashed. <br> I had moved on, and followed willingly <br> The footsteps of my Master, and we both <br> Already showed how light of foot we were, <br> When unto me he said: “Cast down thine eyes; <br> ’Twere well for thee, to alleviate the way, <br> To look upon the bed beneath thy feet.” <br> As, that some memory may exist of them, <br> Above the buried dead their tombs in earth<sup>736</sup> <br> Bear sculptured on them what they were before; <br> Whence often there we weep for them afresh, <br> From pricking of remembrance, which alone <br> To the compassionate doth set its spur; <br> So saw I there, but of a better semblance <br> In point of artifice, with figures covered <br> Whate’er as pathway from the mount projects. <br> I saw that one who was created noble<sup>737</sup> <br> More than all other creatures, down from heaven <br> Flaming with lightnings fall upon one side.<sup>738</sup> <br> I saw Briareus smitten by the dart<sup>739</sup> <br> Celestial, lying on the other side, <br> Heavy upon the earth by mortal frost. <br> I saw Thymbraeus, Pallas saw, and Mars,<sup>740</sup> <br> Still clad in armour round about their father, <br> Gaze at the scattered members of the giants. <br> I saw, at foot of his great labor, Nimrod,<sup>741</sup> <br> As if bewildered, looking at the people <br> Who had been proud with him in Sennaar.<sup>742</sup> <br> O Niobe! with what afflicted eyes<sup>743</sup> <br> Thee I beheld upon the pathway traced, <br> Between thy seven and seven children slain!<sup>744</sup> <br> O Saul! how fallen upon thy proper sword<sup>745</sup> <br> Didst thou appear there lifeless in Gilboa, <br> That felt thereafter neither rain nor dew!<sup>746</sup> <br> O mad Arachne! so I thee beheld<sup>747</sup> <br> E’en then half spider, sad upon the shreds <br> Of fabric wrought in evil hour for thee! <br> O Rehoboam! no more seems to threaten<sup>748</sup> <br> Thine image there; but full of consternation <br> A chariot bears it off, when none pursues! <br> Displayed moreo’er the adamantine pavement <br> How unto his own mother made Alcmaeon<sup>749</sup> <br> Costly appear the luckless ornament; <br> Displayed how his own sons did throw themselves <br> Upon Sennacherib within the temple,<sup>750</sup> <br> And how, he being dead, they left him there; <br> Displayed the ruin and the cruel carnage <br> That Tomyris wrought, when she to Cyrus said,<sup>751</sup> <br> “Blood didst thou thirst for, and with blood I glut thee!” <br> Displayed how routed fled the Assyrians <br> After that Holofernes had been slain,<sup>752</sup> <br> And likewise the remainder of that slaughter. <br> I saw there Troy in ashes and in caverns;<sup>753</sup> <br> O Ilion! thee, how abject and debased, <br> Displayed the image that is there discerned! <br> Who e’er of pencil master was or stile, <br> That could portray the shades and traits which there <br> Would cause each subtile genius to admire? <br> Dead seemed the dead, the living seemed alive;<sup>754</sup> <br> Better than I saw not who saw the truth, <br> All that I trod upon while bowed I went. <br> Now wax ye proud, and on with looks uplifted, <br> Ye sons of Eve, and bow not down your faces <br> So that ye may behold your evil ways! <br> More of the mount by us was now encompassed, <br> And far more spent the circuit of the sun, <br> Than had the mind preoccupied imagined, <br> When he, who ever watchful in advance <br> Was going on, began: “Lift up thy head, <br> ’Tis no more time to go thus meditating. <br> Lo there an Angel who is making haste <br> To come towards us; lo, returning is <br> From service of the day the sixth handmaiden.<sup>755</sup> <br> With reverence thine acts and looks adorn, <br> So that he may delight to speed us upward; <br> Think that this day will never dawn again.” <br> I was familiar with his admonition <br> Ever to lose no time; so on this theme <br> He could not unto me speak covertly. <br> Towards us came the being beautiful <br> Vested in white, and in his countenance <br> Such as appears the tremulous morning star. <br> His arms he opened, and opened then his wings; <br> “Come,” said he, “near at hand here are the steps, <br> And easy from henceforth is the ascent.” <br> At this announcement few are they who come! <br> O human creatures, born to soar aloft, <br> Why fall ye thus before a little wind? <br> He led us on to where the rock was cleft; <br> There smote upon my forehead with his wings, <br> Then a safe passage promised unto me. <br> As on the right hand, to ascend the mount <br> Where seated is the church that lordeth it <br> O’er the well-guided, above Rubaconte,<sup>756</sup> <br> The bold abruptness of the ascent is broken <br> By stairways that were made there in the age <br> When still were safe the ledger and the stave,<sup>757</sup> <br> E’en thus attempered is the bank which falls <br> Sheer downward from the second circle there; <br> But on this side and that the high rock grazes. <br> As we were turning thitherward our persons, <br> “<i>Beati pauperes spiritu</i>,” voices<sup>758</sup> <br> Sang in such wise that speech could tell it not. <br> Ah me! how different are these entrances <br> From the Infernal! for with anthems here <br> One enters, and below with wild laments. <br> We now were mounting up the sacred stairs, <br> And it appeared to me by far more easy <br> Than on the plain it had appeared before. <br> Whence I: “My Master, say, what heavy thing <br> Has been uplifted from me, so that hardly <br> Aught of fatigue is felt by me in walking?” <br> He answered: “When the P’s which have remained <br> Still on thy face almost obliterate <br> Shall wholly, as the first is, be erased, <br> Thy feet will be so vanquished by good will, <br> That not alone they shall not feel fatigue, <br> But urging up will be to them delight.” <br> Then did I even as they do who are going <br> With something on the head to them unknown, <br> Unless the signs of others make them doubt, <br> Wherefore the hand to ascertain is helpful, <br> And seeks and finds, and doth fulfill the office <br> Which cannot be accomplished by the sight; <br> And with the fingers of the right hand spread <br> I found but six the letters, that had carved <br> Upon my temples he who bore the keys; <br> Upon beholding which my Leader smiled.</p>
