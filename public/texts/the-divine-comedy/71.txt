<p class="lede">The ascent to the First Heaven.</p>
<p class="verse">The glory of Him who moveth everything<sup>1196</sup> <br> Doth penetrate the universe, and shine<sup>1197</sup> <br> In one part more and in another less. <br> Within that heaven which most his light receives<sup>1198</sup> <br> Was I, and things beheld which to repeat<sup>1199</sup> <br> Nor knows, nor can, who from above descends; <br> Because in drawing near to its desire<sup>1200</sup> <br> Our intellect engulfs itself so far, <br> That after it the memory cannot go. <br> Truly whatever of the holy realm <br> I had the power to treasure in my mind <br> Shall now become the subject of my song. <br> O good Apollo, for this last emprise<sup>1201</sup> <br> Make of me such a vessel of thy power <br> As giving the beloved laurel asks! <br> One summit of Parnassus hitherto <br> Has been enough for me, but now with both <br> I needs must enter the arena left. <br> Enter into my bosom, thou, and breathe<sup>1202</sup> <br> As at the time when Marsyas thou didst draw<sup>1203</sup> <br> Out of the scabbard of those limbs of his. <br> O power divine, lend’st thou thyself to me <br> So that the shadow of the blessed realm <br> Stamped in my brain I can make manifest, <br> Thou’lt see me come unto thy darling tree, <br> And crown myself thereafter with those leaves <br> Of which the theme and thou shall make me worthy. <br> So seldom, Father, do we gather them <br> For triumph or of Caesar or of Poet, <br> (The fault and shame of human inclinations,) <br> That the Peneian foliage should bring forth <br> Joy to the joyous Delphic deity, <br> When anyone it makes to thirst for it. <br> A little spark is followed by great flame; <br> Perchance with better voices after me <br> Shall prayer be made that Cyrrha may respond!<sup>1204</sup> <br> To mortal men by passages diverse <br> Uprises the world’s lamp; but by that one <br> Which circles four uniteth with three crosses,<sup>1205</sup> <br> With better course and with a better star <br> Conjoined it issues, and the mundane wax<sup>1206</sup> <br> Tempers and stamps more after its own fashion. <br> Almost that passage had made morning there <br> And evening here, and there was wholly white<sup>1207</sup> <br> That hemisphere, and black the other part, <br> When Beatrice towards the left-hand side <br> I saw turned round, and gazing at the sun; <br> Never did eagle fasten so upon it! <br> And even as a second ray is wont <br> To issue from the first and reascend, <br> Like to a pilgrim who would fain return, <br> Thus of her action, through the eyes infused <br> In my imagination, mine I made, <br> And sunward fixed mine eyes beyond our wont. <br> There much is lawful which is here unlawful <br> Unto our powers, by virtue of the place <br> Made for the human species as its own. <br> Not long I bore it, nor so little while <br> But I beheld it sparkle round about <br> Like iron that comes molten from the fire;<sup>1208</sup> <br> And suddenly it seemed that day to day<sup>1209</sup> <br> Was added, as if He who has the power <br> Had with another sun the heaven adorned. <br> With eyes upon the everlasting wheels <br> Stood Beatrice all intent, and I, on her <br> Fixing my vision from above removed, <br> Such at her aspect inwardly became <br> As Glaucus, tasting of the herb that made him<sup>1210</sup> <br> Peer of the other gods beneath the sea. <br> To represent transhumanize in words <br> Impossible were; the example, then, suffice <br> Him for whom Grace the experience reserves. <br> If I was merely what of me thou newly<sup>1211</sup> <br> Createdst, Love who governest the heaven, <br> Thou knowest, who didst lift me with thy light! <br> When now the wheel, which thou dost make eternal<sup>1212</sup> <br> Desiring thee, made me attentive to it <br> By harmony thou dost modulate and measure,<sup>1213</sup> <br> Then seemed to me so much of heaven enkindled <br> By the sun’s flame, that neither rain nor river <br> E’er made a lake so widely spread abroad. <br> The newness of the sound and the great light <br> Kindled in me a longing for their cause, <br> Never before with such acuteness felt; <br> Whence she, who saw me as I saw myself, <br> To quiet in me my perturbed mind, <br> Opened her mouth, ere I did mine to ask, <br> And she began: “Thou makest thyself so dull <br> With false imagining, that thou seest not <br> What thou wouldst see if thou hadst shaken it off. <br> Thou art not upon earth, as thou believest; <br> But lightning, fleeing its appropriate site,<sup>1214</sup> <br> Ne’er ran as thou, who thitherward returnest.” <br> If of my former doubt I was divested <br> By these brief little words more smiled than spoken, <br> I in a new one was the more ensnared; <br> And said: “Already did I rest content <br> From great amazement; but am now amazed <br> In what way I transcend these bodies light.” <br> Whereupon she, after a pitying sigh, <br> Her eyes directed tow’rds me with that look <br> A mother casts on a delirious child; <br> And she began: “All things whate’er they be <br> Have order among themselves, and this is form, <br> That makes the universe resemble God. <br> Here do the higher creatures see the footprints <br> Of the Eternal Power, which is the end <br> Whereto is made the law already mentioned. <br> In the order that I speak of are inclined<sup>1215</sup> <br> All natures, by their destinies diverse, <br> More or less near unto their origin; <br> Hence they move onward unto ports diverse <br> O’er the great sea of being; and each one <br> With instinct given it which bears it on. <br> This bears away the fire towards the moon; <br> This is in mortal hearts the motive power; <br> This binds together and unites the earth. <br> Nor only the created things that are <br> Without intelligence this bow shoots forth, <br> But those that have both intellect and love. <br> The Providence that regulates all this<sup>1216</sup> <br> Makes with its light the heaven forever quiet,<sup>1217</sup> <br> Wherein that turns which has the greatest haste. <br> And thither now, as to a site decreed, <br> Bears us away the virtue of that cord <br> Which aims its arrows at a joyous mark. <br> True is it, that as oftentimes the form <br> Accords not with the intention of the art, <br> Because in answering is matter deaf, <br> So likewise from this course doth deviate <br> Sometimes the creature, who the power possesses, <br> Though thus impelled, to swerve some other way, <br> (In the same wise as one may see the fire <br> Fall from a cloud,) if the first impetus <br> Earthward is wrested by some false delight. <br> Thou shouldst not wonder more, if well I judge, <br> At thine ascent, than at a rivulet <br> From some high mount descending to the lowland. <br> Marvel it would be in thee, if deprived <br> Of hindrance, thou wert seated down below, <br> As if on earth the living fire were quiet.”<sup>1218</sup> <br> Thereat she heavenward turned again her face.</p>
