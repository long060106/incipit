<p class="lede">Hugh Capet⁠—The earthquake.</p>
<p class="verse">Ill strives the will against a better will;<sup>900</sup> <br> Therefore, to pleasure him, against my pleasure<sup>901</sup> <br> I drew the sponge not saturate from the water. <br> Onward I moved, and onward moved my Leader, <br> Through vacant places, skirting still the rock, <br> As on a wall close to the battlements; <br> For they that through their eyes pour drop by drop <br> The malady which all the world pervades, <br> On the other side too near the verge approach. <br> Accursed mayst thou be, thou old she-wolf, <br> That more than all the other beasts hast prey, <br> Because of hunger infinitely hollow! <br> O heaven, in whose gyrations some appear<sup>902</sup> <br> To think conditions here below are changed, <br> When will he come through whom she shall depart?<sup>903</sup> <br> Onward we went with footsteps slow and scarce, <br> And I attentive to the shades I heard <br> Piteously weeping and bemoaning them; <br> And I by peradventure heard “Sweet Mary!” <br> Uttered in front of us amid the weeping <br> Even as a woman does who is in child-birth; <br> And in continuance: “How poor thou wast <br> Is manifested by that hostelry<sup>904</sup> <br> Where thou didst lay thy sacred burden down.” <br> Thereafterward I heard: “O good Fabricius,<sup>905</sup> <br> Virtue with poverty didst thou prefer <br> To the possession of great wealth with vice.” <br> So pleasurable were these words to me <br> That I drew farther onward to have knowledge <br> Touching that spirit whence they seemed to come. <br> He furthermore was speaking of the largess<sup>906</sup> <br> Which Nicholas unto the maidens gave,<sup>907</sup> <br> In order to conduct their youth to honor. <br> “O soul that dost so excellently speak, <br> Tell me who wast thou,” said I, “and why only <br> Thou dost renew these praises well deserved? <br> Not without recompense shall be thy word, <br> If I return to finish the short journey <br> Of that life which is flying to its end.” <br> And he: “I’ll tell thee, not for any comfort <br> I may expect from earth, but that so much <br> Grace shines in thee or ever thou art dead. <br> I was the root of that malignant plant<sup>908</sup> <br> Which overshadows all the Christian world, <br> So that good fruit is seldom gathered from it; <br> But if Douay and Ghent, and Lille and Bruges <br> Had power, soon vengeance would be taken on it; <br> And this I pray of Him who judges all. <br> Hugh Capet was I called upon the earth; <br> From me were born the Louises and Philips,<sup>909</sup> <br> By whom in later days has France been governed. <br> I was the son of a Parisian butcher,<sup>910</sup> <br> What time the ancient kings had perished all,<sup>911</sup> <br> Excepting one, contrite in cloth of gray. <br> I found me grasping in my hands the rein <br> Of the realm’s government, and so great power <br> Of new acquest, and so with friends abounding, <br> That to the widowed diadem promoted <br> The head of mine own offspring was, from whom<sup>912</sup> <br> The consecrated bones of these began. <br> So long as the great dowry of Provence<sup>913</sup> <br> Out of my blood took not the sense of shame, <br> ’Twas little worth, but still it did no harm. <br> Then it began with falsehood and with force <br> Its rapine; and thereafter, for amends,<sup>914</sup> <br> Took Ponthieu, Normandy, and Gascony. <br> Charles came to Italy, and for amends<sup>915</sup> <br> A victim made of Conradin, and then<sup>916</sup> <br> Thrust Thomas back to heaven, for amends.<sup>917</sup> <br> A time I see, not very distant now, <br> Which draweth forth another Charles from France,<sup>918</sup> <br> The better to make known both him and his. <br> Unarmed he goes, and only with the lance <br> That Judas jousted with; and that he thrusts<sup>919</sup> <br> So that he makes the paunch of Florence burst.<sup>920</sup> <br> He thence not land, but sin and infamy,<sup>921</sup> <br> Shall gain, so much more grievous to himself <br> As the more light such damage he accounts. <br> The other, now gone forth, ta’en in his ship,<sup>922</sup> <br> See I his daughter sell, and chaffer for her <br> As corsairs do with other female slaves. <br> What more, O Avarice, canst thou do to us,<sup>923</sup> <br> Since thou my blood so to thyself hast drawn, <br> It careth not for its own proper flesh? <br> That less may seem the future ill and past, <br> I see the flower-de-luce Alagna enter,<sup>924</sup> <br> And Christ in his own Vicar captive made.<sup>925</sup> <br> I see him yet another time derided; <br> I see renewed the vinegar and gall, <br> And between living thieves I see him slain. <br> I see the modern Pilate so relentless,<sup>926</sup> <br> This does not sate him, but without decretal <br> He to the temple bears his sordid sails! <br> When, O my Lord! shall I be joyful made <br> By looking on the vengeance which, concealed, <br> Makes sweet thine anger in thy secrecy? <br> What I was saying of that only bride<sup>927</sup> <br> Of the Holy Ghost, and which occasioned thee <br> To turn towards me for some commentary, <br> So long has been ordained to all our prayers <br> As the day lasts; but when the night comes on, <br> Contrary sound we take instead thereof. <br> At that time we repeat Pygmalion,<sup>928</sup> <br> Of whom a traitor, thief, and parricide <br> Made his insatiable desire of gold; <br> And the misery of avaricious Midas,<sup>929</sup> <br> That followed his inordinate demand, <br> At which forevermore one needs but laugh. <br> The foolish Achan each one then records,<sup>930</sup> <br> And how he stole the spoils; so that the wrath <br> Of Joshua still appears to sting him here. <br> Then we accuse Sapphira with her husband,<sup>931</sup> <br> We laud the hoof-beats Heliodorus had,<sup>932</sup> <br> And the whole mount in infamy encircles <br> Polymnestor who murdered Polydorus.<sup>933</sup> <br> Here finally is cried: ‘O Crassus, tell us,<sup>934</sup> <br> For thou dost know, what is the taste of gold?’ <br> Sometimes we speak, one loud, another low, <br> According to desire of speech, that spurs us <br> To greater now and now to lesser pace. <br> But in the good that here by day is talked of, <br> Erewhile alone I was not; yet near by<sup>935</sup> <br> No other person lifted up his voice.” <br> From him already we departed were, <br> And made endeavor to o’ercome the road <br> As much as was permitted to our power, <br> When I perceived, like something that is falling, <br> The mountain tremble, whence a chill seized on me,<sup>936</sup> <br> As seizes him who to his death is going. <br> Certes so violently shook not Delos,<sup>937</sup> <br> Before Latona made her nest therein <br> To give birth to the two eyes of the heaven. <br> Then upon all sides there began a cry, <br> Such that the Master drew himself towards me, <br> Saying, “Fear not, while I am guiding thee.” <br> “<i>Gloria in excelsis Deo</i>,” all<sup>938</sup> <br> Were saying, from what near I comprehended, <br> Where it was possible to hear the cry. <br> We paused immovable and in suspense, <br> Even as the shepherds who first heard that song,<sup>939</sup> <br> Until the trembling ceased, and it was finished. <br> Then we resumed again our holy path, <br> Watching the shades that lay upon the ground, <br> Already turned to their accustomed plaint. <br> No ignorance ever with so great a strife <br> Had rendered me importunate to know, <br> If erreth not in this my memory, <br> As meditating then I seemed to have; <br> Nor out of haste to question did I dare, <br> Nor of myself I there could aught perceive; <br> So I went onward timorous and thoughtful.</p>
