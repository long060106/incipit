<p class="lede">St. Benedict⁠—His lamentation over the corruption of the monks⁠—The Eighth Heaven, or that of the Fixed Stars.</p>
<p class="verse">Oppressed with stupor, I unto my guide<sup>1790</sup> <br> Turned like a little child who always runs <br> For refuge there where he confideth most; <br> And she, even as a mother who straightway <br> Gives comfort to her pale and breathless boy <br> With voice whose wont it is to reassure him, <br> Said to me: “Knowest thou not thou art in heaven, <br> And knowest thou not that heaven is holy all <br> And what is done here cometh from good zeal? <br> After what wise the singing would have changed thee <br> And I by smiling, thou canst now imagine, <br> Since that the cry has startled thee so much, <br> In which if thou hadst understood its prayers <br> Already would be known to thee the vengeance <br> Which thou shalt look upon before thou diest. <br> The sword above here smiteth not in haste <br> Nor tardily, howe’er it seem to him <br> Who fearing or desiring waits for it. <br> But turn thee round towards the others now, <br> For very illustrious spirits shalt thou see, <br> If thou thy sight directest as I say.” <br> As it seemed good to her mine eyes I turned, <br> And saw a hundred spherules that together <br> With mutual rays each other more embellished. <br> I stood as one who in himself represses <br> The point of his desire, and ventures not <br> To question, he so feareth the too much. <br> And now the largest and most luculent <br> Among those pearls came forward, that it might <br> Make my desire concerning it content. <br> Within it then I heard: “If thou couldst see<sup>1791</sup> <br> Even as myself the charity that burns <br> Among us, thy conceits would be expressed; <br> But, that by waiting thou mayst not come late <br> To the high end, I will make answer even <br> Unto the thought of which thou art so chary. <br> That mountain on whose slope Cassino stands<sup>1792</sup> <br> Was frequented of old upon its summit <br> By a deluded folk and ill-disposed; <br> And I am he who first up thither bore<sup>1793</sup> <br> The name of Him who brought upon the earth <br> The truth that so much sublimateth us. <br> And such abundant grace upon me shone <br> That all the neighboring towns I drew away <br> From the impious worship that seduced the world. <br> These other fires, each one of them, were men <br> Contemplative, enkindled by that heat <br> Which maketh holy flowers and fruits spring up. <br> Here is Macarius, here is Romualdus,<sup>1794</sup> <br> Here are my brethren, who within the cloisters <br> Their footsteps stayed and kept a steadfast heart.” <br> And I to him: “The affection which thou showest <br> Speaking with me, and the good countenance <br> Which I behold and note in all your ardors, <br> In me have so my confidence dilated <br> As the sun doth the rose, when it becomes <br> As far unfolded as it hath the power. <br> Therefore I pray, and thou assure me, father, <br> If I may so much grace receive, that I <br> May thee behold with countenance unveiled.” <br> He thereupon: “Brother, thy high desire <br> In the remotest sphere shall be fulfilled, <br> Where are fulfilled all others and my own. <br> There perfect is, and ripened, and complete, <br> Every desire; within that one alone<sup>1795</sup> <br> Is every part where it has always been; <br> For it is not in space, nor turns on poles, <br> And unto it our stairway reaches up, <br> Whence thus from out thy sight it steals away. <br> Up to that height the Patriarch Jacob saw it<sup>1796</sup> <br> Extending its supernal part, what time <br> So thronged with angels it appeared to him. <br> But to ascend it now no one uplifts <br> His feet from off the earth, and now my Rule <br> Below remaineth for mere waste of paper.<sup>1797</sup> <br> The walls that used of old to be an Abbey <br> Are changed to dens of robbers, and the cowls<sup>1798</sup> <br> Are sacks filled full of miserable flour. <br> But heavy usury is not taken up<sup>1799</sup> <br> So much against God’s pleasure as that fruit <br> Which maketh so insane the heart of monks; <br> For whatsoever hath the Church in keeping <br> Is for the folk that ask it in God’s name, <br> Not for one’s kindred or for something worse. <br> The flesh of mortals is so very soft, <br> That good beginnings down below suffice not <br> From springing of the oak to bearing acorns. <br> Peter began with neither gold nor silver, <br> And I with orison and abstinence, <br> And Francis with humility his convent. <br> And if thou lookest at each one’s beginning, <br> And then regardest whither he has run, <br> Thou shalt behold the white changed into brown. <br> In verity the Jordan backward turned,<sup>1800</sup> <br> And the sea’s fleeing, when God willed were more <br> A wonder to behold, than succor here.” <br> Thus unto me he said; and then withdrew <br> To his own band, and the band closed together; <br> Then like a whirlwind all was upward rapt. <br> The gentle Lady urged me on behind them <br> Up o’er that stairway by a single sign, <br> So did her virtue overcome my nature; <br> Nor here below, where one goes up and down <br> By natural law, was motion e’er so swift <br> That it could be compared unto my wing. <br> Reader, as I may unto that devout <br> Triumph return, on whose account I often<sup>1801</sup> <br> For my transgressions weep and beat my breast⁠— <br> Thou hadst not thrust thy finger in the fire <br> And drawn it out again, before I saw <br> The sign that follows Taurus, and was in it.<sup>1802</sup> <br> O glorious stars, O light impregnated<sup>1803</sup> <br> With mighty virtue, from which I acknowledge <br> All of my genius, whatsoe’er it be, <br> With you was born, and hid himself with you,<sup>1804</sup> <br> He who is father of all mortal life, <br> When first I tasted of the Tuscan air; <br> And then when grace was freely given to me <br> To enter the high wheel which turns you round,<sup>1805</sup> <br> Your region was allotted unto me. <br> To you devoutly at this hour my soul <br> Is sighing, that it virtue may acquire <br> For the stern pass that draws it to itself. <br> “Thou art so near unto the last salvation,” <br> Thus Beatrice began, “thou oughtest now <br> To have thine eyes unclouded and acute; <br> And therefore, ere thou enter farther in, <br> Look down once more, and see how vast a world <br> Thou hast already put beneath thy feet; <br> So that thy heart, as jocund as it may, <br> Present itself to the triumphant throng <br> That comes rejoicing through this rounded ether.” <br> I with my sight returned through one and all <br> The sevenfold spheres, and I beheld this globe <br> Such that I smiled at its ignoble semblance;<sup>1806</sup> <br> And that opinion I approve as best <br> Which doth account it least; and he who thinks <br> Of something else may truly be called just. <br> I saw the daughter of Latona shining<sup>1807</sup> <br> Without that shadow, which to me was cause <br> That once I had believed her rare and dense.<sup>1808</sup> <br> The aspect of thy son, Hyperion,<sup>1809</sup> <br> Here I sustained, and saw how move themselves <br> Around and near him Maia and Dione.<sup>1810</sup> <br> Thence there appeared the temperateness of Jove<sup>1811</sup> <br> ’Twixt son and father, and to me was clear <br> The change that of their whereabout they make; <br> And all the seven made manifest to me <br> How great they are, and eke how swift they are,<sup>1812</sup> <br> And how they are in distant habitations. <br> The threshing-floor that maketh us so proud,<sup>1813</sup> <br> To me revolving with the eternal Twins, <br> Was all apparent made from hill to harbour! <br> Then to the beauteous eyes mine eyes I turned.</p>
