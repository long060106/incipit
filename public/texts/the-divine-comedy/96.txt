<p class="lede">St. John examines Dante upon Charity.</p>
<p class="verse">While I was doubting for my vision quenched,<sup>1903</sup> <br> Out of the flame refulgent that had quenched it <br> Issued a breathing, that attentive made me, <br> Saying: “While thou recoverest the sense <br> Of seeing which in me thou hast consumed, <br> ’Tis well that speaking thou shouldst compensate it. <br> Begin then, and declare to what thy soul <br> Is aimed, and count it for a certainty, <br> Sight is in thee bewildered and not dead; <br> Because the Lady, who through this divine <br> Region conducteth thee, has in her look <br> The power the hand of Ananias had.”<sup>1904</sup> <br> I said: “As pleaseth her, or soon or late <br> Let the cure come to eyes that portals were <br> When she with fire I ever burn with entered. <br> The Good, that gives contentment to this Court, <br> The Alpha and Omega is of all<sup>1905</sup> <br> The writing that love reads me low or loud.” <br> The selfsame voice, that taken had from me <br> The terror of the sudden dazzlement, <br> To speak still farther put it in my thought; <br> And said: “In verity with finer sieve <br> Behoveth thee to sift; thee it behoveth <br> To say who aimed thy bow at such a target.” <br> And I: “By philosophic arguments, <br> And by authority that hence descends, <br> Such love must needs imprint itself in me; <br> For Good, so far as good, when comprehended <br> Doth straight enkindle love, and so much greater <br> As more of goodness in itself it holds; <br> Then to that Essence (whose is such advantage <br> That every good which out of it is found <br> Is nothing but a ray of its own light) <br> More than elsewhither must the mind be moved <br> Of everyone, in loving, who discerns <br> The truth in which this evidence is founded. <br> Such truth he to my intellect reveals <br> Who demonstrates to me the primal love<sup>1906</sup> <br> Of all the sempiternal substances.<sup>1907</sup> <br> The voice reveals it of the truthful Author, <br> Who says to Moses, speaking of Himself, <br> ‘I will make all my goodness pass before thee.’<sup>1908</sup> <br> Thou too revealest it to me, beginning <br> The loud Evangel, that proclaims the secret<sup>1909</sup> <br> Of heaven to earth above all other edict.” <br> And I heard say: “By human intellect<sup>1910</sup> <br> And by authority concordant with it, <br> Of all thy loves reserve for God the highest. <br> But say again if other cords thou feelest, <br> Draw thee towards Him, that thou mayst proclaim <br> With how many teeth this love is biting thee.” <br> The holy purpose of the Eagle of Christ<sup>1911</sup> <br> Not latent was, nay, rather I perceived <br> Whither he fain would my profession lead. <br> Therefore I recommenced: “All of those bites <br> Which have the power to turn the heart to God <br> Unto my charity have been concurrent. <br> The being of the world, and my own being, <br> The death which He endured that I may live, <br> And that which all the faithful hope, as I do, <br> With the forementioned vivid consciousness <br> Have drawn me from the sea of love perverse, <br> And of the right have placed me on the shore. <br> The leaves, wherewith embowered is all the garden<sup>1912</sup> <br> Of the Eternal Gardener, do I love <br> As much as he has granted them of good.” <br> As soon as I had ceased, a song most sweet <br> Throughout the heaven resounded, and my Lady <br> Said with the others, “Holy, holy, holy!”<sup>1913</sup> <br> And as at some keen light one wakes from sleep <br> By reason of the visual spirit that runs <br> Unto the splendor passed from coat to coat, <br> And he who wakes abhorreth what he sees, <br> So all unconscious is his sudden waking, <br> Until the judgment cometh to his aid, <br> So from before mine eyes did Beatrice <br> Chase every mote with radiance of her own, <br> That cast its light a thousand miles and more. <br> Whence better after than before I saw, <br> And in a kind of wonderment I asked <br> About a fourth light that I saw with us. <br> And said my Lady: “There within those rays <br> Gazes upon its Maker the first soul<sup>1914</sup> <br> That ever the first virtue did create.” <br> Even as the bough that downward bends its top <br> At transit of the wind, and then is lifted <br> By its own virtue, which inclines it upward, <br> Likewise did I, the while that she was speaking, <br> Being amazed, and then I was made bold <br> By a desire to speak wherewith I burned. <br> And I began: “O apple, that mature<sup>1915</sup> <br> Alone hast been produced, O ancient father, <br> To whom each wife is daughter and daughter-in-law, <br> Devoutly as I can I supplicate thee <br> That thou wouldst speak to me; thou seest my wish; <br> And I, to hear thee quickly, speak it not.” <br> Sometimes an animal, when covered, struggles <br> So that his impulse needs must be apparent, <br> By reason of the wrappage following it; <br> And in like manner the primeval soul <br> Made clear to me athwart its covering <br> How jubilant it was to give me pleasure. <br> Then breathed: “Without thy uttering it to me, <br> Thine inclination better I discern <br> Than thou whatever thing is surest to thee; <br> For I behold it in the truthful mirror, <br> That of Himself all things parhelion makes,<sup>1916</sup> <br> And none makes Him parhelion of itself. <br> Thou fain wouldst hear how long ago God placed me <br> Within the lofty garden, where this Lady <br> Unto so long a stairway thee disposed. <br> And how long to mine eyes it was a pleasure, <br> And of the great disdain the proper cause, <br> And the language that I used and that I made. <br> Now, son of mine, the tasting of the tree <br> Not in itself was cause of so great exile, <br> But solely the o’erstepping of the bounds. <br> There, whence thy Lady moved Virgilius,<sup>1917</sup> <br> Four thousand and three hundred and two circuits <br> Made by the sun, this Council I desired; <br> And him I saw return to all the lights <br> Of his highway nine hundred times and thirty, <br> Whilst I upon the earth was tarrying. <br> The language that I spake was quite extinct<sup>1918</sup> <br> Before that in the work interminable <br> The people under Nimrod were employed; <br> For nevermore result of reasoning <br> (Because of human pleasure that doth change, <br> Obedient to the heavens) was durable.<sup>1919</sup> <br> A natural action is it that man speaks; <br> But whether thus or thus, doth nature leave <br> To your own art, as seemeth best to you. <br> Ere I descended to the infernal anguish, <br> <i>El</i> was on earth the name of the Chief Good,<sup>1920</sup> <br> From whom comes all the joy that wraps me round <br> <i>Eli</i> he then was called, and that is proper,<sup>1921</sup> <br> Because the use of men is like a leaf<sup>1922</sup> <br> On bough, which goeth and another cometh. <br> Upon the mount that highest o’er the wave<sup>1923</sup> <br> Rises was I, in life or pure or sinful, <br> From the first hour to that which is the second, <br> As the sun changes quadrant, to the sixth.”<sup>1924</sup></p>
