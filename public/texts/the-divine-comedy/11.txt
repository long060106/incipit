<p class="lede">Farinata and Cavalcante de’ Cavalcanti.</p>
<p class="verse">Now onward goes, along a narrow path<sup>133</sup> <br> Between the torments and the city wall, <br> My Master, and I follow at his back. <br> “O power supreme, that through these impious circles <br> Turnest me,” I began, “as pleases thee, <br> Speak to me, and my longings satisfy; <br> The people who are lying in these tombs, <br> Might they be seen? already are uplifted <br> The covers all, and no one keepeth guard.” <br> And he to me: “They all will be closed up <br> When from Jehosaphat they shall return <br> Here with the bodies they have left above. <br> Their cemetery have upon this side <br> With Epicurus all his followers,<sup>134</sup> <br> Who with the body mortal make the soul; <br> But in the question thou dost put to me, <br> Within here shalt thou soon be satisfied, <br> And likewise in the wish thou keepest silent.” <br> And I: “Good Leader, I but keep concealed <br> From thee my heart, that I may speak the less, <br> Nor only now hast thou thereto disposed me.” <br> “O Tuscan, thou who through the city of fire <br> Goest alive, thus speaking modestly, <br> Be pleased to stay thy footsteps in this place. <br> Thy mode of speaking makes thee manifest <br> A native of that noble fatherland, <br> To which perhaps I too molestful was.” <br> Upon a sudden issued forth this sound <br> From out one of the tombs; wherefore I pressed, <br> Fearing, a little nearer to my Leader. <br> And unto me he said: “Turn thee; what dost thou? <br> Behold there Farinata who has risen;<sup>135</sup> <br> From the waist upwards wholly shalt thou see him.” <br> I had already fixed mine eyes on his, <br> And he uprose erect with breast and front <br> E’en as if Hell he had in great despite. <br> And with courageous hands and prompt my Leader <br> Thrust me between the sepulchres towards him, <br> Exclaiming, “Let thy words explicit be.” <br> As soon as I was at the foot of his tomb, <br> Somewhat he eyed me, and, as if disdainful, <br> Then asked of me, “Who were thine ancestors?” <br> I, who desirous of obeying was, <br> Concealed it not, but all revealed to him; <br> Whereat he raised his brows a little upward. <br> Then said he: “Fiercely adverse have they been<sup>136</sup> <br> To me, and to my fathers, and my party; <br> So that two several times I scattered them.” <br> “If they were banished, they returned on all sides,” <br> I answered him, “the first time and the second; <br> But yours have not acquired that art aright.”<sup>137</sup> <br> Then there uprose upon the sight, uncovered <br> Down to the chin, a shadow at his side;<sup>138</sup> <br> I think that he had risen on his knees. <br> Round me he gazed, as if solicitude <br> He had to see if someone else were with me; <br> But after his suspicion was all spent, <br> Weeping, he said to me: “If through this blind <br> Prison thou goest by loftiness of genius, <br> Where is my son? and why is he not with thee?”<sup>139</sup> <br> And I to him: “I come not of myself; <br> He who is waiting yonder leads me here, <br> Whom in disdain perhaps your Guido had.”<sup>140</sup> <br> His language and the mode of punishment <br> Already unto me had read his name; <br> On that account my answer was so full. <br> Up starting suddenly, he cried out: “How <br> Saidst thou⁠—he had? Is he not still alive? <br> Does not the sweet light strike upon his eyes?” <br> When he became aware of some delay, <br> Which I before my answer made, supine <br> He fell again, and forth appeared no more. <br> But the other, magnanimous, at whose desire <br> I had remained, did not his aspect change, <br> Neither his neck he moved, nor bent his side.<sup>141</sup> <br> “And if,” continuing his first discourse, <br> “They have that art,” he said, “not learned aright, <br> That more tormenteth me, than doth this bed. <br> But fifty times shall not rekindled be <br> The countenance of the Lady who reigns here,<sup>142</sup> <br> Ere thou shalt know how heavy is that art; <br> And as thou wouldst to the sweet world return, <br> Say why that people is so pitiless <br> Against my race in each one of its laws?” <br> Whence I to him: “The slaughter and great carnage <br> Which have with crimson stained the Arbia, cause<sup>143</sup> <br> Such orisons in our temple to be made.” <br> After his head he with a sigh had shaken, <br> “There I was not alone,” he said, “nor surely <br> Without a cause had with the others moved. <br> But there I was alone, where everyone <br> Consented to the laying waste of Florence, <br> He who defended her with open face.” <br> “Ah! so hereafter may your seed repose,”<sup>144</sup> <br> I him entreated, “solve for me that knot, <br> Which has entangled my conceptions here. <br> It seems that you can see, if I hear rightly, <br> Beforehand whatsoe’er time brings with it, <br> And in the present have another mode.” <br> “We see, like those who have imperfect sight, <br> The things,” he said, “that distant are from us; <br> So much still shines on us the Sovereign Ruler. <br> When they draw near, or are, is wholly vain <br> Our intellect, and if none brings it to us, <br> Not anything know we of your human state. <br> Hence thou canst understand, that wholly dead <br> Will be our knowledge from the moment when <br> The portal of the future shall be closed.” <br> Then I, as if compunctious for my fault, <br> Said: “Now, then, you will tell that fallen one, <br> That still his son is with the living joined. <br> And if just now, in answering, I was dumb, <br> Tell him I did it because I was thinking <br> Already of the error you have solved me.” <br> And now my Master was recalling me, <br> Wherefore more eagerly I prayed the spirit <br> That he would tell me who was with him there. <br> He said: “With more than a thousand here I lie; <br> Within here is the second Frederick,<sup>145</sup> <br> And the Cardinal, and of the rest I speak not.”<sup>146</sup> <br> Thereon he hid himself; and I towards <br> The ancient poet turned my steps, reflecting <br> Upon that saying, which seemed hostile to me. <br> He moved along; and afterward, thus going, <br> He said to me, “Why art thou so bewildered?” <br> And I in his inquiry satisfied him. <br> “Let memory preserve what thou hast heard <br> Against thyself,” that Sage commanded me, <br> “And now attend here”; and he raised his finger. <br> “When thou shalt be before the radiance sweet <br> Of her whose beauteous eyes all things behold, <br> From her thou’lt know the journey of thy life.” <br> Unto the left hand then he turned his feet; <br> We left the wall, and went towards the middle, <br> Along a path that strikes into a valley, <br> Which even up there unpleasant made its stench.</p>
