<p class="lede">The Eighth Circle: Malebolge⁠—The fraudulent⁠—The First Bolgia: seducers and panders⁠—Venedico Caccianimico⁠—Jason⁠—The Second Bolgia: flatterers⁠—Allessio Interminelli⁠—Thaïs.</p>
<p class="verse">There is a place in Hell called Malebolge,<sup>251</sup> <br> Wholly of stone and of an iron color,<sup>252</sup> <br> As is the circle that around it turns. <br> Right in the middle of the field malign <br> There yawns a well exceeding wide and deep, <br> Of which its place the structure will recount. <br> Round, then, is that enclosure which remains <br> Between the well and foot of the high, hard bank, <br> And has distinct in valleys ten its bottom. <br> As where for the protection of the walls <br> Many and many moats surround the castles, <br> The part in which they are a figure forms, <br> Just such an image those presented there; <br> And as about such strongholds from their gates <br> Unto the outer bank are little bridges, <br> So from the precipice’s base did crags <br> Project, which intersected dikes and moats, <br> Unto the well that truncates and collects them. <br> Within this place, down shaken from the back <br> Of Geryon, we found us; and the Poet <br> Held to the left, and I moved on behind. <br> Upon my right hand I beheld new anguish, <br> New torments, and new wielders of the lash, <br> Wherewith the foremost Bolgia was replete. <br> Down at the bottom were the sinners naked; <br> This side the middle came they facing us, <br> Beyond it, with us, but with greater steps; <br> Even as the Romans, for the mighty host, <br> The year of Jubilee, upon the bridge,<sup>253</sup> <br> Have chosen a mode to pass the people over; <br> For all upon one side towards the Castle<sup>254</sup> <br> Their faces have, and go unto Saint Peter’s; <br> On the other side they go towards the Mountain. <br> This side and that, along the livid stone <br> Beheld I horned demons with great scourges, <br> Who cruelly were beating them behind. <br> Ah me! how they did make them lift their legs <br> At the first blows! and sooth not any one <br> The second waited for, nor for the third. <br> While I was going on, mine eyes by one <br> Encountered were; and straight I said: “Already <br> With sight of this one I am not unfed.” <br> Therefore I stayed my feet to make him out, <br> And with me the sweet Guide came to a stand, <br> And to my going somewhat back assented; <br> And he, the scourged one, thought to hide himself, <br> Lowering his face, but little it availed him; <br> For said I: “Thou that castest down thine eyes, <br> If false are not the features which thou bearest, <br> Thou art Venedico Caccianimico;<sup>255</sup> <br> But what doth bring thee to such pungent sauces?”<sup>256</sup> <br> And he to me: “Unwillingly I tell it; <br> But forces me thine utterance distinct, <br> Which makes me recollect the ancient world. <br> I was the one who the fair Ghisola <br> Induced to grant the wishes of the Marquis, <br> Howe’er the shameless story may be told. <br> Not the sole Bolognese am I who weeps here; <br> Nay, rather is this place so full of them, <br> That not so many tongues today are taught <br> ’Twixt Reno and Savena to say <i>sipa</i>;<sup>257</sup> <br> And if thereof thou wishest pledge or proof, <br> Bring to thy mind our avaricious heart.” <br> While speaking in this manner, with his scourge <br> A demon smote him, and said: “Get thee gone <br> Pander, there are no women here for coin.” <br> I joined myself again unto mine Escort; <br> Thereafterward with footsteps few we came <br> To where a crag projected from the bank. <br> This very easily did we ascend, <br> And turning to the right along its ridge, <br> From those eternal circles we departed.<sup>258</sup> <br> When we were there, where it is hollowed out <br> Beneath, to give a passage to the scourged, <br> The Guide said: “Wait, and see that on thee strike <br> The vision of those others evil-born, <br> Of whom thou hast not yet beheld the faces, <br> Because together with us they have gone.” <br> From the old bridge we looked upon the train <br> Which tow’rds us came upon the other border, <br> And which the scourges in like manner smite. <br> And the good Master, without my inquiring, <br> Said to me: “See that tall one who is coming, <br> And for his pain seems not to shed a tear; <br> Still what a royal aspect he retains! <br> That Jason is, who by his heart and cunning<sup>259</sup> <br> The Colchians of the Ram made destitute. <br> He by the isle of Lemnos passed along <br> After the daring women pitiless <br> Had unto death devoted all their males. <br> There with his tokens and with ornate words <br> Did he deceive Hypsipyle, the maiden<sup>260</sup> <br> Who first, herself, had all the rest deceived. <br> There did he leave her pregnant and forlorn; <br> Such sin unto such punishment condemns him, <br> And also for Medea is vengeance done. <br> With him go those who in such wise deceive; <br> And this sufficient be of the first valley <br> To know, and those that in its jaws it holds.” <br> We were already where the narrow path <br> Crosses athwart the second dike, and forms <br> Of that a buttress for another arch. <br> Thence we heard people, who are making moan <br> In the next Bolgia, snorting with their muzzles, <br> And with their palms beating upon themselves. <br> The margins were encrusted with a mould <br> By exhalation from below, that sticks there, <br> And with the eyes and nostrils wages war. <br> The bottom is so deep, no place suffices <br> To give us sight of it, without ascending <br> The arch’s back, where most the crag impends. <br> Thither we came, and thence down in the moat <br> I saw a people smothered in a filth <br> That out of human privies seemed to flow; <br> And whilst below there with mine eye I search, <br> I saw one with his head so foul with ordure, <br> It was not clear if he were clerk or layman. <br> He screamed to me: “Wherefore art thou so eager <br> To look at me more than the other foul ones?” <br> And I to him: “Because, if I remember, <br> I have already seen thee with dry hair, <br> And thou ’rt Alessio Interminei of Lucca;<sup>261</sup> <br> Therefore I eye thee more than all the others.” <br> And he thereon, belaboring his pumpkin: <br> “The flatteries have submerged me here below, <br> Wherewith my tongue was never surfeited.” <br> Then said to me the Guide: “See that thou thrust <br> Thy visage somewhat farther in advance, <br> That with thine eyes thou well the face attain <br> Of that uncleanly and dishevelled drab, <br> Who there doth scratch herself with filthy nails, <br> And crouches now, and now on foot is standing. <br> Thaïs the harlot is it, who replied<sup>262</sup> <br> Unto her paramour, when he said, ‘Have I <br> Great gratitude from thee?’⁠—‘Nay, marvellous’; <br> And herewith let our sight be satisfied.”<sup>263</sup></p>
