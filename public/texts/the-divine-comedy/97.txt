<p class="lede">St. Peter’s reproof of bad popes⁠—The Ascent to the Ninth Heaven, or the Primum Mobile.</p>
<p class="verse">“Glory be to the Father, to the Son,<sup>1925</sup> <br> And Holy Ghost!” all Paradise began, <br> So that the melody inebriate made me. <br> What I beheld seemed unto me a smile <br> Of the universe; for my inebriation <br> Found entrance through the hearing and the sight. <br> O joy! O gladness inexpressible! <br> O perfect life of love and peacefulness! <br> O riches without hankering secure!<sup>1926</sup> <br> Before mine eyes were standing the four torches<sup>1927</sup> <br> Enkindled, and the one that first had come <br> Began to make itself more luminous; <br> And even such in semblance it became <br> As Jupiter would become, if he and Mars<sup>1928</sup> <br> Were birds, and they should interchange their feathers. <br> That Providence, which here distributeth <br> Season and service, in the blessed choir <br> Had silence upon every side imposed. <br> When I heard say: “If I my color change, <br> Marvel not at it; for while I am speaking <br> Thou shalt behold all these their color change. <br> He who usurps upon the earth my place,<sup>1929</sup> <br> My place, my place, which vacant has become <br> Before the presence of the Son of God, <br> Has of my cemetery made a sewer<sup>1930</sup> <br> Of blood and stench, whereby the Perverse One, <br> Who fell from here, below there is appeased!” <br> With the same color which, through sun adverse, <br> Painteth the clouds at evening or at morn, <br> Beheld I then the whole of heaven suffused. <br> And as a modest woman, who abides <br> Sure of herself, and at another’s failing, <br> From listening only, timorous becomes, <br> Even thus did Beatrice change countenance; <br> And I believe in heaven was such eclipse, <br> When suffered the supreme Omnipotence;<sup>1931</sup> <br> Thereafterward proceeded forth his words <br> With voice so much transmuted from itself, <br> The very countenance was not more changed. <br> “The spouse of Christ has never nurtured been <br> On blood of mine, of Linus and of Cletus,<sup>1932</sup> <br> To be made use of in acquest of gold; <br> But in acquest of this delightful life <br> Sixtus and Pius, Urban and Calixtus,<sup>1933</sup> <br> After much lamentation, shed their blood. <br> Our purpose was not, that on the right hand <br> Of our successors should in part be seated<sup>1934</sup> <br> The Christian folk, in part upon the other; <br> Nor that the keys which were to me confided <br> Should e’er become the escutcheon on a banner,<sup>1935</sup> <br> That should wage war on those who are baptized;<sup>1936</sup> <br> Nor I be made the figure of a seal <br> To privileges venal and mendacious,<sup>1937</sup> <br> Whereat I often redden and flash with fire. <br> In garb of shepherds the rapacious wolves<sup>1938</sup> <br> Are seen from here above o’er all the pastures! <br> O wrath of God, why dost thou slumber still?<sup>1939</sup> <br> To drink our blood the Caorsines and Gascons<sup>1940</sup> <br> Are making ready. O thou good beginning, <br> Unto how vile an end must thou needs fall! <br> But the high Providence, that with Scipio<sup>1941</sup> <br> At Rome the glory of the world defended, <br> Will speedily bring aid, as I conceive; <br> And thou, my son, who by thy mortal weight <br> Shalt down return again, open thy mouth; <br> What I conceal not, do not thou conceal.” <br> As with its frozen vapors downward falls <br> In flakes our atmosphere, what time the horn<sup>1942</sup> <br> Of the celestial Goat doth touch the sun,<sup>1943</sup> <br> Upward in such array saw I the ether <br> Become, and flaked with the triumphant vapors, <br> Which there together with us had remained.<sup>1944</sup> <br> My sight was following up their semblances, <br> And followed till the medium, by excess,<sup>1945</sup> <br> The passing farther onward took from it; <br> Whereat the Lady, who beheld me freed <br> From gazing upward, said to me: “Cast down <br> Thy sight, and see how far thou art turned round.” <br> Since the first time that I had downward looked,<sup>1946</sup> <br> I saw that I had moved through the whole arc <br> Which the first climate makes from midst to end;<sup>1947</sup> <br> So that I saw the mad track of Ulysses<sup>1948</sup> <br> Past Gades, and this side, well nigh the shore<sup>1949</sup> <br> Whereon became Europa a sweet burden.<sup>1950</sup> <br> And of this threshing-floor the site to me<sup>1951</sup> <br> Were more unveiled, but the sun was proceeding <br> Under my feet, a sign and more removed.<sup>1952</sup> <br> My mind enamoured, which is dallying<sup>1953</sup> <br> At all times with my Lady, to bring back <br> To her mine eyes was more than ever ardent. <br> And if or Art or Nature has made bait<sup>1954</sup> <br> To catch the eyes and so possess the mind, <br> In human flesh or in its portraiture, <br> All joined together would appear as nought <br> To the divine delight which shone upon me <br> When to her smiling face I turned me round. <br> The virtue that her look endowed me with <br> From the fair nest of Leda tore me forth,<sup>1955</sup> <br> And up into the swiftest heaven impelled me. <br> Its parts exceeding full of life and lofty <br> Are all so uniform, I cannot say <br> Which Beatrice selected for my place. <br> But she, who was aware of my desire,<sup>1956</sup> <br> Began, the while she smiled so joyously <br> That God seemed in her countenance to rejoice: <br> “The nature of that motion, which keeps quiet <br> The centre and all the rest about it moves, <br> From hence begins as from its starting point. <br> And in this heaven there is no other Where<sup>1957</sup> <br> Than in the Mind Divine, wherein is kindled <br> The love that turns it, and the power it rains. <br> Within a circle light and love embrace it, <br> Even as this doth the others, and that precinct<sup>1958</sup> <br> He who encircles it alone controls. <br> Its motion is not by another meted, <br> But all the others measured are by this, <br> As ten is by the half and by the fifth.<sup>1959</sup> <br> And in what manner time in such a pot <br> May have its roots, and in the rest its leaves, <br> Now unto thee can manifest be made. <br> O Covetousness, that mortals dost engulf <br> Beneath thee so, that no one hath the power <br> Of drawing back his eyes from out thy waves! <br> Full fairly blossoms in mankind the will; <br> But the uninterrupted rain converts <br> Into abortive wildings the true plums. <br> Fidelity and innocence are found<sup>1960</sup> <br> Only in children; afterwards they both <br> Take flight or e’er the cheeks with down are covered. <br> One, while he prattles still, observes the fasts, <br> Who, when his tongue is loosed, forthwith devours <br> Whatever food under whatever moon; <br> Another, while he prattles, loves and listens <br> Unto his mother, who when speech is perfect <br> Forthwith desires to see her in her grave. <br> Even thus is swarthy made the skin so white <br> In its first aspect of the daughter fair<sup>1961</sup> <br> Of him who brings the morn, and leaves the night. <br> Thou, that it may not be a marvel to thee, <br> Think that on earth there is no one who governs;<sup>1962</sup> <br> Whence goes astray the human family. <br> Ere January be unwintered wholly <br> By the centesimal on earth neglected,<sup>1963</sup> <br> Shall these supernal circles roar so loud <br> The tempest that has been so long awaited<sup>1964</sup> <br> Shall whirl the poops about where are the prows; <br> So that the fleet shall run its course direct, <br> And the true fruit shall follow on the flower.”</p>
