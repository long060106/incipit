<p class="lede">St. Peter examines Dante upon Faith.</p>
<p class="verse">“O company elect to the great supper<sup>1841</sup> <br> Of the Lamb benedight, who feedeth you <br> So that forever full is your desire, <br> If by the grace of God this man foretaste <br> Something of that which falleth from your table, <br> Or ever death prescribe to him the time, <br> Direct your mind to his immense desire, <br> And him somewhat bedew; ye drinking are <br> Forever at the fount whence comes his thought.” <br> Thus Beatrice; and those souls beatified <br> Transformed themselves to spheres on steadfast poles, <br> Flaming intensely in the guise of comets. <br> And as the wheels in works of horologes <br> Revolve so that the first to the beholder <br> Motionless seems, and the last one to fly, <br> So in like manner did those carols, dancing<sup>1842</sup> <br> In different measure, of their affluence<sup>1843</sup> <br> Give me the gauge, as they were swift or slow. <br> From that one which I noted of most beauty<sup>1844</sup> <br> Beheld I issue forth a fire so happy<sup>1845</sup> <br> That none it left there of a greater brightness; <br> And around Beatrice three several times<sup>1846</sup> <br> It whirled itself with so divine a song, <br> My fantasy repeats it not to me; <br> Therefore the pen skips, and I write it not, <br> Since our imagination for such folds, <br> Much more our speech, is of a tint too glaring.<sup>1847</sup> <br> “O holy sister mine, who us implorest<sup>1848</sup> <br> With such devotion, by thine ardent love <br> Thou dost unbind me from that beautiful sphere!” <br> Thereafter, having stopped, the blessed fire <br> Unto my Lady did direct its breath, <br> Which spake in fashion as I here have said. <br> And she: “O light eterne of the great man <br> To whom our Lord delivered up the keys <br> He carried down of this miraculous joy, <br> This one examine on points light and grave, <br> As good beseemeth thee, about the Faith <br> By means of which thou on the sea didst walk. <br> If he love well, and hope well, and believe, <br> From thee ’tis hid not; for thou hast thy sight<sup>1849</sup> <br> There where depicted everything is seen. <br> But since this kingdom has made citizens <br> By means of the true Faith, to glorify it <br> ’Tis well he have the chance to speak thereof.” <br> As baccalaureate arms himself, and speaks not <br> Until the master doth propose the question, <br> To argue it, and not to terminate it, <br> So did I arm myself with every reason, <br> While she was speaking, that I might be ready <br> For such a questioner and such profession. <br> “Say, thou good Christian; manifest thyself; <br> What is the Faith?” Whereat I raised my brow <br> Unto that light wherefrom was this breathed forth. <br> Then turned I round to Beatrice, and she <br> Prompt signals made to me that I should pour <br> The water forth from my internal fountain. <br> “May grace, that suffers me to make confession,” <br> Began I, “to the great centurion,<sup>1850</sup> <br> Cause my conceptions all to be explicit!” <br> And I continued: “As the truthful pen, <br> Father, of thy dear brother wrote of it,<sup>1851</sup> <br> Who put with thee Rome into the good way, <br> Faith is the substance of the things we hope for,<sup>1852</sup> <br> And evidence of those that are not seen; <br> And this appears to me its quiddity.”<sup>1853</sup> <br> Then heard I: “Very rightly thou perceivest, <br> If well thou understandest why he placed it <br> With substances and then with evidences.” <br> And I thereafterward: “The things profound, <br> That here vouchsafe to me their apparition, <br> Unto all eyes below are so concealed, <br> That they exist there only in belief, <br> Upon the which is founded the high hope, <br> And hence it takes the nature of a substance. <br> And it behoveth us from this belief <br> To reason without having other sight, <br> And hence it has the nature of evidence.”<sup>1854</sup> <br> Then heard I: “If whatever is acquired <br> Below by doctrine were thus understood, <br> No sophist’s subtlety would there find place.” <br> Thus was breathed forth from that enkindled love; <br> Then added: “Very well has been gone over <br> Already of this coin the alloy and weight; <br> But tell me if thou hast it in thy purse?” <br> And I: “Yes, both so shining and so round <br> That in its stamp there is no peradventure.”<sup>1855</sup> <br> Thereafter issued from the light profound <br> That there resplendent was: “This precious jewel, <br> Upon the which is every virtue founded, <br> Whence hadst thou it?” And I: “The large outpouring <br> Of Holy Spirit, which has been diffused <br> Upon the ancient parchments and the new,<sup>1856</sup> <br> A syllogism is, which proved it to me <br> With such acuteness, that, compared therewith, <br> All demonstration seems to me obtuse.” <br> And then I heard: “The ancient and the new <br> Postulates, that to thee are so conclusive, <br> Why dost thou take them for the word divine?” <br> And I: “The proofs, which show the truth to me, <br> Are the works subsequent, whereunto Nature <br> Ne’er heated iron yet, nor anvil beat.” <br> ’Twas answered me: “Say, who assureth thee <br> That those works ever were? the thing itself <br> That must be proved, nought else to thee affirms it.” <br> “Were the world to Christianity converted,” <br> I said, “withouten miracles, this one <br> Is such, the rest are not its hundredth part; <br> Because that poor and fasting thou didst enter <br> Into the field to sow there the good plant, <br> Which was a vine and has become a thorn!” <br> This being finished, the high, holy Court <br> Resounded through the spheres, “One God we praise!” <br> In melody that there above is chanted. <br> And then that Baron, who from branch to branch,<sup>1857</sup> <br> Examining, had thus conducted me, <br> Till the extremest leaves we were approaching, <br> Again began: “The Grace that dallying<sup>1858</sup> <br> Plays with thine intellect thy mouth has opened, <br> Up to this point, as it should opened be, <br> So that I do approve what forth emerged; <br> But now thou must express what thou believest, <br> And whence to thy belief it was presented.” <br> “O holy father, spirit who beholdest <br> What thou believedst so that thou o’ercamest, <br> Towards the sepulchre, more youthful feet,”<sup>1859</sup> <br> Began I, “thou dost wish me in this place <br> The form to manifest of my prompt belief, <br> And likewise thou the cause thereof demandest. <br> And I respond: In one God I believe, <br> Sole and eterne, who moveth all the heavens <br> With love and with desire, himself unmoved;<sup>1860</sup> <br> And of such faith not only have I proofs <br> Physical and metaphysical, but gives them <br> Likewise the truth that from this place rains down <br> Through Moses, through the Prophets and the Psalms, <br> Through the Evangel, and through you, who wrote<sup>1861</sup> <br> After the fiery Spirit sanctified you; <br> In Persons three eterne believe, and these <br> One essence I believe, so one and trine <br> They bear conjunction both with <i>sunt</i> and <i>est</i>.<sup>1862</sup> <br> With the profound condition and divine <br> Which now I touch upon, doth stamp my mind <br> Ofttimes the doctrine evangelical. <br> This the beginning is, this is the spark <br> Which afterwards dilates to vivid flame, <br> And, like a star in heaven, is sparkling in me.” <br> Even as a lord who hears what pleaseth him <br> His servant straight embraces, gratulating <br> For the good news as soon as he is silent; <br> So, giving me its benediction, singing, <br> Three times encircled me, when I was silent,<sup>1863</sup> <br> The apostolic light, at whose command <br> I spoken had, in speaking I so pleased him.</p>
