<p class="lede">Compensations. Ascent to the Second Heaven, or that of Mercury, where are seen the spirits of those who for the love of fame achieved great deeds.</p>
<p class="verse">“If in the heat of love I flame upon thee<sup>1263</sup> <br> Beyond the measure that on earth is seen, <br> So that the valor of thine eyes I vanquish, <br> Marvel thou not thereat; for this proceeds <br> From perfect sight, which as it apprehends <br> To the good apprehended moves its feet. <br> Well I perceive how is already shining <br> Into thine intellect the eternal light, <br> That only seen enkindles always love; <br> And if some other thing your love seduce,<sup>1264</sup> <br> ’Tis nothing but a vestige of the same, <br> Ill understood, which there is shining through. <br> Thou fain wouldst know if with another service <br> For broken vow can such return be made <br> As to secure the soul from further claim.” <br> This Canto thus did Beatrice begin; <br> And, as a man who breaks not off his speech, <br> Continued thus her holy argument: <br> “The greatest gift that in his largess God <br> Creating made, and unto his own goodness <br> Nearest conformed, and that which he doth prize <br> Most highly, is the freedom of the will, <br> Wherewith the creatures of intelligence <br> Both all and only were and are endowed.<sup>1265</sup> <br> Now wilt thou see, if thence thou reasonest, <br> The high worth of a vow, if it he made <br> So that when thou consentest God consents: <br> For, closing between God and man the compact, <br> A sacrifice is of this treasure made, <br> Such as I say, and made by its own act. <br> What can be rendered then as compensation? <br> Think’st thou to make good use of what thou’st offered, <br> With gains ill gotten thou wouldst do good deed.<sup>1266</sup> <br> Now art thou certain of the greater point; <br> But because Holy Church in this dispenses, <br> Which seems against the truth which I have shown thee, <br> Behoves thee still to sit awhile at table, <br> Because the solid food which thou hast taken <br> Requireth further aid for thy digestion. <br> Open thy mind to that which I reveal, <br> And fix it there within; for ’tis not knowledge, <br> The having heard without retaining it. <br> In the essence of this sacrifice two things <br> Convene together; and the one is that <br> Of which ’tis made, the other is the agreement. <br> This last for evermore is cancelled not <br> Unless complied with, and concerning this <br> With such precision has above been spoken. <br> Therefore it was enjoined upon the Hebrews <br> To offer still, though sometimes what was offered <br> Might be commuted, as thou ought’st to know. <br> The other, which is known to thee as matter,<sup>1267</sup> <br> May well indeed be such that one errs not <br> If it for other matter be exchanged. <br> But let none shift the burden on his shoulder <br> At his arbitrament, without the turning <br> Both of the white and of the yellow key;<sup>1268</sup> <br> And every permutation deem as foolish, <br> If in the substitute the thing relinquished, <br> As the four is in six, be not contained.<sup>1269</sup> <br> Therefore whatever thing has so great weight <br> In value that it drags down every balance, <br> Cannot be satisfied with other spending. <br> Let mortals never take a vow in jest; <br> Be faithful and not blind in doing that, <br> As Jephthah was in his first offering,<sup>1270</sup> <br> Whom more beseemed to say, ‘I have done wrong, <br> Than to do worse by keeping; and as foolish <br> Thou the great leader of the Greeks wilt find,<sup>1271</sup> <br> Whence wept Iphigenia her fair face,<sup>1272</sup> <br> And made for her both wise and simple weep, <br> Who heard such kind of worship spoken of.’ <br> Christians, be ye more serious in your movements; <br> Be ye not like a feather at each wind, <br> And think not every water washes you. <br> Ye have the Old and the New Testament, <br> And the Pastor of the Church who guideth you <br> Let this suffice you unto your salvation. <br> If evil appetite cry aught else to you, <br> Be ye as men, and not as silly sheep,<sup>1273</sup> <br> So that the Jew among you may not mock you. <br> Be ye not as the lamb that doth abandon<sup>1274</sup> <br> Its mother’s milk, and frolicsome and simple <br> Combats at its own pleasure with itself.” <br> Thus Beatrice to me even as I write it; <br> Then all desireful turned herself again <br> To that part where the world is most alive.<sup>1275</sup> <br> Her silence and her change of countenance <br> Silence imposed upon my eager mind, <br> That had already in advance new questions; <br> And as an arrow that upon the mark <br> Strikes ere the bowstring quiet hath become, <br> So did we speed into the second realm. <br> My Lady there so joyful I beheld, <br> As into the brightness of that heaven she entered,<sup>1276</sup> <br> More luminous thereat the planet grew; <br> And if the star itself was changed and smiled,<sup>1277</sup> <br> What became I, who by my nature am <br> Exceeding mutable in every guise! <br> As, in a fishpond which is pure and tranquil, <br> The fishes draw to that which from without <br> Comes in such fashion that their food they deem it; <br> So I beheld more than a thousand splendors <br> Drawing towards us, and in each was heard: <br> “Lo, this is she who shall increase our love.” <br> And as each one was coming unto us, <br> Full of beatitude the shade was seen, <br> By the effulgence clear that issued from it.<sup>1278</sup> <br> Think, Reader, if what here is just beginning <br> No farther should proceed, how thou wouldst have <br> An agonizing need of knowing more; <br> And of thyself thou’lt see how I from these <br> Was in desire of hearing their conditions, <br> As they unto mine eyes were manifest. <br> “O thou wellborn, unto whom Grace concedes <br> To see the thrones of the eternal triumph, <br> Or ever yet the warfare be abandoned <br> With light that through the whole of heaven is spread <br> Kindled are we, and hence if thou desirest <br> To know of us, at thine own pleasure sate thee.” <br> Thus by someone among those holy spirits<sup>1279</sup> <br> Was spoken, and by Beatrice: “Speak, speak <br> Securely, and believe them even as Gods.” <br> “Well I perceive how thou dost nest thyself <br> In thine own light, and drawest it from thine eyes, <br> Because they coruscate when thou dost smile, <br> But know not who thou art, nor why thou hast, <br> Spirit august, thy station in the sphere <br> That veils itself to men in alien rays.”<sup>1280</sup> <br> This said I in direction of the light <br> Which first had spoken to me; whence it became <br> By far more lucent than it was before. <br> Even as the sun, that doth conceal himself<sup>1281</sup> <br> By too much light, when heat has worn away <br> The tempering influence of the vapors dense, <br> By greater rapture thus concealed itself <br> In its own radiance the figure saintly, <br> And thus close, close enfolded answered me <br> In fashion as the following Canto sings.</p>
