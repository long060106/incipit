<p class="lede">Buonagiunta da Lucca⁠—Pope Martin IV, and others.</p>
<p class="verse">Nor speech the going, nor the going that<sup>998</sup> <br> Slackened; but talking we went bravely on, <br> Even as a vessel urged by a good wind. <br> And shadows, that appeared things doubly dead, <br> From out the sepulchres of their eyes betrayed <br> Wonder at me, aware that I was living. <br> And I, continuing my colloquy,<sup>999</sup> <br> Said: “Peradventure he goes up more slowly <br> Than he would do, for other people’s sake. <br> But tell me, if thou knowest, where is Piccarda;<sup>1000</sup> <br> Tell me if anyone of note I see <br> Among this folk that gazes at me so.” <br> “My sister, who, ’twixt beautiful and good, <br> I know not which was more, triumphs rejoicing <br> Already in her crown on high Olympus.” <br> So said he first, and then: “ ’Tis not forbidden <br> To name each other here, so milked away <br> Is our resemblance by our dieting. <br> This,” pointing with his finger, “is Buonagiunta,<sup>1001</sup> <br> Buonagiunta, of Lucca; and that face <br> Beyond him there, more peaked than the others, <br> Has held the holy Church within his arms;<sup>1002</sup> <br> From Tours was he, and purges by his fasting <br> Bolsena’s eels and the Vernaccia wine.”<sup>1003</sup> <br> He named me many others one by one; <br> And all contented seemed at being named, <br> So that for this I saw not one dark look. <br> I saw for hunger bite the empty air<sup>1004</sup> <br> Ubaldin dalla Pila, and Boniface,<sup>1005</sup> <br> Who with his crook had pastured many people. <br> I saw Messer Marchese, who had leisure<sup>1006</sup> <br> Once at Forli for drinking with less dryness, <br> And he was one who ne’er felt satisfied. <br> But as he does who scans, and then doth prize <br> One more than others, did I him of Lucca, <br> Who seemed to take most cognizance of me. <br> He murmured, and I know not what Gentucca<sup>1007</sup> <br> From that place heard I, where he felt the wound<sup>1008</sup> <br> Of justice, that doth macerate them so. <br> “O soul,” I said, “that seemest so desirous <br> To speak with me, do so that I may hear thee, <br> And with thy speech appease thyself and me.” <br> “A maid is born, and wears not yet the veil,” <br> Began he, “who to thee shall pleasant make <br> My city, howsoever men may blame it. <br> Thou shalt go on thy way with this prevision; <br> If by my murmuring thou hast been deceived, <br> True things hereafter will declare it to thee. <br> But say if him I here behold, who forth <br> Evoked the new-invented rhymes, beginning,<sup>1009</sup> <br> <em>Ladies, that have intelligence of love?</em>”<sup>1010</sup> <br> And I to him: “One am I, who, whenever <br> Love doth inspire me, note, and in that measure <br> Which he within me dictates, singing go.” <br> “O brother, now I see,” he said, “the knot <br> Which me, the Notary, and Guittone held<sup>1011</sup> <br> Short of the sweet new style that now I hear. <br> I do perceive full clearly how your pens <br> Go closely following after him who dictates, <br> Which with our own forsooth came not to pass; <br> And he who sets himself to go beyond, <br> No difference sees from one style to another”; <br> And as if satisfied, he held his peace. <br> Even as the birds, that winter tow’rds the Nile, <br> Sometimes into a phalanx form themselves, <br> Then fly in greater haste, and go in file; <br> In such wise all the people who were there, <br> Turning their faces, hurried on their steps, <br> Both by their leanness and their wishes light. <br> And as a man, who weary is with trotting, <br> Lets his companions onward go, and walks, <br> Until he vents the panting of his chest; <br> So did Forese let the holy flock <br> Pass by, and came with me behind it, saying, <br> “When will it be that I again shall see thee?” <br> “How long,” I answered, “I may live, I know not; <br> Yet my return will not so speedy be, <br> But I shall sooner in desire arrive; <br> Because the place where I was set to live <br> From day to day of good is more depleted, <br> And unto dismal ruin seems ordained.” <br> “Now go,” he said, “for him most guilty of it<sup>1012</sup> <br> At a beast’s tail behold I dragged along <br> Towards the valley where is no repentance. <br> Faster at every step the beast is going, <br> Increasing evermore until it smites him, <br> And leaves the body vilely mutilated. <br> Not long those wheels shall turn,” and he uplifted <br> His eyes to heaven, “ere shall be clear to thee <br> That which my speech no farther can declare. <br> Now stay behind; because the time so precious <br> Is in this kingdom, that I lose too much <br> By coming onward thus abreast with thee.” <br> As sometimes issues forth upon a gallop <br> A cavalier from out a troop that ride, <br> And seeks the honor of the first encounter, <br> So he with greater strides departed from us; <br> And on the road remained I with those two, <br> Who were such mighty marshals of the world.<sup>1013</sup> <br> And when before us he had gone so far <br> Mine eyes became to him such pursuivants <br> As was my understanding to his words, <br> Appeared to me with laden and living boughs <br> Another apple-tree, and not far distant, <br> From having but just then turned thitherward.<sup>1014</sup> <br> People I saw beneath it lift their hands, <br> And cry I know not what towards the leaves, <br> Like little children eager and deluded, <br> Who pray, and he they pray to doth not answer, <br> But, to make very keen their appetite, <br> Holds their desire aloft, and hides it not. <br> Then they departed as if undeceived; <br> And now we came unto the mighty tree <br> Which prayers and tears so manifold refuses. <br> “Pass farther onward without drawing near; <br> The tree of which Eve ate is higher up,<sup>1015</sup> <br> And out of that one has this tree been raised.” <br> Thus said I know not who among the branches; <br> Whereat Virgilius, Statius, and myself <br> Went crowding forward on the side that rises. <br> “Be mindful,” said he, “of the accursed ones<sup>1016</sup> <br> Formed of the cloud-rack, who inebriate <br> Combated Theseus with their double breasts; <br> And of the Jews who showed them soft in drinking, <br> Whence Gideon would not have them for companions<sup>1017</sup> <br> When he tow’rds Midian the hills descended.” <br> Thus, closely pressed to one of the two borders, <br> On passed we, hearing sins of gluttony, <br> Followed forsooth by miserable gains; <br> Then set at large upon the lonely road, <br> A thousand steps and more we onward went, <br> In contemplation, each without a word. <br> “What go ye thinking thus, ye three alone?” <br> Said suddenly a voice, whereat I started <br> As terrified and timid beasts are wont. <br> I raised my head to see who this might be, <br> And never in a furnace was there seen <br> Metals or glass so lucent and so red <br> As one I saw who said: “If it may please you<sup>1018</sup> <br> To mount aloft, here it behoves you turn; <br> This way goes he who goeth after peace.” <br> His aspect had bereft me of my sight, <br> So that I turned me back unto my Teachers, <br> Like one who goeth as his hearing guides him. <br> And as, the harbinger of early dawn, <br> The air of May doth move and breathe out fragrance, <br> Impregnate all with herbage and with flowers, <br> So did I feel a breeze strike in the midst <br> My front, and felt the moving of the plumes <br> That breathed around an odor of ambrosia; <br> And heard it said: “Blessed are they whom grace <br> So much illumines, that the love of taste <br> Excites not in their breasts too great desire, <br> Hungering at all times so far as is just.”</p>
