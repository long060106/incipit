<p class="lede">God and the Celestial Hierarchies.</p>
<p class="verse">After the truth against the present life<sup>1965</sup> <br> Of miserable mortals was unfolded <br> By her who doth imparadise my mind,<sup>1966</sup> <br> As in a looking-glass a taper’s flame <br> He sees who from behind is lighted by it, <br> Before he has it in his sight or thought, <br> And turns him round to see if so the glass <br> Tell him the truth, and sees that it accords <br> Therewith as doth a music with its metre, <br> In similar wise my memory recollecteth <br> That I did, looking into those fair eyes, <br> Of which Love made the springes to ensnare me. <br> And as I turned me round, and mine were touched <br> By that which is apparent in that volume,<sup>1967</sup> <br> Whenever on its gyre we gaze intent, <br> A point beheld I, that was raying out<sup>1968</sup> <br> Light so acute, the sight which it enkindles <br> Must close perforce before such great acuteness. <br> And whatsoever star seems smallest here <br> Would seem to be a moon, if placed beside it. <br> As one star with another star is placed. <br> Perhaps at such a distance as appears <br> A halo cincturing the light that paints it, <br> When densest is the vapor that sustains it, <br> Thus distant round the point a circle of fire <br> So swiftly whirled, that it would have surpassed <br> Whatever motion soonest girds the world; <br> And this was by another circumcinct, <br> That by a third, the third then by a fourth, <br> By a fifth the fourth, and then by a sixth the fifth; <br> The seventh followed thereupon in width <br> So ample now, that Juno’s messenger<sup>1969</sup> <br> Entire would be too narrow to contain it. <br> Even so the eighth and ninth; and everyone<sup>1970</sup> <br> More slowly moved, according as it was <br> In number distant farther from the first. <br> And that one had its flame most crystalline <br> From which less distant was the stainless spark, <br> I think because more with its truth imbued. <br> My Lady, who in my anxiety <br> Beheld me much perplexed, said: “From that point <br> Dependent is the heaven and nature all. <br> Behold that circle most conjoined to it, <br> And know thou, that its motion is so swift <br> Through burning love whereby it is spurred on.” <br> And I to her: “If the world were arranged <br> In the order which I see in yonder wheels, <br> What’s set before me would have satisfied me; <br> But in the world of sense we can perceive <br> That evermore the circles are diviner <br> As they are from the centre more remote <br> Wherefore if my desire is to be ended <br> In this miraculous and angelic temple, <br> That has for confines only love and light, <br> To hear behoves me still how the example<sup>1971</sup> <br> And the exemplar go not in one fashion, <br> Since for myself in vain I contemplate it.” <br> “If thine own fingers unto such a knot <br> Be insufficient, it is no great wonder, <br> So hard hath it become for want of trying.”<sup>1972</sup> <br> My Lady thus; then said she: “Do thou take <br> What I shall tell thee, if thou wouldst be sated, <br> And exercise on that thy subtlety. <br> The circles corporal are wide and narrow<sup>1973</sup> <br> According to the more or less of virtue <br> Which is distributed through all their parts. <br> The greater goodness works the greater weal, <br> The greater weal the greater body holds, <br> If perfect equally are all its parts. <br> Therefore this one which sweeps along with it<sup>1974</sup> <br> The universe sublime, doth correspond <br> Unto the circle which most loves and knows. <br> On which account, if thou unto the virtue <br> Apply thy measure, not to the appearance <br> Of substances that unto thee seem round, <br> Thou wilt behold a marvellous agreement, <br> Of more to greater, and of less to smaller,<sup>1975</sup> <br> In every heaven, with its Intelligence.” <br> Even as remaineth splendid and serene <br> The hemisphere of air, when Boreas<sup>1976</sup> <br> Is blowing from that cheek where he is mildest, <br> Because is purified and resolved the rack <br> That erst disturbed it, till the welkin laughs <br> With all the beauties of its pageantry; <br> Thus did I likewise, after that my Lady <br> Had me provided with her clear response, <br> And like a star in heaven the truth was seen. <br> And soon as to a stop her words had come, <br> Not otherwise does iron scintillate <br> When molten, than those circles scintillated.<sup>1977</sup> <br> Their coruscation all the sparks repeated, <br> And they so many were, their number makes <br> More millions than the doubling of the chess.<sup>1978</sup> <br> I heard them sing hosanna choir by choir <br> To the fixed point which holds them at the <i>Ubi</i>,<sup>1979</sup> <br> And ever will, where they have ever been. <br> And she, who saw the dubious meditations <br> Within my mind, “The primal circles,” said, <br> “Have shown thee Seraphim and Cherubim.<sup>1980</sup> <br> Thus rapidly they follow their own bonds,<sup>1981</sup> <br> To be as like the point as most they can, <br> And can as far as they are high in vision. <br> Those other Loves, that round about them go, <br> Thrones of the countenance divine are called,<sup>1982</sup> <br> Because they terminate the primal Triad. <br> And thou shouldst know that they all have delight <br> As much as their own vision penetrates <br> The Truth, in which all intellect finds rest. <br> From this it may be seen how blessedness <br> Is founded in the faculty which sees,<sup>1983</sup> <br> And not in that which loves, and follows next; <br> And of this seeing merit is the measure, <br> Which is brought forth by grace, and by good will;<sup>1984</sup> <br> Thus on from grade to grade doth it proceed. <br> The second Triad, which is germinating <br> In such wise in this sempiternal spring,<sup>1985</sup> <br> That no nocturnal Aries despoils, <br> Perpetually hosanna warbles forth <br> With threefold melody, that sounds in three <br> Orders of joy, with which it is intrined. <br> The three Divine are in this hierarchy, <br> First the Dominions, and the Virtues next;<sup>1986</sup> <br> And the third order is that of the Powers. <br> Then in the dances twain penultimate <br> The Principalities and Archangels wheel; <br> The last is wholly of angelic sports. <br> These orders upward all of them are gazing, <br> And downward so prevail, that unto God <br> They all attracted are and all attract. <br> And Dionysius with so great desire<sup>1987</sup> <br> To contemplate these Orders set himself, <br> He named them and distinguished them as I do. <br> But Gregory afterwards dissented from him;<sup>1988</sup> <br> Wherefore, as soon as he unclosed his eyes <br> Within this heaven, he at himself did smile. <br> And if so much of secret truth a mortal <br> Proffered on earth, I would not have thee marvel, <br> For he who saw it here revealed it to him,<sup>1989</sup> <br> With much more of the truth about these circles.”</p>
