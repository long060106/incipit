<p class="lede">The foot of the mountain⁠—Those who have died in contumacy of Holy Church⁠—Manfredi.</p>
<p class="verse">Inasmuch as the instantaneous flight <br> Had scattered them asunder o’er the plain, <br> Turned to the mountain whither reason spurs us, <br> I pressed me close unto my faithful comrade, <br> And how without him had I kept my course? <br> Who would have led me up along the mountain? <br> He seemed to me within himself remorseful; <br> O noble conscience, and without a stain, <br> How sharp a sting is trivial fault to thee! <br> After his feet had laid aside the haste <br> Which mars the dignity of every act, <br> My mind, that hitherto had been restrained, <br> Let loose its faculties as if delighted, <br> And I my sight directed to the hill <br> That highest tow’rds the heaven uplifts itself.<sup>560</sup> <br> The sun, that in our rear was flaming red, <br> Was broken in front of me into the figure <br> Which had in me the stoppage of its rays; <br> Unto one side I turned me, with the fear <br> Of being left alone, when I beheld <br> Only in front of me the ground obscured. <br> “Why dost thou still mistrust?” my Comforter <br> Began to say to me turned wholly round; <br> “Dost thou not think me with thee, and that I guide thee? <br> ’Tis evening there already where is buried <br> The body within which I cast a shadow; <br> ’Tis from Brundusium ta’en, and Naples has it.<sup>561</sup> <br> Now if in front of me no shadow fall, <br> Marvel not at it more than at the heavens, <br> Because one ray impedeth not another <br> To suffer torments, both of cold and heat, <br> Bodies like this that Power provides, which wills <br> That how it works be not unveiled to us. <br> Insane is he who hopeth that our reason <br> Can traverse the illimitable way, <br> Which the one Substance in three Persons follows! <br> Mortals, remain contented at the <i>Quia</i>;<sup>562</sup> <br> For if ye had been able to see all, <br> No need there were for Mary to give birth; <br> And ye have seen desiring without fruit, <br> Those whose desire would have been quieted, <br> Which evermore is given them for a grief. <br> I speak of Aristotle and of Plato, <br> And many others”;⁠—and here bowed his head, <br> And more he said not, and remained disturbed. <br> We came meanwhile unto the mountain’s foot; <br> There so precipitate we found the rock, <br> That nimble legs would there have been in vain. <br> ’Twixt Lerici and Turbìa, the most desert,<sup>563</sup> <br> The most secluded pathway is a stair <br> Easy and open, if compared with that. <br> “Who knoweth now upon which hand the hill <br> Slopes down,” my Master said, his footsteps staying, <br> “So that who goeth without wings may mount?” <br> And while he held his eyes upon the ground <br> Examining the nature of the path, <br> And I was looking up around the rock, <br> On the left hand appeared to me a throng <br> Of souls, that moved their feet in our direction, <br> And did not seem to move, they came so slowly. <br> “Lift up thine eyes,” I to the Master said; <br> “Behold, on this side, who will give us counsel, <br> If thou of thine own self can have it not.” <br> Then he looked at me, and with frank expression <br> Replied: “Let us go there, for they come slowly, <br> And thou be steadfast in thy hope, sweet son.” <br> Still was that people as far off from us,<sup>564</sup> <br> After a thousand steps of ours I say, <br> As a good thrower with his hand would reach, <br> When they all crowded unto the hard masses <br> Of the high bank, and motionless stood and close, <br> As he stands still to look who goes in doubt. <br> “O happy dead! O spirits elect already!” <br> Virgilius made beginning, “by that peace <br> Which I believe is waiting for you all, <br> Tell us upon what side the mountain slopes, <br> So that the going up be possible, <br> For to lose time irks him most who most knows.” <br> As sheep come issuing forth from out the fold <br> By ones and twos and threes, and the others stand <br> Timidly, holding down their eyes and nostrils, <br> And what the foremost does the others do,<sup>565</sup> <br> Huddling themselves against her, if she stop, <br> Simple and quiet and the wherefore know not; <br> So moving to approach us thereupon <br> I saw the leader of that fortunate flock, <br> Modest in face and dignified in gait. <br> As soon as those in the advance saw broken <br> The light upon the ground at my right side, <br> So that from me the shadow reached the rock, <br> They stopped, and backward drew themselves somewhat; <br> And all the others, who came after them, <br> Not knowing why nor wherefore, did the same. <br> “Without your asking, I confess to you <br> This is a human body which you see, <br> Whereby the sunshine on the ground is cleft. <br> Marvel ye not thereat, but be persuaded <br> That not without a power which comes from Heaven <br> Doth he endeavor to surmount this wall.” <br> The Master thus; and said those worthy people: <br> “Return ye then, and enter in before us,” <br> Making a signal with the back o’ the hand <br> And one of them began: “Whoe’er thou art, <br> Thus going turn thine eyes, consider well <br> If e’er thou saw me in the other world.” <br> I turned me tow’rds him, and looked at him closely; <br> Blond was he, beautiful, and of noble aspect, <br> But one of his eyebrows had a blow divided. <br> When with humility I had disclaimed <br> E’er having seen him, “Now behold!” he said, <br> And showed me high upon his breast a wound. <br> Then said he with a smile: “I am Manfredi,<sup>566</sup> <br> The grandson of the Empress Costanza;<sup>567</sup> <br> Therefore, when thou returnest, I beseech thee <br> Go to my daughter beautiful, the mother<sup>568</sup> <br> Of Sicily’s honor and of Aragon’s, <br> And the truth tell her, if aught else be told. <br> After I had my body lacerated <br> By these two mortal stabs, I gave myself <br> Weeping to Him, who willingly doth pardon. <br> Horrible my iniquities had been; <br> But Infinite Goodness hath such ample arms, <br> That it receives whatever turns to it. <br> Had but Cosenza’s pastor, who in chase<sup>569</sup> <br> Of me was sent by Clement at that time, <br> In God read understandingly this page, <br> The bones of my dead body still would be <br> At the bridge-head, near unto Benevento, <br> Under the safeguard of the heavy cairn. <br> Now the rain bathes and moveth them the wind, <br> Beyond the realm, almost beside the Verde,<sup>570</sup> <br> Where he transported them with tapers quenched.<sup>571</sup> <br> By malison of theirs is not so lost <br> Eternal Love, that it cannot return, <br> So long as hope has anything of green. <br> True is it, who in contumacy dies <br> Of Holy Church, though penitent at last, <br> Must wait upon the outside this bank <br> Thirty times told the time that he has been <br> In his presumption, unless such decree <br> Shorter by means of righteous prayers become. <br> See now if thou hast power to make me happy, <br> By making known unto my good Costanza <br> How thou hast seen me, and this ban beside, <br> For those on earth can much advance us here.”</p>
