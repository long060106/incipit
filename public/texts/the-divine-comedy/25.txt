<p class="lede">The Seventh Bolgia: thieves⁠—Vanni Fucci.</p>
<p class="verse">In that part of the youthful year wherein<sup>343</sup> <br> The Sun his locks beneath Aquarius tempers,<sup>344</sup> <br> And now the nights draw near to half the day, <br> What time the hoar-frost copies on the ground <br> The outward semblance of her sister white, <br> But little lasts the temper of her pen, <br> The husbandman, whose forage faileth him, <br> Rises, and looks, and seeth the champaign <br> All gleaming white, whereat he beats his flank, <br> Returns in doors, and up and down laments, <br> Like a poor wretch, who knows not what to do; <br> Then he returns, and hope revives again, <br> Seeing the world has changed its countenance <br> In little time, and takes his shepherd’s crook, <br> And forth the little lambs to pasture drives. <br> Thus did the Master fill me with alarm, <br> When I beheld his forehead so disturbed, <br> And to the ailment came as soon the plaster. <br> For as we came unto the ruined bridge, <br> The Leader turned to me with that sweet look <br> Which at the mountain’s foot I first beheld.<sup>345</sup> <br> His arms he opened, after some advisement <br> Within himself elected, looking first <br> Well at the ruin, and laid hold of me. <br> And even as he who acts and meditates, <br> For aye it seems that he provides beforehand, <br> So upward lifting me towards the summit <br> Of a huge rock, he scanned another crag, <br> Saying: “To that one grapple afterwards, <br> But try first if ’tis such that it will hold thee.” <br> This was no way for one clothed with a cloak; <br> For hardly we, he light, and I pushed upward, <br> Were able to ascend from jag to jag. <br> And had it not been, that upon that precinct <br> Shorter was the ascent than on the other, <br> He I know not, but I had been dead beat. <br> But because Malebolge tow’rds the mouth <br> Of the profoundest well is all inclining, <br> The structure of each valley doth import <br> That one bank rises and the other sinks. <br> Still we arrived at length upon the point <br> Wherefrom the last stone breaks itself asunder. <br> The breath was from my lungs so milked away,<sup>346</sup> <br> When I was up, that I could go no farther, <br> Nay, I sat down upon my first arrival. <br> “Now it behoves thee thus to put off sloth,” <br> My Master said; “for sitting upon down, <br> Or under quilt, one cometh not to fame, <br> Withouten which whoso his life consumes <br> Such vestige leaveth of himself on earth, <br> As smoke in air or in the water foam. <br> And therefore raise thee up, o’ercome the anguish <br> With spirit that o’ercometh every battle, <br> If with its heavy body it sink not. <br> A longer stairway it behoves thee mount;<sup>347</sup> <br> ’Tis not enough from these to have departed; <br> Let it avail thee, if thou understand me.” <br> Then I uprose, showing myself provided <br> Better with breath than I did feel myself, <br> And said: “Go on, for I am strong and bold.” <br> Upward we took our way along the crag, <br> Which jagged was, and narrow, and difficult, <br> And more precipitous far than that before. <br> Speaking I went, not to appear exhausted; <br> Whereat a voice from the next moat came forth, <br> Not well adapted to articulate words. <br> I know not what it said, though o’er the back <br> I now was of the arch that passes there; <br> But he seemed moved to anger who was speaking. <br> I was bent downward, but my living eyes <br> Could not attain the bottom, for the dark; <br> Wherefore I: “Master, see that thou arrive <br> At the next round, and let us descend the wall;<sup>348</sup> <br> For as from hence I hear and understand not, <br> So I look down and nothing I distinguish.” <br> “Other response,” he said, “I make thee not, <br> Except the doing; for the modest asking <br> Ought to be followed by the deed in silence.” <br> We from the bridge descended at its head, <br> Where it connects itself with the eighth bank, <br> And then was manifest to me the Bolgia; <br> And I beheld therein a terrible throng <br> Of serpents, and of such a monstrous kind, <br> That the remembrance still congeals my blood. <br> Let Lybia boast no longer with her sand; <br> For if Chelydri, Jaculi, and Phareae<sup>349</sup> <br> She breeds, with Cenchri and with Amphisbaena, <br> Neither so many plagues nor so malignant <br> E’er showed she with all Ethiopia, <br> Nor with whatever on the Red Sea is! <br> Among this cruel and most dismal throng <br> People were running naked and affrighted, <br> Without the hope of hole or heliotrope.<sup>350</sup> <br> They had their hands with serpents bound behind them; <br> These riveted upon their reins the tail <br> And head, and were in front of them entwined. <br> And lo! at one who was upon our side <br> There darted forth a serpent, which transfixed him <br> There where the neck is knotted to the shoulders. <br> Nor <em>O</em> so quickly e’er, nor <em>I</em> was written, <br> As he took fire, and burned; and ashes wholly <br> Behoved it that in falling he became. <br> And when he on the ground was thus destroyed, <br> The ashes drew together, and of themselves <br> Into himself they instantly returned. <br> Even thus by the great sages ’tis confessed <br> The phoenix dies, and then is born again,<sup>351</sup> <br> When it approaches its five-hundredth year; <br> On herb or grain it feeds not in its life, <br> But only on tears of incense and amomum, <br> And nard and myrrh are its last winding-sheet. <br> And as he is who falls, and knows not how, <br> By force of demons who to earth down drag him, <br> Or other oppilation that binds man,<sup>352</sup> <br> When he arises and around him looks, <br> Wholly bewildered by the mighty anguish <br> Which he has suffered, and in looking sighs; <br> Such was that sinner after he had risen. <br> Justice of God! O how severe it is, <br> That blows like these in vengeance poureth down! <br> The Guide thereafter asked him who he was; <br> Whence he replied: “I rained from Tuscany <br> A short time since into this cruel gorge. <br> A bestial life, and not a human, pleased me, <br> Even as the mule I was; I’m Vanni Fucci,<sup>353</sup> <br> Beast, and Pistoia was my worthy den.” <br> And I unto the Guide: “Tell him to stir not, <br> And ask what crime has thrust him here below, <br> For once a man of blood and wrath I saw him.” <br> And the sinner, who had heard, dissembled not, <br> But unto me directed mind and face, <br> And with a melancholy shame was painted. <br> Then said: “It pains me more that thou hast caught me <br> Amid this misery where thou seest me, <br> Than when I from the other life was taken. <br> What thou demandest I cannot deny; <br> So low am I put down because I robbed <br> The sacristy of the fair ornaments, <br> And falsely once ’twas laid upon another; <br> But that thou mayst not such a sight enjoy, <br> If thou shalt e’er be out of the dark places, <br> Thine ears to my announcement ope and hear: <br> Pistoia first of Neri groweth meagre;<sup>354</sup> <br> Then Florence doth renew her men and manners; <br> Mars draws a vapor up from Val di Magra,<sup>355</sup> <br> Which is with turbid clouds enveloped round, <br> And with impetuous and bitter tempest <br> Over Campo Picen shall be the battle; <br> When it shall suddenly rend the mist asunder, <br> So that each Bianco shall thereby be smitten. <br> And this I’ve said that it may give thee pain.”</p>
