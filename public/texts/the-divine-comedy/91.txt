<p class="lede">The Seventh Heaven, or that of Saturn, where are seen the spirits of the contemplative⁠—The Celestial Stairway⁠—St. Peter Damiano⁠—His invectives against the luxury of the Prelates.</p>
<p class="verse">Already on my Lady’s face mine eyes<sup>1769</sup> <br> Again were fastened, and with these my mind, <br> And from all other purpose was withdrawn; <br> And she smiled not; but “If I were to smile,” <br> She unto me began, “thou wouldst become <br> Like Semele, when she was turned to ashes.<sup>1770</sup> <br> Because my beauty, that along the stairs <br> Of the eternal palace more enkindles, <br> As thou hast seen, the farther we ascend, <br> If it were tempered not, is so resplendent <br> That all thy mortal power in its effulgence <br> Would seem a leaflet that the thunder crushes. <br> We are uplifted to the seventh splendor,<sup>1771</sup> <br> That underneath the burning Lion’s breast <br> Now radiates downward mingled with his power. <br> Fix in direction of thine eyes the mind, <br> And make of them a mirror for the figure <br> That in this mirror shall appear to thee.” <br> He who could know what was the pasturage <br> My sight had in that blessed countenance, <br> When I transferred me to another care, <br> Would recognize how grateful was to me <br> Obedience unto my celestial escort, <br> By counterpoising one side with the other. <br> Within the crystal which, around the world <br> Revolving, bears the name of its dear leader, <br> Under whom every wickedness lay dead,<sup>1772</sup> <br> Coloured like gold, on which the sunshine gleams, <br> A stairway I beheld to such a height<sup>1773</sup> <br> Uplifted, that mine eye pursued it not. <br> Likewise beheld I down the steps descending <br> So many splendors, that I thought each light <br> That in the heaven appears was there diffused. <br> And as accordant with their natural custom <br> The rooks together at the break of day<sup>1774</sup> <br> Bestir themselves to warm their feathers cold; <br> Then some of them fly off without return, <br> Others come back to where they started from, <br> And others, wheeling round, still keep at home; <br> Such fashion it appeared to me was there <br> Within the sparkling that together came, <br> As soon as on a certain step it struck, <br> And that which nearest unto us remained<sup>1775</sup> <br> Became so clear, that in my thought I said, <br> “Well I perceive the love thou showest me; <br> But she, from whom I wait the how and when<sup>1776</sup> <br> Of speech and silence, standeth still; whence I <br> Against desire do well if I ask not.” <br> She thereupon, who saw my silentness <br> In the sight of Him who seeth everything, <br> Said unto me, “Let loose thy warm desire.” <br> And I began: “No merit of my own <br> Renders me worthy of response from thee; <br> But for her sake who granteth me the asking, <br> Thou blessed life that dost remain concealed <br> In thy beatitude, make known to me <br> The cause which draweth thee so near my side; <br> And tell me why is silent in this wheel <br> The dulcet symphony of Paradise, <br> That through the rest below sounds so devoutly.” <br> “Thou hast thy hearing mortal as thy sight,” <br> It answer made to me; “they sing not here, <br> For the same cause that Beatrice has not smiled.<sup>1777</sup> <br> Thus far adown the holy stairway’s steps <br> Have I descended but to give thee welcome <br> With words, and with the light that mantles me; <br> Nor did more love cause me to be more ready, <br> For love as much and more up there is burning, <br> As doth the flaming manifest to thee. <br> But the high charity, that makes us servants <br> Prompt to the counsel which controls the world, <br> Allotteth here, even as thou dost observe.” <br> “I see full well,” said I, “O sacred lamp! <br> How love unfettered in this court sufficeth <br> To follow the eternal Providence; <br> But this is what seems hard for me to see, <br> Wherefore predestinate wast thou alone <br> Unto this office from among thy consorts.” <br> No sooner had I come to the last word, <br> Than of its middle made the light a centre, <br> Whirling itself about like a swift millstone.<sup>1778</sup> <br> When answer made the love that was therein: <br> “On me directed is a light divine, <br> Piercing through this in which I am embosomed, <br> Of which the virtue with my sight conjoined <br> Lifts me above myself so far, I see <br> The supreme essence from which this is drawn. <br> Hence comes the joyfulness with which I flame, <br> For to my sight, as far as it is clear, <br> The clearness of the flame I equal make.<sup>1779</sup> <br> But that soul in the heaven which is most pure, <br> That seraph which his eye on God most fixes, <br> Could this demand of thine not satisfy; <br> Because so deeply sinks in the abyss <br> Of the eternal statute what thou askest, <br> From all created sight it is cut off. <br> And to the mortal world, when thou returnest, <br> This carry back, that it may not presume <br> Longer tow’rd such a goal to move its feet. <br> The mind, that shineth here, on earth doth smoke; <br> From this observe how can it do below <br> That which it cannot though the heaven assume it?” <br> Such limit did its words prescribe to me, <br> The question I relinquished, and restricted <br> Myself to ask it humbly who it was. <br> “Between two shores of Italy rise cliffs,<sup>1780</sup> <br> And not far distant from thy native place, <br> So high, the thunders far below them sound, <br> And form a ridge that Catria is called, <br> ’Neath which is consecrate a hermitage<sup>1781</sup> <br> Wont to be dedicate to worship only.” <br> Thus unto me the third speech recommenced,<sup>1782</sup> <br> And then, continuing, it said: “Therein <br> Unto God’s service I became so steadfast, <br> That feeding only on the juice of olives <br> Lightly I passed away the heats and frosts, <br> Contented in my thoughts contemplative. <br> That cloister used to render to these heavens <br> Abundantly, and now is empty grown, <br> So that perforce it soon must be revealed. <br> I in that place was Peter Damiano;<sup>1783</sup> <br> And Peter the Sinner was I in the house<sup>1784</sup> <br> Of Our Lady on the Adriatic shore. <br> Little of mortal life remained to me, <br> When I was called and dragged forth to the hat<sup>1785</sup> <br> Which shifteth evermore from bad to worse. <br> Came Cephas, and the mighty Vessel came<sup>1786</sup> <br> Of the Holy Spirit, meagre and barefooted, <br> Taking the food of any hostelry.<sup>1787</sup> <br> Now someone to support them on each side<sup>1788</sup> <br> The modern shepherds need, and some to lead them, <br> So heavy are they, and to hold their trains. <br> They cover up their palfreys with their cloaks, <br> So that two beasts go underneath one skin; <br> O Patience, that dost tolerate so much!” <br> At this voice saw I many little flames <br> From step to step descending and revolving, <br> And every revolution made them fairer. <br> Round about this one came they and stood still, <br> And a cry uttered of so loud a sound, <br> It here could find no parallel, nor I <br> Distinguished it, the thunder so o’ercame me.<sup>1789</sup></p>
