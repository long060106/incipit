<p class="lede">Beatrice.</p>
<p class="verse">When the Septentrion of the highest heaven<sup>1109</sup> <br> (Which never either setting knew or rising, <br> Nor veil of other cloud than that of sin, <br> And which made everyone therein aware <br> Of his own duty, as the lower makes <br> Whoever turns the helm to come to port) <br> Motionless halted, the veracious people, <br> That came at first between it and the Griffin, <br> Turned themselves to the car, as to their peace. <br> And one of them, as if by Heaven commissioned, <br> Singing, “<i>Veni, sponsa, de Libano</i>”<sup>1110</sup> <br> Shouted three times, and all the others after. <br> Even as the Blessed at the final summons <br> Shall rise up quickened each one from his cavern, <br> Uplifting light the reinvested flesh, <br> So upon that celestial chariot <br> A hundred rose <i>ad vocem tanti senis</i>,<sup>1111</sup> <br> Ministers and messengers of life eternal. <br> They all were saying, “<i>Benedictus qui venis</i>,”<sup>1112</sup> <br> And, scattering flowers above and round about, <br> “<i>Manibus o date lilia plenis.</i>”<sup>1113</sup> <br> Ere now have I beheld, as day began, <br> The eastern hemisphere all tinged with rose, <br> And the other heaven with fair serene adorned; <br> And the sun’s face, uprising, overshadowed<sup>1114</sup> <br> So that by tempering influence of vapors <br> For a long interval the eye sustained it; <br> Thus in the bosom of a cloud of flowers <br> Which from those hands angelical ascended, <br> And downward fell again inside and out, <br> Over her snow-white veil with olive cinct <br> Appeared a lady under a green mantle,<sup>1115</sup> <br> Vested in color of the living flame. <br> And my own spirit, that already now <br> So long a time had been, that in her presence<sup>1116</sup> <br> Trembling with awe it had not stood abashed,<sup>1117</sup> <br> Without more knowledge having by mine eyes, <br> Through occult virtue that from her proceeded <br> Of ancient love the mighty influence felt. <br> As soon as on my vision smote the power <br> Sublime, that had already pierced me through <br> Ere from my boyhood I had yet come forth, <br> To the left hand I turned with that reliance <br> With which the little child runs to his mother, <br> When he has fear, or when he is afflicted, <br> To say unto Virgilius: “Not a drachm <br> Of blood remains in me, that does not tremble; <br> I know the traces of the ancient flame.”<sup>1118</sup> <br> But us Virgilius of himself deprived <br> Had left, Virgilius, sweetest of all fathers, <br> Virgilius, to whom I for safety gave me: <br> Nor whatsoever lost the ancient mother<sup>1119</sup> <br> Availed my cheeks now purified from dew, <br> That weeping they should not again be darkened. <br> “Dante, because Virgilius has departed <br> Do not weep yet, do not weep yet awhile; <br> For by another sword thou need’st must weep.” <br> E’en as an admiral, who on poop and prow <br> Comes to behold the people that are working <br> In other ships, and cheers them to well-doing, <br> Upon the left hand border of the car, <br> When at the sound I turned of my own name, <br> Which of necessity is here recorded, <br> I saw the Lady, who erewhile appeared <br> Veiled underneath the angelic festival, <br> Direct her eyes to me across the river. <br> Although the veil, that from her head descended, <br> Encircled with the foliage of Minerva, <br> Did not permit her to appear distinctly, <br> In attitude still royally majestic <br> Continued she, like unto one who speaks, <br> And keeps his warmest utterance in reserve: <br> “Look at me well; in sooth I’m Beatrice! <br> How didst thou deign to come unto the Mountain? <br> Didst thou not know that man is happy here?” <br> Mine eyes fell downward into the clear fountain, <br> But, seeing myself therein, I sought the grass, <br> So great a shame did weigh my forehead down. <br> As to the son the mother seems superb, <br> So she appeared to me; for somewhat bitter <br> Tasteth the savor of severe compassion. <br> Silent became she, and the Angels sang <br> Suddenly, “<i>In te, Domine, speravi</i>”:<sup>1120</sup> <br> But beyond <i>pedes meos</i> did not pass. <br> Even as the snow among the living rafters<sup>1121</sup> <br> Upon the back of Italy congeals, <br> Blown on and drifted by Sclavonian winds,<sup>1122</sup> <br> And then, dissolving, trickles through itself <br> Whene’er the land that loses shadow breathes, <br> So that it seems a fire that melts a taper; <br> E’en thus was I without a tear or sigh, <br> Before the song of those who sing forever <br> After the music of the eternal spheres. <br> But when I heard in their sweet melodies <br> Compassion for me, more than had they said, <br> “O wherefore, lady, dost thou thus upbraid him?” <br> The ice, that was about my heart congealed, <br> To air and water changed, and in my anguish <br> Through mouth and eyes came gushing from my breast. <br> She, on the right-hand border of the car <br> Still firmly standing, to those holy beings <br> Thus her discourse directed afterwards: <br> “Ye keep your watch in the eternal day, <br> So that nor night nor sleep can steal from you <br> One step the ages make upon their path; <br> Therefore my answer is with greater care, <br> That he may hear me who is weeping yonder, <br> So that the sin and dole be of one measure. <br> Not only by the work of those great wheels, <br> That destine every seed unto some end, <br> According as the stars are in conjunction, <br> But by the largess of celestial graces, <br> Which have such lofty vapors for their rain<sup>1123</sup> <br> That near to them our sight approaches not, <br> Such had this man become in his new life <br> Potentially, that every righteous habit <br> Would have made admirable proof in him; <br> But so much more malignant and more savage <br> Becomes the land untilled and with bad seed, <br> The more good earthly vigor it possesses. <br> Some time did I sustain him with my look; <br> Revealing unto him my youthful eyes, <br> I led him with me turned in the right way. <br> As soon as ever of my second age <br> I was upon the threshold and changed life,<sup>1124</sup> <br> Himself from me he took and gave to others. <br> When from the flesh to spirit I ascended, <br> And beauty and virtue were in me increased, <br> I was to him less dear and less delightful; <br> And into ways untrue he turned his steps, <br> Pursuing the false images of good, <br> That never any promises fulfil; <br> Nor prayer for inspiration me availed, <br> By means of which in dreams and otherwise <br> I called him back, so little did he heed them. <br> So low he fell, that all appliances<sup>1125</sup> <br> For his salvation were already short, <br> Save showing him the people of perdition. <br> For this I visited the gates of death, <br> And unto him, who so far up has led him, <br> My intercessions were with weeping borne. <br> God’s lofty fiat would be violated, <br> If Lethe should be passed, and if such viands <br> Should tasted be, withouten any scot <br> Of penitence, that gushes forth in tears.”</p>
