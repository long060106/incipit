<p class="lede">Reproaches of Beatrice and confession of Dante⁠—The passage of Lethe.</p>
<p class="verse">“O thou who art beyond the sacred river,”<sup>1126</sup> <br> Turning to me the point of her discourse,<sup>1127</sup> <br> That edgewise even had seemed to me so keen, <br> She recommenced, continuing without pause, <br> “Say, say if this be true; to such a charge, <br> Thy own confession needs must be conjoined.” <br> My faculties were in so great confusion, <br> That the voice moved, but sooner was extinct <br> Than by its organs it was set at large. <br> Awhile she waited; then she said: “What thinkest? <br> Answer me; for the mournful memories <br> In thee not yet are by the waters injured.” <br> Confusion and dismay together mingled <br> Forced such a Yes! from out my mouth, that sight <br> Was needful to the understanding of it. <br> Even as a cross-bow breaks, when ’tis discharged <br> Too tensely drawn the bowstring and the bow, <br> And with less force the arrow hits the mark, <br> So I gave way beneath that heavy burden, <br> Outpouring in a torrent tears and sighs, <br> And the voice flagged upon its passage forth. <br> Whence she to me: “In those desires of mine <br> Which led thee to the loving of that good, <br> Beyond which there is nothing to aspire to, <br> What trenches lying traverse or what chains<sup>1128</sup> <br> Didst thou discover, that of passing onward <br> Thou shouldst have thus despoiled thee of the hope? <br> And what allurements or what vantages <br> Upon the forehead of the others showed, <br> That thou shouldst turn thy footsteps unto them?”<sup>1129</sup> <br> After the heaving of a bitter sigh, <br> Hardly had I the voice to make response, <br> And with fatigue my lips did fashion it. <br> Weeping I said: “The things that present were <br> With their false pleasure turned aside my steps, <br> Soon as your countenance concealed itself.” <br> And she: “Shouldst thou be silent, or deny <br> What thou confessest, not less manifest <br> Would be thy fault, by such a Judge ’tis known. <br> But when from one’s own cheeks comes bursting forth <br> The accusal of the sin, in our tribunal <br> Against the edge the wheel doth turn itself.<sup>1130</sup> <br> But still, that thou mayst feel a greater shame <br> For thy transgression, and another time <br> Hearing the Sirens thou mayst be more strong, <br> Cast down the seed of weeping and attend;<sup>1131</sup> <br> So shalt thou hear, how in an opposite way <br> My buried flesh should have directed thee. <br> Never to thee presented art or nature <br> Pleasure so great as the fair limbs wherein <br> I was enclosed, which scattered are in earth. <br> And if the highest pleasure thus did fail thee <br> By reason of my death, what mortal thing <br> Should then have drawn thee into its desire? <br> Thou oughtest verily at the first shaft <br> Of things fallacious to have risen up <br> To follow me, who was no longer such. <br> Thou oughtest not to have stooped thy pinions downward <br> To wait for further blows, or little girl,<sup>1132</sup> <br> Or other vanity of such brief use. <br> The callow birdlet waits for two or three, <br> But to the eyes of those already fledged, <br> In vain the net is spread or shaft is shot.”<sup>1133</sup> <br> Even as children silent in their shame <br> Stand listening with their eyes upon the ground, <br> And conscious of their fault, and penitent; <br> So was I standing; and she said: “If thou <br> In hearing sufferest pain, lift up thy beard <br> And thou shalt feel a greater pain in seeing.” <br> With less resistance is a robust holm <br> Uprooted, either by a native wind <br> Or else by that from regions of Iarbas,<sup>1134</sup> <br> Than I upraised at her command my chin; <br> And when she by the beard the face demanded, <br> Well I perceived the venom of her meaning. <br> And as my countenance was lifted up, <br> Mine eye perceived those creatures beautiful<sup>1135</sup> <br> Had rested from the strewing of the flowers; <br> And, still but little reassured, mine eyes <br> Saw Beatrice turned round towards the monster, <br> That is one person only in two natures. <br> Beneath her veil, beyond the margent green, <br> She seemed to me far more her ancient self <br> To excel, than others here, when she was here. <br> So pricked me then the thorn of penitence, <br> That of all other things the one which turned me <br> Most to its love became the most my foe. <br> Such self-conviction stung me at the heart <br> O’erpowered I fell, and what I then became <br> She knoweth who had furnished me the cause. <br> Then, when the heart restored my outward sense, <br> The lady I had found alone, above me<sup>1136</sup> <br> I saw, and she was saying, “Hold me, hold me.” <br> Up to my throat she in the stream had drawn me, <br> And, dragging me behind her, she was moving<sup>1137</sup> <br> Upon the water lightly as a shuttle. <br> When I was near unto the blessed shore, <br> “<i>Asperges me</i>,” I heard so sweetly sung,<sup>1138</sup> <br> Remember it I cannot, much less write it. <br> The beautiful lady opened wide her arms, <br> Embraced my head, and plunged me underneath, <br> Where I was forced to swallow of the water. <br> Then forth she drew me, and all dripping brought <br> Into the dance of the four beautiful,<sup>1139</sup> <br> And each one with her arm did cover me. <br> “We here are Nymphs, and in the Heaven are stars;<sup>1140</sup> <br> Ere Beatrice descended to the world, <br> We as her handmaids were appointed her. <br> We’ll lead thee to her eyes; but for the pleasant <br> Light that within them is, shall sharpen thine <br> The three beyond, who more profoundly look.”<sup>1141</sup> <br> Thus singing they began; and afterwards <br> Unto the Griffin’s breast they led me with them, <br> Where Beatrice was standing, turned towards us.<sup>1142</sup> <br> “See that thou dost not spare thine eyes,” they said; <br> “Before the emeralds have we stationed thee,<sup>1143</sup> <br> Whence Love aforetime drew for thee his weapons.” <br> A thousand longings, hotter than the flame, <br> Fastened mine eyes upon those eyes relucent, <br> That still upon the Griffin steadfast stayed. <br> As in a glass the sun, not otherwise <br> Within them was the twofold monster shining,<sup>1144</sup> <br> Now with the one, now with the other nature.<sup>1145</sup> <br> Think, Reader, if within myself I marvelled, <br> When I beheld the thing itself stand still, <br> And in its image it transformed itself. <br> While with amazement filled and jubilant, <br> My soul was tasting of the food, that while <br> It satisfies us makes us hunger for it, <br> Themselves revealing of the highest rank <br> In bearing, did the other three advance, <br> Singing to their angelic saraband.<sup>1146</sup> <br> “Turn, Beatrice, O turn thy holy eyes,” <br> Such was their song, “unto thy faithful one, <br> Who has to see thee ta’en so many steps. <br> In grace do us the grace that thou unveil <br> Thy face to him, so that he may discern <br> The second beauty which thou dost conceal.” <br> O splendor of the living light eternal! <br> Who underneath the shadow of Parnassus <br> Has grown so pale, or drunk so at its cistern, <br> He would not seem to have his mind encumbered <br> Striving to paint thee as thou didst appear, <br> Where the harmonious heaven o’ershadowed thee, <br> When in the open air thou didst unveil?</p>
