<p class="lede">The Fourth Bolgia: soothsayers⁠—Amphiaraüs, Tiresias, Aruns, Manto, Eryphylus, Michael Scott, Guido Bonatti, and Asdente.</p>
<p class="verse">Of a new pain behoves me to make verses<sup>280</sup> <br> And give material to the twentieth canto <br> Of the first song, which is of the submerged. <br> I was already thoroughly disposed <br> To peer down into the uncovered depth, <br> Which bathed itself with tears of agony; <br> And people saw I through the circular valley, <br> Silent and weeping, coming at the pace <br> Which in this world the Litanies assume.<sup>281</sup> <br> As lower down my sight descended on them, <br> Wondrously each one seemed to be distorted <br> From chin to the beginning of the chest; <br> For tow’rds the reins the countenance was turned,<sup>282</sup> <br> And backward it behoved them to advance, <br> As to look forward had been taken from them. <br> Perchance indeed by violence of palsy <br> Someone has been thus wholly turned awry; <br> But I ne’er saw it, nor believe it can be. <br> As God may let thee, Reader, gather fruit <br> From this thy reading, think now for thyself <br> How I could ever keep my face unmoistened, <br> When our own image near me I beheld, <br> Distorted so, the weeping of the eyes <br> Along the fissure bathed the hinder parts. <br> Truly I wept, leaning upon a peak <br> Of the hard crag, so that my Escort said <br> To me: “Art thou, too, of the other fools? <br> Here pity lives when it is wholly dead; <br> Who is a greater reprobate than he <br> Who feels compassion at the doom divine? <br> Lift up, lift up thy head, and see for whom <br> Opened the earth before the Thebans’ eyes; <br> Wherefore they all cried: ‘Whither rushest thou, <br> Amphiaraus? Why dost leave the war?’<sup>283</sup> <br> And downward ceased he not to fall amain <br> As far as Minos, who lays hold on all. <br> See, he has made a bosom of his shoulders! <br> Because he wished to see too far before him <br> Behind he looks, and backward goes his way: <br> Behold Tiresias, who his semblance changed,<sup>284</sup> <br> When from a male a female he became, <br> His members being all of them transformed; <br> And afterwards was forced to strike once more <br> The two entangled serpents with his rod, <br> Ere he could have again his manly plumes.<sup>285</sup> <br> That Aruns is, who backs the other’s belly,<sup>286</sup> <br> Who in the hills of Luni, there where grubs <br> The Carrarese who houses underneath, <br> Among the marbles white a cavern had <br> For his abode; whence to behold the stars <br> And sea, the view was not cut off from him. <br> And she there, who is covering up her breasts, <br> Which thou beholdest not, with loosened tresses, <br> And on that side has all the hairy skin, <br> Was Manto, who made quest through many lands,<sup>287</sup> <br> Afterwards tarried there where I was born; <br> Whereof I would thou list to me a little. <br> After her father had from life departed, <br> And the city of Bacchus had become enslaved, <br> She a long season wandered through the world. <br> Above in beauteous Italy lies a lake <br> At the Alp’s foot that shuts in Germany <br> Over Tyrol, and has the name Benaco.<sup>288</sup> <br> By a thousand springs, I think, and more, is bathed, <br> ’Twixt Garda and Val Camonica, Pennino,<sup>289</sup> <br> With water that grows stagnant in that lake. <br> Midway a place is where the Trentine Pastor, <br> And he of Brescia, and the Veronese <br> Might give his blessing, if he passed that way.<sup>290</sup> <br> Sitteth Peschiera, fortress fair and strong,<sup>291</sup> <br> To front the Brescians and the Bergamasks, <br> Where round about the bank descendeth lowest. <br> There of necessity must fall whatever <br> In bosom of Benaco cannot stay, <br> And grows a river down through verdant pastures. <br> Soon as the water doth begin to run, <br> No more Benaco is it called, but Mincio,<sup>292</sup> <br> Far as Governo, where it falls in Po. <br> Not far it runs before it finds a plain <br> In which it spreads itself, and makes it marshy, <br> And oft ’tis wont in summer to be sickly. <br> Passing that way the virgin pitiless<sup>293</sup> <br> Land in the middle of the fen descried, <br> Untilled and naked of inhabitants; <br> There to escape all human intercourse, <br> She with her servants stayed, her arts to practise, <br> And lived, and left her empty body there. <br> The men, thereafter, who were scattered round, <br> Collected in that place, which was made strong <br> By the lagoon it had on every side; <br> They built their city over those dead bones, <br> And, after her who first the place selected, <br> Mantua named it, without other omen.<sup>294</sup> <br> Its people once within more crowded were, <br> Ere the stupidity of Casalodi<sup>295</sup> <br> From Pinamonte had received deceit. <br> Therefore I caution thee, if e’er thou hearest <br> Originate my city otherwise, <br> No falsehood may the verity defraud.” <br> And I: “My Master, thy discourses are <br> To me so certain, and so take my faith, <br> That unto me the rest would be spent coals. <br> But tell me of the people who are passing, <br> If any one noteworthy thou beholdest, <br> For only unto that my mind reverts.” <br> Then said he to me: “He who from the cheek <br> Thrusts out his beard upon his swarthy shoulders <br> Was, at the time when Greece was void of males, <br> So that there scarce remained one in the cradle, <br> An augur, and with Calchas gave the moment,<sup>296</sup> <br> In Aulis, when to sever the first cable. <br> Eryphylus his name was, and so sings<sup>297</sup> <br> My lofty Tragedy in some part or other; <br> That knowest thou well, who knowest the whole of it. <br> The next, who is so slender in the flanks, <br> Was Michael Scott, who of a verity<sup>298</sup> <br> Of magical illusions knew the game. <br> Behold Guido Bonatti, behold Asdente,<sup>299</sup> <br> Who now unto his leather and his thread <br> Would fain have stuck, but he too late repents. <br> Behold the wretched ones, who left the needle, <br> The spool and rock, and made them fortune-tellers; <br> They wrought their magic spells with herb and image. <br> But come now, for already holds the confines <br> Of both the hemispheres, and under Seville <br> Touches the ocean-wave, Cain and the thorns,<sup>300</sup> <br> And yesternight the moon was round already; <br> Thou shouldst remember well it did not harm thee <br> From time to time within the forest deep.” <br> Thus spake he to me, and we walked the while.</p>
