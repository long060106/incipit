<p class="lede">The violent against nature⁠—Brunetto Latini.</p>
<p class="verse">Now bears us onward one of the hard margins,<sup>207</sup> <br> And so the brooklet’s mist o’ershadows it, <br> From fire it saves the water and the dikes. <br> Even as the Flemings, ’twixt Cadsand and Bruges,<sup>208</sup> <br> Fearing the flood that tow’rds them hurls itself,<sup>209</sup> <br> Their bulwarks build to put the sea to flight; <br> And as the Paduans along the Brenta, <br> To guard their villas and their villages, <br> Or ever Chiarentana feel the heat;<sup>210</sup> <br> In such similitude had those been made, <br> Albeit not so lofty nor so thick, <br> Whoever he might be, the master made them. <br> Now were we from the forest so remote, <br> I could not have discovered where it was, <br> Even if backward I had turned myself, <br> When we a company of souls encountered, <br> Who came beside the dike, and everyone <br> Gazed at us, as at evening we are wont <br> To eye each other under a new moon, <br> And so towards us sharpened they their brows <br> As an old tailor at the needle’s eye. <br> Thus scrutinised by such a family, <br> By someone I was recognized, who seized <br> My garment’s hem, and cried out, “What a marvel!” <br> And I, when he stretched forth his arm to me, <br> On his baked aspect fastened so mine eyes, <br> That the scorched countenance prevented not <br> His recognition by my intellect; <br> And bowing down my face unto his own,<sup>211</sup> <br> I made reply, “Are you here, Ser Brunetto?”<sup>212</sup> <br> And he: “May ’t not displease thee, O my son, <br> If a brief space with thee Brunetto Latini <br> Backward return and let the trail go on.” <br> I said to him: “With all my power I ask it; <br> And if you wish me to sit down with you, <br> I will, if he please, for I go with him.” <br> “O son,” he said, “whoever of this herd <br> A moment stops, lies then a hundred years, <br> Nor fans himself when smiteth him the fire. <br> Therefore go on; I at thy skirts will come, <br> And afterward will I rejoin my band, <br> Which goes lamenting its eternal doom.” <br> I did not dare to go down from the road <br> Level to walk with him; but my head bowed <br> I held as one who goeth reverently. <br> And he began: “What fortune or what fate <br> Before the last day leadeth thee down here? <br> And who is this that showeth thee the way?” <br> “Up there above us in the life serene,” <br> I answered him, “I lost me in a valley, <br> Or ever yet my age had been completed. <br> But yestermorn I turned my back upon it; <br> This one appeared to me, returning thither, <br> And homeward leadeth me along this road.” <br> And he to me: “If thou thy star do follow, <br> Thou canst not fail thee of a glorious port, <br> If well I judged in the life beautiful. <br> And if I had not died so prematurely, <br> Seeing Heaven thus benignant unto thee, <br> I would have given thee comfort in the work. <br> But that ungrateful and malignant people, <br> Which of old time from Fesole descended,<sup>213</sup> <br> And smacks still of the mountain and the granite, <br> Will make itself, for thy good deeds, thy foe; <br> And it is right; for among crabbed sorbs <br> It ill befits the sweet fig to bear fruit. <br> Old rumor in the world proclaims them blind;<sup>214</sup> <br> A people avaricious, envious, proud; <br> Take heed that of their customs thou do cleanse thee. <br> Thy fortune so much honor doth reserve thee, <br> One party and the other shall be hungry <br> For thee; but far from goat shall be the grass. <br> Their litter let the beasts of Fesole <br> Make of themselves, nor let them touch the plant, <br> If any still upon their dunghill rise, <br> In which may yet revive the consecrated <br> Seed of those Romans, who remained there when <br> The nest of such great malice it became.” <br> “If my entreaty wholly were fulfilled,” <br> Replied I to him, “not yet would you be <br> In banishment from human nature placed; <br> For in my mind is fixed, and touches now <br> My heart the dear and good paternal image <br> Of you, when in the world from hour to hour <br> You taught me how a man becomes eternal; <br> And how much I am grateful, while I live <br> Behoves that in my language be discerned. <br> What you narrate of my career I write, <br> And keep it to be glossed with other text<sup>215</sup> <br> By a Lady who can do it, if I reach her. <br> This much will I have manifest to you; <br> Provided that my conscience do not chide me, <br> For whatsoever Fortune I am ready. <br> Such hansel is not new unto mine ears; <br> Therefore let Fortune turn her wheel around <br> As it may please her, and the churl his mattock.”<sup>216</sup> <br> My Master thereupon on his right cheek <br> Did backward turn himself, and looked at me; <br> Then said: “He listeneth well who noteth it.” <br> Nor speaking less on that account, I go <br> With Ser Brunetto, and I ask who are <br> His most known and most eminent companions. <br> And he to me: “To know of some is well; <br> Of others it were laudable to be silent, <br> For short would be the time for so much speech. <br> Know then, in sum, that all of them were clerks, <br> And men of letters great and of great fame, <br> In the world tainted with the selfsame sin. <br> Priscian goes yonder with that wretched crowd,<sup>217</sup> <br> And Francis of Accorso; and thou hadst seen there,<sup>218</sup> <br> If thou hadst had a hankering for such scurf, <br> That one, who by the Servant of the Servants <br> From Arno was transferred to Bacchiglione,<sup>219</sup> <br> Where he has left his sin-excited nerves. <br> More would I say, but coming and discoursing <br> Can be no longer; for that I behold <br> New smoke uprising yonder from the sand. <br> A people comes with whom I may not be; <br> Commended unto thee be my Tesoro,<sup>220</sup> <br> In which I still live, and no more I ask.” <br> Then he turned round, and seemed to be of those <br> Who at Verona run for the Green Mantle<sup>221</sup> <br> Across the plain; and seemed to be among them <br> The one who wins, and not the one who loses.</p>
