<p class="lede">Fourth division of the Ninth Circle, the Judecca: traitors to their lords and benefactors⁠—Lucifer, Judas Iscariot, Brutus and Cassius.</p>
<p class="verse">“<i>Vexilla Regis prodeunt Inferni</i><sup>516</sup> <br> Towards us; therefore look in front of thee,” <br> My Master said, “if thou discernest him.” <br> As, when there breathes a heavy fog, or when <br> Our hemisphere is darkening into night, <br> Appears far off a mill the wind is turning, <br> Methought that such a building then I saw; <br> And, for the wind, I drew myself behind <br> My Guide, because there was no other shelter. <br> Now was I, and with fear in verse I put it, <br> There where the shades were wholly covered up, <br> And glimmered through like unto straws in glass. <br> Some prone are lying, others stand erect, <br> This with the head, and that one with the soles; <br> Another, bow-like, face to feet inverts. <br> When in advance so far we had proceeded, <br> That it my Master pleased to show to me <br> The creature who once had the beauteous semblance,<sup>517</sup> <br> He from before me moved and made me stop, <br> Saying: “Behold Dis, and behold the place <br> Where thou with fortitude must arm thyself.” <br> How frozen I became and powerless then, <br> Ask it not, Reader, for I write it not, <br> Because all language would be insufficient. <br> I did not die, and I alive remained not; <br> Think for thyself now, hast thou aught of wit, <br> What I became, being of both deprived. <br> The Emperor of the kingdom dolorous<sup>518</sup> <br> From his mid-breast forth issued from the ice; <br> And better with a giant I compare <br> Than do the giants with those arms of his; <br> Consider now how great must be that whole, <br> Which unto such a part conforms itself. <br> Were he as fair once, as he now is foul, <br> And lifted up his brow against his Maker, <br> Well may proceed from him all tribulation. <br> O, what a marvel it appeared to me, <br> When I beheld three faces on his head!<sup>519</sup> <br> The one in front, and that vermilion was; <br> Two were the others, that were joined with this <br> Above the middle part of either shoulder, <br> And they were joined together at the crest; <br> And the right-hand one seemed ’twixt white and yellow; <br> The left was such to look upon as those <br> Who come from where the Nile falls valley-ward.<sup>520</sup> <br> Underneath each came forth two mighty wings, <br> Such as befitting were so great a bird; <br> Sails of the sea I never saw so large.<sup>521</sup> <br> No feathers had they, but as of a bat <br> Their fashion was; and he was waving them, <br> So that three winds proceeded forth therefrom. <br> Thereby Cocytus wholly was congealed. <br> With six eyes did he weep, and down three chins <br> Trickled the tear-drops and the bloody drivel. <br> At every mouth he with his teeth was crunching<sup>522</sup> <br> A sinner, in the manner of a brake, <br> So that he three of them tormented thus. <br> To him in front the biting was as naught <br> Unto the clawing, for sometimes the spine <br> Utterly stripped of all the skin remained. <br> “That soul up there which has the greatest pain,” <br> The Master said, “is Judas Iscariot;<sup>523</sup> <br> With head inside, he plies his legs without. <br> Of the two others, who head downward are, <br> The one who hangs from the black jowl is Brutus; <br> See how he writhes himself, and speaks no word. <br> And the other, who so stalwart seems, is Cassius. <br> But night is reascending, and ’tis time<sup>524</sup> <br> That we depart, for we have seen the whole.” <br> As seemed him good, I clasped him round the neck, <br> And he the vantage seized of time and place, <br> And when the wings were opened wide apart, <br> He laid fast hold upon the shaggy sides; <br> From fell to fell descended downward then <br> Between the thick hair and the frozen crust. <br> When we were come to where the thigh revolves <br> Exactly on the thickness of the haunch,<sup>525</sup> <br> The Guide, with labor and with hard-drawn breath, <br> Turned round his head where he had had his legs, <br> And grappled to the hair, as one who mounts, <br> So that to Hell I thought we were returning. <br> “Keep fast thy hold, for by such stairs as these,” <br> The Master said, panting as one fatigued, <br> “Must we perforce depart from so much evil.” <br> Then through the opening of a rock he issued, <br> And down upon the margin seated me; <br> Then tow’rds me he outstretched his wary step. <br> I lifted up mine eyes and thought to see <br> Lucifer in the same way I had left him; <br> And I beheld him upward hold his legs. <br> And if I then became disquieted, <br> Let stolid people think who do not see <br> What the point is beyond which I had passed. <br> “Rise up,” the Master said, “upon thy feet; <br> The way is long, and difficult the road,<sup>526</sup> <br> And now the sun to middle-tierce returns.” <br> It was not any palace corridor <br> There where we were, but dungeon natural, <br> With floor uneven and unease of light. <br> “Ere from the abyss I tear myself away, <br> My Master,” said I when I had arisen, <br> “To draw me from an error speak a little; <br> Where is the ice? and how is this one fixed <br> Thus upside down? and how in such short time <br> From eve to morn has the sun made his transit?” <br> And he to me: “Thou still imaginest <br> Thou art beyond the centre, where I grasped <br> The hair of the fell worm, who mines the world. <br> That side thou wast, so long as I descended; <br> When round I turned me, thou didst pass the point <br> To which things heavy draw from every side, <br> And now beneath the hemisphere art come <br> Opposite that which overhangs the vast <br> Dry-land, and ’neath whose cope was put to death<sup>527</sup> <br> The Man who without sin was born and lived. <br> Thou hast thy feet upon the little sphere <br> Which makes the other face of the Judecca. <br> Here it is morn when it is evening there; <br> And he who with his hair a stairway made us <br> Still fixed remaineth as he was before. <br> Upon this side he fell down out of heaven; <br> And all the land, that whilom here emerged, <br> For fear of him made of the sea a veil, <br> And came to our hemisphere; and peradventure <br> To flee from him, what on this side appears<sup>528</sup> <br> Left the place vacant here, and back recoiled.” <br> A place there is below, from Beelzebub <br> As far receding as the tomb extends, <br> Which not by sight is known, but by the sound <br> Of a small rivulet, that there descendeth<sup>529</sup> <br> Through chasm within the stone, which it has gnawed <br> With course that winds about and slightly falls. <br> The Guide and I into that hidden road <br> Now entered, to return to the bright world; <br> And without care of having any rest <br> We mounted up, he first and I the second, <br> Till I beheld through a round aperture <br> Some of the beauteous things that Heaven doth bear;<sup>530</sup> <br> Thence we came forth to rebehold the stars.</p>
