<p class="lede">The Eighth Bolgia: evil counsellors⁠—Ulysses and Diomed.</p>
<p class="verse">Rejoice, O Florence, since thou art so great,<sup>374</sup> <br> That over sea and land thou beatest thy wings, <br> And throughout Hell thy name is spread abroad! <br> Among the thieves five citizens of thine<sup>375</sup> <br> Like these I found, whence shame comes unto me, <br> And thou thereby to no great honor risest. <br> But if when morn is near our dreams are true,<sup>376</sup> <br> Feel shalt thou in a little time from now <br> What Prato, if none other, craves for thee.<sup>377</sup> <br> And if it now were, it were not too soon;<sup>378</sup> <br> Would that it were, seeing it needs must be, <br> For ’twill aggrieve me more the more I age. <br> We went our way, and up along the stairs <br> The bourns had made us to descend before, <br> Remounted my Conductor and drew me. <br> And following the solitary path <br> Among the rocks and ridges of the crag, <br> The foot without the hand sped not at all. <br> Then sorrowed I, and sorrow now again, <br> When I direct my mind to what I saw, <br> And more my genius curb than I am wont, <br> That it may run not unless virtue guide it; <br> So that if some good star, or better thing,<sup>379</sup> <br> Have given me good, I may myself not grudge it.<sup>380</sup> <br> As many as the hind (who on the hill <br> Rests at the time when he who lights the world <br> His countenance keeps least concealed from us, <br> While as the fly gives place unto the gnat) <br> Seeth the glow-worms down along the valley, <br> Perchance there where he ploughs and makes his vintage; <br> With flames as manifold resplendent all <br> Was the eighth Bolgia, as I grew aware <br> As soon as I was where the depth appeared. <br> And such as he who with the bears avenged him<sup>381</sup> <br> Beheld Elijah’s chariot at departing,<sup>382</sup> <br> What time the steeds to heaven erect uprose, <br> For with his eye he could not follow it <br> So as to see aught else than flame alone, <br> Even as a little cloud ascending upward, <br> Thus each along the gorge of the intrenchment <br> Was moving; for not one reveals the theft, <br> And every flame a sinner steals away. <br> I stood upon the bridge uprisen to see, <br> So that, if I had seized not on a rock, <br> Down had I fallen without being pushed. <br> And the Leader, who beheld me so attent, <br> Exclaimed: “Within the fires the spirits are; <br> Each swathes himself with that wherewith he burns.” <br> “My Master,” I replied, “by hearing thee <br> I am more sure; but I surmised already <br> It might be so, and already wished to ask thee <br> Who is within that fire, which comes so cleft <br> At top, it seems uprising from the pyre <br> Where was Eteocles with his brother placed.”<sup>383</sup> <br> He answered me: “Within there are tormented <br> Ulysses and Diomed, and thus together<sup>384</sup> <br> They unto vengeance run as unto wrath. <br> And there within their flame do they lament <br> The ambush of the horse, which made the door<sup>385</sup> <br> Whence issued forth the Romans’ gentle seed; <br> Therein is wept the craft, for which being dead <br> Deidamia still deplores Achilles,<sup>386</sup> <br> And pain for the Palladium there is borne.”<sup>387</sup> <br> “If they within those sparks possess the power <br> To speak,” I said, “thee, Master, much I pray, <br> And re-pray, that the prayer be worth a thousand, <br> That thou make no denial of awaiting <br> Until the horned flame shall hither come; <br> Thou seest that with desire I lean towards it.” <br> And he to me: “Worthy is thy entreaty <br> Of much applause, and therefore I accept it; <br> But take heed that thy tongue restrain itself. <br> Leave me to speak, because I have conceived <br> That which thou wishest; for they might disdain <br> Perchance, since they were Greeks, discourse of thine.”<sup>388</sup> <br> When now the flame had come unto that point, <br> Where to my Leader it seemed time and place, <br> After this fashion did I hear him speak: <br> “O ye, who are twofold within one fire, <br> If I deserved of you, while I was living, <br> If I deserved of you or much or little <br> When in the world I wrote the lofty verses, <br> Do not move on, but one of you declare <br> Whither, being lost, he went away to die.” <br> Then of the antique flame the greater horn, <br> Murmuring, began to wave itself about <br> Even as a flame doth which the wind fatigues. <br> Thereafterward, the summit to and fro <br> Moving as if it were the tongue that spake, <br> It uttered forth a voice, and said: “When I <br> From Circe had departed, who concealed me <br> More than a year there near unto Gaëta, <br> Or ever yet Aeneas named it so, <br> Nor fondness for my son, nor reverence <br> For my old father, nor the due affection <br> Which joyous should have made Penelope, <br> Could overcome within me the desire <br> I had to be experienced of the world, <br> And of the vice and virtue of mankind; <br> But I put forth on the high open sea <br> With one sole ship, and that small company <br> By which I never had deserted been. <br> Both of the shores I saw as far as Spain, <br> Far as Morocco, and the isle of Sardes, <br> And the others which that sea bathes round about. <br> I and my company were old and slow <br> When at that narrow passage we arrived <br> Where Hercules his landmarks set as signals,<sup>389</sup> <br> That man no farther onward should adventure. <br> On the right hand behind me left I Seville, <br> And on the other already had left Ceuta. <br> ‘O brothers, who amid a hundred thousand <br> Perils,’ I said, ‘have come unto the West, <br> To this so inconsiderable vigil <br> Which is remaining of your senses still, <br> Be ye unwilling to deny the knowledge, <br> Following the sun, of the unpeopled world. <br> Consider ye the seed from which ye sprang; <br> Ye were not made to live like unto brutes, <br> But for pursuit of virtue and of knowledge.’ <br> So eager did I render my companions, <br> With this brief exhortation, for the voyage, <br> That then I hardly could have held them back. <br> And having turned our stern unto the morning, <br> We of the oars made wings for our mad flight,<sup>390</sup> <br> Evermore gaining on the larboard side. <br> Already all the stars of the other pole<sup>391</sup> <br> The night beheld, and ours so very low <br> It did not rise above the ocean floor. <br> Five times rekindled and as many quenched <br> Had been the splendor underneath the moon, <br> Since we had entered into the deep pass, <br> When there appeared to us a mountain, dim <br> From distance, and it seemed to me so high <br> As I had never any one beheld. <br> Joyful were we, and soon it turned to weeping; <br> For out of the new land a whirlwind rose, <br> And smote upon the fore part of the ship. <br> Three times it made it whirl with all the waters, <br> At the fourth time it made the stern uplift, <br> And the prow downward go, as pleased Another, <br> Until the sea above us closed again.”<sup>392</sup></p>
