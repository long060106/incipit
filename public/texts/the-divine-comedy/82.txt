<p class="lede">St. Buonaventura recounts the life of St. Dominic.</p>
<p class="verse">Soon as the blessed flame had taken up<sup>1481</sup> <br> The final word to give it utterance, <br> Began the holy millstone to revolve,<sup>1482</sup> <br> And in its gyre had not turned wholly round, <br> Before another in a ring enclosed it, <br> And motion joined to motion, song to song; <br> Song that as greatly doth transcend our Muses, <br> Our Sirens, in those dulcet clarions, <br> As primal splendor that which is reflected. <br> And as are spanned athwart a tender cloud <br> Two rainbows parallel and like in color,<sup>1483</sup> <br> When Juno to her handmaid gives command,<sup>1484</sup> <br> (The one without born of the one within, <br> Like to the speaking of that vagrant one<sup>1485</sup> <br> Whom love consumed as doth the sun the vapors,) <br> And make the people here, through covenant<sup>1486</sup> <br> God set with Noah, presageful of the world <br> That shall no more be covered with a flood, <br> In such wise of those sempiternal roses <br> The garlands twain encompassed us about, <br> And thus the outer to the inner answered. <br> After the dance, and other grand rejoicings, <br> Both of the singing, and the flaming forth <br> Effulgence with effulgence blithe and tender, <br> Together, at once, with one accord had stopped, <br> (Even as the eyes, that, as volition moves them, <br> Must needs together shut and lift themselves,) <br> Out of the heart of one of the new lights <br> There came a voice, that needle to the star <br> Made me appear in turning thitherward. <br> And it began: “The love that makes me fair<sup>1487</sup> <br> Draws me to speak about the other leader,<sup>1488</sup> <br> By whom so well is spoken here of mine. <br> ’Tis right, where one is, to bring in the other,<sup>1489</sup> <br> That, as they were united in their warfare, <br> Together likewise may their glory shine. <br> The soldiery of Christ, which it had cost <br> So dear to arm again, behind the standard<sup>1490</sup> <br> Moved slow and doubtful and in numbers few, <br> When the Emperor who reigneth evermore <br> Provided for the host that was in peril, <br> Through grace alone and not that it was worthy; <br> And, as was said, he to his Bride brought succor<sup>1491</sup> <br> With champions twain, at whose deed, at whose word <br> The straggling people were together drawn. <br> Within that region where the sweet west wind<sup>1492</sup> <br> Rises to open the new leaves, wherewith <br> Europe is seen to clothe herself afresh, <br> Not far off from the beating of the waves, <br> Behind which in his long career the sun <br> Sometimes conceals himself from every man, <br> Is situate the fortunate Calahorra,<sup>1493</sup> <br> Under protection of the mighty shield<sup>1494</sup> <br> In which the Lion subject is and sovereign. <br> Therein was born the amorous paramour<sup>1495</sup> <br> Of Christian Faith, the athlete consecrate, <br> Kind to his own and cruel to his foes; <br> And when it was created was his mind<sup>1496</sup> <br> Replete with such a living energy, <br> That in his mother her it made prophetic.<sup>1497</sup> <br> As soon as the espousals were complete <br> Between him and the Faith at holy font, <br> Where they with mutual safety dowered each other, <br> The woman, who for him had given assent,<sup>1498</sup> <br> Saw in a dream the admirable fruit <br> That issue would from him and from his heirs; <br> And that he might be construed as he was, <br> A spirit from this place went forth to name him <br> With His possessive whose he wholly was.<sup>1499</sup> <br> Dominic was he called; and him I speak of<sup>1500</sup> <br> Even as of the husbandman whom Christ <br> Elected to his garden to assist him. <br> Envoy and servant sooth he seemed of Christ, <br> For the first love made manifest in him <br> Was the first counsel that was given by Christ.<sup>1501</sup> <br> Silent and wakeful many a time was he <br> Discovered by his nurse upon the ground, <br> As if he would have said, ‘For this I came.’ <br> O thou his father, Felix verily!<sup>1502</sup> <br> O thou his mother, verily Joanna, <br> If this, interpreted, means as is said! <br> Not for the world which people toil for now <br> In following Ostiense and Taddeo,<sup>1503</sup> <br> But through his longing after the true manna, <br> He in short time became so great a teacher, <br> That he began to go about the vineyard, <br> Which fadeth soon, if faithless be the dresser; <br> And of the See, (that once was more benignant<sup>1504</sup> <br> Unto the righteous poor, not through itself, <br> But him who sits there and degenerates,)<sup>1505</sup> <br> Not to dispense or two or three for six,<sup>1506</sup> <br> Not any fortune of first vacancy, <br>  <i>Non decimas quae sunt pauperum Dei,</i>  <br> He asked for, but against the errant world <br> Permission to do battle for the seed, <br> Of which these four and twenty plants surround thee. <br> Then with the doctrine and the will together, <br> With office apostolical he moved, <br> Like torrent which some lofty vein out-presses; <br> And in among the shoots heretical <br> His impetus with greater fury smote, <br> Wherever the resistance was the greatest. <br> Of him were made thereafter divers runnels, <br> Whereby the garden catholic is watered, <br> So that more living its plantations stand. <br> If such the one wheel of the Biga was,<sup>1507</sup> <br> In which the Holy Church itself defended <br> And in the field its civic battle won, <br> Truly full manifest should be to thee <br> The excellence of the other, unto whom <br> Thomas so courteous was before my coming. <br> But still the orbit, which the highest part<sup>1508</sup> <br> Of its circumference made, is derelict, <br> So that the mould is where was once the crust.<sup>1509</sup> <br> His family, that had straight forward moved <br> With feet upon his footprints, are turned round <br> So that they set the point upon the heel.<sup>1510</sup> <br> And soon aware they will be of the harvest <br> Of this bad husbandry, when shall the tares <br> Complain the granary is taken from them.<sup>1511</sup> <br> Yet say I, he who searcheth leaf by leaf<sup>1512</sup> <br> Our volume through, would still some page discover <br> Where he could read, ‘I am as I am wont.’ <br> ’Twill not be from Casal nor Acquasparta,<sup>1513</sup> <br> From whence come such unto the written word <br> That one avoids it, and the other narrows. <br> Bonaventura of Bagnoregio’s life<sup>1514</sup> <br> Am I, who always in great offices <br> Postponed considerations sinister. <br> Here are Illuminato and Agostino,<sup>1515</sup> <br> Who of the first barefooted beggars were <br> That with the cord the friends of God became. <br> Hugh of Saint Victor is among them here,<sup>1516</sup> <br> And Peter Mangiador, and Peter of Spain,<sup>1517</sup> <br> Who down below in volumes twelve is shining; <br> Nathan the seer, and metropolitan<sup>1518</sup> <br> Chrysostom, and Anselmus, and Donatus<sup>1519</sup> <br> Who deigned to lay his hand to the first art; <br> Here is Rabanus, and beside me here<sup>1520</sup> <br> Shines the Calabrian Abbot Joachim,<sup>1521</sup> <br> He with the spirit of prophecy endowed. <br> To celebrate so great a paladin <br> Have moved me the impassioned courtesy <br> And the discreet discourses of Friar Thomas, <br> And with me they have moved this company.”</p>
