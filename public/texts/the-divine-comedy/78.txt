<p class="lede">Ascent to the Third Heaven, or that of Venus, where are seen the spirits of lovers⁠—Charles Martel.</p>
<p class="verse">The world used in its peril to believe<sup>1335</sup> <br> That the fair Cypria delirious love<sup>1336</sup> <br> Rayed out, in the third epicycle turning;<sup>1337</sup> <br> Wherefore not only unto her paid honor <br> Of sacrifices and of votive cry <br> The ancient nations in the ancient error, <br> But both Dione honored they and Cupid,<sup>1338</sup> <br> That as her mother, this one as her son, <br> And said that he had sat in Dido’s lap;<sup>1339</sup> <br> And they from her, whence I beginning take,<sup>1340</sup> <br> Took the denomination of the star <br> That woos the sun, now following, now in front.<sup>1341</sup> <br> I was not ware of our ascending to it; <br> But of our being in it gave full faith <br> My Lady whom I saw more beauteous grow. <br> And as within a flame a spark is seen, <br> And as within a voice a voice discerned, <br> When one is steadfast, and one comes and goes, <br> Within that light beheld I other lamps <br> Move in a circle, speeding more and less, <br> Methinks in measure of their inward vision.<sup>1342</sup> <br> From a cold cloud descended never winds, <br> Or visible or not, so rapidly<sup>1343</sup> <br> They would not laggard and impeded seem <br> To anyone who had those lights divine <br> Seen come towards us, leaving the gyration <br> Begun at first in the high Seraphim.<sup>1344</sup> <br> And behind those that most in front appeared <br> Sounded “Osanna!” so that never since <br> To hear again was I without desire. <br> Then unto us more nearly one approached, <br> And it alone began: “We all are ready <br> Unto thy pleasure, that thou joy in us. <br> We turn around with the celestial Princes,<sup>1345</sup> <br> One gyre and one gyration and one thirst, <br> To whom thou in the world of old didst say, <br> ‘<em>Ye who, intelligent, the third heaven are moving</em>’;<sup>1346</sup> <br> And are so full of love, to pleasure thee <br> A little quiet will not be less sweet.” <br> After these eyes of mine themselves had offered<sup>1347</sup> <br> Unto my Lady reverently, and she <br> Content and certain of herself had made them, <br> Back to the light they turned, which so great promise <br> Made of itself, and “Say, who art thou?” was <br> My voice, imprinted with a great affection. <br> O how and how much I beheld it grow<sup>1348</sup> <br> With the new joy that superadded was <br> Unto its joys, as soon as I had spoken! <br> Thus changed, it said to me: “The world possessed me<sup>1349</sup> <br> Short time below; and, if it had been more, <br> Much evil will be which would not have been. <br> My gladness keepeth me concealed from thee, <br> Which rayeth round about me, and doth hide me <br> Like as a creature swathed in its own silk. <br> Much didst thou love me, and thou hadst good reason; <br> For had I been below, I should have shown thee <br> Somewhat beyond the foliage of my love. <br> That left-hand margin, which doth bathe itself<sup>1350</sup> <br> In Rhone, when it is mingled with the Sorgue, <br> Me for its lord awaited in due time, <br> And that horn of Ausonia, which is towned<sup>1351</sup> <br> With Bari, with Gaeta and Catona, <br> Whence Tronto and Verde in the sea disgorge. <br> Already flashed upon my brow the crown <br> Of that dominion which the Danube waters<sup>1352</sup> <br> After the German borders it abandons; <br> And beautiful Trinacria, that is murky<sup>1353</sup> <br> ’Twixt Pachino and Peloro, (on the gulf<sup>1354</sup> <br> Which greatest scath from Eurus doth receive,) <br> Not through Typhoeus, but through nascent sulphur,<sup>1355</sup> <br> Would have awaited her own monarchs still, <br> Through me from Charles descended and from Rudolph,<sup>1356</sup> <br> If evil lordship, that exasperates ever <br> The subject populations, had not moved <br> Palermo to the outcry of ‘Death! death!’<sup>1357</sup> <br> And if my brother could but this foresee,<sup>1358</sup> <br> The greedy poverty of Catalonia <br> Straight would he flee, that it might not molest him; <br> For verily ’tis needful to provide, <br> Through him or other, so that on his bark<sup>1359</sup> <br> Already freighted no more freight be placed. <br> His nature, which from liberal covetous<sup>1360</sup> <br> Descended, such a soldiery would need <br> As should not care for hoarding in a chest.”<sup>1361</sup> <br> “Because I do believe the lofty joy <br> Thy speech infuses into me, my Lord, <br> Where every good thing doth begin and end<sup>1362</sup> <br> Thou seest as I see it, the more grateful <br> Is it to me; and this too hold I dear, <br> That gazing upon God thou dost discern it. <br> Glad hast thou made me; so make clear to me, <br> Since speaking thou hast stirred me up to doubt, <br> How from sweet seed can bitter issue forth.” <br> This I to him; and he to me: “If I <br> Can show to thee a truth, to what thou askest <br> Thy face thou’lt hold as thou dost hold thy back. <br> The Good which all the realm thou art ascending<sup>1363</sup> <br> Turns and contents, maketh its providence <br> To be a power within these bodies vast; <br> And not alone the natures are foreseen <br> Within the mind that in itself is perfect, <br> But they together with their preservation. <br> For whatsoever thing this bow shoots forth <br> Falls foreordained unto an end foreseen, <br> Even as a shaft directed to its mark. <br> If that were not, the heaven which thou dost walk <br> Would in such manner its effects produce, <br> That they no longer would be arts, but ruins. <br> This cannot be, if the Intelligences <br> That keep these stars in motion are not maimed, <br> And maimed the First that has not made them perfect. <br> Wilt thou this truth have clearer made to thee?” <br> And I: “Not so; for ’tis impossible <br> That nature tire, I see, in what is needful.” <br> Whence he again: “Now say, would it be worse <br> For men on earth were they not citizens?”<sup>1364</sup> <br> “Yes,” I replied; “and here I ask no reason.” <br> “And can they be so, if below they live not <br> Diversely unto offices diverse? <br> No, if your master writeth well for you.”<sup>1365</sup> <br> So came he with deductions to this point; <br> Then he concluded: “Therefore it behoves <br> The roots of your effects to be diverse. <br> Hence one is Solon born, another Xerxes,<sup>1366</sup> <br> Another Melchisedec, and another he <br> Who, flying through the air, his son did lose. <br> Revolving Nature, which a signet is <br> To mortal wax, doth practise well her art, <br> But not one inn distinguish from another;<sup>1367</sup> <br> Thence happens it that Esau differeth<sup>1368</sup> <br> In seed from Jacob; and Quirinus comes<sup>1369</sup> <br> From sire so vile that he is given to Mars. <br> A generated nature its own way <br> Would always make like its progenitors, <br> If Providence divine were not triumphant. <br> Now that which was behind thee is before thee; <br> But that thou know that I with thee am pleased, <br> With a corollary will I mantle thee. <br> Evermore nature, if it fortune find <br> Discordant to it, like each other seed <br> Out of its region, maketh evil thrift;<sup>1370</sup> <br> And if the world below would fix its mind <br> On the foundation which is laid by nature, <br> Pursuing that, ’twould have the people good. <br> But you unto religion wrench aside<sup>1371</sup> <br> Him who was born to gird him with the sword, <br> And make a king of him who is for sermons; <br> Therefore your footsteps wander from the road.”</p>
