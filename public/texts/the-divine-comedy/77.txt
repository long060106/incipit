<p class="lede">Beatrice’s discourse of the incarnation, the immortality of the soul, and the resurrection of the body.</p>
<p class="verse">“<i>Osanna sanctus Deus Sabaoth,</i><sup>1321</sup> <br>  <i>Superillustrans claritate tua</i>  <br> <i>Felices ignes horum malahoth!</i>” <br> In this wise, to his melody returning, <br> This substance, upon which a double light<sup>1322</sup> <br> Doubles itself, was seen by me to sing, <br> And to their dance this and the others moved,<sup>1323</sup> <br> And in the manner of swift-hurrying sparks <br> Veiled themselves from me with a sudden distance. <br> Doubting was I, and saying, “Tell her, tell her,”<sup>1324</sup> <br> Within me, “tell her,” saying, “tell my Lady,” <br> Who slakes my thirst with her sweet effluences; <br> And yet that reverence which doth lord it over <br> The whole of me only by <b>b</b> and <b>ice</b>,<sup>1325</sup> <br> Bowed me again like unto one who drowses. <br> Short while did Beatrice endure me thus; <br> And she began, lighting me with a smile<sup>1326</sup> <br> Such as would make one happy in the fire: <br> “According to infallible advisement, <br> After what manner a just vengeance justly<sup>1327</sup> <br> Could be avenged has put thee upon thinking, <br> But I will speedily thy mind unloose; <br> And do thou listen, for these words of mine <br> Of a great doctrine will a present make thee. <br> By not enduring on the power that wills <br> Curb for his good, that man who ne’er was born, <br> Damning himself damned all his progeny;<sup>1328</sup> <br> Whereby the human species down below <br> Lay sick for many centuries in great error, <br> Till to descend it pleased the Word of God <br> To where the nature, which from its own Maker <br> Estranged itself, he joined to him in person <br> By the sole act of his eternal love. <br> Now unto what is said direct thy sight; <br> This nature when united to its Maker, <br> Such as created, was sincere and good;<sup>1329</sup> <br> But by itself alone was banished forth <br> From Paradise, because it turned aside <br> Out of the way of truth and of its life. <br> Therefore the penalty the cross held out, <br> If measured by the nature thus assumed, <br> None ever yet with so great justice stung, <br> And none was ever of so great injustice, <br> Considering who the Person was that suffered, <br> Within whom such a nature was contracted. <br> From one act therefore issued things diverse; <br> To God and to the Jews one death was pleasing; <br> Earth trembled at it and the Heaven was opened. <br> It should no longer now seem difficult <br> To thee, when it is said that a just vengeance <br> By a just court was afterward avenged. <br> But now do I behold thy mind entangled <br> From thought to thought within a knot, from which <br> With great desire it waits to free itself. <br> Thou sayest, ‘Well discern I what I hear; <br> But it is hidden from me why God willed <br> For our redemption only this one mode.’ <br> Buried remaineth, brother, this decree <br> Unto the eyes of everyone whose nature <br> Is in the flame of love not yet adult. <br> Verily, inasmuch as at this mark <br> One gazes long and little is discerned, <br> Wherefore this mode was worthiest will I say. <br> Goodness Divine, which from itself doth spurn <br> All envy, burning in itself so sparkles<sup>1330</sup> <br> That the eternal beauties it unfolds. <br> Whate’er from this immediately distils<sup>1331</sup> <br> Has afterwards no end, for ne’er removed <br> Is its impression when it sets its seal. <br> Whate’er from this immediately rains down <br> Is wholly free, because it is not subject <br> Unto the influences of novel things. <br> The more conformed thereto, the more it pleases; <br> For the blest ardor that irradiates all things <br> In that most like itself is most vivacious. <br> With all of these things has advantaged been<sup>1332</sup> <br> The human creature; and if one be wanting, <br> From his nobility he needs must fall. <br> ’Tis sin alone which doth disfranchise him, <br> And render him unlike the Good Supreme, <br> So that he little with its light is blanched, <br> And to his dignity no more returns, <br> Unless he fill up where transgression empties <br> With righteous pains for criminal delights. <br> Your nature when it sinned so utterly <br> In its own seed, out of these dignities <br> Even as out of Paradise was driven, <br> Nor could itself recover, if thou notest <br> With nicest subtlety, by anyway, <br> Except by passing one of these two fords: <br> Either that God through clemency alone <br> Had pardon granted, or that man himself <br> Had satisfaction for his folly made. <br> Fix now thine eye deep into the abyss <br> Of the eternal counsel, to my speech <br> As far as may be fastened steadfastly! <br> Man in his limitations had not power <br> To satisfy, not having power to sink <br> In his humility obeying then, <br> Far as he disobeying thought to rise; <br> And for this reason man has been from power <br> Of satisfying by himself excluded. <br> Therefore it God behoved in his own ways <br> Man to restore unto his perfect life, <br> I say in one, or else in both of them. <br> But since the action of the doer is <br> So much more grateful, as it more presents <br> The goodness of the heart from which it issues, <br> Goodness Divine, that doth imprint the world, <br> Has been contented to proceed by each <br> And all its ways to lift you up again; <br> Nor ’twixt the first day and the final night <br> Such high and such magnificent proceeding <br> By one or by the other was or shall be; <br> For God more bounteous was himself to give <br> To make man able to uplift himself, <br> Than if he only of himself had pardoned; <br> And all the other modes were insufficient <br> For justice, were it not the Son of God <br> Himself had humbled to become incarnate. <br> Now, to fill fully each desire of thine, <br> Return I to elucidate one place, <br> In order that thou there mayst see as I do. <br> Thou sayst: ‘I see the air, I see the fire, <br> The water, and the earth, and all their mixtures <br> Come to corruption, and short while endure; <br> And these things notwithstanding were created’; <br> Therefore if that which I have said were true, <br> They should have been secure against corruption. <br> The Angels, brother, and the land sincere<sup>1333</sup> <br> In which thou art, created may be called <br> Just as they are in their entire existence; <br> But all the elements which thou hast named, <br> And all those things which out of them are made, <br> By a created virtue are informed. <br> Created was the matter which they have; <br> Created was the informing influence <br> Within these stars that round about them go. <br> The soul of every brute and of the plants <br> By its potential temperament attracts <br> The ray and motion of the holy lights; <br> But your own life immediately inspires<sup>1334</sup> <br> Supreme Beneficence, and enamours it <br> So with herself, it evermore desires her. <br> And thou from this mayst argue furthermore <br> Your resurrection, if thou think again <br> How human flesh was fashioned at that time <br> When the first parents both of them were made.”</p>
