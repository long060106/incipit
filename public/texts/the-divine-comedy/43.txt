<p class="lede">The Valley of the Princes.</p>
<p class="verse">After the gracious and glad salutations <br> Had three and four times been reiterated, <br> Sordello backward drew and said, “Who are you?” <br> “Or ever to this mountain were directed <br> The souls deserving to ascend to God, <br> My bones were buried by Octavian.<sup>627</sup> <br> I am Virgilius; and for no crime else <br> Did I lose heaven, than for not having faith”; <br> In this wise then my Leader made reply. <br> As one who suddenly before him sees <br> Something whereat he marvels, who believes <br> And yet does not, saying, “It is! it is not!” <br> So he appeared; and then bowed down his brow, <br> And with humility returned towards him, <br> And, where inferiors embrace, embraced him. <br> “O glory of the Latians, thou,” he said, <br> “Through whom our language showed what it could do, <br> O pride eternal of the place I came from, <br> What merit or what grace to me reveals thee? <br> If I to hear thy words be worthy, tell me <br> If thou dost come from Hell, and from what cloister.” <br> “Through all the circles of the doleful realm,” <br> Responded he, “have I come hitherward; <br> Heaven’s power impelled me, and with that I come. <br> I by not doing, not by doing, lost <br> The sight of that high sun which thou desirest, <br> And which too late by me was recognized. <br> A place there is below not sad with torments,<sup>628</sup> <br> But darkness only, where the lamentations <br> Have not the sound of wailing, but are sighs. <br> There dwell I with the little innocents <br> Snatched by the teeth of Death, or ever they <br> Were from our human sinfulness exempt. <br> There dwell I among those who the three saintly<sup>629</sup> <br> Virtues did not put on, and without vice <br> The others knew and followed all of them.<sup>630</sup> <br> But if thou know and can, some indication <br> Give us by which we may the sooner come <br> Where Purgatory has its right beginning.” <br> He answered: “No fixed place has been assigned us; <br> ’Tis lawful for me to go up and round; <br> So far as I can go, as guide I join thee. <br> But see already how the day declines, <br> And to go up by night we are not able;<sup>631</sup> <br> Therefore ’tis well to think of some fair sojourn. <br> Souls are there on the right hand here withdrawn; <br> If thou permit me I will lead thee to them, <br> And thou shalt know them not without delight.” <br> “How is this?” was the answer; “should one wish <br> To mount by night would he prevented be <br> By others? or mayhap would not have power?” <br> And on the ground the good Sordello drew <br> His finger, saying, “See, this line alone <br> Thou couldst not pass after the sun is gone; <br> Not that aught else would hindrance give, however, <br> To going up, save the nocturnal darkness; <br> This with the want of power the will perplexes. <br> We might indeed therewith return below, <br> And, wandering, walk the hill-side round about, <br> While the horizon holds the day imprisoned.” <br> Thereon my Lord, as if in wonder, said: <br> “Do thou conduct us thither, where thou sayest <br> That we can take delight in tarrying.” <br> Little had we withdrawn us from that place, <br> When I perceived the mount was hollowed out <br> In fashion as the valleys here are hollowed. <br> “Thitherward,” said that shade, “will we repair, <br> Where of itself the hill-side makes a lap, <br> And there for the new day will we await.” <br> ’Twixt hill and plain there was a winding path<sup>632</sup> <br> Which led us to the margin of that dell, <br> Where dies the border more than half away. <br> Gold and fine silver, and scarlet and pearl-white,<sup>633</sup> <br> The Indian wood resplendent and serene, <br> Fresh emerald the moment it is broken, <br> By herbage and by flowers within that hollow <br> Planted, each one in color would be vanquished, <br> As by its greater vanquished is the less. <br> Nor in that place had nature painted only, <br> But of the sweetness of a thousand odors <br> Made there a mingled fragrance and unknown. <br> “<i>Salve Regina</i>,” on the green and flowers<sup>634</sup> <br> There seated, singing, spirits I beheld, <br> Which were not visible outside the valley. <br> “Before the scanty sun now seeks his nest,” <br> Began the Mantuan who had led us thither, <br> “Among them do not wish me to conduct you. <br> Better from off this ledge the acts and faces <br> Of all of them will you discriminate, <br> Than in the plain below received among them. <br> He who sits highest, and the semblance bears <br> Of having what he should have done neglected, <br> And to the others’ song moves not his lips, <br> Rudolph the Emperor was, who had the power<sup>635</sup> <br> To heal the wounds that Italy have slain, <br> So that through others slowly she revives. <br> The other, who in look doth comfort him, <br> Governed the region where the water springs, <br> The Moldau bears the Elbe, and Elbe the sea. <br> His name was Ottocar; and in swaddling-clothes<sup>636</sup> <br> Far better he than bearded Winceslaus<sup>637</sup> <br> His son, who feeds in luxury and ease. <br> And the small-nosed, who close in council seems<sup>638</sup> <br> With him that has an aspect so benign,<sup>639</sup> <br> Died fleeing and disflowering the lily; <br> Look there, how he is beating at his breast! <br> Behold the other one, who for his cheek <br> Sighing has made of his own palm a bed; <br> Father and father-in-law of France’s Pest<sup>640</sup> <br> Are they, and know his vicious life and lewd, <br> And hence proceeds the grief that so doth pierce them. <br> He who appears so stalwart, and chimes in,<sup>641</sup> <br> Singing, with that one of the manly nose,<sup>642</sup> <br> The cord of every valor wore begirt; <br> And if as King had after him remained <br> The stripling who in rear of him is sitting,<sup>643</sup> <br> Well had the valor passed from vase to vase, <br> Which cannot of the other heirs be said. <br> Frederick and Jacomo possess the realms, <br> But none the better heritage possesses. <br> Not oftentimes upriseth through the branches<sup>644</sup> <br> The probity of man; and this He wills <br> Who gives it, so that we may ask of Him. <br> Eke to the large-nosed reach my words, no less<sup>645</sup> <br> Than to the other, Pier, who with him sings; <br> Whence Provence and Apulia grieve already<sup>646</sup> <br> The plant is as inferior to its seed, <br> As more than Beatrice and Margaret<sup>647</sup> <br> Costanza boasteth of her husband still.<sup>648</sup> <br> Behold the monarch of the simple life, <br> Harry of England, sitting there alone;<sup>649</sup> <br> He in his branches has a better issue. <br> He who the lowest on the ground among them <br> Sits looking upward, is the Marquis William,<sup>650</sup> <br> For whose sake Alessandria and her war<sup>651</sup> <br> Make Monferrat and Canavese weep.”</p>
