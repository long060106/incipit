<p class="lede">The Fourth Heaven, or that of the Sun, where are seen the spirits of theologians and fathers of the Church⁠—St. Thomas Aquinas.</p>
<p class="verse">Looking into his Son with all the Love<sup>1417</sup> <br> Which each of them eternally breathes forth,<sup>1418</sup> <br> The Primal and unutterable Power <br> Whate’er before the mind or eye revolves <br> With so much order made, there can be none <br> Who this beholds without enjoying Him. <br> Lift up then, Reader, to the lofty wheels <br> With me thy vision straight unto that part <br> Where the one motion on the other strikes,<sup>1419</sup> <br> And there begin to contemplate with joy <br> That Master’s art, who in himself so loves it <br> That never doth his eye depart therefrom. <br> Behold how from that point goes branching off <br> The oblique circle, which conveys the planets,<sup>1420</sup> <br> To satisfy the world that calls upon them; <br> And if their pathway were not thus inflected,<sup>1421</sup> <br> Much virtue in the heavens would be in vain, <br> And almost every power below here dead. <br> If from the straight line distant more or less <br> Were the departure, much would wanting be <br> Above and underneath of mundane order. <br> Remain now, Reader, still upon thy bench, <br> In thought pursuing that which is foretasted, <br> If thou wouldst jocund be instead of weary. <br> I’ve set before thee; henceforth feed thyself, <br> For to itself diverteth all my care <br> That theme whereof I have been made the scribe. <br> The greatest of the ministers of nature,<sup>1422</sup> <br> Who with the power of heaven the world imprints <br> And measures with his light the time for us, <br> With that part which above is called to mind<sup>1423</sup> <br> Conjoined, along the spirals was revolving,<sup>1424</sup> <br> Where each time earlier he presents himself; <br> And I was with him; but of the ascending <br> I was not conscious, saving as a man <br> Of a first thought is conscious ere it come; <br> And Beatrice, she who is seen to pass <br> From good to better, and so suddenly <br> That not by time her action is expressed, <br> How lucent in herself must she have been! <br> And what was in the sun, wherein I entered, <br> Apparent not by color but by light, <br> I, though I call on genius, art, and practice, <br> Cannot so tell that it could be imagined; <br> Believe one can, and let him long to see it. <br> And if our fantasies too lowly are <br> For altitude so great, it is no marvel, <br> Since o’er the sun was never eye could go.<sup>1425</sup> <br> Such in this place was the fourth family <br> Of the high Father, who forever sates it, <br> Showing how he breathes forth and how begets.<sup>1426</sup> <br> And Beatrice began: “Give thanks, give thanks <br> Unto the Sun of Angels, who to this <br> Sensible one has raised thee by his grace!” <br> Never was heart of mortal so disposed <br> To worship, nor to give itself to God <br> With all its gratitude was it so ready, <br> As at those words did I myself become; <br> And all my love was so absorbed in Him, <br> That in oblivion Beatrice was eclipsed. <br> Nor this displeased her; but she smiled at it <br> So that the splendor of her laughing eyes <br> My single mind on many things divided. <br> Lights many saw I, vivid and triumphant, <br> Make us a centre and themselves a circle, <br> More sweet in voice than luminous in aspect. <br> Thus girt about the daughter of Latona<sup>1427</sup> <br> We sometimes see, when pregnant is the air, <br> So that it holds the thread which makes her zone. <br> Within the court of Heaven, whence I return, <br> Are many jewels found, so fair and precious <br> They cannot be transported from the realm; <br> And of them was the singing of those lights. <br> Who takes not wings that he may fly up thither, <br> The tidings thence may from the dumb await! <br> As soon as singing thus those burning suns <br> Had round about us whirled themselves three times, <br> Like unto stars neighboring the steadfast poles, <br> Ladies they seemed, not from the dance released, <br> But who stop short, in silence listening <br> Till they have gathered the new melody. <br> And within one I heard beginning: “When<sup>1428</sup> <br> The radiance of grace, by which is kindled <br> True love, and which thereafter grows by loving, <br> Within thee multiplied is so resplendent <br> That it conducts thee upward by that stair, <br> Where without reascending none descends,<sup>1429</sup> <br> Who should deny the wine out of his vial <br> Unto thy thirst, in liberty were not<sup>1430</sup> <br> Except as water which descends not seaward. <br> Fain wouldst thou know with what plants is enflowered <br> This garland that encircles with delight <br> The Lady fair who makes thee strong for heaven. <br> Of the lambs was I of the holy flock <br> Which Dominic conducteth by a road <br> Where well one fattens if he strayeth not. <br> He who is nearest to me on the right <br> My brother and master was; and he Albertus<sup>1431</sup> <br> Is of Cologne, I Thomas of Aquinum.<sup>1432</sup> <br> If thou of all the others wouldst be certain, <br> Follow behind my speaking with thy sight <br> Upward along the blessed garland turning. <br> That next effulgence issues from the smile <br> Of Gratian, who assisted both the courts<sup>1433</sup> <br> In such wise that it pleased in Paradise. <br> The other which near by adorns our choir <br> That Peter was who, e’en as the poor widow,<sup>1434</sup> <br> Offered his treasure unto Holy Church. <br> The fifth light, that among us is the fairest,<sup>1435</sup> <br> Breathes forth from such a love, that all the world <br> Below is greedy to learn tidings of it.<sup>1436</sup> <br> Within it is the lofty mind, where knowledge <br> So deep was put, that, if the true be true, <br> To see so much there never rose a second. <br> Thou seest next the lustre of that taper,<sup>1437</sup> <br> Which in the flesh below looked most within <br> The angelic nature and its ministry. <br> Within that other little light is smiling <br> The advocate of the Christian centuries,<sup>1438</sup> <br> Out of whose rhetoric Augustine was furnished. <br> Now if thou trainest thy mind’s eye along <br> From light to light pursuant of my praise, <br> With thirst already of the eighth thou waitest. <br> By seeing every good therein exults <br> The sainted soul, which the fallacious world<sup>1439</sup> <br> Makes manifest to him who listeneth well; <br> The body whence ’twas hunted forth is lying <br> Down in Cieldauro, and from martyrdom<sup>1440</sup> <br> And banishment it came unto this peace. <br> See farther onward flame the burning breath <br> Of Isidore, of Beda, and of Richard<sup>1441</sup> <br> Who was in contemplation more than man. <br> This, whence to me returneth thy regard, <br> The light is of a spirit unto whom <br> In his grave meditations death seemed slow.<sup>1442</sup> <br> It is the light eternal of Sigier,<sup>1443</sup> <br> Who, reading lectures in the Street of Straw,<sup>1444</sup> <br> Did syllogize invidious verities.”<sup>1445</sup> <br> Then, as a horologe that calleth us <br> What time the Bride of God is rising up <br> With matins to her Spouse that he may love her, <br> Wherein one part the other draws and urges, <br> Ting! ting! resounding with so sweet a note, <br> That swells with love the spirit well disposed, <br> Thus I beheld the glorious wheel move round, <br> And render voice to voice, in modulation <br> And sweetness that can not be comprehended,<sup>1446</sup> <br> Excepting there where joy is made eternal.</p>
