<p class="lede">The Sixth Circle⁠—The Gluttonous⁠—The Mystic Tree.</p>
<p class="verse">Already was the Angel left behind us,<sup>957</sup> <br> The Angel who to the sixth round had turned us, <br> Having erased one mark from off my face; <br> And those who have in justice their desire <br> Had said to us, “<i>Beati</i>,” in their voices,<sup>958</sup> <br> With “<i>sitio</i>,” and without more ended it. <br> And I, more light than through the other passes, <br> Went onward so, that without any labor <br> I followed upward the swift-footed spirits; <br> When thus Virgilius began: “The love <br> Kindled by virtue aye another kindles, <br> Provided outwardly its flame appear. <br> Hence from the hour that Juvenal descended<sup>959</sup> <br> Among us into the infernal Limbo, <br> Who made apparent to me thy affection, <br> My kindliness towards thee was as great <br> As ever bound one to an unseen person, <br> So that these stairs will now seem short to me. <br> But tell me, and forgive me as a friend, <br> If too great confidence let loose the rein, <br> And as a friend now hold discourse with me; <br> How was it possible within thy breast <br> For avarice to find place, ’mid so much wisdom <br> As thou wast filled with by thy diligence?” <br> These words excited Statius at first <br> Somewhat to laughter; afterward he answered: <br> “Each word of thine is love’s dear sign to me. <br> Verily oftentimes do things appear <br> Which give fallacious matter to our doubts, <br> Instead of the true causes which are hidden! <br> Thy question shows me thy belief to be <br> That I was niggard in the other life, <br> It may be from the circle where I was; <br> Therefore know thou, that avarice was removed <br> Too far from me; and this extravagance <br> Thousands of lunar periods have punished. <br> And were it not that I my thoughts uplifted, <br> When I the passage heard where thou exclaimest, <br> As if indignant, unto human nature, <br> ‘To what impellest thou not, O cursed hunger<sup>960</sup> <br> Of gold, the appetite of mortal men?’ <br> Revolving I should feel the dismal joustings.<sup>961</sup> <br> Then I perceived the hands could spread too wide <br> Their wings in spending, and repented me <br> As well of that as of my other sins; <br> How many with shorn hair shall rise again<sup>962</sup> <br> Because of ignorance, which from this sin <br> Cuts off repentance living and in death! <br> And know that the transgression which rebuts <br> By direct opposition any sin <br> Together with it here its verdure dries. <br> Therefore if I have been among that folk <br> Which mourns its avarice, to purify me, <br> For its opposite has this befallen me.” <br> “Now when thou sangest the relentless weapons <br> Of the twofold affliction of Jocasta,”<sup>963</sup> <br> The singer of the Songs Bucolic said, <br> “From that which Clio there with thee preludes,<sup>964</sup> <br> It does not seem that yet had made thee faithful <br> That faith without which no good works suffice. <br> If this be so, what candles or what sun <br> Scattered thy darkness so that thou didst trim <br> Thy sails behind the Fisherman thereafter?”<sup>965</sup> <br> And he to him: “Thou first directedst me <br> Towards Parnassus, in its grots to drink, <br> And first concerning God didst me enlighten. <br> Thou didst as he who walketh in the night, <br> Who bears his light behind, which helps him not, <br> But maketh wise the persons after him, <br> When thou didst say: ‘The age renews itself,<sup>966</sup> <br> Justice returns, and man’s primeval time, <br> And a new progeny descends from heaven.’ <br> Through thee I Poet was, through thee a Christian; <br> But that thou better see what I design, <br> To color it will I extend my hand. <br> Already was the world in every part <br> Pregnant with the true creed, disseminated <br> By messengers of the eternal kingdom; <br> And thy assertion, spoken of above, <br> With the new preachers was in unison; <br> Whence I to visit them the custom took. <br> Then they became so holy in my sight, <br> That, when Domitian persecuted them, <br> Not without tears of mine were their laments; <br> And all the while that I on earth remained, <br> Them I befriended, and their upright customs <br> Made me disparage all the other sects. <br> And ere I led the Greeks unto the rivers <br> Of Thebes, in poetry, I was baptized, <br> But out of fear was covertly a Christian, <br> For a long time professing paganism; <br> And this lukewarmness caused me the fourth circle<sup>967</sup> <br> To circuit round more than four centuries. <br> Thou, therefore, who hast raised the covering <br> That hid from me whatever good I speak of, <br> While in ascending we have time to spare, <br> Tell me, in what place is our friend Terentius,<sup>968</sup> <br> Caecilius, Plautus, Varro, if thou knowest;<sup>969</sup> <br> Tell me if they are damned, and in what alley.” <br> “These, Persius and myself, and others many,”<sup>970</sup> <br> Replied my Leader, “with that Grecian are<sup>971</sup> <br> Whom more than all the rest the Muses suckled, <br> In the first circle of the prison blind; <br> Ofttimes we of the mountain hold discourse <br> Which has our nurses ever with itself. <br> Euripides is with us, Antiphon,<sup>972</sup> <br> Simonides, Agatho, and many other<sup>973</sup> <br> Greeks who of old their brows with laurel decked. <br> There some of thine own people may be seen, <br> Antigone, Deiphile and Argìa,<sup>974</sup> <br> And there Ismene mournful as of old. <br> There she is seen who pointed out Langìa;<sup>975</sup> <br> There is Tiresias’ daughter, and there Thetis,<sup>976</sup> <br> And there Deidamia with her sisters.”<sup>977</sup> <br> Silent already were the poets both, <br> Attent once more in looking round about, <br> From the ascent and from the walls released; <br> And four handmaidens of the day already<sup>978</sup> <br> Were left behind, and at the pole the fifth <br> Was pointing upward still its burning horn, <br> What time my Guide: “I think that tow’rds the edge <br> Our dexter shoulders it behoves us turn, <br> Circling the mount as we are wont to do.” <br> Thus in that region custom was our ensign; <br> And we resumed our way with less suspicion <br> For the assenting of that worthy soul <br> They in advance went on, and I alone <br> Behind them, and I listened to their speech, <br> Which gave me lessons in the art of song. <br> But soon their sweet discourses interrupted <br> A tree which midway in the road we found,<sup>979</sup> <br> With apples sweet and grateful to the smell. <br> And even as a fir-tree tapers upward <br> From bough to bough, so downwardly did that; <br> I think in order that no one might climb it. <br> On that side where our pathway was enclosed <br> Fell from the lofty rock a limpid water, <br> And spread itself abroad upon the leaves. <br> The Poets twain unto the tree drew near, <br> And from among the foliage a voice <br> Cried: “Of this food ye shall have scarcity.” <br> Then said: “More thoughtful Mary was of making<sup>980</sup> <br> The marriage feast complete and honorable, <br> Than of her mouth which now for you responds; <br> And for their drink the ancient Roman women <br> With water were content; and Daniel<sup>981</sup> <br> Disparaged food, and understanding won. <br> The primal age was beautiful as gold;<sup>982</sup> <br> Acorns it made with hunger savorous, <br> And nectar every rivulet with thirst. <br> Honey and locusts were the aliments <br> That fed the Baptist in the wilderness; <br> Whence he is glorious, and so magnified <br> As by the Evangel is revealed to you.”</p>
