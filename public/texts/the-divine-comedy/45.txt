<p class="lede">Dante’s dream of the eagle⁠—The gate of Purgatory.</p>
<p class="verse">The concubine of old Tithonus now<sup>675</sup> <br> Gleamed white upon the eastern balcony,<sup>676</sup> <br> Forth from the arms of her sweet paramour; <br> With gems her forehead all relucent was, <br> Set in the shape of that cold animal<sup>677</sup> <br> Which with its tail doth smite amain the nations, <br> And of the steps, with which she mounts, the Night <br> Had taken two in that place where we were,<sup>678</sup> <br> And now the third was bending down its wings; <br> When I, who something had of Adam in me,<sup>679</sup> <br> Vanquished by sleep, upon the grass reclined, <br> There were all five of us already sat.<sup>680</sup> <br> Just at the hour when her sad lay begins <br> The little swallow, near unto the morning,<sup>681</sup> <br> Perchance in memory of her former woes, <br> And when the mind of man, a wanderer <br> More from the flesh, and less by thought imprisoned, <br> Almost prophetic in its visions is,<sup>682</sup> <br> In dreams it seemed to me I saw suspended <br> An eagle in the sky, with plumes of gold, <br> With wings wide open, and intent to stoop, <br> And this, it seemed to me, was where had been<sup>683</sup> <br> By Ganymede his kith and kin abandoned, <br> When to the high consistory he was rapt. <br> I thought within myself, perchance he strikes <br> From habit only here, and from elsewhere <br> Disdains to bear up any in his feet. <br> Then wheeling somewhat more, it seemed to me, <br> Terrible as the lightning he descended, <br> And snatched me upward even to the fire.<sup>684</sup> <br> Therein it seemed that he and I were burning, <br> And the imagined fire did scorch me so, <br> That of necessity my sleep was broken. <br> Not otherwise Achilles started up, <br> Around him turning his awakened eyes, <br> And knowing not the place in which he was, <br> What time from Chiron stealthily his mother<sup>685</sup> <br> Carried him sleeping in her arms to Scyros, <br> Wherefrom the Greeks withdrew him afterwards, <br> Than I upstarted, when from off my face <br> Sleep fled away; and pallid I became, <br> As doth the man who freezes with affright. <br> Only my Comforter was at my side, <br> And now the sun was more than two hours high, <br> And turned towards the sea-shore was my face. <br> “Be not intimidated,” said my Lord, <br> “Be reassured, for all is well with us; <br> Do not restrain, but put forth all thy strength. <br> Thou hast at length arrived at Purgatory; <br> See there the cliff that closes it around; <br> See there the entrance, where it seems disjoined. <br> Whilom at dawn, which doth precede the day, <br> When inwardly thy spirit was asleep<sup>686</sup> <br> Upon the flowers that deck the land below, <br> There came a Lady and said: ‘I am Lucìa;<sup>687</sup> <br> Let me take this one up, who is asleep; <br> So will I make his journey easier for him.’ <br> Sordello and the other noble shapes<sup>688</sup> <br> Remained; she took thee, and, as day grew bright, <br> Upward she came, and I upon her footsteps. <br> She laid thee here; and first her beauteous eyes <br> That open entrance pointed out to me; <br> Then she and sleep together went away.”<sup>689</sup> <br> In guise of one whose doubts are reassured, <br> And who to confidence his fear doth change, <br> After the truth has been discovered to him, <br> So did I change; and when without disquiet <br> My Leader saw me, up along the cliff <br> He moved, and I behind him, tow’rd the height. <br> Reader, thou seest well how I exalt <br> My theme, and therefore if with greater art <br> I fortify it, marvel not thereat. <br> Nearer approached we, and were in such place, <br> That there, where first appeared to me a rift <br> Like to a crevice that disparts a wall, <br> I saw a portal, and three stairs beneath, <br> Diverse in color, to go up to it, <br> And a gate-keeper, who yet spake no word. <br> And as I opened more and more mine eyes, <br> I saw him seated on the highest stair, <br> Such in the face that I endured it not. <br> And in his hand he had a naked sword, <br> Which so reflected back the sunbeams tow’rds us, <br> That oft in vain I lifted up mine eyes. <br> “Tell it from where you are, what is’t you wish?” <br> Began he to exclaim; “where is the escort? <br> Take heed your coming hither harm you not!” <br> “A Lady of Heaven, with these things conversant,” <br> My Master answered him, “but even now <br> Said to us, ‘Thither go; there is the portal.’ ” <br> “And may she speed your footsteps in all good,” <br> Again began the courteous janitor; <br> “Come forward then unto these stairs of ours.” <br> Thither did we approach; and the first stair<sup>690</sup> <br> Was marble white, so polished and so smooth, <br> I mirrored myself therein as I appear. <br> The second, tinct of deeper hue than perse,<sup>691</sup> <br> Was of a calcined and uneven stone, <br> Cracked all asunder lengthwise and across. <br> The third, that uppermost rests massively, <br> Porphyry seemed to me, as flaming red <br> As blood that from a vein is spirting forth. <br> Both of his feet was holding upon this <br> The Angel of God, upon the threshold seated, <br> Which seemed to me a stone of diamond.<sup>692</sup> <br> Along the three stairs upward with good will <br> Did my Conductor draw me, saying: “Ask <br> Humbly that he the fastening may undo.” <br> Devoutly at the holy feet I cast me, <br> For mercy’s sake besought that he would open, <br> But first upon my breast three times I smote. <br> Seven P’s upon my forehead he described<sup>693</sup> <br> With the sword’s point, and, “Take heed that thou wash <br> These wounds, when thou shalt be within,” he said. <br> Ashes, or earth that dry is excavated, <br> Of the same color were with his attire, <br> And from beneath it he drew forth two keys. <br> One was of gold, and the other was of silver;<sup>694</sup> <br> First with the white, and after with the yellow, <br> Plied he the door, so that I was content. <br> “Whenever faileth either of these keys <br> So that it turn not rightly in the lock,” <br> He said to us, “this entrance doth not open. <br> More precious one is, but the other needs <br> More art and intellect ere it unlock, <br> For it is that which doth the knot unloose. <br> From Peter I have them; and he bade me err <br> Rather in opening than in keeping shut, <br> If people but fall down before my feet.” <br> Then pushed the portals of the sacred door, <br> Exclaiming: “Enter; but I give you warning <br> That forth returns whoever looks behind.”<sup>695</sup> <br> And when upon their hinges were turned round <br> The swivels of that consecrated gate, <br> Which are of metal, massive and sonorous, <br> Roared not so loud, nor so discordant seemed<sup>696</sup> <br> Tarpeia, when was ta’en from it the good <br> Metellus, wherefore meagre it remained.<sup>697</sup> <br> At the first thunder-peal I turned attentive, <br> And “<i>Te Deum laudamus</i>” seemed to hear<sup>698</sup> <br> In voices mingled with sweet melody. <br> Exactly such an image rendered me <br> That which I heard, as we are wont to catch, <br> When people singing with the organ stand;<sup>699</sup> <br> For now we hear, and now hear not, the words.</p>
