<p class="lede">The Third Bolgia: the simoniacs⁠—Pope Nicholas III.</p>
<p class="verse">O Simon Magus, O forlorn disciples,<sup>264</sup> <br> Ye who the things of God, which ought to be <br> The brides of holiness, rapaciously <br> For silver and for gold do prostitute, <br> Now it behoves for you the trumpet sound,<sup>265</sup> <br> Because in this third Bolgia ye abide. <br> We had already on the following tomb <br> Ascended to that portion of the crag <br> Which o’er the middle of the moat hangs plumb. <br> Wisdom supreme, O how great art thou showest <br> In heaven, in earth, and in the evil world, <br> And with what justice doth thy power distribute! <br> I saw upon the sides and on the bottom <br> The livid stone with perforations filled, <br> All of one size, and everyone was round. <br> To me less ample seemed they not, nor greater <br> Than those that in my beautiful Saint John <br> Are fashioned for the place of the baptizers, <br> And one of which, not many years ago,<sup>266</sup> <br> I broke for someone, who was drowning in it;<sup>267</sup> <br> Be this a seal all men to undeceive. <br> Out of the mouth of each one there protruded <br> The feet of a transgressor, and the legs <br> Up to the calf, the rest within remained. <br> In all of them the soles were both on fire; <br> Wherefore the joints so violently quivered, <br> They would have snapped asunder withes and bands. <br> Even as the flame of unctuous things is wont <br> To move upon the outer surface only, <br> So likewise was it there from heel to point. <br> “Master, who is that one who writhes himself, <br> More than his other comrades quivering,” <br> I said, “and whom a redder flame is sucking?”<sup>268</sup> <br> And he to me: “If thou wilt have me bear thee <br> Down there along that bank which lowest lies, <br> From him thou ’lt know his errors and himself.” <br> And I: “What pleases thee, to me is pleasing; <br> Thou art my Lord, and knowest that I depart not <br> From thy desire, and knowest what is not spoken.” <br> Straightway upon the fourth dike we arrived; <br> We turned, and on the left-hand side descended <br> Down to the bottom full of holes and narrow. <br> And the good Master yet from off his haunch <br> Deposed me not, till to the hole he brought me <br> Of him who so lamented with his shanks. <br> “Whoe’er thou art, that standest upside down, <br> O doleful soul, implanted like a stake,” <br> To say began I, “if thou canst, speak out.” <br> I stood even as the friar who is confessing <br> The false assassin, who, when he is fixed,<sup>269</sup> <br> Recalls him, so that death may be delayed. <br> And he cried out: “Dost thou stand there already, <br> Dost thou stand there already, Boniface?<sup>270</sup> <br> By many years the record lied to me. <br> Art thou so early satiate with that wealth, <br> For which thou didst not fear to take by fraud <br> The beautiful Lady, and then work her woe?” <br> Such I became, as people are who stand, <br> Not comprehending what is answered them, <br> As if bemocked, and know not how to answer. <br> Then said Virgilius: “Say to him straightway, <br> I am not he, I am not he thou thinkest.” <br> And I replied as was imposed on me. <br> Whereat the spirit writhed with both his feet, <br> Then, sighing, with a voice of lamentation <br> Said to me: “Then what wantest thou of me? <br> If who I am thou carest so much to know, <br> That thou on that account hast crossed the bank, <br> Know that I vested was with the great mantle; <br> And truly was I son of the She-bear,<sup>271</sup> <br> So eager to advance the cubs, that wealth <br> Above, and here myself, I pocketed. <br> Beneath my head the others are dragged down <br> Who have preceded me in simony, <br> Flattened along the fissure of the rock. <br> Below there I shall likewise fall, whenever <br> That one shall come who I believed thou wast, <br> What time the sudden question I proposed. <br> But longer I my feet already toast, <br> And here have been in this way upside down, <br> Than he will planted stay with reddened feet; <br> For after him shall come of fouler deed <br> From tow’rds the west a Pastor without law,<sup>272</sup> <br> Such as befits to cover him and me. <br> New Jason will he be, of whom we read<sup>273</sup> <br> In Maccabees; and as his king was pliant, <br> So he who governs France shall be to this one.”<sup>274</sup> <br> I do not know if I were here too bold, <br> That him I answered only in this metre: <br> “I pray thee tell me now how great a treasure <br> Our Lord demanded of Saint Peter first, <br> Before he put the keys into his keeping? <br> Truly he nothing asked but ‘Follow me.’ <br> Nor Peter nor the rest asked of Matthias<sup>275</sup> <br> Silver or gold, when he by lot was chosen <br> Unto the place the guilty soul had lost. <br> Therefore stay here, for thou art justly punished, <br> And keep safe guard o’er the ill-gotten money, <br> Which caused thee to be valiant against Charles.<sup>276</sup> <br> And were it not that still forbids it me <br> The reverence for the keys superlative <br> Thou hadst in keeping in the gladsome life, <br> I would make use of words more grievous still; <br> Because your avarice afflicts the world, <br> Trampling the good and lifting the depraved. <br> The Evangelist you Pastors had in mind, <br> When she who sitteth upon many waters<sup>277</sup> <br> To fornicate with kings by him was seen; <br> The same who with the seven heads was born, <br> And power and strength from the ten horns received,<sup>278</sup> <br> So long as virtue to her spouse was pleasing. <br> Ye have made yourselves a god of gold and silver; <br> And from the idolater how differ ye, <br> Save that he one, and ye a hundred worship? <br> Ah, Constantine! of how much ill was mother, <br> Not thy conversion, but that marriage-dower <br> Which the first wealthy Father took from thee!”<sup>279</sup> <br> And while I sang to him such notes as these, <br> Either that anger or that conscience stung him, <br> He struggled violently with both his feet. <br> I think in sooth that it my Leader pleased, <br> With such contented lip he listened ever <br> Unto the sound of the true words expressed. <br> Therefore with both his arms he took me up, <br> And when he had me all upon his breast, <br> Remounted by the way where he descended. <br> Nor did he tire to have me clasped to him; <br> But bore me to the summit of the arch <br> Which from the fourth dike to the fifth is passage. <br> There tenderly he laid his burden down, <br> Tenderly on the crag uneven and steep, <br> That would have been hard passage for the goats: <br> Thence was unveiled to me another valley.</p>
