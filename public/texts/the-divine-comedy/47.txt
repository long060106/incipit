<p class="lede">Omberto di Santafiore⁠—Oderisi d’ Agobbio⁠—Provenzan Salvani.</p>
<p class="verse">“Our Father, thou who dwellest in the heavens, <br> Not circumscribed, but from the greater love <br> Thou bearest to the first effects on high,<sup>714</sup> <br> Praised be thy name and thine omnipotence <br> By every creature, as befitting is <br> To render thanks to thy sweet effluence.<sup>715</sup> <br> Come unto us the peace of thy dominion, <br> For unto it we cannot of ourselves, <br> If it come not, with all our intellect. <br> Even as thine own Angels of their will <br> Make sacrifice to thee, Hosanna singing, <br> So may all men make sacrifice of theirs. <br> Give unto us this day our daily manna, <br> Withouten which in this rough wilderness <br> Backward goes he who toils most to advance. <br> And even as we the trespass we have suffered <br> Pardon in one another, pardon thou <br> Benignly, and regard not our desert. <br> Our virtue, which is easily o’ercome, <br> Put not to proof with the old Adversary, <br> But thou from him who spurs it so, deliver. <br> This last petition verily, dear Lord, <br> Not for ourselves is made, who need it not, <br> But for their sake who have remained behind us.” <br> Thus for themselves and us good furtherance <br> Those shades imploring, went beneath a weight <br> Like unto that of which we sometimes dream, <br> Unequally in anguish round and round <br> And weary all, upon that foremost cornice, <br> Purging away the smoke-stains of the world. <br> If there good words are always said for us, <br> What may not here be said and done for them, <br> By those who have a good root to their will? <br> Well may we help them wash away the marks <br> That hence they carried, so that clean and light <br> They may ascend unto the starry wheels! <br> “Ah! so may pity and justice you disburden <br> Soon, that ye may have power to move the wing, <br> That shall uplift you after your desire, <br> Show us on which hand tow’rd the stairs the way <br> Is shortest, and if more than one the passes, <br> Point us out that which least abruptly falls; <br> For he who cometh with me, through the burden <br> Of Adam’s flesh wherewith he is invested, <br> Against his will is chary of his climbing.”<sup>716</sup> <br> The words of theirs which they returned to those <br> That he whom I was following had spoken, <br> It was not manifest from whom they came, <br> But it was said: “To the right hand come with us <br> Along the bank, and ye shall find a pass <br> Possible for living person to ascend. <br> And were I not impeded by the stone, <br> Which this proud neck of mine doth subjugate, <br> Whence I am forced to hold my visage down, <br> Him, who still lives and does not name himself, <br> Would I regard, to see if I may know him <br> And make him piteous unto this burden. <br> A Latian was I, and born of a great Tuscan;<sup>717</sup> <br> Guglielmo Aldobrandeschi was my father; <br> I know not if his name were ever with you. <br> The ancient blood and deeds of gallantry <br> Of my progenitors so arrogant made me <br> That, thinking not upon the common mother, <br> All men I held in scorn to such extent <br> I died therefor, as know the Sienese, <br> And every child in Campagnatico. <br> I am Omberto; and not to me alone <br> Has pride done harm, but all my kith and kin <br> Has with it dragged into adversity. <br> And here must I this burden bear for it <br> Till God be satisfied, since I did not <br> Among the living, here among the dead.” <br> Listening I downward bent my countenance; <br> And one of them, not this one who was speaking, <br> Twisted himself beneath the weight that cramps him, <br> And looked at me, and knew me, and called out, <br> Keeping his eyes laboriously fixed <br> On me, who all bowed down was going with them. <br> “O,” asked I him, “art thou not Oderisi,<sup>718</sup> <br> Agobbio’s honor, and honor of that art <br> Which is in Paris called illuminating?”<sup>719</sup> <br> “Brother,” said he, “more laughing are the leaves <br> Touched by the brush of Franco Bolognese;<sup>720</sup> <br> All his the honor now, and mine in part. <br> In sooth I had not been so courteous <br> While I was living, for the great desire <br> Of excellence, on which my heart was bent. <br> Here of such pride is paid the forfeiture; <br> And yet I should not be here, were it not <br> That, having power to sin, I turned to God. <br> O thou vain glory of the human powers, <br> How little green upon thy summit lingers, <br> If’t be not followed by an age of grossness! <br> In painting Cimabue thought that he<sup>721</sup> <br> Should hold the field, now Giotto has the cry,<sup>722</sup> <br> So that the other’s fame is growing dim. <br> So has one Guido from the other taken<sup>723</sup> <br> The glory of our tongue, and he perchance <br> Is born, who from the nest shall chase them both.<sup>724</sup> <br> Naught is this mundane rumor but a breath <br> Of wind, that comes now this way and now that, <br> And changes name, because it changes side. <br> What fame shalt thou have more, if old peel off<sup>725</sup> <br> From thee thy flesh, than if thou hadst been dead <br> Before thou left the <em>pappo</em> and the <em>dindi</em>,<sup>726</sup> <br> Ere pass a thousand years? which is a shorter <br> Space to the eterne, than twinkling of an eye <br> Unto the circle that in heaven wheels slowest.<sup>727</sup> <br> With him, who takes so little of the road<sup>728</sup> <br> In front of me, all Tuscany resounded; <br> And now he scarce is lisped of in Siena, <br> Where he was lord, what time was overthrown<sup>729</sup> <br> The Florentine delirium, that superb <br> Was at that day as now ’tis prostitute. <br> Your reputation is the color of grass <br> Which comes and goes, and that discolors it <br> By which it issues green from out the earth.” <br> And I: “Thy true speech fills my heart with good<sup>730</sup> <br> Humility, and great tumor thou assuagest; <br> But who is he, of whom just now thou spakest?” <br> “That,” he replied, “is Provenzan Salvani,<sup>731</sup> <br> And he is here because he had presumed <br> To bring Siena all into his hands. <br> He has gone thus, and goeth without rest <br> E’er since he died; such money renders back <br> In payment he who is on earth too daring.” <br> And I: “If every spirit who awaits <br> The verge of life before that he repent, <br> Remains below there and ascends not hither, <br> (Unless good orison shall him bestead,) <br> Until as much time as he lived be passed, <br> How was the coming granted him in largess?” <br> “When he in greatest splendor lived,” said he, <br> “Freely upon the Campo of Siena, <br> All shame being laid aside, he placed himself; <br> And there to draw his friend from the duress <br> Which in the prison-house of Charles he suffered, <br> He brought himself to tremble in each vein.<sup>732</sup> <br> I say no more, and know that I speak darkly; <br> Yet little time shall pass before thy neighbors <br> Will so demean themselves that thou canst gloss it.<sup>733</sup> <br> This action has released him from those confines.”</p>
