<p class="lede">The giants, Nimrod, Ephialtes, and Antaeus.</p>
<p class="verse">One and the self-same tongue first wounded me,<sup>463</sup> <br> So that it tinged the one cheek and the other, <br> And then held out to me the medicine; <br> Thus do I hear that once Achilles’ spear,<sup>464</sup> <br> His and his father’s, used to be the cause <br> First of a sad and then a gracious boon. <br> We turned our backs upon the wretched valley, <br> Upon the bank that girds it round about, <br> Going across it without any speech. <br> There it was less than night, and less than day, <br> So that my sight went little in advance; <br> But I could hear the blare of a loud horn, <br> So loud it would have made each thunder faint, <br> Which, counter to it following its way, <br> Mine eyes directed wholly to one place. <br> After the dolorous discomfiture<sup>465</sup> <br> When Charlemagne the holy emprise lost, <br> So terribly Orlando sounded not.<sup>466</sup> <br> Short while my head turned thitherward I held <br> When many lofty towers I seemed to see, <br> Whereat I: “Master, say, what town is this?” <br> And he to me: “Because thou peerest forth <br> Athwart the darkness at too great a distance, <br> It happens that thou errest in thy fancy. <br> Well shalt thou see, if thou arrivest there, <br> How much the sense deceives itself by distance; <br> Therefore a little faster spur thee on.” <br> Then tenderly he took me by the hand, <br> And said: “Before we farther have advanced, <br> That the reality may seem to thee <br> Less strange, know that these are not towers, but giants, <br> And they are in the well, around the bank, <br> From navel downward, one and all of them.” <br> As, when the fog is vanishing away, <br> Little by little doth the sight refigure <br> Whate’er the mist that crowds the air conceals, <br> So, piercing through the dense and darksome air, <br> More and more near approaching tow’rd the verge, <br> My error fled, and fear came over me; <br> Because as on its circular parapets <br> Montereggione crowns itself with towers,<sup>467</sup> <br> E’en thus the margin which surrounds the well <br> With one half of their bodies turreted <br> The horrible giants, whom Jove menaces <br> E’en now from out the heavens when he thunders. <br> And I of one already saw the face, <br> Shoulders, and breast, and great part of the belly, <br> And down along his sides both of the arms. <br> Certainly Nature, when she left the making <br> Of animals like these, did well indeed, <br> By taking such executors from Mars; <br> And if of elephants and whales she doth not <br> Repent her, whosoever looketh subtly <br> More just and more discreet will hold her for it; <br> For where the argument of intellect <br> Is added unto evil will and power, <br> No rampart can the people make against it. <br> His face appeared to me as long and large <br> As is at Rome the pine-cone of Saint Peter’s,<sup>468</sup> <br> And in proportion were the other bones; <br> So that the margin, which an apron was <br> Down from the middle, showed so much of him <br> Above it, that to reach up to his hair <br> Three Frieslanders in vain had vaunted them; <br> For I beheld thirty great palms of him <br> Down from the place where man his mantle buckles. <br> “Raphel mai amech izabi almi,”<sup>469</sup> <br> Began to clamor the ferocious mouth, <br> To which were not befitting sweeter psalms. <br> And unto him my Guide: “Soul idiotic, <br> Keep to thy horn, and vent thyself with that, <br> When wrath or other passion touches thee. <br> Search round thy neck, and thou wilt find the belt <br> Which keeps it fastened, O bewildered soul, <br> And see it, where it bars thy mighty breast.” <br> Then said to me: “He doth himself accuse; <br> This one is Nimrod, by whose evil thought<sup>470</sup> <br> One language in the world is not still used. <br> Here let us leave him and not speak in vain; <br> For even such to him is every language <br> As his to others, which to none is known.” <br> Therefore a longer journey did we make, <br> Turned to the left, and a crossbow-shot off <br> We found another far more fierce and large. <br> In binding him, who might the master be <br> I cannot say; but he had pinioned close <br> Behind the right arm, and in front the other, <br> With chains, that held him so begirt about <br> From the neck down, that on the part uncovered <br> It wound itself as far as the fifth gyre. <br> “This proud one wished to make experiment <br> Of his own power against the Supreme Jove,” <br> My Leader said, “whence he has such a guerdon. <br> Ephialtes is his name; he showed great prowess,<sup>471</sup> <br> What time the giants terrified the Gods; <br> The arms he wielded never more he moves.” <br> And I to him: “If possible, I should wish <br> That of the measureless Briareus<sup>472</sup> <br> These eyes of mine might have experience.” <br> Whence he replied: “Thou shalt behold Antaeus<sup>473</sup> <br> Close by here, who can speak and is unbound, <br> Who at the bottom of all crime shall place us. <br> Much farther yon is he whom thou wouldst see, <br> And he is bound, and fashioned like to this one, <br> Save that he seems in aspect more ferocious.” <br> There never was an earthquake of such might <br> That it could shake a tower so violently, <br> As Ephialtes suddenly shook himself. <br> Then was I more afraid of death than ever, <br> For nothing more was needful than the fear, <br> If I had not beheld the manacles. <br> Then we proceeded farther in advance, <br> And to Antaeus came, who, full five ells <br> Without the head, forth issued from the cavern. <br> “O thou, who in the valley fortunate,<sup>474</sup> <br> Which Scipio the heir of glory made, <br> When Hannibal turned back with all his hosts, <br> Once brought’st a thousand lions for thy prey, <br> And who, hadst thou been at the mighty war <br> Among thy brothers, some it seems still think <br> The sons of Earth the victory would have gained; <br> Place us below, nor be disdainful of it, <br> There where the cold doth lock Cocytus up. <br> Make us not go to Tityus nor Typhoeus;<sup>475</sup> <br> This one can give of that which here is longed for; <br> Therefore stoop down, and do not curl thy lip. <br> Still in the world can he restore thy fame; <br> Because he lives, and still expects long life, <br> If to itself Grace call him not untimely.” <br> So said the Master; and in haste the other <br> His hands extended and took up my Guide⁠— <br> Hands whose great pressure Hercules once felt.<sup>476</sup> <br> Virgilius, when he felt himself embraced, <br> Said unto me: “Draw nigh, that I may take thee”; <br> Then of himself and me one bundle made. <br> As seems the Carisenda, to behold<sup>477</sup> <br> Beneath the leaning side, when goes a cloud <br> Above it so that opposite it hangs; <br> Such did Antaeus seem to me, who stood <br> Watching to see him stoop, and then it was <br> I could have wished to go some other way. <br> But lightly in the abyss, which swallows up <br> Judas with Lucifer, he put us down; <br> Nor thus bowed downward made he there delay, <br> But, as a mast does in a ship, uprose.</p>
