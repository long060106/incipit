<p class="lede">The Sixth Heaven, or that of Jupiter, where are seen the spirits of righteous kings and rulers⁠—The celestial eagle.</p>
<p class="verse">Now was alone rejoicing in its word<sup>1683</sup> <br> That soul beatified, and I was tasting<sup>1684</sup> <br> My own, the bitter tempering with the sweet, <br> And the Lady who to God was leading me <br> Said: “Change thy thought; consider that I am <br> Near unto Him who every wrong disburdens.” <br> Unto the loving accents of my comfort <br> I turned me round, and then what love I saw <br> Within those holy eyes I here relinquish;<sup>1685</sup> <br> Not only that my language I distrust, <br> But that my mind cannot return so far<sup>1686</sup> <br> Above itself, unless another guide it. <br> Thus much upon that point can I repeat, <br> That, her again beholding, my affection <br> From every other longing was released. <br> While the eternal pleasure, which direct <br> Rayed upon Beatrice, from her fair face <br> Contented me with its reflected aspect, <br> Conquering me with the radiance of a smile, <br> She said to me, “Turn thee about and listen; <br> Not in mine eyes alone is Paradise.” <br> Even as sometimes here do we behold <br> The affection in the look, if it be such <br> That all the soul is wrapt away by it, <br> So, by the flaming of the effulgence holy <br> To which I turned, I recognized therein <br> The wish of speaking to me somewhat farther. <br> And it began: “In this fifth resting-place <br> Upon the tree that liveth by its summit,<sup>1687</sup> <br> And aye bears fruit, and never loses leaf, <br> Are blessed spirits that below, ere yet <br> They came to Heaven, were of such great renown <br> That every Muse therewith would affluent be. <br> Therefore look thou upon the cross’s horns; <br> He whom I now shall name will there enact <br> What doth within a cloud its own swift fire.” <br> I saw athwart the Cross a splendor drawn <br> By naming Joshua, (even as he did it,)<sup>1688</sup> <br> Nor noted I the word before the deed; <br> And at the name of the great Maccabee<sup>1689</sup> <br> I saw another move itself revolving, <br> And gladness was the whip unto that top.<sup>1690</sup> <br> Likewise for Charlemagne and for Orlando,<sup>1691</sup> <br> Two of them my regard attentive followed <br> As followeth the eye its falcon flying. <br> William thereafterward, and Renouard,<sup>1692</sup> <br> And the Duke Godfrey, did attract my sight<sup>1693</sup> <br> Along upon that Cross, and Robert Guiscard.<sup>1694</sup> <br> Then, moved and mingled with the other lights, <br> The soul that had addressed me showed how great <br> An artist ’twas among the heavenly singers. <br> To my right side I turned myself around, <br> My duty to behold in Beatrice <br> Either by words or gesture signified; <br> And so translucent I beheld her eyes, <br> So full of pleasure, that her countenance <br> Surpassed its other and its latest wont. <br> And as, by feeling greater delectation, <br> A man in doing good from day to day <br> Becomes aware his virtue is increasing, <br> So I became aware that my gyration <br> With heaven together had increased its arc, <br> That miracle beholding more adorned.<sup>1695</sup> <br> And such as is the change, in little lapse <br> Of time, in a pale woman, when her face <br> Is from the load of bashfulness unladen, <br> Such was it in mine eyes, when I had turned,<sup>1696</sup> <br> Caused by the whiteness of the temperate star, <br> The sixth, which to itself had gathered me. <br> Within that Jovial torch did I behold <br> The sparkling of the love which was therein <br> Delineate our language to mine eyes. <br> And even as birds uprisen from the shore, <br> As in congratulation o’er their food, <br> Make squadrons of themselves, now round, now long,<sup>1697</sup> <br> So from within those lights the holy creatures <br> Sang flying to and fro, and in their figures <br> Made of themselves now <b>d</b>, now <b>i</b>, now <b>l</b>.<sup>1698</sup> <br> First singing they to their own music moved; <br> Then one becoming of these characters, <br> A little while they rested and were silent. <br> O divine Pegasea, thou who genius<sup>1699</sup> <br> Dost glorious make, and render it long-lived, <br> And this through thee the cities and the kingdoms, <br> Illume me with thyself, that I may bring <br> Their figures out as I have them conceived! <br> Apparent be thy power in these brief verses! <br> Themselves then they displayed in five times seven <br> Vowels and consonants; and I observed <br> The parts as they seemed spoken unto me. <br> <i>Diligite justitiam</i>, these were <br> First verb and noun of all that was depicted; <br> <i>Qui judicatis terram</i> were the last.<sup>1700</sup> <br> Thereafter in the <b>m</b> of the fifth word <br> Remained they so arranged, that Jupiter <br> Seemed to be silver there with gold inlaid. <br> And other lights I saw descend where was <br> The summit of the <b>m</b>, and pause there singing <br> The good, I think, that draws them to itself. <br> Then, as in striking upon burning logs<sup>1701</sup> <br> Upward there fly innumerable sparks, <br> Whence fools are wont to look for auguries, <br> More than a thousand lights seemed thence to rise,<sup>1702</sup> <br> And to ascend, some more, and others less, <br> Even as the Sun that lights them had allotted; <br> And, each one being quiet in its place, <br> The head and neck beheld I of an eagle<sup>1703</sup> <br> Delineated by that inlaid fire. <br> He who there paints has none to be his guide; <br> But Himself guides; and is from Him remembered <br> That virtue which is form unto the nest.<sup>1704</sup> <br> The other beatitude, that contented seemed<sup>1705</sup> <br> At first to bloom a lily on the <b>m</b>, <br> By a slight motion followed out the imprint. <br> O gentle star! what and how many gems <br> Did demonstrate to me, that all our justice <br> Effect is of that heaven which thou ingemmest! <br> Wherefore I pray the Mind, in which begin <br> Thy motion and thy virtue, to regard <br> Whence comes the smoke that vitiates thy rays; <br> So that a second time it now be wroth <br> With buying and with selling in the temple <br> Whose walls were built with signs and martyrdoms!<sup>1706</sup> <br> O soldiery of heaven, whom I contemplate, <br> Implore for those who are upon the earth <br> All gone astray after the bad example!<sup>1707</sup> <br> Once ’twas the custom to make war with swords; <br> But now ’tis made by taking here and there<sup>1708</sup> <br> The bread the pitying Father shuts from none. <br> Yet thou, who writest but to cancel, think<sup>1709</sup> <br> That Peter and that Paul, who for this vineyard <br> Which thou art spoiling died, are still alive! <br> Well canst thou say: “So steadfast my desire <br> Is unto him who willed to live alone, <br> And for a dance was led to martyrdom,<sup>1710</sup> <br> That I know not the Fisherman nor Paul.”</p>
