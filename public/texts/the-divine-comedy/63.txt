<p class="lede">Dante’s sleep upon the stairway, and his dream of Leah⁠—Arrival at the Terrestrial Paradise.</p>
<p class="verse">As when he vibrates forth his earliest rays,<sup>1054</sup> <br> In regions where his Maker shed his blood,<sup>1055</sup> <br> (The Ebro falling under lofty Libra, <br> And waters in the Ganges burnt with noon,) <br> So stood the Sun; hence was the day departing, <br> When the glad Angel of God appeared to us. <br> Outside the flame he stood upon the verge, <br> And chanted forth, “<i>Beati mundo corde</i>,”<sup>1056</sup> <br> In voice by far more living than our own. <br> Then: “No one farther goes, souls sanctified, <br> If first the fire bite not; within it enter, <br> And be not deaf unto the song beyond.” <br> When we were close beside him thus he said; <br> Wherefore e’en such became I, when I heard him, <br> As he is who is put into the grave. <br> Upon my clasped hands I straightened me,<sup>1057</sup> <br> Scanning the fire, and vividly recalling <br> The human bodies I had once seen burned. <br> Towards me turned themselves my good Conductors, <br> And unto me Virgilius said: “My son, <br> Here may indeed be torment, but not death. <br> Remember thee, remember! and if I <br> On Geryon have safely guided thee,<sup>1058</sup> <br> What shall I do now I am nearer God? <br> Believe for certain, shouldst thou stand a full <br> Millennium in the bosom of this flame, <br> It could not make thee bald a single hair. <br> And if perchance thou think that I deceive thee, <br> Draw near to it, and put it to the proof <br> With thine own hands upon thy garment’s hem. <br> Now lay aside, now lay aside all fear, <br> Turn hitherward, and onward come securely”; <br> And I still motionless, and ’gainst my conscience!<sup>1059</sup> <br> Seeing me stand still motionless and stubborn, <br> Somewhat disturbed he said: “Now look thou, Son, <br> ’Twixt Beatrice and thee there is this wall.” <br> As at the name of Thisbe oped his lids<sup>1060</sup> <br> The dying Pyramus, and gazed upon her, <br> What time the mulberry became vermilion, <br> Even thus, my obduracy being softened, <br> I turned to my wise Guide, hearing the name <br> That in my memory evermore is welling. <br> Whereat he wagged his head, and said: “How now? <br> Shall we stay on this side?” then smiled as one <br> Does at a child who’s vanquished by an apple. <br> Then into the fire in front of me he entered, <br> Beseeching Statius to come after me, <br> Who a long way before divided us.<sup>1061</sup> <br> When I was in it, into molten glass <br> I would have cast me to refresh myself, <br> So without measure was the burning there! <br> And my sweet Father, to encourage me, <br> Discoursing still of Beatrice went on, <br> Saying: “Her eyes I seem to see already!” <br> A voice, that on the other side was singing, <br> Directed us, and we, attent alone <br> On that, came forth where the ascent began. <br> “<i>Venite, benedicti Patris mei</i>,”<sup>1062</sup> <br> Sounded within a splendor, which was there <br> Such it o’ercame me, and I could not look. <br> “The sun departs,” it added, “and night cometh; <br> Tarry ye not, but onward urge your steps, <br> So long as yet the west becomes not dark.” <br> Straight forward through the rock the path ascended <br> In such a way that I cut off the rays <br> Before me of the sun, that now was low. <br> And of few stairs we yet had made assay, <br> Ere by the vanished shadow the sun’s setting <br> Behind us we perceived, I and my Sages. <br> And ere in all its parts immeasurable<sup>1063</sup> <br> The horizon of one aspect had become, <br> And Night her boundless dispensation held, <br> Each of us of a stair had made his bed; <br> Because the nature of the mount took from us <br> The power of climbing, more than the delight. <br> Even as in ruminating passive grow <br> The goats, who have been swift and venturesome <br> Upon the mountaintops ere they were fed, <br> Hushed in the shadow, while the sun is hot, <br> Watched by the herdsman, who upon his staff <br> Is leaning, and in leaning tendeth them; <br> And as the shepherd, lodging out of doors, <br> Passes the night beside his quiet flock, <br> Watching that no wild beast may scatter it, <br> Such at that hour were we, all three of us, <br> I like the goat, and like the herdsmen they, <br> Begirt on this side and on that by rocks. <br> Little could there be seen of things without; <br> But through that little I beheld the stars <br> More luminous and larger than their wont.<sup>1064</sup> <br> Thus ruminating, and beholding these, <br> Sleep seized upon me⁠—sleep, that oftentimes <br> Before a deed is done has tidings of it.<sup>1065</sup> <br> It was the hour, I think, when from the East <br> First on the mountain Citherea beamed,<sup>1066</sup> <br> Who with the fire of love seems always burning; <br> Youthful and beautiful in dreams methought <br> I saw a lady walking in a meadow, <br> Gathering flowers; and singing she was saying: <br> “Know whosoever may my name demand<sup>1067</sup> <br> That I am Leah, and go moving round <br> My beauteous hands to make myself a garland. <br> To please me at the mirror, here I deck me, <br> But never does my sister Rachel leave <br> Her looking-glass, and sitteth all day long. <br> To see her beauteous eyes as eager is she, <br> As I am to adorn me with my hands; <br> Her, seeing, and me, doing satisfies.” <br> And now before the antelucan splendors <br> That unto pilgrims the more grateful rise, <br> As, home-returning, less remote they lodge, <br> The darkness fled away on every side,<sup>1068</sup> <br> And slumber with it; whereupon I rose, <br> Seeing already the great Masters risen. <br> “That apple sweet, which through so many branches<sup>1069</sup> <br> The care of mortals goeth in pursuit of, <br> Today shall put in peace thy hungerings.” <br> Speaking to me, Virgilius of such words <br> As these made use; and never were there guerdons <br> That could in pleasantness compare with these. <br> Such longing upon longing came upon me <br> To be above, that at each step thereafter <br> For flight I felt in me the pinions growing. <br> When underneath us was the stairway all <br> Run o’er, and we were on the highest step, <br> Virgilius fastened upon me his eyes, <br> And said: “The temporal fire and the eternal, <br> Son, thou hast seen, and to a place art come <br> Where of myself no farther I discern. <br> By intellect and art I here have brought thee; <br> Take thine own pleasure for thy guide henceforth; <br> Beyond the steep ways and the narrow art thou. <br> Behold the sun, that shines upon thy forehead; <br> Behold the grass, the flowerets, and the shrubs <br> Which of itself alone this land produces. <br> Until rejoicing come the beauteous eyes <br> Which weeping caused me to come unto thee, <br> Thou canst sit down, and thou canst walk among them. <br> Expect no more or word or sign from me; <br> Free and upright and sound is thy free-will, <br> And error were it not to do its bidding; <br> Thee o’er thyself I therefore crown and mitre!”</p>
