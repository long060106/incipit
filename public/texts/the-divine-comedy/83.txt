<p class="lede">Of the wisdom of Solomon.</p>
<p class="verse">Let him imagine, who would well conceive<sup>1522</sup> <br> What now I saw, and let him while I speak <br> Retain the image as a steadfast rock, <br> The fifteen stars, that in their divers regions <br> The sky enliven with a light so great <br> That it transcends all clusters of the air; <br> Let him the Wain imagine unto which<sup>1523</sup> <br> Our vault of heaven sufficeth night and day, <br> So that in turning of its pole it fails not; <br> Let him the mouth imagine of the horn<sup>1524</sup> <br> That in the point beginneth of the axis <br> Round about which the primal wheel revolves⁠— <br> To have fashioned of themselves two signs in heaven, <br> Like unto that which Minos’ daughter made,<sup>1525</sup> <br> The moment when she felt the frost of death; <br> And one to have its rays within the other, <br> And both to whirl themselves in such a manner <br> That one should forward go, the other backward; <br> And he will have some shadowing forth of that <br> True constellation and the double dance <br> That circled round the point at which I was; <br> Because it is as much beyond our wont, <br> As swifter than the motion of the Chiana<sup>1526</sup> <br> Moveth the heaven that all the rest outspeeds.<sup>1527</sup> <br> There sang they neither Bacchus, nor Apollo, <br> But in the divine nature Persons three, <br> And in one person the divine and human. <br> The singing and the dance fulfilled their measure, <br> And unto us those holy lights gave need, <br> Growing in happiness from care to care. <br> Then broke the silence of those saints concordant <br> The light in which the admirable life<sup>1528</sup> <br> Of God’s own mendicant was told to me, <br> And said: “Now that one straw is trodden out<sup>1529</sup> <br> Now that its seed is garnered up already, <br> Sweet love invites me to thresh out the other. <br> Into that bosom, thou believest, whence<sup>1530</sup> <br> Was drawn the rib to form the beauteous cheek <br> Whose taste to all the world is costing dear, <br> And into that which, by the lance transfixed,<sup>1531</sup> <br> Before and since, such satisfaction made <br> That it weighs down the balance of all sin, <br> Whate’er of light it has to human nature <br> Been lawful to possess was all infused <br> By the same power that both of them created; <br> And hence at what I said above dost wonder, <br> When I narrated that no second had <br> The good which in the fifth light is enclosed.<sup>1532</sup> <br> Now ope thine eyes to what I answer thee, <br> And thou shalt see thy creed and my discourse <br> Fit in the truth as centre in a circle. <br> That which can die, and that which dieth not,<sup>1533</sup> <br> Are nothing but the splendor of the idea <br> Which by his love our Lord brings into being; <br> Because that living Light, which from its fount<sup>1534</sup> <br> Effulgent flows, so that it disunites not <br> From Him nor from the Love in them intrined, <br> Through its own goodness reunites its rays<sup>1535</sup> <br> In nine subsistences, as in a mirror, <br> Itself eternally remaining One. <br> Thence it descends to the last potencies,<sup>1536</sup> <br> Downward from act to act becoming such <br> That only brief contingencies it makes; <br> And these contingencies I hold to be<sup>1537</sup> <br> Things generated, which the heaven produces <br> By its own motion, with seed and without. <br> Neither their wax, nor that which tempers it,<sup>1538</sup> <br> Remains immutable, and hence beneath <br> The ideal signet more and less shines through; <br> Therefore it happens, that the selfsame tree <br> After its kind bears worse and better fruit, <br> And ye are born with characters diverse. <br> If in perfection tempered were the wax,<sup>1539</sup> <br> And were the heaven in its supremest virtue, <br> The brilliance of the seal would all appear; <br> But nature gives it evermore deficient, <br> In the like manner working as the artist, <br> Who has the skill of art and hand that trembles. <br> If then the fervent Love, the Vision clear,<sup>1540</sup> <br> Of primal Virtue do dispose and seal, <br> Perfection absolute is there acquired. <br> Thus was of old the earth created worthy <br> Of all and every animal perfection; <br> And thus the Virgin was impregnate made; <br> So that thine own opinion I commend, <br> That human nature never yet has been, <br> Nor will be, what it was in those two persons. <br> Now if no farther forth I should proceed, <br> ‘Then in what way was he without a peer?’<sup>1541</sup> <br> Would be the first beginning of thy words. <br> But, that may well appear what now appears not, <br> Think who he was, and what occasion moved him <br> To make request, when it was told him, ‘Ask.’<sup>1542</sup> <br> I’ve not so spoken that thou canst not see <br> Clearly he was a king who asked for wisdom, <br> That he might be sufficiently a king; <br> ’Twas not to know the number in which are <br> The motors here above, or if <i>necesse</i><sup>1543</sup> <br> With a contingent e’er <i>necesse</i> make,<sup>1544</sup> <br>  <i>Non si est dare primum motum esse,<sup>1545</sup></i>  <br> Or if in semicircle can be made <br> Triangle so that it have no right angle.<sup>1546</sup> <br> Whence, if thou notest this and what I said,<sup>1547</sup> <br> A regal prudence is that peerless seeing<sup>1548</sup> <br> In which the shaft of my intention strikes. <br> And if on ‘rose’ thou turnest thy clear eyes, <br> Thou’lt see that it has reference alone <br> To kings who’re many, and the good are rare. <br> With this distinction take thou what I said, <br> And thus it can consist with thy belief <br> Of the first father and of our Delight. <br> And lead shall this be always to thy feet, <br> To make thee, like a weary man, move slowly <br> Both to the Yes and No thou seest not; <br> For very low among the fools is he <br> Who affirms without distinction, or denies, <br> As well in one as in the other case; <br> Because it happens that full often bends <br> Current opinion in the false direction, <br> And then the feelings bind the intellect. <br> Far more than uselessly he leaves the shore, <br> (Since he returneth not the same he went,) <br> Who fishes for the truth, and has no skill; <br> And in the world proofs manifest thereof <br> Parmenides, Melissus, Brissus are,<sup>1549</sup> <br> And many who went on and knew not whither; <br> Thus did Sabellius, Arius, and those fools<sup>1550</sup> <br> Who have been even as swords unto the Scriptures<sup>1551</sup> <br> In rendering distorted their straight faces. <br> Nor yet shall people be too confident <br> In judging, even as he is who doth count <br> The corn in field or ever it be ripe. <br> For I have seen all winter long the thorn <br> First show itself intractable and fierce, <br> And after bear the rose upon its top; <br> And I have seen a ship direct and swift <br> Run o’er the sea throughout its course entire, <br> To perish at the harbour’s mouth at last. <br> Let not Dame Bertha nor Ser Martin think,<sup>1552</sup> <br> Seeing one steal, another offering make, <br> To see them in the arbitrament divine;<sup>1553</sup> <br> For one may rise, and fall the other may.”</p>
