<p class="lede">The Celestial Pilot⁠—Casella.</p>
<p class="verse">Already had the sun the horizon reached<sup>550</sup> <br> Whose circle of meridian covers o’er <br> Jerusalem with its most lofty point, <br> And night that opposite to him revolves <br> Was issuing forth from Ganges with the Scales <br> That fall from out her hand when she exceedeth; <br> So that the white and the vermilion cheeks<sup>551</sup> <br> Of beautiful Aurora, where I was, <br> By too great age were changing into orange. <br> We still were on the border of the sea, <br> Like people who are thinking of their road, <br> Who go in heart, and with the body stay; <br> And lo! as when, upon the approach of morning, <br> Through the gross vapors Mars grows fiery red <br> Down in the West upon the ocean floor, <br> Appeared to me⁠—may I again behold it!⁠— <br> A light along the sea so swiftly coming, <br> Its motion by no flight of wing is equalled; <br> From which when I a little had withdrawn <br> Mine eyes, that I might question my Conductor, <br> Again I saw it brighter grown and larger. <br> Then on each side of it appeared to me <br> I knew not what of white, and underneath it <br> Little by little there came forth another. <br> My Master yet had uttered not a word <br> While the first whiteness into wings unfolded; <br> But when he clearly recognized the pilot, <br> He cried: “Make haste, make haste to bow the knee! <br> Behold the Angel of God! fold thou thy hands! <br> Henceforward shalt thou see such officers! <br> See how he scorneth human arguments,<sup>552</sup> <br> So that nor oar he wants, nor other sail <br> Than his own wings, between so distant shores. <br> See how he holds them pointed up to heaven, <br> Fanning the air with the eternal pinions, <br> That do not moult themselves like mortal hair!” <br> Then as still nearer and more near us came <br> The Bird Divine, more radiant he appeared, <br> So that near by the eye could not endure him, <br> But down I cast it; and he came to shore <br> With a small vessel, very swift and light, <br> So that the water swallowed naught thereof. <br> Upon the stern stood the Celestial Pilot; <br> Beatitude seemed written in his face,<sup>553</sup> <br> And more than a hundred spirits sat within. <br> “<i>In exitu Israel de Aegypto!</i>” <br> They chanted all together in one voice, <br> With whatso in that psalm is after written. <br> Then made he sign of holy rood upon them, <br> Whereat all cast themselves upon the shore, <br> And he departed swiftly as he came. <br> The throng which still remained there unfamiliar <br> Seemed with the place, all round about them gazing, <br> As one who in new matters makes essay. <br> On every side was darting forth the day <br> The sun, who had with his resplendent shafts <br> From the mid-heaven chased forth the Capricorn,<sup>554</sup> <br> When the new people lifted up their faces <br> Towards us, saying to us: “If ye know, <br> Show us the way to go unto the mountain.” <br> And answer made Virgilius: “Ye believe <br> Perchance that we have knowledge of this place, <br> But we are strangers even as yourselves. <br> Just now we came, a little while before you, <br> Another way, which was so rough and steep, <br> That mounting will henceforth seem sport to us.” <br> The souls who had, from seeing me draw breath, <br> Become aware that I was still alive, <br> Pallid in their astonishment became; <br> And as to messenger who bears the olive <br> The people throng to listen to the news, <br> And no one shows himself afraid of crowding, <br> So at the sight of me stood motionless <br> Those fortunate spirits, all of them, as if <br> Oblivious to go and make them fair. <br> One from among them saw I coming forward, <br> As to embrace me, with such great affection, <br> That it incited me to do the like. <br> O empty shadows, save in aspect only! <br> Three times behind it did I clasp my hands,<sup>555</sup> <br> As oft returned with them to my own breast! <br> I think with wonder I depicted me; <br> Whereat the shadow smiled and backward drew; <br> And I, pursuing it, pressed farther forward. <br> Gently it said that I should stay my steps; <br> Then knew I who it was, and I entreated <br> That it would stop awhile to speak with me. <br> It made reply to me: “Even as I loved thee <br> In mortal body, so I love thee free; <br> Therefore I stop; but wherefore goest thou?” <br> “My own Casella! to return once more<sup>556</sup> <br> There where I am, I make this journey,” said I; <br> “But how from thee has so much time be taken?” <br> And he to me: “No outrage has been done me, <br> If he who takes both when and whom he pleases <br> Has many times denied to me this passage, <br> For of a righteous will his own is made. <br> He, sooth to say, for three months past has taken<sup>557</sup> <br> Whoever wished to enter with all peace; <br> Whence I, who now had turned unto that shore<sup>558</sup> <br> Where salt the waters of the Tiber grow, <br> Benignantly by him have been received.<sup>559</sup> <br> Unto that outlet now his wing is pointed, <br> Because for evermore assemble there <br> Those who tow’rds Acheron do not descend.” <br> And I: “If some new law take not from thee <br> Memory or practice of the song of love, <br> Which used to quiet in me all my longings, <br> Thee may it please to comfort therewithal <br> Somewhat this soul of mine, that with its body <br> Hitherward coming is so much distressed.” <br> “<em>Love, that within my mind discourses with me</em>,” <br> Forthwith began he so melodiously, <br> The melody within me still is sounding. <br> My Master, and myself, and all that people <br> Which with him were, appeared as satisfied <br> As if naught else might touch the mind of any. <br> We all of us were moveless and attentive <br> Unto his notes; and lo! the grave old man, <br> Exclaiming: “What is this, ye laggard spirits? <br> What negligence, what standing still is this? <br> Run to the mountain to strip off the slough, <br> That lets not God be manifest to you.” <br> Even as when, collecting grain or tares, <br> The doves, together at their pasture met, <br> Quiet, nor showing their accustomed pride, <br> If aught appear of which they are afraid, <br> Upon a sudden leave their food alone, <br> Because they are assailed by greater care; <br> So that fresh company did I behold <br> The song relinquish, and go tow’rds the hill, <br> As one who goes, and knows not whitherward; <br> Nor was our own departure less in haste.</p>
