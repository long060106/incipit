<p class="lede">The shores of Purgatory⁠—Cato of Utica.</p>
<p class="verse">To run o’er better waters hoists its sail<sup>531</sup> <br> The little vessel of my genius now, <br> That leaves behind itself a sea so cruel; <br> And of that second kingdom will I sing <br> Wherein the human spirit doth purge itself, <br> And to ascend to heaven becometh worthy. <br> But let dead Poesy here rise again, <br> O holy Muses, since that I am yours, <br> And here Calliope somewhat ascend,<sup>532</sup> <br> My song accompanying with that sound, <br> Of which the miserable magpies felt<sup>533</sup> <br> The blow so great, that they despaired of pardon. <br> Sweet color of the oriental sapphire, <br> That was upgathered in the cloudless aspect <br> Of the pure air, as far as the first circle,<sup>534</sup> <br> Unto mine eyes did recommence delight <br> Soon as I issued forth from the dead air, <br> Which had with sadness filled mine eyes and breast. <br> The beauteous planet, that to love incites,<sup>535</sup> <br> Was making all the orient to laugh,<sup>536</sup> <br> Veiling the Fishes that were in her escort. <br> To the right hand I turned, and fixed my mind <br> Upon the other pole, and saw four stars<sup>537</sup> <br> Ne’er seen before save by the primal people.<sup>538</sup> <br> Rejoicing in their flamelets seemed the heaven. <br> O thou septentrional and widowed site, <br> Because thou art deprived of seeing these! <br> When from regarding them I had withdrawn, <br> Turning a little to the other pole, <br> There where the Wain had disappeared already,<sup>539</sup> <br> I saw beside me an old man alone,<sup>540</sup> <br> Worthy of so much reverence in his look, <br> That more owes not to father any son. <br> A long beard and with white hair intermingled <br> He wore, in semblance like unto the tresses, <br> Of which a double list fell on his breast. <br> The rays of the four consecrated stars <br> Did so adorn his countenance with light, <br> That him I saw as were the sun before him. <br> “Who are you? ye who, counter the blind river,<sup>541</sup> <br> Have fled away from the eternal prison?” <br> Moving those venerable plumes, he said:<sup>542</sup> <br> “Who guided you? or who has been your lamp <br> In issuing forth out of the night profound, <br> That ever black makes the infernal valley? <br> The laws of the abyss, are they thus broken? <br> Or is there changed in heaven some counsel new, <br> That being damned ye come unto my crags?” <br> Then did my Leader lay his grasp upon me, <br> And with his words, and with his hands and signs, <br> Reverent he made in me my knees and brow; <br> Then answered him: “I came not of myself;<sup>543</sup> <br> A Lady from Heaven descended, at whose prayers <br> I aided this one with my company. <br> But since it is thy will more be unfolded <br> Of our condition, how it truly is, <br> Mine cannot be that this should be denied thee. <br> This one has never his last evening seen, <br> But by his folly was so near to it <br> That very little time was there to turn. <br> As I have said, I unto him was sent <br> To rescue him, and other way was none <br> Than this to which I have myself betaken. <br> I’ve shown him all the people of perdition, <br> And now those spirits I intend to show <br> Who purge themselves beneath thy guardianship. <br> How I have brought him would be long to tell thee. <br> Virtue descendeth from on high that aids me <br> To lead him to behold thee and to hear thee. <br> Now may it please thee to vouchsafe his coming; <br> He seeketh Liberty, which is so dear, <br> As knoweth he who life for her refuses. <br> Thou know’st it; since, for her, to thee not bitter <br> Was death in Utica, where thou didst leave <br> The vesture, that will shine so, the great day. <br> By us the eternal edicts are not broken; <br> Since this one lives, and Minos binds not me;<sup>544</sup> <br> But of that circle I, where are the chaste<sup>545</sup> <br> Eyes of thy Marcia, who in looks still prays thee, <br> O holy breast, to hold her as thine own; <br> For her love, then, incline thyself to us. <br> Permit us through thy sevenfold realm to go; <br> I will take back this grace from thee to her, <br> If to be mentioned there below thou deignest.” <br> “Marcia so pleasing was unto mine eyes <br> While I was on the other side,” then said he, <br> “That every grace she wished of me I granted; <br> Now that she dwells beyond the evil river, <br> She can no longer move me, by that law <br> Which, when I issued forth from there, was made. <br> But if a Lady of Heaven do move and rule thee, <br> As thou dost say, no flattery is needful; <br> Let it suffice thee that for her thou ask me. <br> Go, then, and see thou gird this one about <br> With a smooth rush, and that thou wash his face,<sup>546</sup> <br> So that thou cleanse away all stain therefrom, <br> For ’twere not fitting that the eye o’ercast <br> By any mist should go before the first <br> Angel, who is of those of Paradise. <br> This little island round about its base <br> Below there, yonder, where the billow beats it, <br> Doth rushes bear upon its washy ooze; <br> No other plant that putteth forth the leaf, <br> Or that doth indurate, can there have life, <br> Because it yieldeth not unto the shocks. <br> Thereafter be not this way your return; <br> The sun, which now is rising, will direct you <br> To take the mount by easier ascent.” <br> With this he vanished; and I raised me up <br> Without a word, and wholly drew myself <br> Unto my Guide, and turned mine eyes to him. <br> And he began: “Son, follow thou my steps; <br> Let us turn back, for on this side declines <br> The plain unto its lower boundaries.” <br> The dawn was vanquishing the matin hour<sup>547</sup> <br> Which fled before it, so that from afar <br> I recognized the trembling of the sea. <br> Along the solitary plain we went <br> As one who unto the lost road returns, <br> And till he finds it seems to go in vain. <br> As soon as we were come to where the dew <br> Fights with the sun, and, being in a part <br> Where shadow falls, little evaporates,<sup>548</sup> <br> Both of his hands upon the grass outspread <br> In gentle manner did my Master place; <br> Whence I, who of his action was aware, <br> Extended unto him my tearful cheeks; <br> There did he make in me uncovered wholly <br> That hue which Hell had covered up in me. <br> Then came we down upon the desert shore <br> Which never yet saw navigate its waters <br> Any that afterward had known return. <br> There he begirt me as the other pleased; <br> O marvellous! for even as he culled <br> The humble plant, such it sprang up again<sup>549</sup> <br> Suddenly there where he uprooted it.</p>
