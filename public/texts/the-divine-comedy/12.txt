<p class="lede">Pope Anastasius⁠—General description of the Inferno and its divisions.</p>
<p class="verse">Upon the margin of a lofty bank <br> Which great rocks broken in a circle made, <br> We came upon a still more cruel throng; <br> And there, by reason of the horrible <br> Excess of stench the deep abyss throws out, <br> We drew ourselves aside behind the cover <br> Of a great tomb, whereon I saw a writing, <br> Which said: “Pope Anastasius I hold,<sup>147</sup> <br> Whom out of the right way Photinus drew.”<sup>148</sup> <br> “Slow it behoveth our descent to be, <br> So that the sense be first a little used <br> To the sad blast, and then we shall not heed it.” <br> The Master thus; and unto him I said, <br> “Some compensation find, that the time pass not <br> Idly”; and he: “Thou seest I think of that. <br> My son, upon the inside of these rocks,” <br> Began he then to say, “are three small circles, <br> From grade to grade, like those which thou art leaving. <br> They all are full of spirits maledict; <br> But that hereafter sight alone suffice thee, <br> Hear how and wherefore they are in constraint. <br> Of every malice that wins hate in Heaven, <br> Injury is the end; and all such end <br> Either by force or fraud afflicteth others. <br> But because fraud is man’s peculiar vice, <br> More it displeases God; and so stand lowest <br> The fraudulent, and greater dole assails them. <br> All the first circle of the Violent is; <br> But since force may be used against three persons, <br> In three rounds ’tis divided and constructed. <br> To God, to ourselves, and to our neighbor can we <br> Use force; I say on them and on their things, <br> As thou shalt hear with reason manifest. <br> A death by violence, and painful wounds, <br> Are to our neighbor given; and in his substance <br> Ruin, and arson, and injurious levies; <br> Whence homicides, and he who smites unjustly, <br> Marauders, and freebooters, the first round <br> Tormenteth all in companies diverse. <br> Man may lay violent hands upon himself <br> And his own goods; and therefore in the second <br> Round must perforce without avail repent <br> Whoever of your world deprives himself, <br> Who games, and dissipates his property, <br> And weepeth there, where he should jocund be. <br> Violence can be done the Deity, <br> In heart denying and blaspheming Him, <br> And by disdaining Nature and her bounty. <br> And for this reason doth the smallest round <br> Seal with its signet Sodom and Cahors,<sup>149</sup> <br> And who, disdaining God, speaks from the heart. <br> Fraud, wherewithal is every conscience stung, <br> A man may practise upon him who trusts, <br> And him who doth no confidence imburse. <br> This latter mode, it would appear, dissevers <br> Only the bond of love which Nature makes; <br> Wherefore within the second circle nestle <br> Hypocrisy, flattery, and who deals in magic, <br> Falsification, theft, and simony, <br> Panders, and barrators, and the like filth. <br> By the other mode, forgotten is that love <br> Which Nature makes, and what is after added, <br> From which there is a special faith engendered. <br> Hence in the smallest circle, where the point is <br> Of the Universe, upon which Dis is seated, <br> Whoe’er betrays forever is consumed.” <br> And I: “My Master, clear enough proceeds <br> Thy reasoning, and full well distinguishes <br> This cavern and the people who possess it. <br> But tell me, those within the fat lagoon,<sup>150</sup> <br> Whom the wind drives, and whom the rain doth beat,<sup>151</sup> <br> And who encounter with such bitter tongues,<sup>152</sup> <br> Wherefore are they inside of the red city <br> Not punished, if God has them in his wrath, <br> And if he has not, wherefore in such fashion?” <br> And unto me he said: “Why wanders so <br> Thine intellect from that which it is wont? <br> Or, sooth, thy mind where is it elsewhere looking? <br> Hast thou no recollection of those words <br> With which thine Ethics thoroughly discusses<sup>153</sup> <br> The dispositions three, that Heaven abides not⁠— <br> Incontinence, and Malice, and insane <br> Bestiality? and how Incontinence <br> Less God offendeth, and less blame attracts? <br> If thou regardest this conclusion well, <br> And to thy mind recallest who they are <br> That up outside are undergoing penance, <br> Clearly wilt thou perceive why from these felons <br> They separated are, and why less wroth <br> Justice divine doth smite them with its hammer.” <br> “O Sun, that healest all distempered vision, <br> Thou dost content me so, when thou resolvest, <br> That doubting pleases me no less than knowing! <br> Once more a little backward turn thee,” said I, <br> “There where thou sayest that usury offends <br> Goodness divine, and disengage the knot.” <br> “Philosophy,” he said, “to him who heeds it, <br> Noteth, not only in one place alone, <br> After what manner Nature takes her course <br> From Intellect Divine, and from its art; <br> And if thy Physics carefully thou notest,<sup>154</sup> <br> After not many pages shalt thou find, <br> That this your art as far as possible <br> Follows, as the disciple doth the master; <br> So that your art is, as it were, God’s grandchild. <br> From these two, if thou bringest to thy mind <br> Genesis at the beginning, it behoves<sup>155</sup> <br> Mankind to gain their life and to advance; <br> And since the usurer takes another way,<sup>156</sup> <br> Nature herself and in her follower<sup>157</sup> <br> Disdains he, for elsewhere he puts his hope. <br> But follow, now, as I would fain go on, <br> For quivering are the Fishes on the horizon, <br> And the Wain wholly over Caurus lies,<sup>158</sup> <br> And far beyond there we descend the crag.”</p>
