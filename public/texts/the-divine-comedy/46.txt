<p class="lede">The First Circle⁠—The Proud⁠—The sculptures on the wall.</p>
<p class="verse">When we had crossed the threshold of the door<sup>700</sup> <br> Which the perverted love of souls disuses, <br> Because it makes the crooked way seem straight, <br> Re-echoing I heard it closed again; <br> And if I had turned back mine eyes upon it, <br> What for my failing had been fit excuse? <br> We mounted upward through a rifted rock, <br> Which undulated to this side and that, <br> Even as a wave receding and advancing. <br> “Here it behoves us use a little art,” <br> Began my Leader, “to adapt ourselves <br> Now here, now there, to the receding side.” <br> And this our footsteps so infrequent made, <br> That sooner had the moon’s decreasing disk<sup>701</sup> <br> Regained its bed to sink again to rest, <br> Than we were forth from out that needle’s eye; <br> But when we free and in the open were, <br> There where the mountain backward piles itself, <br> I wearied out, and both of us uncertain <br> About our way, we stopped upon a plain <br> More desolate than roads across the deserts. <br> From where its margin borders on the void, <br> To foot of the high bank that ever rises, <br> A human body three times told would measure; <br> And far as eye of mine could wing its flight, <br> Now on the left, and on the right flank now, <br> The same this cornice did appear to me. <br> Thereon our feet had not been moved as yet, <br> When I perceived the embankment round about, <br> Which all right of ascent had interdicted,<sup>702</sup> <br> To be of marble white, and so adorned <br> With sculptures, that not only Polycletus,<sup>703</sup> <br> But Nature’s self, had there been put to shame.<sup>704</sup> <br> The Angel, who came down to earth with tidings <br> Of peace, that had been wept for many a year, <br> And opened Heaven from its long interdict, <br> In front of us appeared so truthfully <br> There sculptured in a gracious attitude, <br> He did not seem an image that is silent. <br> One would have sworn that he was saying, “Ave”;<sup>705</sup> <br> For she was there in effigy portrayed <br> Who turned the key to ope the exalted love, <br> And in her mien this language had impressed, <br> “<i>Ecce ancilla Dei</i>,” as distinctly<sup>706</sup> <br> As any figure stamps itself in wax. <br> “Keep not thy mind upon one place alone,” <br> The gentle Master said, who had me standing <br> Upon that side where people have their hearts; <br> Whereat I moved mine eyes, and I beheld <br> In rear of Mary, and upon that side <br> Where he was standing who conducted me, <br> Another story on the rock imposed; <br> Wherefore I passed Virgilius and drew near, <br> So that before mine eyes it might be set. <br> There sculptured in the self-same marble were <br> The cart and oxen, drawing the holy ark, <br> Wherefore one dreads an office not appointed.<sup>707</sup> <br> People appeared in front, and all of them <br> In seven choirs divided, of two senses <br> Made one say, “No,” the other, “Yes, they sing.” <br> Likewise unto the smoke of the frankincense, <br> Which there was imaged forth, the eyes and nose <br> Were in the yes and no discordant made. <br> Preceded there the vessel benedight, <br> Dancing with girded loins, the humble Psalmist,<sup>708</sup> <br> And more and less than King was he in this. <br> Opposite, represented at the window <br> Of a great palace, Michal looked upon him,<sup>709</sup> <br> Even as a woman scornful and afflicted. <br> I moved my feet from where I had been standing, <br> To examine near at hand another story, <br> Which after Michal glimmered white upon me. <br> There the high glory of the Roman Prince<sup>710</sup> <br> Was chronicled, whose great beneficence <br> Moved Gregory to his great victory;<sup>711</sup> <br> ’Tis of the Emperor Trajan I am speaking; <br> And a poor widow at his bridle stood, <br> In attitude of weeping and of grief. <br> Around about him seemed it thronged and full <br> Of cavaliers, and the eagles in the gold <br> Above them visibly in the wind were moving. <br> The wretched woman in the midst of these <br> Seemed to be saying: “Give me vengeance, Lord, <br> For my dead son, for whom my heart is breaking.” <br> And he to answer her: “Now wait until <br> I shall return.” And she: “My Lord,” like one <br> In whom grief is impatient, “shouldst thou not <br> Return?” And he: “Who shall be where I am <br> Will give it thee.” And she: “Good deed of others <br> What boots it thee, if thou neglect thine own?” <br> Whence he: “Now comfort thee, for it behoves me <br> That I discharge my duty ere I move; <br> Justice so wills, and pity doth retain me.” <br> He who on no new thing has ever looked <br> Was the creator of this visible language, <br> Novel to us, for here it is not found. <br> While I delighted me in contemplating <br> The images of such humility, <br> And dear to look on for their Maker’s sake, <br> “Behold, upon this side, but rare they make <br> Their steps,” the Poet murmured, “many people; <br> These will direct us to the lofty stairs.” <br> Mine eyes, that in beholding were intent <br> To see new things, of which they curious are, <br> In turning round towards him were not slow. <br> But still I wish not, Reader, thou shouldst swerve <br> From thy good purposes, because thou hearest <br> How God ordaineth that the debt be paid; <br> Attend not to the fashion of the torment, <br> Think of what follows; think that at the worst <br> It cannot reach beyond the mighty sentence. <br> “Master,” began I, “that which I behold <br> Moving towards us seems to me not persons, <br> And what I know not, so in sight I waver.” <br> And he to me: “The grievous quality <br> Of this their torment bows them so to earth, <br> That my own eyes at first contended with it; <br> But look there fixedly, and disentangle <br> By sight what cometh underneath those stones; <br> Already canst thou see how each is stricken.” <br> O ye proud Christians! wretched, weary ones! <br> Who, in the vision of the mind infirm, <br> Confidence have in your backsliding steps, <br> Do ye not comprehend that we are worms,<sup>712</sup> <br> Born to bring forth the angelic butterfly <br> That flieth unto judgment without screen? <br> Why floats aloft your spirit high in air?<sup>713</sup> <br> Like are ye unto insects undeveloped, <br> Even as the worm in whom formation fails! <br> As to sustain a ceiling or a roof, <br> In place of corbel, oftentimes a figure <br> Is seen to join its knees unto its breast, <br> Which makes of the unreal real anguish <br> Arise in him who sees it; fashioned thus <br> Beheld I those, when I had ta’en good heed. <br> True is it, they were more or less bent down, <br> According as they more or less were laden; <br> And he who had most patience in his looks <br> Weeping did seem to say, “I can no more!”</p>
