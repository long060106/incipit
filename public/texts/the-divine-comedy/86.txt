<p class="lede">Cacciaguida’s discourse of the great Florentines.</p>
<p class="verse">O thou our poor nobility of blood,<sup>1607</sup> <br> If thou dost make the people glory in thee <br> Down here where our affection languishes, <br> A marvellous thing it ne’er will be to me; <br> For there where appetite is not perverted, <br> I say in Heaven, of thee I made a boast! <br> Truly thou art a cloak that quickly shortens, <br> So that unless we piece thee day by day <br> Time goeth round about thee with his shears! <br> With <em>You</em>, which Rome was first to tolerate,<sup>1608</sup> <br> (Wherein her family less perseveres,) <br> Yet once again my words beginning made; <br> Whence Beatrice, who stood somewhat apart, <br> Smiling, appeared like unto her who coughed<sup>1609</sup> <br> At the first failing writ of Guenever. <br> And I began: “You are my ancestor, <br> You give to me all hardihood to speak, <br> You lift me so that I am more than I. <br> So many rivulets with gladness fill <br> My mind, that of itself it makes a joy<sup>1610</sup> <br> Because it can endure this and not burst. <br> Then tell me, my beloved root ancestral, <br> Who were your ancestors, and what the years <br> That in your boyhood chronicled themselves? <br> Tell me about the sheepfold of Saint John,<sup>1611</sup> <br> How large it was, and who the people were <br> Within it worthy of the highest seats.” <br> As at the blowing of the winds a coal <br> Quickens to flame, so I beheld that light <br> Become resplendent at my blandishments. <br> And as unto mine eyes it grew more fair, <br> With voice more sweet and tender, but not in <br> This modern dialect, it said to me:<sup>1612</sup> <br> “From uttering of the Ave, till the birth<sup>1613</sup> <br> In which my mother, who is now a saint, <br> Of me was lightened who had been her burden, <br> Unto its Lion had this fire returned <br> Five hundred fifty times and thirty more, <br> To reinflame itself beneath his paw. <br> My ancestors and I our birthplace had <br> Where first is found the last ward of the city<sup>1614</sup> <br> By him who runneth in your annual game.<sup>1615</sup> <br> Suffice it of my elders to hear this; <br> But who they were, and whence they thither came, <br> Silence is more considerate than speech. <br> All those who at that time were there between <br> Mars and the Baptist, fit for bearing arms,<sup>1616</sup> <br> Were a fifth part of those who now are living; <br> But the community, that now is mixed <br> With Campi and Certaldo and Figghine,<sup>1617</sup> <br> Pure in the lowest artisan was seen. <br> O how much better ’twere to have as neighbors <br> The folk of whom I speak, and at Galluzzo<sup>1618</sup> <br> And at Trespiano have your boundary, <br> Than have them in the town, and bear the stench <br> Of Aguglione’s churl, and him of Signa<sup>1619</sup> <br> Who has sharp eyes for trickery already. <br> Had not the folk, which most of all the world<sup>1620</sup> <br> Degenerates, been a step-dame unto Caesar, <br> But as a mother to her son benignant, <br> Some who turn Florentines, and trade and discount, <br> Would have gone back again to Simifonte<sup>1621</sup> <br> There where their grandsires went about as beggars. <br> At Montemurlo still would be the Counts,<sup>1622</sup> <br> The Cerchi in the parish of Acone,<sup>1623</sup> <br> Perhaps in Valdigrieve the Buondelmonti.<sup>1624</sup> <br> Ever the intermingling of the people <br> Has been the source of malady in cities, <br> As in the body food it surfeits on; <br> And a blind bull more headlong plunges down<sup>1625</sup> <br> Than a blind lamb; and very often cuts <br> Better and more a single sword than five.<sup>1626</sup> <br> If Luni thou regard, and Urbisaglia,<sup>1627</sup> <br> How they have passed away, and how are passing <br> Chiusi and Sinigaglia after them,<sup>1628</sup> <br> To hear how races waste themselves away,<sup>1629</sup> <br> Will seem to thee no novel thing nor hard, <br> Seeing that even cities have an end.<sup>1630</sup> <br> All things of yours have their mortality,<sup>1631</sup> <br> Even as yourselves; but it is hidden in some <br> That a long while endure, and lives are short;<sup>1632</sup> <br> And as the turning of the lunar heaven <br> Covers and bares the shores without a pause, <br> In the like manner fortune does with Florence. <br> Therefore should not appear a marvellous thing <br> What I shall say of the great Florentines<sup>1633</sup> <br> Of whom the fame is hidden in the Past. <br> I saw the Ughi, saw the Catellini, <br> Filippi, Greci, Ormanni, and Alberichi, <br> Even in their fall illustrious citizens; <br> And saw, as mighty as they ancient were, <br> With him of La Sannella him of Arca, <br> And Soldanier, Ardinghi, and Bostichi.<sup>1634</sup> <br> Near to the gate that is at present laden <br> With a new felony of so much weight<sup>1635</sup> <br> That soon it shall be jetsam from the bark, <br> The Ravignani were, from whom descended <br> The County Guido, and whoe’er the name<sup>1636</sup> <br> Of the great Bellincione since hath taken.<sup>1637</sup> <br> He of La Pressa knew the art of ruling <br> Already, and already Galigajo <br> Had hilt and pommel gilded in his house.<sup>1638</sup> <br> Mighty already was the Column Vair,<sup>1639</sup> <br> Sacchetti, Giuochi, Fifant, and Barucci, <br> And Galli, and they who for the bushel blush.<sup>1640</sup> <br> The stock from which were the Calfucci born <br> Was great already, and already chosen <br> To curule chairs the Sizii and Arrigucci. <br> O how beheld I those who are undone<sup>1641</sup> <br> By their own pride! and how the Balls of Gold<sup>1642</sup> <br> Florence enflowered in all their mighty deeds! <br> So likewise did the ancestors of those<sup>1643</sup> <br> Who evermore, when vacant is your church, <br> Fatten by staying in consistory. <br> The insolent race, that like a dragon follows<sup>1644</sup> <br> Whoever flees, and unto him that shows <br> His teeth or purse is gentle as a lamb, <br> Already rising was, but from low people; <br> So that it pleased not Ubertin Donato<sup>1645</sup> <br> That his wife’s father should make him their kin. <br> Already had Caponsacco to the Market<sup>1646</sup> <br> From Fesole descended, and already <br> Giuda and Infangato were good burghers. <br> I’ll tell a thing incredible, but true;<sup>1647</sup> <br> One entered the small circuit by a gate <br> Which from the Della Pera took its name! <br> Each one that bears the beautiful escutcheon<sup>1648</sup> <br> Of the great baron whose renown and name <br> The festival of Thomas keepeth fresh, <br> Knighthood and privilege from him received;<sup>1649</sup> <br> Though with the populace unites himself <br> Today the man who binds it with a border. <br> Already were Gualterotti and Importuni; <br> And still more quiet would the Borgo be<sup>1650</sup> <br> If with new neighbors it remained unfed. <br> The house from which is born your lamentation,<sup>1651</sup> <br> Through just disdain that death among you brought <br> And put an end unto your joyous life, <br> Was honored in itself and its companions. <br> O Buondelmonte, how in evil hour<sup>1652</sup> <br> Thou fled’st the bridal at another’s promptings! <br> Many would be rejoicing who are sad,<sup>1653</sup> <br> If God had thee surrendered to the Ema <br> The first time that thou camest to the city. <br> But it behoved the mutilated stone<sup>1654</sup> <br> Which guards the bridge, that Florence should provide <br> A victim in her latest hour of peace. <br> With all these families, and others with them, <br> Florence beheld I in so great repose, <br> That no occasion had she whence to weep; <br> With all these families beheld so just <br> And glorious her people, that the lily <br> Never upon the spear was placed reversed,<sup>1655</sup> <br> Nor by division was vermilion made.”<sup>1656</sup></p>
