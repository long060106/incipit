<p class="lede">Cunizza, Folco of Marseilles, and Rahab.</p>
<p class="verse">Beautiful Clemence, after that thy Charles<sup>1372</sup> <br> Had me enlightened, he narrated to me <br> The treacheries his seed should undergo;<sup>1373</sup> <br> But said: “Be still and let the years roll round”; <br> So I can only say, that lamentation <br> Legitimate shall follow on your wrongs. <br> And of that holy light the life already <br> Had to the Sun which fills it turned again, <br> As to that good which for each thing sufficeth. <br> Ah, souls deceived, and creatures impious, <br> Who from such good do turn away your hearts, <br> Directing upon vanity your foreheads! <br> And now, behold, another of those splendors <br> Approached me, and its will to pleasure me <br> It signified by brightening outwardly. <br> The eyes of Beatrice, that fastened were <br> Upon me, as before, of dear assent <br> To my desire assurance gave to me. <br> “Ah, bring swift compensation to my wish, <br> Thou blessed spirit,” I said, “and give me proof <br> That what I think in thee I can reflect!” <br> Whereat the light, that still was new to me,<sup>1374</sup> <br> Out of its depths, whence it before was singing, <br> As one delighted to do good, continued: <br> “Within that region of the land depraved<sup>1375</sup> <br> Of Italy, that lies between Rialto <br> And fountainheads of Brenta and of Piava, <br> Rises a hill, and mounts not very high,<sup>1376</sup> <br> Wherefrom descended formerly a torch <br> That made upon that region great assault. <br> Out of one root were born both I and it; <br> Cunizza was I called, and here I shine<sup>1377</sup> <br> Because the splendor of this star o’ercame me.<sup>1378</sup> <br> But gladly to myself the cause I pardon<sup>1379</sup> <br> Of my allotment, and it does not grieve me; <br> Which would perhaps seem strong unto your vulgar. <br> Of this so luculent and precious jewel,<sup>1380</sup> <br> Which of our heaven is nearest unto me, <br> Great fame remained; and ere it die away <br> This hundredth year shall yet quintupled be. <br> See if man ought to make him excellent, <br> So that another life the first may leave! <br> And thus thinks not the present multitude <br> Shut in by Adige and Tagliamento,<sup>1381</sup> <br> Nor yet for being scourged is penitent. <br> But soon ’twill be that Padua in the marsh<sup>1382</sup> <br> Will change the water that Vicenza bathes, <br> Because the folk are stubborn against duty; <br> And where the Sile and Cagnano join<sup>1383</sup> <br> One lordeth it, and goes with lofty head,<sup>1384</sup> <br> For catching whom e’en now the net is making. <br> Feltro moreover of her impious pastor <br> Shall weep the crime, which shall so monstrous be<sup>1385</sup> <br> That for the like none ever entered Malta.<sup>1386</sup> <br> Ample exceedingly would be the vat <br> That of the Ferrarese could hold the blood, <br> And weary who should weigh it ounce by ounce, <br> Of which this courteous priest shall make a gift<sup>1387</sup> <br> To show himself a partisan; and such gifts <br> Will to the living of the land conform.<sup>1388</sup> <br> Above us there are mirrors, Thrones you call them,<sup>1389</sup> <br> From which shines out on us God Judicant, <br> So that this utterance seems good to us.” <br> Here it was silent, and it had the semblance <br> Of being turned elsewhither, by the wheel <br> On which it entered as it was before. <br> The other joy, already known to me, <br> Became a thing transplendent in my sight, <br> As a fine ruby smitten by the sun.<sup>1390</sup> <br> Through joy effulgence is acquired above,<sup>1391</sup> <br> As here a smile; but down below, the shade <br> Outwardly darkens, as the mind is sad. <br> “God seeth all things, and in Him, blest spirit,<sup>1392</sup> <br> Thy sight is,” said I, “so that never will <br> Of his can possibly from thee be hidden; <br> Thy voice, then, that forever makes the heavens<sup>1393</sup> <br> Glad, with the singing of those holy fires<sup>1394</sup> <br> Which of their six wings make themselves a cowl, <br> Wherefore does it not satisfy my longings? <br> Indeed, I would not wait thy questioning <br> If I in thee were as thou art in me.”<sup>1395</sup> <br> “The greatest of the valleys where the water<sup>1396</sup> <br> Expands itself,” forthwith its words began, <br> “That sea excepted which the earth engarlands, <br> Between discordant shores against the sun<sup>1397</sup> <br> Extends so far, that it meridian makes <br> Where it was wont before to make the horizon. <br> I was a dweller on that valley’s shore <br> ’Twixt Ebro and Magra that with journey short<sup>1398</sup> <br> Doth from the Tuscan part the Genoese. <br> With the same sunset and same sunrise nearly <br> Sit Buggia and the city whence I was,<sup>1399</sup> <br> That with its blood once made the harbour hot.<sup>1400</sup> <br> Folco that people called me unto whom<sup>1401</sup> <br> My name was known; and now with me this heaven <br> Imprints itself, as I did once with it; <br> For more the daughter of Belus never burned,<sup>1402</sup> <br> Offending both Sichaeus and Creusa, <br> Than I, so long as it became my locks, <br> Nor yet that Rodophean, who deluded<sup>1403</sup> <br> Was by Demophoon, nor yet Alcides,<sup>1404</sup> <br> When Iole he in his heart had locked. <br> Yet here is no repenting, but we smile,<sup>1405</sup> <br> Not at the fault, which comes not back to mind, <br> But at the power which ordered and foresaw. <br> Here we behold the art that doth adorn<sup>1406</sup> <br> With such affection, and the good discover <br> Whereby the world above turns that below. <br> But that thou wholly satisfied mayst bear <br> Thy wishes hence which in this sphere are born, <br> Still farther to proceed behoveth me. <br> Thou fain wouldst know who is within this light <br> That here beside me thus is scintillating, <br> Even as a sunbeam in the limpid water. <br> Then know thou, that within there is at rest <br> Rahab, and being to our order joined,<sup>1407</sup> <br> With her in its supremest grade ’tis sealed. <br> Into this heaven, where ends the shadowy cone<sup>1408</sup> <br> Cast by your world, before all other souls <br> First of Christ’s triumph was she taken up.<sup>1409</sup> <br> Full meet it was to leave her in some heaven, <br> Even as a palm of the high victory <br> Which he acquired with one palm and the other,<sup>1410</sup> <br> Because she favored the first glorious deed<sup>1411</sup> <br> Of Joshua upon the Holy Land,<sup>1412</sup> <br> That little stirs the memory of the Pope. <br> Thy city, which an offshoot is of him<sup>1413</sup> <br> Who first upon his Maker turned his back, <br> And whose ambition is so sorely wept, <br> Brings forth and scatters the accursed flower<sup>1414</sup> <br> Which both the sheep and lambs hath led astray <br> Since it has turned the shepherd to a wolf. <br> For this the Evangel and the mighty Doctors<sup>1415</sup> <br> Are derelict, and only the Decretals <br> So studied that it shows upon their margins. <br> On this are Pope and Cardinals intent; <br> Their meditations reach not Nazareth, <br> There where his pinions Gabriel unfolded;<sup>1416</sup> <br> But Vatican and the other parts elect <br> Of Rome, which have a cemetery been <br> Unto the soldiery that followed Peter <br> Shall soon be free from this adultery.”</p>
