<p class="lede">Geryon⁠—The violent against art⁠—Usurers⁠—Descent into the abyss of Malebolge.</p>
<p class="verse">“Behold the monster with the pointed tail,<sup>235</sup> <br> Who cleaves the hills, and breaketh walls and weapons, <br> Behold him who infecteth all the world.” <br> Thus unto me my Guide began to say, <br> And beckoned him that he should come to shore, <br> Near to the confine of the trodden marble; <br> And that uncleanly image of deceit <br> Came up and thrust ashore its head and bust, <br> But on the border did not drag its tail. <br> The face was as the face of a just man,<sup>236</sup> <br> Its semblance outwardly was so benign, <br> And of a serpent all the trunk beside. <br> Two paws it had, hairy unto the armpits; <br> The back, and breast, and both the sides it had <br> Depicted o’er with nooses and with shields. <br> With colors more, groundwork or broidery <br> Never in cloth did Tartars make nor Turks,<sup>237</sup> <br> Nor were such tissues by Arachne laid.<sup>238</sup> <br> As sometimes wherries lie upon the shore, <br> That part are in the water, part on land; <br> And as among the guzzling Germans there, <br> The beaver plants himself to wage his war; <br> So that vile monster lay upon the border, <br> Which is of stone, and shutteth in the sand <br> His tail was wholly quivering in the void, <br> Contorting upwards the envenomed fork, <br> That in the guise of scorpion armed its point. <br> The Guide said: “Now perforce must turn aside <br> Our way a little, even to that beast <br> Malevolent, that yonder coucheth him.” <br> We therefore on the right-hand side descended, <br> And made ten steps upon the outer verge, <br> Completely to avoid the sand and flame; <br> And after we are come to him, I see <br> A little farther off upon the sand <br> A people sitting near the hollow place. <br> Then said to me the Master: “So that full <br> Experience of this round thou bear away, <br> Now go and see what their condition is. <br> There let thy conversation be concise; <br> Till thou returnest I will speak with him, <br> That he concede to us his stalwart shoulders.” <br> Thus farther still upon the outermost <br> Head of that seventh circle all alone <br> I went, where sat the melancholy folk. <br> Out of their eyes was gushing forth their woe; <br> This way, that way, they helped them with their hands <br> Now from the flames and now from the hot soil. <br> Not otherwise in summer do the dogs, <br> Now with the foot, now with the muzzle, when <br> By fleas, or flies, or gadflies, they are bitten. <br> When I had turned mine eyes upon the faces <br> Of some, on whom the dolorous fire is falling, <br> Not one of them I knew; but I perceived <br> That from the neck of each there hung a pouch, <br> Which certain color had, and certain blazon; <br> And thereupon it seems their eyes are feeding.<sup>239</sup> <br> And as I gazing round me come among them, <br> Upon a yellow pouch I azure saw<sup>240</sup> <br> That had the face and posture of a lion. <br> Proceeding then the current of my sight, <br> Another of them saw I, red as blood, <br> Display a goose more white than butter is.<sup>241</sup> <br> And one, who with an azure sow and gravid<sup>242</sup> <br> Emblazoned had his little pouch of white, <br> Said unto me: “What dost thou in this moat? <br> Now get thee gone; and since thou ’rt still alive, <br> Know that a neighbor of mine, Vitaliano,<sup>243</sup> <br> Will have his seat here on my left-hand side. <br> A Paduan am I with these Florentines; <br> Full many a time they thunder in mine ears, <br> Exclaiming, ‘Come the sovereign cavalier, <br> He who shall bring the satchel with three goats’ ”;<sup>244</sup> <br> Then twisted he his mouth, and forth he thrust<sup>245</sup> <br> His tongue, like to an ox that licks its nose. <br> And fearing lest my longer stay might vex <br> Him who had warned me not to tarry long, <br> Backward I turned me from those weary souls.<sup>246</sup> <br> I found my Guide, who had already mounted <br> Upon the back of that wild animal, <br> And said to me: “Now be both strong and bold. <br> Now we descend by stairways such as these; <br> Mount thou in front, for I will be midway, <br> So that the tail may have no power to harm thee.” <br> Such as he is who has so near the ague <br> Of quartan that his nails are blue already, <br> And trembles all, but looking at the shade; <br> Even such became I at those proffered words; <br> But shame in me his menaces produced, <br> Which maketh servant strong before good master. <br> I seated me upon those monstrous shoulders; <br> I wished to say, and yet the voice came not <br> As I believed, “Take heed that thou embrace me.” <br> But he, who other times had rescued me <br> In other peril, soon as I had mounted, <br> Within his arms encircled and sustained me, <br> And said: “Now, Geryon, bestir thyself; <br> The circles large, and the descent be little; <br> Think of the novel burden which thou hast.” <br> Even as the little vessel shoves from shore, <br> Backward, still backward, so he thence withdrew; <br> And when he wholly felt himself afloat, <br> There where his breast had been he turned his tail, <br> And that extended like an eel he moved, <br> And with his paws drew to himself the air. <br> A greater fear I do not think there was <br> What time abandoned Phaeton the reins,<sup>247</sup> <br> Whereby the heavens, as still appears, were scorched;<sup>248</sup> <br> Nor when the wretched Icarus his flanks<sup>249</sup> <br> Felt stripped of feathers by the melting wax, <br> His father crying, “An ill way thou takest!” <br> Than was my own, when I perceived myself <br> On all sides in the air, and saw extinguished <br> The sight of everything but of the monster. <br> Onward he goeth, swimming slowly, slowly; <br> Wheels and descends, but I perceive it only <br> By wind upon my face and from below. <br> I heard already on the right the whirlpool <br> Making a horrible crashing under us; <br> Whence I thrust out my head with eyes cast downward. <br> Then was I still more fearful of the abyss; <br> Because I fires beheld, and heard laments, <br> Whereat I, trembling, all the closer cling. <br> I saw then, for before I had not seen it, <br> The turning and descending, by great horrors <br> That were approaching upon divers sides. <br> As falcon who has long been on the wing, <br> Who, without seeing either lure or bird, <br> Maketh the falconer say, “Ah me, thou stoopest,” <br> Descendeth weary, whence he started swiftly, <br> Thorough a hundred circles, and alights <br> Far from his master, sullen and disdainful; <br> Even thus did Geryon place us on the bottom, <br> Close to the bases of the rough-hewn rock, <br> And being disencumbered of our persons, <br> He sped away as arrow from the string.<sup>250</sup></p>
