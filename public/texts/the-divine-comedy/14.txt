<p class="lede">The wood of thorns⁠—The Harpies⁠—The violent against themselves⁠—Suicides⁠—Pier della Vigna⁠—Lano and Jacopo da Sant’ Andrea.</p>
<p class="verse">Not yet had Nessus reached the other side,<sup>179</sup> <br> When we had put ourselves within a wood,<sup>180</sup> <br> That was not marked by any path whatever. <br> Not foliage green, but of a dusky color, <br> Not branches smooth, but gnarled and intertangled, <br> Not apple trees were there, but thorns with poison. <br> Such tangled thickets have not, nor so dense, <br> Those savage wild beasts, that in hatred hold <br> ’Twixt Cecina and Corneto the tilled places.<sup>181</sup> <br> There do the hideous Harpies make their nests, <br> Who chased the Trojans from the Strophades,<sup>182</sup> <br> With sad announcement of impending doom; <br> Broad wings have they, and necks and faces human, <br> And feet with claws, and their great bellies fledged; <br> They make laments upon the wondrous trees. <br> And the good Master: “Ere thou enter farther, <br> Know that thou art within the second round,” <br> Thus he began to say, “and shalt be, till <br> Thou comest out upon the horrible sand; <br> Therefore look well around, and thou shalt see <br> Things that will credence give unto my speech.”<sup>183</sup> <br> I heard on all sides lamentations uttered, <br> And person none beheld I who might make them, <br> Whence, utterly bewildered, I stood still. <br> I think he thought that I perhaps might think <br> So many voices issued through those trunks <br> From people who concealed themselves from us; <br> Therefore the Master said: “If thou break off <br> Some little spray from any of these trees, <br> The thoughts thou hast will wholly be made vain.” <br> Then stretched I forth my hand a little forward, <br> And plucked a branchlet off from a great thorn; <br> And the trunk cried, “Why dost thou mangle me?” <br> After it had become embrowned with blood, <br> It recommenced its cry: “Why dost thou rend me? <br> Hast thou no spirit of pity whatsoever? <br> Men once we were, and now are changed to trees; <br> Indeed, thy hand should be more pitiful, <br> Even if the souls of serpents we had been.” <br> As out of a green brand, that is on fire<sup>184</sup> <br> At one of the ends, and from the other drips <br> And hisses with the wind that is escaping; <br> So from that splinter issued forth together <br> Both words and blood; whereat I let the tip <br> Fall, and stood like a man who is afraid. <br> “Had he been able sooner to believe,” <br> My Sage made answer, “O thou wounded soul, <br> What only in my verses he has seen, <br> Not upon thee had he stretched forth his hand; <br> Whereas the thing incredible has caused me <br> To put him to an act which grieveth me. <br> But tell him who thou wast, so that by way <br> Of some amends thy fame he may refresh <br> Up in the world, to which he can return.” <br> And the trunk said: “So thy sweet words allure me, <br> I can not silent be; and you be vexed not, <br> That I a little to discourse am tempted. <br> I am the one who both keys had in keeping<sup>185</sup> <br> Of Frederick’s heart, and turned them to and fro <br> So softly in unlocking and in locking, <br> That from his secrets most men I withheld; <br> Fidelity I bore the glorious office <br> So great, I lost thereby my sleep and pulses. <br> The courtesan who never from the dwelling <br> Of Caesar turned aside her strumpet eyes, <br> Death universal and the vice of courts, <br> Inflamed against me all the other minds, <br> And they, inflamed, did so inflame Augustus, <br> That my glad honors turned to dismal mournings. <br> My spirit, in disdainful exultation, <br> Thinking by dying to escape disdain, <br> Made me unjust against myself, the just. <br> I, by the roots unwonted of this wood, <br> Do swear to you that never broke I faith <br> Unto my lord, who was so worthy of honor; <br> And to the world if one of you return, <br> Let him my memory comfort, which is lying <br> Still prostrate from the blow that envy dealt it.” <br> Waited awhile, and then: “Since he is silent,” <br> The Poet said to me, “lose not the time, <br> But speak, and question him, if more may please thee.” <br> Whence I to him: “Do thou again inquire <br> Concerning what thou thinks’t will satisfy me; <br> For I can not, such pity is in my heart.” <br> Therefore he recommenced: “So may the man <br> Do for thee freely what thy speech implores, <br> Spirit incarcerate, again be pleased <br> To tell us in what way the soul is bound <br> Within these knots; and tell us, if thou canst, <br> If any from such members e’er is freed.” <br> Then blew the trunk amain, and afterward <br> The wind was into such a voice converted: <br> “With brevity shall be replied to you. <br> When the exasperated soul abandons <br> The body whence it rent itself away, <br> Minos consigns it to the seventh abyss. <br> It falls into the forest, and no part <br> Is chosen for it; but where Fortune hurls it, <br> There like a grain of spelt it germinates. <br> It springs a sapling, and a forest tree; <br> The Harpies, feeding then upon its leaves, <br> Do pain create, and for the pain an outlet. <br> Like others for our spoils shall we return; <br> But not that any one may them revest, <br> For ’tis not just to have what one casts off. <br> Here we shall drag them, and along the dismal <br> Forest our bodies shall suspended be, <br> Each to the thorn of his molested shade.” <br> We were attentive still unto the trunk, <br> Thinking that more it yet might wish to tell us, <br> When by a tumult we were overtaken, <br> In the same way as he is who perceives<sup>186</sup> <br> The boar and chase approaching to his stand, <br> Who hears the crashing of the beasts and branches; <br> And two behold! upon our left-hand side, <br> Naked and scratched, fleeing so furiously, <br> That of the forest every fan they broke. <br> He who was in advance: “Now help, Death, help!” <br> And the other one, who seemed to lag too much, <br> Was shouting: “Lano, were not so alert<sup>187</sup> <br> Those legs of thine at joustings of the Toppo!” <br> And then, perchance because his breath was failing, <br> He grouped himself together with a bush. <br> Behind them was the forest full of black <br> She-mastiffs, ravenous, and swift of foot<sup>188</sup> <br> As greyhounds, who are issuing from the chain. <br> On him who had crouched down they set their teeth, <br> And him they lacerated piece by piece, <br> Thereafter bore away those aching members. <br> Thereat my Escort took me by the hand, <br> And led me to the bush, that all in vain <br> Was weeping from its bloody lacerations. <br> “O Jacopo,” it said, “of Sant’ Andrea,<sup>189</sup> <br> What helped it thee of me to make a screen? <br> What blame have I in thy nefarious life?” <br> When near him had the Master stayed his steps, <br> He said: “Who wast thou, that through wounds so many <br> Art blowing out with blood thy dolorous speech?” <br> And he to us: “O souls, that hither come <br> To look upon the shameful massacre <br> That has so rent away from me my leaves, <br> Gather them up beneath the dismal bush; <br> I of that city was which to the Baptist<sup>190</sup> <br> Changed its first patron, wherefore he for this <br> Forever with his art will make it sad. <br> And were it not that on the pass of Arno <br> Some glimpses of him are remaining still, <br> Those citizens, who afterwards rebuilt it <br> Upon the ashes left by Attila,<sup>191</sup> <br> In vain had caused their labor to be done.<sup>192</sup> <br> Of my own house I made myself a gibbet.”</p>
