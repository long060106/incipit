<p class="lede">Phlegyas⁠—Philippe Argenti⁠—The gate of the city of Dis.</p>
<p class="verse">I say, continuing, that long before<sup>112</sup> <br> We to the foot of that high tower had come, <br> Our eyes went upward to the summit of it, <br> By reason of two flamelets we saw placed there,<sup>113</sup> <br> And from afar another answer them, <br> So far, that hardly could the eye attain it. <br> And, to the sea of all discernment turned, <br> I said: “What sayeth this, and what respondeth <br> That other fire? and who are they that made it?” <br> And he to me: “Across the turbid waves <br> What is expected thou canst now discern, <br> If reek of the morass conceal it not.” <br> Cord never shot an arrow from itself <br> That sped away athwart the air so swift, <br> As I beheld a very little boat <br> Come o’er the water tow’rds us at that moment, <br> Under the guidance of a single pilot, <br> Who shouted, “Now art thou arrived, fell soul?” <br> “Phlegyas, Phlegyas, thou criest out in vain<sup>114</sup> <br> For this once,” said my Lord; “thou shalt not have us <br> Longer than in the passing of the slough.” <br> As he who listens to some great deceit <br> That has been done to him, and then resents it, <br> Such became Phlegyas, in his gathered wrath. <br> My Guide descended down into the boat, <br> And then he made me enter after him, <br> And only when I entered seemed it laden.<sup>115</sup> <br> Soon as the Guide and I were in the boat, <br> The antique prow goes on its way, dividing <br> More of the water than ’tis wont with others. <br> While we were running through the dead canal, <br> Uprose in front of me one full of mire, <br> And said, “Who ’rt thou that comest ere the hour?” <br> And I to him: “Although I come, I stay not; <br> But who art thou that hast become so squalid?” <br> “Thou seest that I am one who weeps,” he answered. <br> And I to him: “With weeping and with wailing, <br> Thou spirit maledict, do thou remain; <br> For thee I know, though thou art all defiled.” <br> Then stretched he both his hands unto the boat; <br> Whereat my wary Master thrust him back, <br> Saying, “Away there with the other dogs!” <br> Thereafter with his arms he clasped my neck; <br> He kissed my face, and said: “Disdainful soul, <br> Blessed be she who bore thee in her bosom. <br> That was an arrogant person in the world; <br> Goodness is none, that decks his memory; <br> So likewise here his shade is furious. <br> How many are esteemed great kings up there,<sup>116</sup> <br> Who here shall be like unto swine in mire, <br> Leaving behind them horrible dispraises!”<sup>117</sup> <br> And I: “My Master, much should I be pleased, <br> If I could see him soused into this broth, <br> Before we issue forth out of the lake.” <br> And he to me: “Ere unto thee the shore <br> Reveal itself, thou shalt be satisfied; <br> Such a desire ’tis meet thou shouldst enjoy.” <br> A little after that, I saw such havoc <br> Made of him by the people of the mire, <br> That still I praise and thank my God for it. <br> They all were shouting, “At Philippo Argenti!”<sup>118</sup> <br> And that exasperate spirit Florentine <br> Turned round upon himself with his own teeth. <br> We left him there, and more of him I tell not; <br> But on mine ears there smote a lamentation, <br> Whence forward I intent unbar mine eyes. <br> And the good Master said: “Even now, my son, <br> The city draweth near whose name is Dis, <br> With the grave citizens, with the great throng.” <br> And I: “Its mosques already, Master, clearly<sup>119</sup> <br> Within there in the valley I discern <br> Vermilion, as if issuing from the fire <br> They were.” And he to me: “The fire eternal <br> That kindles them within makes them look red, <br> As thou beholdest in this nether Hell.” <br> Then we arrived within the moats profound, <br> That circumvallate that disconsolate city; <br> The walls appeared to me to be of iron.<sup>120</sup> <br> Not without making first a circuit wide, <br> We came unto a place where loud the pilot <br> Cried out to us, “Debark, here is the entrance.” <br> More than a thousand at the gates I saw <br> Out of the Heavens rained down, who angrily <br> Were saying, “Who is this that without death <br> Goes through the kingdom of the people dead?” <br> And my sagacious Master made a sign <br> Of wishing secretly to speak with them. <br> A little then they quelled their great disdain, <br> And said: “Come thou alone, and he begone <br> Who has so boldly entered these dominions. <br> Let him return alone by his mad road; <br> Try, if he can; for thou shalt here remain, <br> Who hast escorted him through such dark regions.” <br> Think, Reader, if I was discomforted <br> At utterance of the accursed words; <br> For never to return here I believed. <br> “O my dear Guide, who more than seven times <br> Hast rendered me security, and drawn me <br> From imminent peril that before me stood, <br> Do not desert me,” said I, “thus undone; <br> And if the going farther be denied us, <br> Let us retrace our steps together swiftly.” <br> And that Lord, who had led me thitherward, <br> Said unto me: “Fear not; because our passage <br> None can take from us, it by Such is given. <br> But here await me, and thy weary spirit <br> Comfort and nourish with a better hope; <br> For in this nether world I will not leave thee.” <br> So onward goes and there abandons me <br> My Father sweet, and I remain in doubt, <br> For No and Yes within my head contend. <br> I could not hear what he proposed to them; <br> But with them there he did not linger long, <br> Ere each within in rivalry ran back. <br> They closed the portals, those our adversaries, <br> On my Lord’s breast, who had remained without <br> And turned to me with footsteps far between. <br> His eyes cast down, his forehead shorn had he <br> Of all its boldness, and he said, with sighs, <br> “Who has denied to me the dolesome houses?” <br> And unto me: “Thou, because I am angry, <br> Fear not, for I will conquer in the trial, <br> Whatever for defence within be planned. <br> This arrogance of theirs is nothing new;<sup>121</sup> <br> For once they used it at less secret gate,<sup>122</sup> <br> Which finds itself without a fastening still. <br> O’er it didst thou behold the dead inscription; <br> And now this side of it descends the steep, <br> Passing across the circles without escort, <br> One by whose means the city shall be opened.”<sup>123</sup></p>
