<p class="lede">Cacciaguida’s prophecy of Dante’s banishment.</p>
<p class="verse">As came to Clymene, to be made certain<sup>1657</sup> <br> Of that which he had heard against himself,<sup>1658</sup> <br> He who makes fathers chary still to children,<sup>1659</sup> <br> Even such was I, and such was I perceived <br> By Beatrice and by the holy light <br> That first on my account had changed its place. <br> Therefore my Lady said to me: “Send forth <br> The flame of thy desire, so that it issue <br> Imprinted well with the internal stamp; <br> Not that our knowledge may be greater made <br> By speech of thine, but to accustom thee <br> To tell thy thirst, that we may give thee drink.” <br> “O my beloved tree, (that so dost lift thee, <br> That even as minds terrestrial perceive <br> No triangle containeth two obtuse, <br> So thou beholdest the contingent things<sup>1660</sup> <br> Ere in themselves they are, fixing thine eyes <br> Upon the point in which all times are present,)<sup>1661</sup> <br> While I was with Virgilius conjoined <br> Upon the mountain that the souls doth heal,<sup>1662</sup> <br> And when descending into the dead world,<sup>1663</sup> <br> Were spoken to me of my future life <br> Some grievous words; although I feel myself <br> In sooth foursquare against the blows of chance.<sup>1664</sup> <br> On this account my wish would be content <br> To hear what fortune is approaching me, <br> Because foreseen an arrow comes more slowly.” <br> Thus did I say unto that selfsame light<sup>1665</sup> <br> That unto me had spoken before; and even <br> As Beatrice willed was my own will confessed. <br> Not in vague phrase, in which the foolish folk<sup>1666</sup> <br> Ensnared themselves of old, ere yet was slain <br> The Lamb of God who taketh sins away, <br> But with clear words and unambiguous <br> Language responded that paternal love,<sup>1667</sup> <br> Hid and revealed by its own proper smile: <br> “Contingency, that outside of the volume<sup>1668</sup> <br> Of your materiality extends not, <br> Is all depicted in the eternal aspect. <br> Necessity however thence it takes not,<sup>1669</sup> <br> Except as from the eye, in which ’tis mirrored, <br> A ship that with the current down descends. <br> From thence, e’en as there cometh to the ear <br> Sweet harmony from an organ, comes in sight <br> To me the time that is preparing for thee. <br> As forth from Athens went Hippolytus,<sup>1670</sup> <br> By reason of his step-dame false and cruel, <br> So thou from Florence must perforce depart. <br> Already this is willed, and this is sought for; <br> And soon it shall be done by him who thinks it,<sup>1671</sup> <br> Where every day the Christ is bought and sold. <br> The blame shall follow the offended party<sup>1672</sup> <br> In outcry as is usual; but the vengeance<sup>1673</sup> <br> Shall witness to the truth that doth dispense it. <br> Thou shalt abandon everything beloved <br> Most tenderly, and this the arrow is <br> Which first the bow of banishment shoots forth. <br> Thou shalt have proof how savoureth of salt<sup>1674</sup> <br> The bread of others, and how hard a road <br> The going down and up another’s stairs. <br> And that which most shall weigh upon thy shoulders <br> Will be the bad and foolish company<sup>1675</sup> <br> With which into this valley thou shalt fall; <br> For all ingrate, all mad and impious <br> Will they become against thee; but soon after <br> They, and not thou, shall have the forehead scarlet.<sup>1676</sup> <br> Of their bestiality their own proceedings <br> Shall furnish proof; so ’twill be well for thee <br> A party to have made thee by thyself. <br> Thine earliest refuge and thine earliest inn <br> Shall be the mighty Lombard’s courtesy,<sup>1677</sup> <br> Who on the Ladder bears the holy bird, <br> Who such benign regard shall have for thee <br> That ’twixt you twain, in doing and in asking, <br> That shall be first which is with others last. <br> With him shalt thou see one who at his birth<sup>1678</sup> <br> Has by this star of strength been so impressed, <br> That notable shall his achievements be. <br> Not yet the people are aware of him <br> Through his young age, since only nine years yet <br> Around about him have these wheels revolved. <br> But ere the Gascon cheat the noble Henry,<sup>1679</sup> <br> Some sparkles of his virtue shall appear <br> In caring not for silver nor for toil. <br> So recognized shall his magnificence <br> Become hereafter, that his enemies <br> Will not have power to keep mute tongues about it. <br> On him rely, and on his benefits; <br> By him shall many people be transformed, <br> Changing condition rich and mendicant; <br> And written in thy mind thou hence shalt bear <br> Of him, but shalt not say it”⁠—and things said he <br> Incredible to those who shall be present. <br> Then added: “Son, these are the commentaries<sup>1680</sup> <br> On what was said to thee; behold the snares <br> That are concealed behind few revolutions; <br> Yet would I not thy neighbors thou shouldst envy, <br> Because thy life into the future reaches <br> Beyond the punishment of their perfidies.” <br> When by its silence showed that sainted soul <br> That it had finished putting in the woof <br> Into that web which I had given it warped, <br> Began I, even as he who yearneth after, <br> Being in doubt, some counsel from a person <br> Who seeth, and uprightly wills, and loves: <br> “Well see I, father mine, how spurreth on <br> The time towards me such a blow to deal me <br> As heaviest is to him who most gives way. <br> Therefore with foresight it is well I arm me, <br> That, if the dearest place be taken from me, <br> I may not lose the others by my songs. <br> Down through the world of infinite bitterness, <br> And o’er the mountain, from whose beauteous summit <br> The eyes of my own Lady lifted me, <br> And afterward through heaven from light to light, <br> I have learned that which, if I tell again, <br> Will be a savor of strong herbs to many. <br> And if I am a timid friend to truth, <br> I fear lest I may lose my life with those <br> Who will hereafter call this time the olden.” <br> The light in which was smiling my own treasure <br> Which there I had discovered, flashed at first <br> As in the sunshine doth a golden mirror; <br> Then made reply: “A conscience overcast <br> Or with its own or with another’s shame, <br> Will taste forsooth the tartness of thy word; <br> But ne’ertheless, all falsehood laid aside, <br> Make manifest thy vision utterly,<sup>1681</sup> <br> And let them scratch wherever is the itch;<sup>1682</sup> <br> For if thine utterance shall offensive be <br> At the first taste, a vital nutriment <br> ’Twill leave thereafter, when it is digested. <br> This cry of thine shall do as doth the wind, <br> Which smiteth most the most exalted summits, <br> And that is no slight argument of honor. <br> Therefore are shown to thee within these wheels, <br> Upon the mount and in the dolorous valley, <br> Only the souls that unto fame are known; <br> Because the spirit of the hearer rests not, <br> Nor doth confirm its faith by an example <br> Which has the root of it unknown and hidden, <br> Or other reason that is not apparent.”</p>
