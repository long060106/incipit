<p class="lede">The Guardian Angels and the serpent⁠—Nino di Gallura⁠—Currado Malaspina.</p>
<p class="verse">’Twas now the hour that turneth back desire<sup>652</sup> <br> In those who sail the sea, and melts the heart, <br> The day they’ve said to their sweet friends farewell, <br> And the new pilgrim penetrates with love,<sup>653</sup> <br> If he doth hear from far away a bell <br> That seemeth to deplore the dying day,<sup>654</sup> <br> When I began to make of no avail <br> My hearing, and to watch one of the souls <br> Uprisen, that begged attention with its hand. <br> It joined and lifted upward both its palms, <br> Fixing its eyes upon the orient, <br> As if it said to God, “Naught else I care for.” <br> “<i>Te lucis ante</i>” so devoutly issued<sup>655</sup> <br> Forth from its mouth, and with such dulcet notes, <br> It made me issue forth from my own mind. <br> And then the others, sweetly and devoutly, <br> Accompanied it through all the hymn entire, <br> Having their eyes on the supernal wheels. <br> Here, Reader, fix thine eyes well on the truth, <br> For now indeed so subtle is the veil, <br> Surely to penetrate within is easy. <br> I saw that army of the gentle-born <br> Thereafterward in silence upward gaze, <br> As if in expectation, pale and humble; <br> And from on high come forth and down descend, <br> I saw two Angels with two flaming swords,<sup>656</sup> <br> Truncated and deprived of their points.<sup>657</sup> <br> Green as the little leaflets just now born<sup>658</sup> <br> Their garments were, which, by their verdant pinions <br> Beaten and blown abroad, they trailed behind. <br> One just above us came to take his station, <br> And one descended to the opposite bank, <br> So that the people were contained between them. <br> Clearly in them discerned I the blond head; <br> But in their faces was the eye bewildered, <br> As faculty confounded by excess. <br> “From Mary’s bosom both of them have come,” <br> Sordello said, “as guardians of the valley <br> Against the serpent, that will come anon.” <br> Whereupon I, who knew not by what road, <br> Turned round about, and closely drew myself, <br> Utterly frozen, to the faithful shoulders. <br> And once again Sordello: “Now descend we <br> ’Mid the grand shades, and we will speak to them; <br> Right pleasant will it be for them to see you.” <br> Only three steps I think that I descended, <br> And was below, and saw one who was looking <br> Only at me, as if he fain would know me. <br> Already now the air was growing dark, <br> But not so that between his eyes and mine<sup>659</sup> <br> It did not show what it before locked up. <br> Tow’rds me he moved, and I tow’rds him did move; <br> Noble Judge Nino! how it me delighted,<sup>660</sup> <br> When I beheld thee not among the damned! <br> No greeting fair was left unsaid between us; <br> Then asked he: “How long is it since thou camest <br> O’er the far waters to the mountain’s foot?” <br> “Oh!” said I to him, “through the dismal places <br> I came this morn; and am in the first life, <br> Albeit the other, going thus, I gain.” <br> And on the instant my reply was heard, <br> He and Sordello both shrank back from me, <br> Like people who are suddenly bewildered. <br> One to Virgilius, and the other turned <br> To one who sat there, crying, “Up, Currado! <br> Come and behold what God in grace has willed!” <br> Then, turned to me: “By that especial grace <br> Thou owest unto Him, who so conceals <br> His own first wherefore, that it has no ford, <br> When thou shalt be beyond the waters wide, <br> Tell my Giovanna that she pray for me,<sup>661</sup> <br> Where answer to the innocent is made. <br> I do not think her mother loves me more, <br> Since she has laid aside her wimple white, <br> Which she, unhappy, needs must wish again.<sup>662</sup> <br> Through her full easily is comprehended <br> How long in woman lasts the fire of love, <br> If eye or touch do not relight it often. <br> So fair a hatchment will not make for her<sup>663</sup> <br> The Viper marshalling the Milanese<sup>664</sup> <br> A-field, as would have made Gallura’s Cock.”<sup>665</sup> <br> In this wise spake he, with the stamp impressed <br> Upon his aspect of that righteous zeal <br> Which measurably burneth in the heart. <br> My greedy eyes still wandered up to heaven, <br> Still to that point where slowest are the stars, <br> Even as a wheel the nearest to its axle. <br> And my Conductor: “Son, what dost thou gaze at <br> Up there?” And I to him: “At those three torches<sup>666</sup> <br> With which this hither pole is all on fire.” <br> And he to me: “The four resplendent stars <br> Thou sawest this morning are down yonder low, <br> And these have mounted up to where those were.” <br> As he was speaking, to himself Sordello <br> Drew him, and said, “Lo there our Adversary!” <br> And pointed with his finger to look thither. <br> Upon the side on which the little valley <br> No barrier hath, a serpent was; perchance <br> The same which gave to Eve the bitter food. <br> ’Twixt grass and flowers came on the evil streak,<sup>667</sup> <br> Turning at times its head about, and licking <br> Its back like to a beast that smoothes itself. <br> I did not see, and therefore cannot say <br> How the celestial falcons ’gan to move, <br> But well I saw that they were both in motion. <br> Hearing the air cleft by their verdant wings, <br> The serpent fled, and round the Angels wheeled, <br> Up to their stations flying back alike. <br> The shade that to the Judge had near approached <br> When he had called, throughout that whole assault <br> Had not a moment loosed its gaze on me. <br> “So may the light that leadeth thee on high <br> Find in thine own free-will as much of wax <br> As needful is up to the highest azure,”<sup>668</sup> <br> Began it, “if some true intelligence <br> Of Valdimagra or its neighborhood<sup>669</sup> <br> Thou knowest, tell it me, who once was great there. <br> Currado Malaspina was I called;<sup>670</sup> <br> I’m not the elder, but from him descended; <br> To mine I bore the love which here refineth.” <br> “O,” said I unto him, “through your domains <br> I never passed, but where is there a dwelling <br> Throughout all Europe, where they are not known? <br> That fame, which doeth honor to your house, <br> Proclaims its Signors and proclaims its land, <br> So that he knows of them who ne’er was there. <br> And, as I hope for heaven, I swear to you <br> Your honored family in naught abates <br> The glory of the purse and of the sword. <br> It is so privileged by use and nature, <br> That though a guilty head misguide the world,<sup>671</sup> <br> Sole it goes right, and scorns the evil way.” <br> And he: “Now go; for the sun shall not lie <br> Seven times upon the pillow which the Ram<sup>672</sup> <br> With all his four feet covers and bestrides, <br> Before that such a courteous opinion <br> Shall in the middle of thy head be nailed<sup>673</sup> <br> With greater nails than of another’s speech, <br> Unless the course of justice standeth still.”<sup>674</sup></p>
