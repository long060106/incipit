<p class="lede">The Terrestrial Paradise⁠—The River Lethe⁠—Matilda.</p>
<p class="verse">Eager already to search in and round<sup>1070</sup> <br> The heavenly forest, dense and living-green,<sup>1071</sup> <br> Which tempered to the eyes the new-born day, <br> Withouten more delay I left the bank, <br> Taking the level country slowly, slowly <br> Over the soil that everywhere breathes fragrance. <br> A softly-breathing air, that no mutation <br> Had in itself, upon the forehead smote me <br> No heavier blow than of a gentle wind, <br> Whereat the branches, lightly tremulous, <br> Did all of them bow downward toward that side <br> Where its first shadow casts the Holy Mountain; <br> Yet not from their upright direction swayed, <br> So that the little birds upon their tops <br> Should leave the practice of each art of theirs; <br> But with full ravishment the hours of prime, <br> Singing, received they in the midst of leaves, <br> That ever bore a burden to their rhymes, <br> Such as from branch to branch goes gathering on <br> Through the pine forest on the shore of Chiassi,<sup>1072</sup> <br> When Eolus unlooses the Sirocco. <br> Already my slow steps had carried me <br> Into the ancient wood so far, that I <br> Could not perceive where I had entered it. <br> And lo! my further course a stream cut off,<sup>1073</sup> <br> Which tow’rd the left hand with its little waves <br> Bent down the grass that on its margin sprang. <br> All waters that on earth most limpid are <br> Would seem to have within themselves some mixture <br> Compared with that which nothing doth conceal, <br> Although it moves on with a brown, brown current <br> Under the shade perpetual, that never <br> Ray of the sun lets in, nor of the moon. <br> With feet I stayed, and with mine eyes I passed <br> Beyond the rivulet, to look upon <br> The great variety of the fresh may. <br> And there appeared to me (even as appears <br> Suddenly something that doth turn aside <br> Through very wonder every other thought) <br> A lady all alone, who went along<sup>1074</sup> <br> Singing and culling floweret after floweret, <br> With which her pathway was all painted over. <br> “Ah, beauteous lady, who in rays of love <br> Dost warm thyself, if I may trust to looks, <br> Which the heart’s witnesses are wont to be, <br> May the desire come unto thee to draw <br> Near to this river’s bank,” I said to her, <br> “So much that I might hear what thou art singing. <br> Thou makest me remember where and what <br> Proserpina that moment was when lost<sup>1075</sup> <br> Her mother her, and she herself the Spring.” <br> As turns herself, with feet together pressed <br> And to the ground, a lady who is dancing, <br> And hardly puts one foot before the other, <br> On the vermilion and the yellow flowerets <br> She turned towards me, not in other wise <br> Than maiden who her modest eyes casts down; <br> And my entreaties made to be content, <br> So near approaching, that the dulcet sound <br> Came unto me together with its meaning. <br> As soon as she was where the grasses are <br> Bathed by the waters of the beauteous river, <br> To lift her eyes she granted me the boon. <br> I do not think there shone so great a light <br> Under the lids of Venus, when transfixed<sup>1076</sup> <br> By her own son, beyond his usual custom! <br> Erect upon the other bank she smiled, <br> Bearing full many colors in her hands, <br> Which that high land produces without seed. <br> Apart three paces did the river make us; <br> But Hellespont, where Xerxes passed across, <br> (A curb still to all human arrogance,)<sup>1077</sup> <br> More hatred from Leander did not suffer <br> For rolling between Sestos and Abydos, <br> Than that from me, because it oped not then. <br> “Ye are new-comers; and because I smile,” <br> Began she, “peradventure, in this place <br> Elect to human nature for its nest, <br> Some apprehension keeps you marvelling; <br> But the psalm <i>Delectasti</i> giveth light<sup>1078</sup> <br> Which has the power to uncloud your intellect. <br> And thou who foremost art, and didst entreat me, <br> Speak, if thou wouldst hear more; for I came ready <br> To all thy questionings, as far as needful.” <br> “The water,” said I, “and the forest’s sound, <br> Are combating within me my new faith <br> In something which I heard opposed to this.”<sup>1079</sup> <br> Whence she: “I will relate how from its cause <br> Proceedeth that which maketh thee to wonder, <br> And purge away the cloud that smites upon thee. <br> The Good Supreme, sole in itself delighting, <br> Created man good, and this goodly place <br> Gave him as hansel of eternal peace. <br> By his default short while he sojourned here;<sup>1080</sup> <br> By his default to weeping and to toil <br> He changed his innocent laughter and sweet play. <br> That the disturbance which below is made <br> By exhalations of the land and water, <br> (Which far as may be follow after heat,) <br> Might not upon mankind wage any war, <br> This mount ascended tow’rds the heaven so high, <br> And is exempt, from there where it is locked.<sup>1081</sup> <br> Now since the universal atmosphere <br> Turns in a circuit with the primal motion <br> Unless the circle is broken on some side, <br> Upon this height, that all is disengaged <br> In living ether, doth this motion strike <br> And make the forest sound, for it is dense; <br> And so much power the stricken plant possesses <br> That with its virtue it impregns the air, <br> And this, revolving, scatters it around; <br> And yonder earth, according as ’tis worthy <br> In self or in its clime, conceives and bears <br> Of divers qualities the divers trees; <br> It should not seem a marvel then on earth, <br> This being heard, whenever any plant <br> Without seed manifest there taketh root. <br> And thou must know, this holy table-land <br> In which thou art is full of every seed, <br> And fruit has in it never gathered there. <br> The water which thou seest springs not from vein <br> Restored by vapor that the cold condenses, <br> Like to a stream that gains or loses breath; <br> But issues from a fountain safe and certain, <br> Which by the will of God as much regains <br> As it discharges, open on two sides. <br> Upon this side with virtue it descends, <br> Which takes away all memory of sin; <br> On that, of every good deed done restores it. <br> Here Lethe, as upon the other side <br> Eunoë, it is called; and worketh not <br> If first on either side it be not tasted. <br> This every other savor doth transcend; <br> And notwithstanding slaked so far may be <br> Thy thirst, that I reveal to thee no more, <br> I’ll give thee a corollary still in grace, <br> Nor think my speech will be to thee less dear <br> If it spread out beyond my promise to thee. <br> Those who in ancient times have feigned in song <br> The Age of Gold and its felicity, <br> Dreamed of this place perhaps upon Parnassus. <br> Here was the human race in innocence; <br> Here evermore was Spring, and every fruit; <br> This is the nectar of which each one speaks.” <br> Then backward did I turn me wholly round <br> Unto my Poets, and saw that with a smile<sup>1082</sup> <br> They had been listening to these closing words; <br> Then to the beautiful lady turned mine eyes.</p>
