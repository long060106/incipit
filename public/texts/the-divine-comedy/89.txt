<p class="lede">The eagle discourses of salvation by faith.</p>
<p class="verse">Appeared before me with its wings outspread<sup>1711</sup> <br> The beautiful image that in sweet fruition <br> Made jubilant the interwoven souls; <br> Appeared a little ruby each, wherein <br> Ray of the sun was burning so enkindled <br> That each into mine eyes refracted it. <br> And what it now behoves me to retrace <br> Nor voice has e’er reported, nor ink written, <br> Nor was by fantasy e’er comprehended; <br> For speak I saw, and likewise heard, the beak, <br> And utter with its voice both <em>I</em> and <em>My</em>, <br> When in conception it was <em>We</em> and <em>Our</em>.<sup>1712</sup> <br> And it began: “Being just and merciful <br> Am I exalted here unto that glory <br> Which cannot be exceeded by desire; <br> And upon earth I left my memory <br> Such, that the evil-minded people there <br> Commend it, but continue not the story.” <br> So doth a single heat from many embers <br> Make itself felt, even as from many loves <br> Issued a single sound from out that image. <br> Whence I thereafter: “O perpetual flowers <br> Of the eternal joy, that only one <br> Make me perceive your odors manifold, <br> Exhaling, break within me the great fast <br> Which a long season has in hunger held me, <br> Not finding for it any food on earth. <br> Well do I know, that if in heaven its mirror<sup>1713</sup> <br> Justice Divine another realm doth make, <br> Yours apprehends it not through any veil. <br> You know how I attentively address me <br> To listen; and you know what is the doubt<sup>1714</sup> <br> That is in me so very old a fast.” <br> Even as a falcon, issuing from his hood, <br> Doth move his head, and with his wings applaud him, <br> Showing desire, and making himself fine, <br> Saw I become that standard, which of lauds<sup>1715</sup> <br> Was interwoven of the grace divine, <br> With such songs as he knows who there rejoices. <br> Then it began: “He who a compass turned<sup>1716</sup> <br> On the world’s outer verge, and who within it <br> Devised so much occult and manifest, <br> Could not the impress of his power so make <br> On all the universe, as that his Word<sup>1717</sup> <br> Should not remain in infinite excess. <br> And this makes certain that the first proud being, <br> Who was the paragon of every creature, <br> By not awaiting light fell immature.<sup>1718</sup> <br> And hence appears it, that each minor nature<sup>1719</sup> <br> Is scant receptacle unto that good <br> Which has no end, and by itself is measured. <br> In consequence our vision, which perforce <br> Must be some ray of that intelligence <br> With which all things whatever are replete,<sup>1720</sup> <br> Cannot in its own nature be so potent,<sup>1721</sup> <br> That it shall not its origin discern <br> Far beyond that which is apparent to it. <br> Therefore into the justice sempiternal <br> The power of vision that your world receives, <br> As eye into the ocean, penetrates; <br> Which, though it see the bottom near the shore, <br> Upon the deep perceives it not, and yet <br> ’Tis there, but it is hidden by the depth. <br> There is no light but comes from the serene <br> That never is o’ercast, nay, it is darkness<sup>1722</sup> <br> Or shadow of the flesh, or else its poison. <br> Amply to thee is opened now the cavern <br> Which has concealed from thee the living justice <br> Of which thou mad’st such frequent questioning. <br> For saidst thou: ‘Born a man is on the shore <br> Of Indus, and is none who there can speak <br> Of Christ, nor who can read, nor who can write; <br> And all his inclinations and his actions <br> Are good, so far as human reason sees, <br> Without a sin in life or in discourse: <br> He dieth unbaptised and without faith; <br> Where is this justice that condemneth him? <br> Where is his fault, if he do not believe?’ <br> Now who art thou, that on the bench wouldst sit <br> In judgment at a thousand miles away, <br> With the short vision of a single span? <br> Truly to him who with me subtilizes, <br> If so the Scripture were not over you, <br> For doubting there were marvellous occasion. <br> O animals terrene, O stolid minds, <br> The primal will, that in itself is good, <br> Ne’er from itself, the Good Supreme, has moved. <br> So much is just as is accordant with it; <br> No good created draws it to itself, <br> But it, by raying forth, occasions that.” <br> Even as above her nest goes circling round <br> The stork when she has fed her little ones, <br> And he who has been fed looks up at her, <br> So lifted I my brows, and even such <br> Became the blessed image, which its wings <br> Was moving, by so many counsels urged. <br> Circling around it sang, and said: “As are <br> My notes to thee, who dost not comprehend them, <br> Such is the eternal judgment to you mortals.” <br> Those lucent splendors of the Holy Spirit <br> Grew quiet then, but still within the standard <br> That made the Romans reverend to the world. <br> It recommenced: “Unto this kingdom never <br> Ascended one who had not faith in Christ,<sup>1723</sup> <br> Before or since he to the tree was nailed. <br> But look thou, many crying are, ‘Christ, Christ!’<sup>1724</sup> <br> Who at the judgment shall be far less near <br> To him than some shall be who knew not Christ.<sup>1725</sup> <br> Such Christians shall the Ethiop condemn,<sup>1726</sup> <br> When the two companies shall be divided,<sup>1727</sup> <br> The one forever rich, the other poor. <br> What to your kings may not the Persians say, <br> When they that volume opened shall behold<sup>1728</sup> <br> In which are written down all their dispraises? <br> There shall be seen, among the deeds of Albert,<sup>1729</sup> <br> That which ere long shall set the pen in motion, <br> For which the realm of Prague shall be deserted. <br> There shall be seen the woe that on the Seine <br> He brings by falsifying of the coin, <br> Who by the blow of a wild boar shall die.<sup>1730</sup> <br> There shall be seen the pride that causes thirst, <br> Which makes the Scot and Englishman so mad<sup>1731</sup> <br> That they within their boundaries cannot rest; <br> Be seen the luxury and effeminate life <br> Of him of Spain, and the Bohemian,<sup>1732</sup> <br> Who valor never knew and never wished; <br> Be seen the Cripple of Jerusalem,<sup>1733</sup> <br> His goodness represented by an I, <br> While the reverse an <b>m</b> shall represent; <br> Be seen the avarice and poltroonery <br> Of him who guards the Island of the Fire,<sup>1734</sup> <br> Wherein Anchises finished his long life;<sup>1735</sup> <br> And to declare how pitiful he is <br> Shall be his record in contracted letters<sup>1736</sup> <br> Which shall make note of much in little space. <br> And shall appear to each one the foul deeds <br> Of uncle and of brother who a nation<sup>1737</sup> <br> So famous have dishonored, and two crowns. <br> And he of Portugal and he of Norway<sup>1738</sup> <br> Shall there be known, and he of Rascia too,<sup>1739</sup> <br> Who saw in evil hour the coin of Venice.<sup>1740</sup> <br> O happy Hungary, if she let herself<sup>1741</sup> <br> Be wronged no farther! and Navarre the happy, <br> If with the hills that gird her she be armed!<sup>1742</sup> <br> And each one may believe that now, as hansel<sup>1743</sup> <br> Thereof, do Nicosia and Famagosta<sup>1744</sup> <br> Lament and rage because of their own beast, <br> Who from the others’ flank departeth not.”<sup>1745</sup></p>
