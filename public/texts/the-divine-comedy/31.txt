<p class="lede">Other falsifiers or forgers⁠—Gianni Schicchi, Myrrha, Adam of Brescia, Potiphar’s wife, and Sinon of Troy.</p>
<p class="verse">’Twas at the time when Juno was enraged,<sup>449</sup> <br> For Semele, against the Theban blood, <br> As she already more than once had shown, <br> So reft of reason Athamas became,<sup>450</sup> <br> That, seeing his own wife with children twain <br> Walking encumbered upon either hand, <br> He cried: “Spread out the nets, that I may take <br> The lioness and her whelps upon the passage”; <br> And then extended his unpitying claws, <br> Seizing the first, who had the name Learchus, <br> And whirled him round, and dashed him on a rock; <br> And she, with the other burthen, drowned herself;⁠— <br> And at the time when fortune downward hurled <br> The Trojan’s arrogance, that all things dared, <br> So that the king was with his kingdom crushed, <br> Hecuba sad, disconsolate, and captive,<sup>451</sup> <br> When lifeless she beheld Polyxena, <br> And of her Polydorus on the shore <br> Of ocean was the dolorous one aware, <br> Out of her senses like a dog she barked, <br> So much the anguish had her mind distorted; <br> But not of Thebes the furies nor the Trojan <br> Were ever seen in any one so cruel <br> In goading beasts, and much more human members, <br> As I beheld two shadows pale and naked, <br> Who, biting, in the manner ran along <br> That a boar does, when from the sty turned loose. <br> One to Capocchio came, and by the nape <br> Seized with its teeth his neck, so that in dragging <br> It made his belly grate the solid bottom. <br> And the Aretine, who trembling had remained,<sup>452</sup> <br> Said to me: “That mad sprite is Gianni Schicchi, <br> And raving goes thus harrying other people.” <br> “O,” said I to him, “so may not the other <br> Set teeth on thee, let it not weary thee <br> To tell us who it is, ere it dart hence.” <br> And he to me: “That is the ancient ghost <br> Of the nefarious Myrrha, who became <br> Beyond all rightful love her father’s lover. <br> She came to sin with him after this manner, <br> By counterfeiting of another’s form; <br> As he who goeth yonder undertook,<sup>453</sup> <br> That he might gain the lady of the herd, <br> To counterfeit in himself Buoso Donati, <br> Making a will and giving it due form.” <br> And after the two maniacs had passed <br> On whom I held mine eye, I turned it back <br> To look upon the other evil-born. <br> I saw one made in fashion of a lute, <br> If he had only had the groin cut off <br> Just at the point at which a man is forked. <br> The heavy dropsy, that so disproportions <br> The limbs with humors, which it ill concocts, <br> That the face corresponds not to the belly, <br> Compelled him so to hold his lips apart <br> As does the hectic, who because of thirst <br> One tow’rds the chin, the other upward turns. <br> “O ye, who without any torment are, <br> And why I know not, in the world of woe,” <br> He said to us, “behold, and be attentive <br> Unto the misery of Master Adam;<sup>454</sup> <br> I had while living much of what I wished, <br> And now, alas! a drop of water crave. <br> The rivulets, that from the verdant hills<sup>455</sup> <br> Of Cassentin descend down into Arno,<sup>456</sup> <br> Making their channels to be cold and moist, <br> Ever before me stand, and not in vain; <br> For far more doth their image dry me up <br> Than the disease which strips my face of flesh. <br> The rigid justice that chastises me <br> Draweth occasion from the place in which <br> I sinned, to put the more my sighs in flight. <br> There is Romena, where I counterfeited<sup>457</sup> <br> The currency imprinted with the Baptist, <br> For which I left my body burned above. <br> But if I here could see the tristful soul <br> Of Guido, or Alessandro, or their brother, <br> For Branda’s fount I would not give the sight. <br> One is within already, if the raving <br> Shades that are going round about speak truth; <br> But what avails it me, whose limbs are tied? <br> If I were only still so light, that in <br> A hundred years I could advance one inch, <br> I had already started on the way, <br> Seeking him out among this squalid folk, <br> Although the circuit be eleven miles,<sup>458</sup> <br> And be not less than half a mile across. <br> For them am I in such a family; <br> They did induce me into coining florins, <br> Which had three carats of impurity.” <br> And I to him: “Who are the two poor wretches <br> That smoke like unto a wet hand in winter, <br> Lying there close upon thy right-hand confines?” <br> “I found them here,” replied he, “when I rained <br> Into this chasm, and since they have not turned, <br> Nor do I think they will for evermore. <br> One the false woman is who accused Joseph,<sup>459</sup> <br> The other the false Sinon, Greek of Troy;<sup>460</sup> <br> From acute fever they send forth such reek.” <br> And one of them, who felt himself annoyed <br> At being, peradventure, named so darkly, <br> Smote with the fist upon his hardened paunch. <br> It gave a sound, as if it were a drum;<sup>461</sup> <br> And Master Adam smote him in the face, <br> With arm that did not seem to be less hard, <br> Saying to him: “Although be taken from me <br> All motion, for my limbs that heavy are, <br> I have an arm unfettered for such need.” <br> Whereat he answer made: “When thou didst go <br> Unto the fire, thou hadst it not so ready; <br> But hadst it so and more when thou wast coining.” <br> The dropsical: “Thou sayest true in that; <br> But thou wast not so true a witness there, <br> Where thou wast questioned of the truth at Troy.” <br> “If I spake false, thou falsifiedst the coin,” <br> Said Sinon; “and for one fault I am here, <br> And thou for more than any other demon.” <br> “Remember, perjurer, about the horse,” <br> He made reply who had the swollen belly, <br> “And rueful be it thee the whole world knows it.” <br> “Rueful to thee the thirst be wherewith cracks <br> Thy tongue,” the Greek said, “and the putrid water <br> That hedges so thy paunch before thine eyes.” <br> Then the false-coiner: “So is gaping wide <br> Thy mouth for speaking evil, as ’tis wont; <br> Because if I have thirst, and humour stuff me, <br> Thou hast the burning and the head that aches, <br> And to lick up the mirror of Narcissus<sup>462</sup> <br> Thou wouldst not want words many to invite thee.” <br> In listening to them was I wholly fixed, <br> When said the Master to me: “Now just look, <br> For little wants it that I quarrel with thee.” <br> When him I heard in anger speak to me, <br> I turned me round towards him with such shame <br> That still it eddies through my memory. <br> And as he is who dreams of his own harm, <br> Who dreaming wishes it may be a dream, <br> So that he craves what is, as if it were not; <br> Such I became, not having power to speak, <br> For to excuse myself I wished, and still <br> Excused myself, and did not think I did it. <br> “Less shame doth wash away a greater fault,” <br> The Master said, “than this of thine has been; <br> Therefore thyself disburden of all sadness, <br> And make account that I am aye beside thee, <br> If e’er it come to pass that fortune bring thee <br> Where there are people in a like dispute; <br> For a base wish it is to wish to hear it.”</p>
