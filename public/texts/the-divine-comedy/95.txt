<p class="lede">St. James examines Dante upon Hope.</p>
<p class="verse">If e’er it happen that the Poem Sacred,<sup>1864</sup> <br> To which both heaven and earth have set their hand, <br> So that it many a year hath made me lean, <br> O’ercome the cruelty that bars me out <br> From the fair sheepfold, where a lamb I slumbered,<sup>1865</sup> <br> An enemy to the wolves that war upon it, <br> With other voice forthwith, with other fleece<sup>1866</sup> <br> Poet will I return, and at my font<sup>1867</sup> <br> Baptismal will I take the laurel crown; <br> Because into the Faith that maketh known <br> All souls to God there entered I, and then <br> Peter for her sake thus my brow encircled.<sup>1868</sup> <br> Thereafterward towards us moved a light <br> Out of that band whence issued the first-fruits<sup>1869</sup> <br> Which of his vicars Christ behind him left, <br> And then my Lady, full of ecstasy, <br> Said unto me: “Look, look! behold the Baron<sup>1870</sup> <br> For whom below Galicia is frequented.” <br> In the same way as, when a dove alights <br> Near his companion, both of them pour forth, <br> Circling about and murmuring, their affection, <br> So one beheld I by the other grand <br> Prince glorified to be with welcome greeted, <br> Lauding the food that there above is eaten. <br> But when their gratulations were complete, <br> Silently <i>coram me</i> each one stood still,<sup>1871</sup> <br> So incandescent it o’ercame my sight. <br> Smiling thereafterwards, said Beatrice: <br> “Illustrious life, by whom the benefactions<sup>1872</sup> <br> Of our Basilica have been described, <br> Make Hope resound within this altitude; <br> Thou knowest as oft thou dost personify it<sup>1873</sup> <br> As Jesus to the three gave greater clearness.”⁠— <br> “Lift up thy head, and make thyself assured;<sup>1874</sup> <br> For what comes hither from the mortal world <br> Must needs be ripened in our radiance.”<sup>1875</sup> <br> This comfort came to me from the second fire; <br> Wherefore mine eyes I lifted to the hills,<sup>1876</sup> <br> Which bent them down before with too great weight. <br> “Since, through his grace, our Emperor wills that thou <br> Shouldst find thee face to face, before thy death, <br> In the most secret chamber, with his Counts,<sup>1877</sup> <br> So that, the truth beholden of this court, <br> Hope, which below there rightfully enamours, <br> Thereby thou strengthen in thyself and others, <br> Say what it is, and how is flowering with it <br> Thy mind, and say from whence it came to thee.” <br> Thus did the second light again continue. <br> And the Compassionate, who piloted<sup>1878</sup> <br> The plumage of my wings in such high flight, <br> Did in reply anticipate me thus: <br> “No child whatever the Church Militant <br> Of greater hope possesses, as is written <br> In that Sun which irradiates all our band;<sup>1879</sup> <br> Therefore it is conceded him from Egypt <br> To come into Jerusalem to see, <br> Or ever yet his warfare be completed. <br> The two remaining points, that not for knowledge<sup>1880</sup> <br> Have been demanded, but that he report <br> How much this virtue unto thee is pleasing, <br> To him I leave; for hard he will not find them, <br> Nor of self-praise; and let him answer them;<sup>1881</sup> <br> And may the grace of God in this assist him!” <br> As a disciple, who his teacher follows, <br> Ready and willing, where he is expert, <br> That his proficiency may be displayed, <br> “Hope,” said I, “is the certain expectation<sup>1882</sup> <br> Of future glory, which is the effect <br> Of grace divine and merit precedent. <br> From many stars this light comes unto me; <br> But he instilled it first into my heart <br> Who was chief singer unto the chief captain.<sup>1883</sup> <br> <i>Sperent in te</i>, in the high Theody<sup>1884</sup> <br> He sayeth, ‘those who know thy name’; and who <br> Knoweth it not, if he my faith possess? <br> Thou didst instil me, then, with his instilling <br> In the Epistle, so that I am full, <br> And upon others rain again your rain.”<sup>1885</sup> <br> While I was speaking, in the living bosom <br> Of that combustion quivered an effulgence, <br> Sudden and frequent, in the guise of lightning; <br> Then breathed: “The love wherewith I am inflamed <br> Towards the virtue still which followed me <br> Unto the palm and issue of the field,<sup>1886</sup> <br> Wills that I breathe to thee that thou delight <br> In her; and grateful to me is thy telling <br> Whatever things Hope promises to thee.” <br> And I: “The ancient Scriptures and the new <br> The mark establish, and this shows it me,<sup>1887</sup> <br> Of all the souls whom God hath made his friends.<sup>1888</sup> <br> Isaiah saith, that each one garmented<sup>1889</sup> <br> In his own land shall be with twofold garments, <br> And his own land is this delightful life. <br> Thy brother, too, far more explicitly, <br> There where he treateth of the robes of white,<sup>1890</sup> <br> This revelation manifests to us.” <br> And first, and near the ending of these words, <br> “<i>Sperent in te</i>” from over us was heard, <br> To which responsive answered all the carols. <br> Thereafterward a light among them brightened,<sup>1891</sup> <br> So that, if Cancer one such crystal had,<sup>1892</sup> <br> Winter would have a month of one sole day. <br> And as uprises, goes, and enters the dance <br> A winsome maiden, only to do honor <br> To the new bride, and not from any failing,<sup>1893</sup> <br> Even thus did I behold the brightened splendor <br> Approach the two, who in a wheel revolved<sup>1894</sup> <br> As was beseeming to their ardent love. <br> Into the song and music there it entered; <br> And fixed on them my Lady kept her look, <br> Even as a bride silent and motionless. <br> “This is the one who lay upon the breast <br> Of him our Pelican; and this is he<sup>1895</sup> <br> To the great office from the cross elected.”<sup>1896</sup> <br> My Lady thus; but therefore none the more <br> Did move her sight from its attentive gaze <br> Before or afterward these words of hers. <br> Even as a man who gazes, and endeavors <br> To see the eclipsing of the sun a little, <br> And who, by seeing, sightless doth become, <br> So I became before that latest fire,<sup>1897</sup> <br> While it was said, “Why dost thou daze thyself <br> To see a thing which here hath no existence? <br> Earth in the earth my body is, and shall be <br> With all the others there, until our number <br> With the eternal proposition tallies.<sup>1898</sup> <br> With the two garments in the blessed cloister<sup>1899</sup> <br> Are the two lights alone that have ascended:<sup>1900</sup> <br> And this shalt thou take back into your world.” <br> And at this utterance the flaming circle <br> Grew quiet, with the dulcet intermingling <br> Of sound that by the trinal breath was made,<sup>1901</sup> <br> As to escape from danger or fatigue <br> The oars that erst were in the water beaten <br> Are all suspended at a whistle’s sound. <br> Ah, how much in my mind was I disturbed, <br> When I turned round to look on Beatrice, <br> That her I could not see, although I was<sup>1902</sup> <br> Close at her side and in the Happy World!</p>
