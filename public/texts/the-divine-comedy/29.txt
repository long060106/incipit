<p class="lede">The Ninth Bolgia: schismatics⁠—Mahomet and Ali⁠—Pier da Medicina, Curio, Mosca, and Bertrand de Born.</p>
<p class="verse">Who ever could, e’en with untrammelled words,<sup>414</sup> <br> Tell of the blood and of the wounds in full <br> Which now I saw, by many times narrating? <br> Each tongue would for a certainty fall short <br> By reason of our speech and memory, <br> That have small room to comprehend so much. <br> If were again assembled all the people <br> Which formerly upon the fateful land <br> Of Puglia were lamenting for their blood<sup>415</sup> <br> Shed by the Romans and the lingering war<sup>416</sup> <br> That of the rings made such illustrious spoils,<sup>417</sup> <br> As Livy has recorded, who errs not, <br> With those who felt the agony of blows <br> By making counterstand to Robert Guiscard,<sup>418</sup> <br> And all the rest, whose bones are gathered still <br> At Ceperano, where a renegade<sup>419</sup> <br> Was each Apulian, and at Tagliacozzo,<sup>420</sup> <br> Where without arms the old Alardo conquered, <br> And one his limb transpierced, and one lopped off, <br> Should show, it would be nothing to compare <br> With the disgusting mode of the ninth Bolgia. <br> A cask by losing centre-piece or cant <br> Was never shattered so, as I saw one <br> Rent from the chin to where one breaketh wind. <br> Between his legs were hanging down his entrails; <br> His heart was visible, and the dismal sack <br> That maketh excrement of what is eaten. <br> While I was all absorbed in seeing him, <br> He looked at me, and opened with his hands <br> His bosom, saying: “See now how I rend me; <br> How mutilated, see, is Mahomet;<sup>421</sup> <br> In front of me doth Ali weeping go, <br> Cleft in the face from forelock unto chin; <br> And all the others whom thou here beholdest, <br> Disseminators of scandal and of schism <br> While living were, and therefore are cleft thus. <br> A devil is behind here, who doth cleave us <br> Thus cruelly, unto the falchion’s edge <br> Putting again each one of all this ream, <br> When we have gone around the doleful road; <br> By reason that our wounds are closed again <br> Ere any one in front of him repass. <br> But who art thou, that musest on the crag, <br> Perchance to postpone going to the pain <br> That is adjudged upon thine accusations?” <br> “Nor death hath reached him yet, nor guilt doth bring him,” <br> My Master made reply, “to be tormented; <br> But to procure him full experience, <br> Me, who am dead, behoves it to conduct him <br> Down here through Hell, from circle unto circle; <br> And this is true as that I speak to thee.” <br> More than a hundred were there when they heard him, <br> Who in the moat stood still to look at me, <br> Through wonderment oblivious of their torture. <br> “Now say to Fra Dolcino, then, to arm him,<sup>422</sup> <br> Thou, who perhaps wilt shortly see the sun, <br> If soon he wish not here to follow me, <br> So with provisions, that no stress of snow <br> May give the victory to the Novarese,<sup>423</sup> <br> Which otherwise to gain would not be easy.” <br> After one foot to go away he lifted, <br> This word did Mahomet say unto me, <br> Then to depart upon the ground he stretched it. <br> Another one, who had his throat pierced through, <br> And nose cut off close underneath the brows, <br> And had no longer but a single ear, <br> Staying to look in wonder with the others, <br> Before the others did his gullet open, <br> Which outwardly was red in every part, <br> And said: “O thou, whom guilt doth not condemn, <br> And whom I once saw up in Latian land, <br> Unless too great similitude deceive me, <br> Call to remembrance Pier da Medicina,<sup>424</sup> <br> If e’er thou see again the lovely plain<sup>425</sup> <br> That from Vercelli slopes to Marcabò, <br> And make it known to the best two of Fano,<sup>426</sup> <br> To Messer Guido and Angiolello likewise, <br> That if foreseeing here be not in vain, <br> Cast over from their vessel shall they be, <br> And drowned near unto the Cattolica, <br> By the betrayal of a tyrant fell. <br> Between the isles of Cyprus and Majorca <br> Neptune ne’er yet beheld so great a crime, <br> Neither of pirates nor Argolic people. <br> That traitor, who sees only with one eye,<sup>427</sup> <br> And holds the land, which someone here with me<sup>428</sup> <br> Would fain be fasting from the vision of, <br> Will make them come unto a parley with him; <br> Then will do so, that to Focara’s wind<sup>429</sup> <br> They will not stand in need of vow or prayer.” <br> And I to him: “Show to me and declare, <br> If thou wouldst have me bear up news of thee, <br> Who is this person of the bitter vision.” <br> Then did he lay his hand upon the jaw <br> Of one of his companions, and his mouth <br> Oped, crying: “This is he, and he speaks not. <br> This one, being banished, every doubt submerged <br> In Caesar by affirming the forearmed <br> Always with detriment allowed delay.” <br> O how bewildered unto me appeared, <br> With tongue asunder in his windpipe slit, <br> Curio, who in speaking was so bold!<sup>430</sup> <br> And one, who both his hands dissevered had, <br> The stumps uplifting through the murky air, <br> So that the blood made horrible his face, <br> Cried out: “Thou shalt remember Mosca also,<sup>431</sup> <br> Who said, alas! ‘A thing done has an end!’ <br> Which was an ill seed for the Tuscan people”; <br> “And death unto thy race,” thereto I added; <br> Whence he, accumulating woe on woe, <br> Departed, like a person sad and crazed. <br> But I remained to look upon the crowd; <br> And saw a thing which I should be afraid, <br> Without some further proof, even to recount, <br> If it were not that conscience reassures me, <br> That good companion which emboldens man <br> Beneath the hauberk of its feeling pure. <br> I truly saw, and still I seem to see it, <br> A trunk without a head walk in like manner <br> As walked the others of the mournful herd. <br> And by the hair it held the head dissevered, <br> Hung from the hand in fashion of a lantern, <br> And that upon us gazed and said: “O me!” <br> It of itself made to itself a lamp, <br> And they were two in one, and one in two; <br> How that can be, He knows who so ordains it. <br> When it was come close to the bridge’s foot, <br> It lifted high its arm with all the head, <br> To bring more closely unto us its words, <br> Which were: “Behold now the sore penalty, <br> Thou, who dost breathing go the dead beholding; <br> Behold if any be as great as this. <br> And so that thou may carry news of me, <br> Know that Bertram de Born am I, the same<sup>432</sup> <br> Who gave to the Young King the evil comfort.<sup>433</sup> <br> I made the father and the son rebellious; <br> Achitophel not more with Absalom<sup>434</sup> <br> And David did with his accursed goadings. <br> Because I parted persons so united, <br> Parted do I now bear my brain, alas! <br> From its beginning, which is in this trunk. <br> Thus is observed in me the counterpoise.”</p>
