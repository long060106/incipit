<p class="lede">Sordello.</p>
<p class="verse">Whene’er is broken up the game of Zara,<sup>604</sup> <br> He who has lost remains behind despondent, <br> The throws repeating, and in sadness learns; <br> The people with the other all depart; <br> One goes in front, and one behind doth pluck him, <br> And at his side one brings himself to mind; <br> He pauses not, and this and that one hears; <br> They crowd no more to whom his hand he stretches, <br> And from the throng he thus defends himself. <br> Even such was I in that dense multitude, <br> Turning to them this way and that my face, <br> And, promising, I freed myself therefrom. <br> There was the Aretine, who from the arms<sup>605</sup> <br> Untamed of Ghin di Tacco had his death,<sup>606</sup> <br> And he who fleeing from pursuit was drowned.<sup>607</sup> <br> There was imploring with his hands outstretched <br> Frederick Novello, and that one of Pisa<sup>608</sup> <br> Who made the good Marzucco seem so strong. <br> I saw Count Orso; and the soul divided<sup>609</sup> <br> By hatred and by envy from its body, <br> As it declared, and not for crime committed, <br> Pierre de la Brosse I say; and here provide<sup>610</sup> <br> While still on earth the Lady of Brabant, <br> So that for this she be of no worse flock! <br> As soon as I was free from all those shades <br> Who only prayed that someone else may pray, <br> So as to hasten their becoming holy, <br> Began I: “It appears that thou deniest, <br> O light of mine, expressly in some text, <br> That orison can bend decree of Heaven;<sup>611</sup> <br> And ne’ertheless these people pray for this. <br> Might then their expectation bootless be? <br> Or is to me thy saying not quite clear?” <br> And he to me: “My writing is explicit, <br> And not fallacious is the hope of these, <br> If with sane intellect ’tis well regarded; <br> For top of judgment doth not vail itself,<sup>612</sup> <br> Because the fire of love fulfils at once <br> What he must satisfy who here installs him. <br> And there, where I affirmed that proposition, <br> Defect was not amended by a prayer, <br> Because the prayer from God was separate. <br> Verily, in so deep a questioning <br> Do not decide, unless she tell it thee, <br> Who light ’twixt truth and intellect shall be. <br> I know not if thou understand; I speak <br> Of Beatrice; her shalt thou see above, <br> Smiling and happy, on this mountain’s top.” <br> And I: “Good Leader, let us make more haste, <br> For I no longer tire me as before; <br> And see, e’en now the hill a shadow casts.”<sup>613</sup> <br> “We will go forward with this day,” he answered, <br> “As far as now is possible for us; <br> But otherwise the fact is than thou thinkest. <br> Ere thou art up there, thou shalt see return <br> Him, who now hides himself behind the hill, <br> So that thou dost not interrupt his rays. <br> But yonder there behold! a soul that stationed <br> All, all alone is looking hitherward; <br> It will point out to us the quickest way.” <br> We came up unto it; O Lombard soul, <br> How lofty and disdainful thou didst bear thee, <br> And grand and slow in moving of thine eyes! <br> Nothing whatever did it say to us, <br> But let us go our way, eying us only <br> After the manner of a couchant lion; <br> Still near to it Virgilius drew, entreating <br> That it would point us out the best ascent; <br> And it replied not unto his demand, <br> But of our native land and of our life <br> It questioned us; and the sweet Guide began: <br> “Mantua,”⁠—and the shade, all in itself recluse, <br> Rose tow’rds him from the place where first it was, <br> Saying: “O Mantuan, I am Sordello<sup>614</sup> <br> Of thine own land!” and one embraced the other. <br> Ah! servile Italy, grief’s hostelry!<sup>615</sup> <br> A ship without a pilot in great tempest! <br> No Lady thou of Provinces, but brothel! <br> That noble soul was so impatient, only <br> At the sweet sound of his own native land, <br> To make its citizen glad welcome there; <br> And now within thee are not without war <br> Thy living ones, and one doth gnaw the other <br> Of those whom one wall and one fosse shut in! <br> Search, wretched one, all round about the shores <br> Thy seaboard, and then look within thy bosom, <br> If any part of thee enjoyeth peace! <br> What boots it, that for thee Justinian <br> The bridle mend, if empty be the saddle?<sup>616</sup> <br> Withouten this the shame would be the less. <br> Ah! people, thou that oughtest to be devout, <br> And to let Caesar sit upon the saddle,<sup>617</sup> <br> If well thou hearest what God teacheth thee, <br> Behold how fell this wild beast has become, <br> Being no longer by the spur corrected, <br> Since thou hast laid thy hand upon the bridle. <br> O German Albert! who abandonest<sup>618</sup> <br> Her that has grown recalcitrant and savage, <br> And oughtest to bestride her saddle-bow, <br> May a just judgment from the stars down fall <br> Upon thy blood, and be it new and open, <br> That thy successor may have fear thereof; <br> Because thy father and thyself have suffered, <br> By greed of those transalpine lands distrained, <br> The garden of the empire to be waste. <br> Come and behold Montecchi and Cappelletti,<sup>619</sup> <br> Monaldi and Fillippeschi, careless man!<sup>620</sup> <br> Those sad already, and these doubt-depressed! <br> Come, cruel one! come and behold the oppression <br> Of thy nobility, and cure their wounds, <br> And thou shalt see how safe is Santafiore!<sup>621</sup> <br> Come and behold thy Rome, that is lamenting,<sup>622</sup> <br> Widowed, alone, and day and night exclaims, <br> “My Caesar, why hast thou forsaken me?” <br> Come and behold how loving are the people; <br> And if for us no pity moveth thee, <br> Come and be made ashamed of thy renown! <br> And if it lawful be, O Jove Supreme!<sup>623</sup> <br> Who upon earth for us wast crucified, <br> Are thy just eyes averted otherwhere? <br> Or preparation is’t, that, in the abyss <br> Of thine own counsel, for some good thou makest <br> From our perception utterly cut off? <br> For all the towns of Italy are full <br> Of tyrants, and becometh a Marcellus<sup>624</sup> <br> Each peasant churl who plays the partisan! <br> My Florence! well mayst thou contented be<sup>625</sup> <br> With this digression, which concerns thee not, <br> Thanks to thy people who such forethought take! <br> Many at heart have justice, but shoot slowly, <br> That unadvised they come not to the bow, <br> But on their very lips thy people have it! <br> Many refuse to bear the common burden; <br> But thy solicitous people answereth <br> Without being asked, and crieth: “I submit.” <br> Now be thou joyful, for thou hast good reason; <br> Thou affluent, thou in peace, thou full of wisdom! <br> If I speak true, the event conceals it not. <br> Athens and Lacedaemon, they who made <br> The ancient laws, and were so civilized, <br> Made towards living well a little sign <br> Compared with thee, who makest such fine-spun <br> Provisions, that to middle of November <br> Reaches not what thou in October spinnest. <br> How oft, within the time of thy remembrance, <br> Laws, money, offices, and usages <br> Hast thou remodelled, and renewed thy members?<sup>626</sup> <br> And if thou mind thee well, and see the light, <br> Thou shalt behold thyself like a sick woman, <br> Who cannot find repose upon her down, <br> But by her tossing wardeth off her pain.</p>
