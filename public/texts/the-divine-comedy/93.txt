<p class="lede">The triumph of Christ.</p>
<p class="verse">Even as a bird, ’mid the beloved leaves,<sup>1814</sup> <br> Quiet upon the nest of her sweet brood <br> Throughout the night, that hideth all things from us,<sup>1815</sup> <br> Who, that she may behold their longed-for looks <br> And find the food wherewith to nourish them, <br> In which, to her, grave labors grateful are, <br> Anticipates the time on open spray <br> And with an ardent longing waits the sun, <br> Gazing intent as soon as breaks the dawn: <br> Even thus my Lady standing was, erect <br> And vigilant, turned round towards the zone <br> Underneath which the sun displays less haste;<sup>1816</sup> <br> So that beholding her distraught and wistful, <br> Such I became as he is who desiring <br> For something yearns, and hoping is appeased. <br> But brief the space from one When to the other; <br> Of my awaiting, say I, and the seeing <br> The welkin grow resplendent more and more. <br> And Beatrice exclaimed: “Behold the hosts <br> Of Christ’s triumphal march, and all the fruit<sup>1817</sup> <br> Harvested by the rolling of these spheres!”<sup>1818</sup> <br> It seemed to me her face was all aflame; <br> And eyes she had so full of ecstasy <br> That I must needs pass on without describing. <br> As when in nights serene of the full moon <br> Smiles Trivia among the nymphs eternal<sup>1819</sup> <br> Who paint the firmament through all its gulfs, <br> Saw I, above the myriads of lamps, <br> A Sun that one and all of them enkindled,<sup>1820</sup> <br> E’en as our own doth the supernal sights,<sup>1821</sup> <br> And through the living light transparent shone <br> The lucent substance so intensely clear <br> Into my sight, that I sustained it not. <br> O Beatrice, thou gentle guide and dear! <br> To me she said: “What overmasters thee <br> A virtue is from which naught shields itself. <br> There are the wisdom and the omnipotence <br> That oped the thoroughfares ’twixt heaven and earth, <br> For which there erst had been so long a yearning.” <br> As fire from out a cloud unlocks itself, <br> Dilating so it finds not room therein, <br> And down, against its nature, falls to earth, <br> So did my mind, among those aliments <br> Becoming larger, issue from itself, <br> And that which it became cannot remember. <br> “Open thine eyes, and look at what I am:<sup>1822</sup> <br> Thou hast beheld such things, that strong enough <br> Hast thou become to tolerate my smile.” <br> I was as one who still retains the feeling <br> Of a forgotten vision, and endeavors <br> In vain to bring it back into his mind, <br> When I this invitation heard, deserving <br> Of so much gratitude, it never fades <br> Out of the book that chronicles the past. <br> If at this moment sounded all the tongues <br> That Polyhymnia and her sisters made<sup>1823</sup> <br> Most lubrical with their delicious milk, <br> To aid me, to a thousandth of the truth <br> It would not reach, singing the holy smile <br> And how the holy aspect it illumed. <br> And therefore, representing Paradise, <br> The sacred poem must perforce leap over, <br> Even as a man who finds his way cut off; <br> But whoso thinketh of the ponderous theme, <br> And of the mortal shoulder laden with it, <br> Should blame it not, if under this it tremble. <br> It is no passage for a little boat <br> This which goes cleaving the audacious prow, <br> Nor for a pilot who would spare himself. <br> “Why doth my face so much enamour thee,<sup>1824</sup> <br> That to the garden fair thou turnest not, <br> Which under the rays of Christ is blossoming? <br> There is the Rose in which the Word Divine<sup>1825</sup> <br> Became incarnate; there the lilies are<sup>1826</sup> <br> By whose perfume the good way was discovered.” <br> Thus Beatrice; and I, who to her counsels <br> Was wholly ready, once again betook me <br> Unto the battle of the feeble brows.<sup>1827</sup> <br> As in the sunshine, that unsullied streams <br> Through fractured cloud, ere now a meadow of flowers <br> Mine eyes with shadow covered o’er have seen, <br> So troops of splendors manifold I saw <br> Illumined from above with burning rays, <br> Beholding not the source of the effulgence. <br> O power benignant that dost so imprint them!<sup>1828</sup> <br> Thou didst exalt thyself to give more scope <br> There to mine eyes, that were not strong enough. <br> The name of that fair flower I e’er invoke<sup>1829</sup> <br> Morning and evening utterly enthralled <br> My soul to gaze upon the greater fire.<sup>1830</sup> <br> And when in both mine eyes depicted were <br> The glory and greatness of the living star<sup>1831</sup> <br> Which there excelleth, as it here excelled, <br> Athwart the heavens a little torch descended<sup>1832</sup> <br> Formed in a circle like a coronal, <br> And cinctured it, and whirled itself about it. <br> Whatever melody most sweetly soundeth <br> On earth, and to itself most draws the soul, <br> Would seem a cloud that, rent asunder, thunders, <br> Compared unto the sounding of that lyre <br> Wherewith was crowned the sapphire beautiful,<sup>1833</sup> <br> Which gives the clearest heaven its sapphire hue. <br> “I am Angelic Love, that circle round <br> The joy sublime which breathes from out the womb <br> That was the hostelry of our Desire;<sup>1834</sup> <br> And I shall circle, Lady of Heaven, while <br> Thou followest thy Son, and mak’st diviner <br> The sphere supreme, because thou enterest there.” <br> Thus did the circulated melody <br> Seal itself up; and all the other lights <br> Were making to resound the name of Mary. <br> The regal mantle of the volumes all<sup>1835</sup> <br> Of that world, which most fervid is and living <br> With breath of God and with his works and ways, <br> Extended over us its inner border,<sup>1836</sup> <br> So very distant, that the semblance of it <br> There where I was not yet appeared to me. <br> Therefore mine eyes did not possess the power <br> Of following the incoronated flame, <br> Which mounted upward near to its own seed.<sup>1837</sup> <br> And as a little child, that towards its mother <br> Stretches its arms, when it the milk has taken, <br> Through impulse kindled into outward flame, <br> Each of those gleams of whiteness upward reached <br> So with its summit, that the deep affection <br> They had for Mary was revealed to me. <br> Thereafter they remained there in my sight, <br> <i>Regina coeli</i> singing with such sweetness,<sup>1838</sup> <br> That ne’er from me has the delight departed. <br> O, what exuberance is garnered up <br> Within those richest coffers, which had been <br> Good husbandmen for sowing here below! <br> There they enjoy and live upon the treasure <br> Which was acquired while weeping in the exile <br> Of Babylon, wherein the gold was left.<sup>1839</sup> <br> There triumpheth, beneath the exalted Son <br> Of God and Mary, in his victory, <br> Both with the ancient council and the new, <br> He who doth keep the keys of such a glory.<sup>1840</sup></p>
