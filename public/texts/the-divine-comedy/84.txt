<p class="lede">The Fifth Heaven, or that of Mars, where are seen the spirits of martyrs, and of crusaders who died fighting for the true faith⁠—The Celestial Cross.</p>
<p class="verse">From centre unto rim, from rim to centre,<sup>1554</sup> <br> In a round vase the water moves itself,<sup>1555</sup> <br> As from without ’tis struck or from within. <br> Into my mind upon a sudden dropped <br> What I am saying, at the moment when <br> Silent became the glorious life of Thomas,<sup>1556</sup> <br> Because of the resemblance that was born <br> Of his discourse and that of Beatrice, <br> Whom, after him, it pleased thus to begin: <br> “This man has need (and does not tell you so, <br> Nor with the voice, nor even in his thought) <br> Of going to the root of one truth more. <br> Declare unto him if the light wherewith <br> Blossoms your substance shall remain with you <br> Eternally the same that it is now; <br> And if it do remain, say in what manner, <br> After ye are again made visible, <br> It can be that it injure not your sight.” <br> As by a greater gladness urged and drawn <br> They who are dancing in a ring sometimes <br> Uplift their voices and their motions quicken; <br> So, at that orison devout and prompt, <br> The holy circles a new joy displayed <br> In their revolving and their wondrous song. <br> Whoso lamenteth him that here we die <br> That we may live above, has never there <br> Seen the refreshment of the eternal rain. <br> The One and Two and Three who ever liveth,<sup>1557</sup> <br> And reigneth ever in Three and Two and One, <br> Not circumscribed and all things circumscribing, <br> Three several times was chanted by each one <br> Among those spirits, with such melody <br> That for all merit it were just reward; <br> And, in the lustre most divine of all <br> The lesser ring, I heard a modest voice,<sup>1558</sup> <br> Such as perhaps the Angel’s was to Mary, <br> Answer: “As long as the festivity <br> Of Paradise shall be, so long our love <br> Shall radiate round about us such a vesture. <br> Its brightness is proportioned to the ardor, <br> The ardor to the vision; and the vision <br> Equals what grace it has above its worth. <br> When, glorious and sanctified, our flesh <br> Is reassumed, then shall our persons be <br> More pleasing by their being all complete; <br> For will increase whate’er bestows on us <br> Of light gratuitous the Good Supreme, <br> Light which enables us to look on Him; <br> Therefore the vision must perforce increase, <br> Increase the ardor which from that is kindled, <br> Increase the radiance which from this proceeds. <br> But even as a coal that sends forth flame, <br> And by its vivid whiteness overpowers it <br> So that its own appearance it maintains, <br> Thus the effulgence that surrounds us now <br> Shall be o’erpowered in aspect by the flesh, <br> Which still today the earth doth cover up; <br> Nor can so great a splendor weary us, <br> For strong will be the organs of the body <br> To everything which hath the power to please us.” <br> So sudden and alert appeared to me <br> Both one and the other choir to say Amen, <br> That well they showed desire for their dead bodies; <br> Nor sole for them perhaps, but for the mothers, <br> The fathers, and the rest who had been dear <br> Or ever they became eternal flames. <br> And lo! all round about of equal brightness <br> Arose a lustre over what was there, <br> Like an horizon that is clearing up. <br> And as at rise of early eve begin <br> Along the welkin new appearances, <br> So that the sight seems real and unreal, <br> It seemed to me that new subsistences<sup>1559</sup> <br> Began there to be seen, and make a circle <br> Outside the other two circumferences. <br> O very sparkling of the Holy Spirit, <br> How sudden and incandescent it became <br> Unto mine eyes, that vanquished bore it not! <br> But Beatrice so beautiful and smiling <br> Appeared to me, that with the other sights <br> That followed not my memory I must leave her. <br> Then to uplift themselves mine eyes resumed <br> The power, and I beheld myself translated <br> To higher salvation with my Lady only. <br> Well was I ware that I was more uplifted <br> By the enkindled smiling of the star,<sup>1560</sup> <br> That seemed to me more ruddy than its wont.<sup>1561</sup> <br> With all my heart, and in that dialect<sup>1562</sup> <br> Which is the same in all, such holocaust <br> To God I made as the new grace beseemed; <br> And not yet from my bosom was exhausted <br> The ardor of sacrifice, before I knew <br> This offering was accepted and auspicious; <br> For with so great a lustre and so red <br> Splendors appeared to me in twofold rays, <br> I said: “O Helios who dost so adorn them!”<sup>1563</sup> <br> Even as distinct with less and greater lights <br> Glimmers between the two poles of the world <br> The Galaxy that maketh wise men doubt,<sup>1564</sup> <br> Thus constellated in the depths of Mars, <br> Those rays described the venerable sign<sup>1565</sup> <br> That quadrants joining in a circle make. <br> Here doth my memory overcome my genius; <br> For on that cross as levin gleamed forth Christ,<sup>1566</sup> <br> So that I cannot find ensample worthy; <br> But he who takes his cross and follows Christ <br> Again will pardon me what I omit, <br> Seeing in that aurora lighten Christ. <br> From horn to horn, and ’twixt the top and base,<sup>1567</sup> <br> Lights were in motion, brightly scintillating <br> As they together met and passed each other; <br> Thus level and aslant and swift and slow<sup>1568</sup> <br> We here behold, renewing still the sight, <br> The particles of bodies long and short, <br> Across the sunbeam move, wherewith is listed <br> Sometimes the shade, which for their own defence <br> People with cunning and with art contrive. <br> And as a lute and harp, accordant strung <br> With many strings, a dulcet tinkling make <br> To him by whom the notes are not distinguished, <br> So from the lights that there to me appeared <br> Upgathered through the cross a melody, <br> Which rapt me, not distinguishing the hymn. <br> Well was I ware it was of lofty laud, <br> Because there came to me, “Arise and conquer!”<sup>1569</sup> <br> As unto him who hears and comprehends not. <br> So much enamoured I became therewith, <br> That until then there was not anything <br> That e’er had fettered me with such sweet bonds. <br> Perhaps my word appears somewhat too bold, <br> Postponing the delight of those fair eyes, <br> Into which gazing my desire has rest; <br> But who bethinks him that the living seals<sup>1570</sup> <br> Of every beauty grow in power ascending, <br> And that I there had not turned round to those,<sup>1571</sup> <br> Can me excuse, if I myself accuse <br> To excuse myself, and see that I speak truly: <br> For here the holy joy is not disclosed, <br> Because ascending it becomes more pure.<sup>1572</sup></p>
