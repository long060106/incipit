<p class="lede">The First Circle⁠—Limbo, or the border land of the unbaptized⁠—The four poets, Homer, Horace, Ovid, and Lucan⁠—The noble castle of philosophy.</p>
<p class="verse">Broke the deep lethargy within my head<sup>58</sup> <br> A heavy thunder, so that I upstarted, <br> Like to a person who by force is wakened; <br> And round about I moved my rested eyes, <br> Uprisen erect, and steadfastly I gazed, <br> To recognise the place wherein I was. <br> True is it, that upon the verge I found me <br> Of the abysmal valley dolorous, <br> That gathers thunder of infinite ululations. <br> Obscure, profound it was, and nebulous,<sup>59</sup> <br> So that by fixing on its depths my sight <br> Nothing whatever I discerned therein. <br> “Let us descend now into the blind world,” <br> Began the Poet, pallid utterly; <br> “I will be first, and thou shalt second be.” <br> And I, who of his color was aware, <br> Said: “How shall I come, if thou art afraid, <br> Who ’rt wont to be a comfort to my fears?” <br> And he to me: “The anguish of the people <br> Who are below here in my face depicts <br> That pity which for terror thou hast taken. <br> Let us go on, for the long way impels us.” <br> Thus he went in, and thus he made me enter <br> The foremost circle that surrounds the abyss. <br> There, in so far as I had power to hear, <br> Were lamentations none, but only sighs, <br> That tremulous made the everlasting air. <br> And this arose from sorrow without torment,<sup>60</sup> <br> Which the crowds had, that many were and great, <br> Of infants and of women and of men.<sup>61</sup> <br> To me the Master good: “Thou dost not ask <br> What spirits these, which thou beholdest, are? <br> Now will I have thee know, ere thou go farther, <br> That they sinned not; and if they merit had, <br> ’Tis not enough, because they had not baptism <br> Which is the portal of the Faith thou holdest; <br> And if they were before Christianity, <br> In the right manner they adored not God; <br> And among such as these am I myself. <br> For such defects, and not for other guilt, <br> Lost are we, and are only so far punished, <br> That without hope we live on in desire.” <br> Great grief seized on my heart when this I heard, <br> Because some people of much worthiness <br> I knew, who in that Limbo were suspended. <br> “Tell me, my Master, tell me, thou my Lord,” <br> Began I, with desire of being certain <br> Of that Faith which o’ercometh every error, <br> “Came any one by his own merit hence, <br> Or by another’s, who was blessed thereafter?” <br> And he, who understood my covert speech, <br> Replied: “I was a novice in this state, <br> When I saw hither come a Mighty One,<sup>62</sup> <br> With sign of victory incoronate. <br> Hence he drew forth the shade of the First Parent, <br> And that of his son Abel, and of Noah, <br> Of Moses the lawgiver, and the obedient <br> Abraham, patriarch, and David, king, <br> Israel with his father and his children, <br> And Rachel, for whose sake he did so much, <br> And others many, and he made them blessed; <br> And thou must know, that earlier than these <br> Never were any human spirits saved.” <br> We ceased not to advance because he spake, <br> But still were passing onward through the forest, <br> The forest, say I, of thick-crowded ghosts. <br> Not very far as yet our way had gone <br> This side the summit, when I saw a fire <br> That overcame a hemisphere of darkness. <br> We were a little distant from it still, <br> But not so far that I in part discerned not <br> That honorable people held that place.<sup>63</sup> <br> “O thou who honorest every art and science, <br> Who may these be, which such great honor have, <br> That from the fashion of the rest it parts them?” <br> And he to me: “The honorable name, <br> That sounds of them above there in thy life, <br> Wins grace in Heaven, that so advances them.” <br> In the meantime a voice was heard by me: <br> “All honor be to the preeminent Poet; <br> His shade returns again, that was departed.” <br> After the voice had ceased and quiet was, <br> Four mighty shades I saw approaching us; <br> Semblance had they nor sorrowful nor glad. <br> To say to me began my gracious Master: <br> “Him with that falchion in his hand behold,<sup>64</sup> <br> Who comes before the three, even as their lord. <br> That one is Homer, Poet sovereign; <br> He who comes next is Horace, the satirist; <br> The third is Ovid, and the last is Lucan. <br> Because to each of these with me applies <br> The name that solitary voice proclaimed, <br> They do me honor, and in that do well.”<sup>65</sup> <br> Thus I beheld assemble the fair school <br> Of that lord of the song preeminent, <br> Who o’er the others like an eagle soars. <br> When they together had discoursed somewhat, <br> They turned to me with signs of salutation, <br> And on beholding this, my Master smiled; <br> And more of honor still, much more, they did me,<sup>66</sup> <br> In that they made me one of their own band; <br> So that the sixth was I, ’mid so much wit. <br> Thus we went on as far as to the light, <br> Things saying ’tis becoming to keep silent, <br> As was the saying of them where I was. <br> We came unto a noble castle’s foot,<sup>67</sup> <br> Seven times encompassed with lofty walls, <br> Defended round by a fair rivulet; <br> This we passed over even as firm ground; <br> Through portals seven I entered with these Sages; <br> We came into a meadow of fresh verdure. <br> People were there with solemn eyes and slow, <br> Of great authority in their countenance; <br> They spake but seldom, and with gentle voices. <br> Thus we withdrew ourselves upon one side <br> Into an opening luminous and lofty, <br> So that they all of them were visible. <br> There opposite, upon the green enamel,<sup>68</sup> <br> Were pointed out to me the mighty spirits, <br> Whom to have seen I feel myself exalted. <br> I saw Electra with companions many, <br> ’Mongst whom I knew both Hector and Aeneas, <br> Caesar in armor with gerfalcon eyes; <br> I saw Camilla and Penthesilea <br> On the other side, and saw the King Latinus, <br> Who with Lavinia his daughter sat; <br> I saw that Brutus who drove Tarquin forth, <br> Lucretia, Julia, Marcia, and Cornelia,<sup>69</sup> <br> And saw alone, apart, the Saladin.<sup>70</sup> <br> When I had lifted up my brows a little, <br> The Master I beheld of those who know, <br> Sit with his philosophic family. <br> All gaze upon him, and all do him honor. <br> There I beheld both Socrates and Plato, <br> Who nearer him before the others stand; <br> Democritus, who puts the world on chance, <br> Diogenes, Anaxagoras, and Thales, <br> Zeno, Empedocles, and Heraclitus; <br> Of qualities I saw the good collector, <br> Hight Dioscorides; and Orpheus saw I, <br> Tully and Livy, and moral Seneca, <br> Euclid, geometrician, and Ptolemy, <br> Galen, Hippocrates, and Avicenna,<sup>71</sup> <br> Averroes, who the great Comment made.<sup>72</sup> <br> I cannot all of them portray in full, <br> Because so drives me onward the long theme, <br> That many times the word comes short of fact. <br> The sixfold company in two divides; <br> Another way my sapient Guide conducts me <br> Forth from the quiet to the air that trembles; <br> And to a place I come where nothing shines.</p>
