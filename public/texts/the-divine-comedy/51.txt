<p class="lede">The Third Circle⁠—The Irascible.</p>
<p class="verse">As much as ’twixt the close of the third hour<sup>816</sup> <br> And dawn of day appeareth of that sphere <br> Which aye in fashion of a child is playing, <br> So much it now appeared, towards the night, <br> Was of his course remaining to the sun; <br> There it was evening, and ’twas midnight here; <br> And the rays smote the middle of our faces, <br> Because by us the mount was so encircled, <br> That straight towards the west we now were going; <br> When I perceived my forehead overpowered <br> Beneath the splendor far more than at first, <br> And stupor were to me the things unknown; <br> Whereat towards the summit of my brow <br> I raised my hands, and made myself the visor <br> Which the excessive glare diminishes. <br> As when from off the water, or a mirror, <br> The sunbeam leaps unto the opposite side, <br> Ascending upward in the selfsame measure <br> That it descends, and deviates as far <br> From falling of a stone in line direct,<sup>817</sup> <br> (As demonstrate experiment and art,) <br> So it appeared to me that by a light <br> Refracted there before me I was smitten; <br> On which account my sight was swift to flee. <br> “What is that, Father sweet, from which I cannot <br> So fully screen my sight that it avail me,” <br> Said I, “and seems towards us to be moving?” <br> “Marvel thou not, if dazzle thee as yet <br> The family of heaven,” he answered me; <br> “An angel ’tis, who comes to invite us upward. <br> Soon will it be, that to behold these things <br> Shall not be grievous, but delightful to thee <br> As much as nature fashioned thee to feel.” <br> When we had reached the Angel benedight, <br> With joyful voice he said: “Here enter in <br> To stairway far less steep than are the others.” <br> We mounting were, already thence departed, <br> And “<i>Beati misericordes</i>” was<sup>818</sup> <br> Behind us sung, “Rejoice, thou that o’ercomest!”<sup>819</sup> <br> My Master and myself, we two alone <br> Were going upward, and I thought, in going, <br> Some profit to acquire from words of his; <br> And I to him directed me, thus asking: <br> “What did the spirit of Romagna mean, <br> Mentioning interdict and partnership?” <br> Whence he to me: “Of his own greatest failing <br> He knows the harm; and therefore wonder not <br> If he reprove us, that we less may rue it. <br> Because are thither pointed your desires <br> Where by companionship each share is lessened, <br> Envy doth ply the bellows to your sighs. <br> But if the love of the supernal sphere <br> Should upwardly direct your aspiration, <br> There would not be that fear within your breast; <br> For there, as much the more as one says <em>Our</em>,<sup>820</sup> <br> So much the more of good each one possesses, <br> And more of charity in that cloister burns.” <br> “I am more hungering to be satisfied,” <br> I said, “than if I had before been silent, <br> And more of doubt within my mind I gather. <br> How can it be, that boon distributed <br> The more possessors can more wealthy make <br> Therein, than if by few it be possessed?” <br> And he to me: “Because thou fixest still <br> Thy mind entirely upon earthly things, <br> Thou pluckest darkness from the very light. <br> That goodness infinite and ineffable<sup>821</sup> <br> Which is above there, runneth unto love, <br> As to a lucid body comes the sunbeam. <br> So much it gives itself as it finds ardor,<sup>822</sup> <br> So that as far as charity extends, <br> O’er it increases the eternal valor. <br> And the more people thitherward aspire, <br> More are there to love well, and more they love there, <br> And, as a mirror, one reflects the other. <br> And if my reasoning appease thee not, <br> Thou shalt see Beatrice; and she will fully <br> Take from thee this and every other longing. <br> Endeavour, then, that soon may be extinct, <br> As are the two already, the five wounds <br> That close themselves again by being painful.” <br> Even as I wished to say, “Thou dost appease me,” <br> I saw that I had reached another circle, <br> So that my eager eyes made me keep silence. <br> There it appeared to me that in a vision <br> Ecstatic on a sudden I was rapt, <br> And in a temple many persons saw; <br> And at the door a woman, with the sweet <br> Behavior of a mother, saying: “Son,<sup>823</sup> <br> Why in this manner hast thou dealt with us? <br> Lo, sorrowing, thy father and myself <br> Were seeking for thee”;⁠—and as here she ceased, <br> That which appeared at first had disappeared. <br> Then I beheld another with those waters <br> Adown her cheeks which grief distils whenever <br> From great disdain of others it is born, <br> And saying: “If of that city thou art lord,<sup>824</sup> <br> For whose name was such strife among the gods, <br> And whence doth every science scintillate, <br> Avenge thyself on those audacious arms <br> That clasped our daughter, O Pisistratus”;<sup>825</sup> <br> And the lord seemed to me benign and mild <br> To answer her with aspect temperate: <br> “What shall we do to those who wish us ill, <br> If he who loves us be by us condemned?” <br> Then saw I people hot in fire of wrath,<sup>826</sup> <br> With stones a young man slaying, clamorously <br> Still crying to each other, “Kill him! kill him!” <br> And him I saw bow down, because of death <br> That weighed already on him, to the earth, <br> But of his eyes made ever gates to heaven, <br> Imploring the high Lord, in so great strife, <br> That he would pardon those his persecutors, <br> With such an aspect as unlocks compassion. <br> Soon as my soul had outwardly returned <br> To things external to it which are true, <br> Did I my not false errors recognize.<sup>827</sup> <br> My Leader, who could see me bear myself <br> Like to a man that rouses him from sleep, <br> Exclaimed: “What ails thee, that thou canst not stand? <br> But hast been coming more than half a league <br> Veiling thine eyes, and with thy legs entangled, <br> In guise of one whom wine or sleep subdues?” <br> “O my sweet Father, if thou listen to me, <br> I’ll tell thee,” said I, “what appeared to me, <br> When thus from me my legs were ta’en away.” <br> And he: “If thou shouldst have a hundred masks <br> Upon thy face, from me would not be shut <br> Thy cogitations, howsoever small. <br> What thou hast seen was that thou mayst not fail <br> To ope thy heart unto the waters of peace, <br> Which from the eternal fountain are diffused. <br> I did not ask, ‘What ails thee?’ as he does <br> Who only looketh with the eyes that see not <br> When of the soul bereft the body lies, <br> But asked it to give vigor to thy feet; <br> Thus must we needs urge on the sluggards, slow <br> To use their wakefulness when it returns.” <br> We passed along, athwart the twilight peering <br> Forward as far as ever eye could stretch <br> Against the sunbeams serotine and lucent; <br> And lo! by slow degrees a smoke approached <br> In our direction, sombre as the night, <br> Nor was there place to hide one’s self therefrom. <br> This of our eyes and the pure air bereft us.</p>
