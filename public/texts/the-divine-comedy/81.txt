<p class="lede">St. Thomas Aquinas recounts the life of St. Francis.</p>
<p class="verse">O Thou insensate care of mortal men,<sup>1447</sup> <br> How inconclusive are the syllogisms <br> That make thee beat thy wings in downward flight! <br> One after laws and one to aphorisms<sup>1448</sup> <br> Was going, and one following the priesthood, <br> And one to reign by force or sophistry, <br> And one in theft, and one in state affairs, <br> One in the pleasures of the flesh involved <br> Wearied himself, one gave himself to ease; <br> When I, from all these things emancipate, <br> With Beatrice above there in the Heavens <br> With such exceeding glory was received! <br> When each one had returned unto that point <br> Within the circle where it was before, <br> It stood as in a candlestick a candle; <br> And from within the effulgence which at first<sup>1449</sup> <br> Had spoken unto me, I heard begin <br> Smiling while it more luminous became: <br> “Even as I am kindled in its ray, <br> So, looking into the Eternal Light,<sup>1450</sup> <br> The occasion of thy thoughts I apprehend. <br> Thou doubtest, and wouldst have me to resift <br> In language so extended and so open <br> My speech, that to thy sense it may be plain, <br> Where just before I said, ‘where well one fattens,’<sup>1451</sup> <br> And where I said, ‘there never rose a second’;<sup>1452</sup> <br> And here ’tis needful we distinguish well. <br> The Providence, which governeth the world <br> With counsel, wherein all created vision <br> Is vanquished ere it reach unto the bottom, <br> (So that towards her own Beloved might go <br> The bride of Him who, uttering a loud cry,<sup>1453</sup> <br> Espoused her with his consecrated blood, <br> Self-confident and unto Him more faithful,)<sup>1454</sup> <br> Two Princes did ordain in her behoof,<sup>1455</sup> <br> Which on this side and that might be her guide. <br> The one was all seraphical in ardor;<sup>1456</sup> <br> The other by his wisdom upon earth <br> A splendor was of light cherubical. <br> One will I speak of, for of both is spoken<sup>1457</sup> <br> In praising one, whichever may be taken, <br> Because unto one end their labors were. <br> Between Tupino and the stream that falls<sup>1458</sup> <br> Down from the hill elect of blessed Ubald, <br> A fertile slope of lofty mountain hangs, <br> From which Perugia feels the cold and heat <br> Through Porta Sole, and behind it weep <br> Gualdo and Nocera their grievous yoke. <br> From out that slope, there where it breaketh most <br> Its steepness, rose upon the world a sun<sup>1459</sup> <br> As this one does sometimes from out the Ganges;<sup>1460</sup> <br> Therefore let him who speaketh of that place, <br> Say not Ascesi, for he would say little,<sup>1461</sup> <br> But Orient, if he properly would speak. <br> He was not yet far distant from his rising <br> Before he had begun to make the earth <br> Some comfort from his mighty virtue feel. <br> For he in youth his father’s wrath incurred <br> For certain Dame, to whom, as unto death,<sup>1462</sup> <br> The gate of pleasure no one doth unlock; <br> And was before his spiritual court<sup>1463</sup> <br> <i>Et coram patre</i> unto her united; <br> Then day by day more fervently he loved her. <br> She, reft of her first husband, scorned, obscure, <br> One thousand and one hundred years and more,<sup>1464</sup> <br> Waited without a suitor till he came. <br> Naught it availed to hear, that with Amyclas<sup>1465</sup> <br> Found her unmoved at sounding of his voice <br> He who struck terror into all the world; <br> Naught it availed being constant and undaunted, <br> So that, when Mary still remained below, <br> She mounted up with Christ upon the cross. <br> But that too darkly I may not proceed, <br> Francis and Poverty for these two lovers<sup>1466</sup> <br> Take thou henceforward in my speech diffuse. <br> Their concord and their joyous semblances, <br> The love, the wonder, and the sweet regard, <br> They made to be the cause of holy thoughts; <br> So much so that the venerable Bernard<sup>1467</sup> <br> First bared his feet, and after so great peace <br> Ran, and, in running, thought himself too slow. <br> O wealth unknown! O veritable good! <br> Giles bares his feet, and bares his feet Sylvester<sup>1468</sup> <br> Behind the bridegroom, so doth please the bride! <br> Then goes his way that father and that master, <br> He and his Lady and that family <br> Which now was girding on the humble cord; <br> Nor cowardice of heart weighed down his brow <br> At being son of Peter Bernardone,<sup>1469</sup> <br> Nor for appearing marvellously scorned; <br> But regally his hard determination <br> To Innocent he opened, and from him <br> Received the primal seal upon his Order.<sup>1470</sup> <br> After the people mendicant increased <br> Behind this man, whose admirable life <br> Better in glory of the heavens were sung,<sup>1471</sup> <br> Incoronated with a second crown <br> Was through Honorius by the Eternal Spirit<sup>1472</sup> <br> The holy purpose of this Archimandrite.<sup>1473</sup> <br> And when he had, through thirst of martyrdom, <br> In the proud presence of the Sultan preached<sup>1474</sup> <br> Christ and the others who came after him, <br> And, finding for conversion too unripe <br> The folk, and not to tarry there in vain,<sup>1475</sup> <br> Returned to fruit of the Italic grass, <br> On the rude rock ’twixt Tiber and the Arno<sup>1476</sup> <br> From Christ did he receive the final seal, <br> Which during two whole years his members bore. <br> When He, who chose him unto so much good, <br> Was pleased to draw him up to the reward <br> That he had merited by being lowly, <br> Unto his friars, as to the rightful heirs, <br> His most dear Lady did he recommend, <br> And bade that they should love her faithfully; <br> And from her bosom the illustrious soul <br> Wished to depart, returning to its realm, <br> And for its body wished no other bier.<sup>1477</sup> <br> Think now what man was he, who was a fit<sup>1478</sup> <br> Companion over the high seas to keep <br> The bark of Peter to its proper bearings. <br> And this man was our Patriarch; hence whoever <br> Doth follow him as he commands can see <br> That he is laden with good merchandise. <br> But for new pasturage his flock has grown <br> So greedy, that it is impossible <br> They be not scattered over fields diverse; <br> And in proportion as his sheep remote <br> And vagabond go farther off from him, <br> More void of milk return they to the fold. <br> Verily some there are that fear a hurt, <br> And keep close to the shepherd; but so few, <br> That little cloth doth furnish forth their hoods. <br> Now if my utterance be not indistinct, <br> If thine own hearing hath attentive been, <br> If thou recall to mind what I have said, <br> In part contented shall thy wishes be; <br> For thou shalt see the plant that’s chipped away,<sup>1479</sup> <br> And the rebuke that lieth in the words,<sup>1480</sup> <br> ‘Where well one fattens, if he strayeth not.’ ”</p>
