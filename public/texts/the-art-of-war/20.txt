<p>Sun Tzǔ said: Raising a host of a hundred thousand men and marching them great distances entails heavy loss on the people and a drain on the resources of the State. The daily expenditure will amount to a thousand ounces of silver.<sup>724</sup> There will be commotion at home and abroad, and men will drop down exhausted on the highways.<sup>725</sup> As many as seven hundred thousand families will be impeded in their labour.<sup>726</sup></p>
<p>Hostile armies may face each other for years, striving for the victory which is decided in a single day. This being so, to remain in ignorance of the enemy’s condition simply because one grudges the outlay of a hundred ounces of silver in honors and emoluments,<sup>727</sup> is the height of inhumanity.<sup>728</sup></p>
<p>One who acts thus is no leader of men, no present help to his sovereign,<sup>729</sup> no master of victory.<sup>730</sup></p>
<p>Thus, what enables the wise sovereign and the good general to strike and conquer, and achieve things beyond the reach of ordinary men, is <em>foreknowledge</em>.<sup>731</sup></p>
<p>Now this foreknowledge cannot be elicited from spirits;<sup>732</sup> it cannot be obtained inductively from experience,<sup>733</sup> nor by any deductive calculation.<sup>734</sup></p>
<p>Knowledge of the enemy’s dispositions can only be obtained from other men.<sup>735</sup></p>
<p>Hence the use of spies, of whom there are five classes: (1) Local spies; (2) inward spies; (3) converted spies; (4) doomed spies; (5) surviving spies.</p>
<p>When these five kinds of spy are all at work, none can discover the secret system.<sup>736</sup> This is called<sup>737</sup> “divine manipulation of the threads.”<sup>738</sup> It is the sovereign’s most precious faculty.<sup>739</sup></p>
<p>Having <em>local spies</em><sup>740</sup> means employing the services of the inhabitants of a district.<sup>741</sup></p>
<p>Having <em>inward spies</em>, making use of officials of the enemy.<sup>742</sup></p>
<p>Having <em>converted spies</em>, getting hold of the enemy’s spies and using them for our own purposes.<sup>743</sup></p>
<p>Having <em>doomed spies</em>, doing certain things openly for purposes of deception, and allowing our spies to know of them and report them to the enemy.<sup>744</sup></p>
<p><em>Surviving spies</em>, finally, are those who bring back news from the enemy’s camp.<sup>745</sup></p>
<p>Hence it is that which none in the whole army are more intimate relations to be maintained than with spies.<sup>746</sup> None should be more liberally rewarded.<sup>747</sup> In no other business should greater secrecy be preserved.<sup>748</sup></p>
<p>Spies cannot be usefully employed<sup>749</sup> without a certain intuitive sagacity.<sup>750</sup></p>
<p>They cannot be properly managed without benevolence and straightforwardness.<sup>751</sup></p>
<p>Without subtle ingenuity of mind, one cannot make certain of the truth of their reports.<sup>752</sup></p>
<p>Be subtle! be subtle!<sup>753</sup> and use your spies for every kind of business.</p>
<p>If a secret piece of news is divulged by a spy before the time is ripe, he must be put to death together with the man to whom the secret was told.<sup>754</sup></p>
<p>Whether the object be to crush an army, to storm a city, or to assassinate an individual, it is always necessary to begin by finding out the names of the attendants,<sup>755</sup> the aides-de-camp,<sup>756</sup> and doorkeepers and sentries<sup>757</sup> of the general in command.<sup>758</sup> Our spies must be commissioned to ascertain these.<sup>759</sup></p>
<p>The enemy’s spies who have come to spy on us must be sought out,<sup>760</sup> tempted with bribes, led away and comfortably housed.<sup>761</sup> Thus they will become converted spies and available for our service.</p>
<p>It is through the information brought by the converted spy that we are able to acquire and employ local and inward spies.<sup>762</sup></p>
<p>It is owing to his information, again, that we can cause the doomed spy to carry false tidings to the enemy.<sup>763</sup></p>
<p>Lastly, it is by his information that the surviving spy can be used on appointed occasions.<sup>764</sup></p>
<p>The end and aim of spying in all its five varieties is knowledge of the enemy;<sup>765</sup> and this knowledge can only be derived, in the first instance, from the converted spy.<sup>766</sup> Hence it is essential that the converted spy be treated with the utmost liberality.</p>
<p>Of old, the rise of the Yin dynasty<sup>767</sup> was due to I Chih<sup>768</sup> who had served under the Hsia. Likewise, the rise of the Chou dynasty was due to Lu Ya<sup>769</sup> who had served under the Yin.<sup>770</sup></p>
<p>Hence it is only the enlightened ruler and the wise general who will use the highest intelligence of the army for purposes of spying<sup>771</sup> and thereby they achieve great results.<sup>772</sup> Spies are a most important element in war, because on them depends an army’s ability to move.<sup>773</sup></p>
