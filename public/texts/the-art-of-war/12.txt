<p>Sun Tzǔ said: The control of a large force is the same principle as the control of a few men: it is merely a question of dividing up their numbers.<sup>273</sup></p>
<p>Fighting with a large army under your command is nowise different from fighting with a small one: it is merely a question of instituting signs and signals.<sup>274</sup></p>
<p>To ensure that your whole host may withstand the brunt of the enemy’s attack and remain unshaken⁠—this is effected by manoeuvres direct and indirect.<sup>275</sup></p>
<p>That the impact of your army may be like a grindstone dashed against an egg⁠—this is effected by the science of weak points and strong.<sup>276</sup></p>
<p>In all fighting, the direct method may be used for joining battle, but indirect methods will be needed in order to secure victory.<sup>277</sup></p>
<p>Indirect tactics, efficiently applied, are inexhaustible as Heaven and Earth, unending as the flow of rivers and streams;<sup>278</sup> like the sun and moon, they end but to begin anew; like the four seasons, they pass away to return once more.<sup>279</sup></p>
<p>There are not more than five musical notes,<sup>280</sup> yet the combinations of these five give rise to more melodies than can ever be heard.</p>
<p>There are not more than five primary colors,<sup>281</sup> yet in combination they produce more hues than can ever been seen.</p>
<p>There are not more than five cardinal tastes,<sup>282</sup> yet combinations of them yield more flavors than can ever be tasted.</p>
<p>In battle, there are not more than two methods of attack⁠—the direct and the indirect; yet these two in combination give rise to an endless series of manoeuvres.</p>
<p>The direct and the indirect lead on to each other in turn. It is like moving in a circle⁠—you never come to an end. Who can exhaust the possibilities of their combination?<sup>283</sup></p>
<p>The onset of troops is like the rush of a torrent which will even roll stones along in its course.</p>
<p>The quality of decision is like the well-timed swoop of a falcon which enables it to strike and destroy its victim.<sup>284</sup></p>
<p>Therefore the good fighter will be terrible in his onset, and prompt in his decision.<sup>285</sup></p>
<p>Energy may be likened to the bending of a crossbow; decision, to the releasing of a trigger.<sup>286</sup></p>
<p>Amid the turmoil and tumult of battle, there may be seeming disorder and yet no real disorder at all; amid confusion and chaos, your array may be without head or tail, yet it will be proof against defeat.<sup>287</sup></p>
<p>Simulated disorder postulates perfect discipline, simulated fear postulates courage; simulated weakness postulates strength.<sup>288</sup></p>
<p>Hiding order beneath the cloak of disorder is simply a question of subdivision;<sup>289</sup> concealing courage under a show of timidity presupposes a fund of latent energy;<sup>290</sup> masking strength with weakness is to be effected by tactical dispositions.<sup>291</sup></p>
<p>Thus one who is skilful at keeping the enemy on the move maintains deceitful appearances, according to which the enemy will act.<sup>292</sup> He sacrifices something, that the enemy may snatch at it.<sup>293</sup></p>
<p>By holding out baits, he keeps him on the march; then with a body of picked men he lies in wait for him.<sup>294</sup></p>
<p>The clever combatant looks to the effect of combined energy, and does not require too much from individuals.<sup>295</sup> Hence his ability to pick out the right men and utilize combined energy.<sup>296</sup></p>
<p>When he utilizes combined energy, his fighting men become as it were like unto rolling logs or stones. For it is the nature of a log or stone to remain motionless on level ground, and to move when on a slope; if four-cornered, to come to a standstill, but if round-shaped, to go rolling down.<sup>297</sup></p>
<p>Thus the energy developed by good fighting men is as the momentum of a round stone rolled down a mountain thousands of feet in height. So much on the subject of energy.<sup>298</sup></p>
