<p>Sun Tzǔ said: There are five ways of attacking with fire. The first is to burn soldiers in their camp;<sup>692</sup> the second is to burn stores;<sup>693</sup> the third is to burn baggage trains;<sup>694</sup> the fourth is to burn arsenals and magazines;<sup>695</sup> the fifth is to hurl dropping fire amongst the enemy.<sup>696</sup></p>
<p>In order to carry out an attack, we must have means available;<sup>697</sup> the material for raising fire should always be kept in readiness.<sup>698</sup></p>
<p>There is a proper season for making attacks with fire, and special days for starting a conflagration.<sup>699</sup></p>
<p>The proper season is when the weather is very dry; the special days are those when the moon is in the constellations of the Sieve, the Wall, the Wing or the Crossbar;<sup>700</sup> for these four are all days of rising wind.<sup>701</sup></p>
<p>In attacking with fire, one should be prepared to meet five possible developments:<sup>702</sup></p>
<ol><li><p>When fire breaks out inside the enemy’s camp, respond at once<sup>703</sup> with an attack from without.</p></li> <li><p>If there is an outbreak of fire, but the enemy’s soldiers remain quiet, bide your time and do not attack.<sup>704</sup></p></li> <li><p>When the force of the flames has reached its height, follow it up with an attack, if that is practicable; if not, stay where you are.<sup>705</sup></p></li> <li><p>If it is possible to make an assault with fire from without, do not wait for it to break out within, but deliver your attack at a favourable moment.<sup>706</sup></p></li> <li><p>When you start a fire, be to windward of it. Do not attack from the leeward.<sup>707</sup></p></li></ol>
<p>A wind that rises in the daytime lasts long, but a night breeze soon falls.<sup>708</sup></p>
<p>In every army, the five developments connected with fire must be known, the movements of the stars calculated, and a watch kept for the proper days.<sup>709</sup></p>
<p>Hence those who use fire as an aid to the attack show intelligence;<sup>710</sup> those who use water as an aid to the attack gain an accession of strength.<sup>711</sup></p>
<p>By means of water, an enemy may be intercepted, but not robbed of all his belongings.<sup>712</sup></p>
<p>Unhappy is the fate of one who tries to win his battles and succeed in his attacks without cultivating the spirit of enterprise; for the result is waste of time and general stagnation.<sup>713</sup></p>
<p>Hence the saying: The enlightened ruler lays his plans well ahead; the good general cultivates his resources.<sup>714</sup></p>
<p>Move not unless you see an advantage;<sup>715</sup> use not your troops unless there is something to be gained; fight not unless the position is critical.<sup>716</sup></p>
<p>No ruler should put troops into the field merely to gratify his own spleen; no general should fight a battle simply out of pique.<sup>717</sup></p>
<p>If it is to your advantage, make a forward move; if not, stay where you are.<sup>718</sup></p>
<p>Anger may in time change to gladness; vexation may be succeeded by content.<sup>719</sup></p>
<p>But a kingdom that has once been destroyed can never come again into being;<sup>720</sup> nor can the dead ever be brought back to life.</p>
<p>Hence the enlightened ruler is heedful, and the good general full of caution.<sup>721</sup> This is the way to keep a country at peace and an army intact.<sup>722</sup></p>
