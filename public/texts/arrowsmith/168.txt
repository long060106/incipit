<p>It happened that Martin returned to New York, as he had come, on the <i>St. Buryan</i>. The ship was haunted with the phantoms of Leora dreaming, of Sondelius shouting on the bridge.</p>
<p>And on the <i>St. Buryan</i> was the country-club Miss Gwilliam who had offended Sondelius.</p>
<p>She had spent the winter importantly making notes on native music in Trinidad and Caracas; at least in planning to make notes. She saw Martin come aboard at Blackwater, and pertly noted the friends who saw him off⁠—two Englishmen, one puffy, one rangy, and a dry-looking Scotsman.</p>
<p>“Your friends all seem to be British,” she enlightened him, when she had claimed him as an old friend.</p>
<p>“Yes.”</p>
<p>“You’ve spent the winter here.”</p>
<p>“Yes.”</p>
<p>“Hard luck to be caught by the quarantine. But I <em>told</em> you you were silly to go ashore! You must have managed to pick up quite a little money practicing. But it must have been unpleasant, really.”</p>
<p>“Ye‑es, I suppose it was.”</p>
<p>“I told you it would be! You ought to have come on to Trinidad. Such a fascinating island! And tell me, how is the Roughneck?”</p>
<p>“Who?”</p>
<p>“Oh, you know⁠—that funny Swede that used to dance and everything.”</p>
<p>“He is dead.”</p>
<p>“Oh, I <em>am</em> sorry. You know, no matter what the others said, I never thought he was so bad. I’m sure he had quite a nice cultured mind, when he wasn’t carousing around. Your wife isn’t with you, is she?”</p>
<p>“No⁠—she isn’t with me. I must go down and unpack now.” Miss Gwilliam looked after him with an expression which said that the least people could do was to learn some manners.</p>
