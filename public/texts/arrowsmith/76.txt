<p>Bert Tozer wailed, “Mart, what you been doing to Dr. Coughlin of Leopolis? Fellow told me he was going around saying you were a booze-hoister and so on.”</p>
<p>“Did he? People do sort of keep an eye on one another around here, don’t they?”</p>
<p>“You bet your life they do, and that’s why I tell you you ought to cut out the poker and the booze. You don’t see <em>me</em> needing any liquor, do you?”</p>
<p>Martin more desperately than ever felt the whole county watching him. He was not a praise-eater; he was not proud that he should feel misplaced; but however sturdily he struggled he saw himself outside the picture of Wheatsylvania and trudging years of country practice.</p>
<p>Suddenly, without planning it, forgetting in his admiration for Sondelius and the health war his pride of the laboratory, he was thrown into a research problem.</p>
