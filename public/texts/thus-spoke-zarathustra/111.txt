<p>If ever my wrath hath burst graves, shifted landmarks, or rolled old shattered tables into precipitous depths:</p>
<p>If ever my scorn hath scattered mouldered words to the winds, and if I have come like a besom to cross-spiders, and as a cleansing wind to old charnel-houses:</p>
<p>If ever I have sat rejoicing where old Gods lie buried, world-blessing, world-loving, beside the monuments of old world-maligners:⁠—</p>
<p>—For even churches and Gods’-graves do I love, if only heaven looketh through their ruined roofs with pure eyes; gladly do I sit like grass and red poppies on ruined churches⁠—</p>
<p>Oh, how could I not be ardent for Eternity, and for the marriage-ring of rings⁠—the ring of the return?</p>
<p>Never yet have I found the woman by whom I should like to have children, unless it be this woman whom I love: for I love thee, O Eternity!</p>
