<p>Cyrano, Le Bret, the cadets, who are eating and drinking at the tables right and left.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (bowing mockingly to those who go out without daring to salute him):<br> Gentlemen. . .Gentlemen. . .</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (coming back, despairingly):<br> Here’s a fine coil!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Oh! scold away!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> At least, you will agree<br> That to annihilate each chance of Fate<br> Exaggerates. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Yes!—I exaggerate!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (triumphantly):<br> Ah!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> But for principle—example too,—<br> I think ’tis well thus to exaggerate.</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Oh! lay aside that pride of musketeer,<br> Fortune and glory wait you!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ay, and then?. . .<br> Seek a protector, choose a patron out,<br> And like the crawling ivy round a tree<br> That licks the bark to gain the trunk’s support,<br> Climb high by creeping ruse instead of force?<br> No, grammercy! What! I, like all the rest<br> Dedicate verse to bankers?—play buffoon<br> In cringing hope to see, at last, a smile<br> Not disapproving, on a patron’s lips?<br> Grammercy, no! What! learn to swallow toads?<br> —With frame aweary climbing stairs?—a skin<br> Grown grimed and horny,—here, about the knees?<br> And, acrobat-like, teach my back to bend?—<br> No, grammercy! Or,—double-faced and sly—<br> Run with the hare, while hunting with the hounds;<br> And, oily-tongued, to win the oil of praise,<br> Flatter the great man to his very nose?<br> No, grammercy! Steal soft from lap to lap,<br> —A little great man in a circle small,<br> Or navigate, with madrigals for sails,<br> Blown gently windward by old ladies’ sighs?<br> No, grammercy! Bribe kindly editors<br> To spread abroad my verses? Grammercy!<br> Or try to be elected as the pope<br> Of tavern-councils held by imbeciles?<br> No, grammercy! Toil to gain reputation<br> By one small sonnet, ’stead of making many?<br> No, grammercy! Or flatter sorry bunglers?<br> Be terrorized by every prating paper?<br> Say ceaselessly, ‘Oh, had I but the chance<br> Of a fair notice in the “Mercury”!’<br> Grammercy, no! Grow pale, fear, calculate?<br> Prefer to make a visit to a rhyme?<br> Seek introductions, draw petitions up?<br> No, grammercy! and no! and no again! But—sing?<br> Dream, laugh, go lightly, solitary, free,<br> With eyes that look straight forward—fearless voice!<br> To cock your beaver just the way you choose,—<br> For ‘yes’ or ‘no’ show fight, or turn a rhyme!<br> —To work without one thought of gain or fame,<br> To realize that journey to the moon!<br> Never to pen a line that has not sprung<br> Straight from the heart within. Embracing then<br> Modesty, say to oneself, ‘Good my friend,<br> Be thou content with flowers,—fruit,—nay, leaves,<br> But pluck them from no garden but thine own!’<br> And then, if glory come by chance your way,<br> To pay no tribute unto Caesar, none,<br> But keep the merit all your own! In short,<br> Disdaining tendrils of the parasite,<br> To be content, if neither oak nor elm—<br> Not to mount high, perchance, but mount alone!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Alone, an if you will! But not with hand<br> ’Gainst every man! How in the devil’s name<br> Have you conceived this lunatic idea,<br> To make foes for yourself at every turn?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> By dint of seeing you at every turn<br> Make friends,—and fawn upon your frequent friends<br> With mouth wide smiling, slit from ear to ear!<br> I pass, still unsaluted, joyfully,<br> And cry,—What, ho! another enemy?</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Lunacy!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Well, what if it be my vice,<br> My pleasure to displease—to love men hate me!<br> Ah, friend of mine, believe me, I march better<br> ’Neath the cross-fire of glances inimical!<br> How droll the stains one sees on fine-laced doublets,<br> From gall of envy, or the poltroon’s drivel!<br> —The enervating friendship which enfolds you<br> Is like an open-laced Italian collar,<br> Floating around your neck in woman’s fashion;<br> One is at ease thus,—but less proud the carriage!<br> The forehead, free from mainstay or coercion,<br> Bends here, there, everywhere. But I, embracing<br> Hatred, she lends,—forbidding, stiffly fluted,<br> The ruff’s starched folds that hold the head so rigid;<br> Each enemy—another fold—a gopher,<br> Who adds constraint, and adds a ray of glory;<br> For Hatred, like the ruff worn by the Spanish,<br> Grips like a vice, but frames you like a halo!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (after a silence, taking his arm):<br> Speak proud aloud, and bitter!—In my ear<br> Whisper me simply this,—She loves thee not!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (vehemently):<br> Hush!</p>
<p>(Christian has just entered, and mingled with the cadets, who do not speak to him; he has seated himself at a table, where Lise serves him.)</p>
