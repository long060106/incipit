<p>Cyrano, Le Bret.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (to Le Bret):<br> Now talk—I listen.<br> (He stands at the buffet, and placing before him first the macaroon):<br> Dinner!. . .<br> (then the grapes):<br> Dessert!. . .<br> (then the glass of water):<br> Wine!. . .<br> (he seats himself):<br> So! And now to table!<br> Ah! I was hungry, friend, nay, ravenous!<br> (eating):<br> You said—?</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> These fops, would-be belligerent,<br> Will, if you heed them only, turn your head!. . .<br> Ask people of good sense if you would know<br> The effect of your fine insolence—</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (finishing his macaroon):<br> Enormous!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> The Cardinal. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (radiant):<br> The Cardinal—was there?</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Must have thought it. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Original, i’ faith!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> But. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> He’s an author. ’Twill not fail to please him<br> That I should mar a brother-author’s play.</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> You make too many enemies by far!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (eating his grapes):<br> How many think you I have made to-night?</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Forty, no less, not counting ladies.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Count!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Montfleury first, the bourgeois, then De Guiche,<br> The Viscount, Baro, the Academy. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Enough! I am o’erjoyed!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> But these strange ways,<br> Where will they lead you, at the end? Explain<br> Your system—come!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> I in a labyrinth<br> Was lost—too many different paths to choose;<br> I took. . .</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Which?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Oh! by far the simplest path. . .<br> Decided to be admirable in all!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (shrugging his shoulders):<br> So be it! But the motive of your hate<br> To Montfleury—come, tell me!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (rising):<br> This Silenus,<br> Big-bellied, coarse, still deems himself a peril—<br> A danger to the love of lovely ladies,<br> And, while he sputters out his actor’s part,<br> Makes sheep’s eyes at their boxes—goggling frog!<br> I hate him since the evening he presumed<br> To raise his eyes to hers. . .Meseemed I saw<br> A slug crawl slavering o’er a flower’s petals!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (stupefied):<br> How now? What? Can it be. . .?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (laughing bitterly):<br> That I should love?. . .<br> (Changing his tone, gravely):<br> I love.</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> And may I know?. . .You never said. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Come now, bethink you!. . .The fond hope to be<br> Beloved, e’en by some poor graceless lady,<br> Is, by this nose of mine for aye bereft me;<br> —This lengthy nose which, go where’er I will,<br> Pokes yet a quarter-mile ahead of me;<br> But I may love—and who? ’Tis Fate’s decree<br> I love the fairest—how were’t otherwise?</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> The fairest?. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ay, the fairest of the world,<br> Most brilliant—most refined—most golden-haired!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Who is this lady?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> She’s a danger mortal,<br> All unsuspicious—full of charms unconscious,<br> Like a sweet perfumed rose—a snare of nature,<br> Within whose petals Cupid lurks in ambush!<br> He who has seen her smile has known perfection,<br> —Instilling into trifles grace’s essence,<br> Divinity in every careless gesture;<br> Not Venus’ self can mount her conch blown sea-ward,<br> As she can step into her chaise a porteurs,<br> Nor Dian fleet across the woods spring-flowered,<br> Light as my Lady o’er the stones of Paris!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Sapristi! all is clear!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> As spiderwebs!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Your cousin, Madeleine Robin?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Roxane!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Well, but so much the better! Tell her so!<br> She saw your triumph here this very night!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Look well at me—then tell me, with what hope<br> This vile protuberance can inspire my heart!<br> I do not lull me with illusions—yet<br> At times I’m weak: in evening hours dim<br> I enter some fair pleasance, perfumed sweet;<br> With my poor ugly devil of a nose<br> I scent spring’s essence—in the silver rays<br> I see some knight—a lady on his arm,<br> And think ‘To saunter thus ’neath the moonshine,<br> I were fain to have my lady, too, beside!’<br> Thought soars to ecstasy. . .O sudden fall!<br> —The shadow of my profile on the wall!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (tenderly):<br> My friend!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> My friend, at times ’tis hard, ’tis bitter,<br> To feel my loneliness—my own ill-favor. . .</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (taking his hand):<br> You weep?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> No, never! Think, how vilely suited<br> Adown this nose a tear its passage tracing!<br> I never will, while of myself I’m master,<br> let the divinity of tears—their beauty<br> Be wedded to such common ugly grossness.<br> Nothing more solemn than a tear—sublimer;<br> And I would not by weeping turn to laughter<br> The grave emotion that a tear engenders!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Never be sad! What’s love?—a chance of Fortune!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (shaking his head):<br> Look I a Caesar to woo Cleopatra?<br> A Tito to aspire to Berenice?</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Your courage and your wit!—The little maid<br> Who offered you refreshment even now,<br> Her eyes did not abhor you—you saw well!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (impressed):<br> True!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Well, how then?. . .I saw Roxane herself<br> Was death-pale as she watched the duel.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Pale?</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Her heart, her fancy, are already caught!<br> Put it to th’ touch!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> That she may mock my face?<br> That is the one thing on this earth I fear!</p>
<p class="verse">THE PORTER (introducing some one to Cyrano):<br> Sir, some one asks for you. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (seeing the duenna):<br> God! her duenna!</p>
