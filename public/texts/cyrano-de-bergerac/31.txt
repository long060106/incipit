<p>Cyrano, Christian, Roxane, the friar, Ragueneau.</p>
<p class="verse">THE FRIAR:<br> ’Tis here,—I’m sure of it—Madame Madeleine Robin.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Why, you said Ro-LIN.</p>
<p class="verse">THE FRIAR:<br> No, not I.<br> B,I,N,BIN!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (appearing on the threshold, followed by Ragueneau, who carries a lantern, and Christian):<br> What is’t?</p>
<p class="verse">THE FRIAR:<br> A letter.</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> What?</p>
<p class="verse">THE FRIAR (to Roxane):<br> Oh, it can boot but a holy business!<br> ’Tis from a worthy lord. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (to Christian):<br> De Guiche!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> He dares. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Oh, he will not importune me forever!<br> (Unsealing the letter):<br> I love you,—therefore—<br> (She reads in a low voice by the aid of Ragueneau’s lantern):<br> ‘Lady,<br> The drums beat;<br> My regiment buckles its harness on<br> And starts; but I,—they deem me gone before—<br> But I stay. I have dared to disobey<br> Your mandate. I am here in convent walls.<br> I come to you to-night. By this poor monk—<br> A simple fool who knows not what he bears—<br> I send this missive to apprise your ear.<br> Your lips erewhile have smiled on me, too sweet:<br> I go not ere I’ve seen them once again!<br> I would be private; send each soul away,<br> Receive alone him,—whose great boldness you<br> Have deigned, I hope, to pardon, ere he asks,—<br> He who is ever your—et cetera.’<br> (To the monk):<br> Father, this is the matter of the letter:—<br> (All come near her, and she reads aloud):<br> ‘Lady,<br> The Cardinal’s wish is law; albeit<br> It be to you unwelcome. For this cause<br> I send these lines—to your fair ear addressed—<br> By a holy man, discreet, intelligent:<br> It is our will that you receive from him,<br> In your own house, the marriage<br> (She turns the page):<br> benediction<br> Straightway, this night. Unknown to all the world<br> Christian becomes your husband. Him we send.<br> He is abhorrent to your choice. Let be.<br> Resign yourself, and this obedience<br> Will be by Heaven well recompensed. Receive,<br> Fair lady, all assurance of respect,<br> From him who ever was, and still remains,<br> Your humble and obliged—et cetera.’</p>
<p class="verse">THE FRIAR (with great delight):<br> O worthy lord! I knew naught was to fear;<br> It could be but holy business!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (to Christian, in a low voice):<br> Am I not apt at reading letters?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Hum!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (aloud, with despair):<br> But this is horrible!</p>
<p class="verse">THE FRIAR (who has turned his lantern on Cyrano):<br> ’Tis you?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> ’Tis I!</p>
<p class="verse">THE FRIAR (turning the light on to him, and as if a doubt struck him on seeing his beauty):<br> But. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (quickly):<br> I have overlooked the postscript—see:—<br> ‘Give twenty pistoles for the Convent.’</p>
<p class="verse">THE FRIAR:<br> . . .Oh!<br> Most worthy lord!<br> (To Roxane):<br> Submit you?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (with a martyr’s look):<br> I submit!<br> (While Ragueneau opens the door, and Christian invites the friar to enter, she whispers to Cyrano):<br> Oh, keep De Guiche at bay! He will be here!<br> Let him not enter till. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> I understand!<br> (To the friar):<br> What time need you to tie the marriage-knot?</p>
<p class="verse">THE FRIAR:<br> A quarter of an hour.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (pushing them all toward the house):<br> Go! I stay.</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (to Christian):<br> Come!. . .</p>
<p>(They enter.)</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Now, how to detain De Guiche so long?<br> (He jumps on the bench, climbs to the balcony by the wall):<br> Come!. . .up I go!. . .I have my plan!. . .<br> (The lutes begin to play a very sad air):<br> What, ho!<br> (The tremolo grows more and more weird):<br> It is a man! ay! ’tis a man this time!<br> (He is on the balcony, pulls his hat over his eyes, takes off his sword, wraps himself in his cloak, then leans over):<br> ’Tis not too high!<br> (He strides across the balcony, and drawing to him a long branch of one of the trees that are by the garden wall, he hangs on to it with both hands, ready to let himself fall):<br> I’ll shake this atmosphere!</p>
