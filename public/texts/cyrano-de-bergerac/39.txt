<p>The same. Roxane.</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> On the King’s service! You?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Ay,—King Love’s! What other king?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Great God!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (rushing forward):<br> Why have you come?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> This siege—’tis too long!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> But why?. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> I will tell you all!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (who, at the sound of her voice, has stood still, rooted to the ground, afraid to raise his eyes):<br> My God! dare I look at her?</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> You cannot remain here!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (merrily):<br> But I say yes! Who will push a drum hither for me?<br> (She seats herself on the drum they roll forward):<br> So! I thank you.<br> (She laughs):<br> My carriage was fired at<br> (proudly):<br> by the patrol! Look! would you not think ’twas made of a pumpkin, like<br> Cinderella’s chariot in the tale,—and the footmen out of rats?<br> (Sending a kiss with her lips to Christian):<br> Good-morrow!<br> (Examining them all):<br> You look not merry, any of you! Ah! know you that ’tis a long road to get<br> to Arras?<br> (Seeing Cyrano):<br> Cousin, delighted!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (coming up to her):<br> But how, in Heaven’s name?. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> How found I the way to the army? It was simple enough, for I had but to<br> pass on and on, as far as I saw the country laid waste. Ah, what horrors were<br> there! Had I not seen, then I could never have believed it! Well, gentlemen,<br> if such be the service of your King, I would fainer serve mine!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> But ’tis sheer madness! Where in the fiend’s name did you get through?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Where? Through the Spanish lines.</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST CADET:<br> —For subtle craft, give me a woman!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> But how did you pass through their lines?</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Faith! that must have been a hard matter!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> None too hard. I but drove quietly forward in my carriage, and when some<br> hidalgo of haughty mien would have stayed me, lo! I showed at the window my<br> sweetest smile, and these Senors being (with no disrespect to you) the most<br> gallant gentlemen in the world,—I passed on!</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON:<br> True, that smile is a passport! But you must have been asked frequently to<br> give an account of where you were going, Madame?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Yes, frequently. Then I would answer, ‘I go to see my lover.’ At that word<br> the very fiercest Spaniard of them all would gravely shut the carriage-door,<br> and, with a gesture that a king might envy, make signal to his men to lower<br> the muskets leveled at me;—then, with melancholy but withal very graceful<br> dignity—his beaver held to the wind that the plumes might flutter bravely, he<br> would bow low, saying to me, ‘Pass on, Senorita!’</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> But, Roxane. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Forgive me that I said, ‘my lover!’ But bethink you, had I said ‘my<br> husband,’ not one of them had let me pass!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> But. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> What ails you?</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> You must leave this place!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> I?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> And that instantly!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> No time to lose.</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Indeed, you must.</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> But wherefore must I?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (embarrassed):<br> ’Tis that. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (the same):<br> —In three quarters of an hour. . .</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (the same):<br> —Or for. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON (the same):<br> It were best. . .</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (the same):<br> You might. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> You are going to fight?—I stay here.</p>
<p class="verse">ALL:<br> No, no!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> He is my husband!<br> (She throws herself into Christian’s arms):<br> They shall kill us both together!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Why do you look at me thus?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> I will tell you why!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (in despair):<br> ’Tis a post of mortal danger!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (turning round):<br> Mortal danger!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Proof enough, that he has put us here!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (to De Guiche):<br> So, Sir, you would have made a widow of me?</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Nay, on my oath. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> I will not go! I am reckless now, and I shall not stir from here!—Besides,<br> ’tis amusing!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Oh-ho! So our precieuse is a heroine!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Monsieur de Bergerac, I am your cousin.</p>
<p class="verse">A CADET:<br> We will defend you well!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (more and more excited):<br> I have no fear of that, my friends!</p>
<p class="verse">ANOTHER (in ecstasy):<br> The whole camp smells sweet of orris-root!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> And, by good luck, I have chosen a hat that will suit well with the<br> battlefield!<br> (Looking at De Guiche):<br> But were it not wisest that the Count retire?<br> They may begin the attack.</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> That is not to be brooked! I go to inspect the cannon, and shall return.<br> You have still time—think better of it!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Never!</p>
<p>(De Guiche goes out.)</p>
