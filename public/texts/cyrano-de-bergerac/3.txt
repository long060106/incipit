<p>The same. Christian, Lignière, then Ragueneau and Le Bret.</p>
<p class="verse">CUIGY:<br> Lignière!</p>
<p class="verse">BRISSAILLE (laughing):<br> Not drunk as yet?</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE (aside to Christian):<br> I may introduce you?<br> (Christian nods in assent):<br> Baron de Neuvillette.</p>
<p>(Bows.)</p>
<p class="verse">THE AUDIENCE (applauding as the first luster is lighted and drawn up):<br> Ah!</p>
<p class="verse">CUIGY (to Brissaille, looking at Christian):<br> ’Tis a pretty fellow!</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST MARQUIS (who has overheard):<br> Pooh!</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE (introducing them to Christian):<br> My lords De Cuigy. De Brissaille. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (bowing):<br> Delighted!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST MARQUIS (to second):<br> He is not ill to look at, but certes, he is not costumed in the latest mode.</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE (to Cuigy):<br> This gentleman comes from Touraine.</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Yes, I have scarce been twenty days in Paris; tomorrow I join the Guards, in<br> the Cadets.</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST MARQUIS (watching the people who are coming into the boxes):<br> There is the wife of the Chief-Justice.</p>
<p class="verse">THE BUFFET-GIRL:<br> Oranges, milk. . .</p>
<p class="verse">THE VIOLINISTS (tuning up):<br> La—la—</p>
<p class="verse">CUIGY (to Christian, pointing to the hall, which is filling fast):<br> ’Tis crowded.</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Yes, indeed.</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST MARQUIS:<br> All the great world!</p>
<p>(They recognize and name the different elegantly dressed ladies who enter the boxes, bowing low to them. The ladies send smiles in answer.)</p>
<p class="verse">SECOND MARQUIS:<br> Madame de Guemenee.</p>
<p class="verse">CUIGY:<br> Madame de Bois-Dauphin.</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST MARQUIS:<br> Adored by us all!</p>
<p class="verse">BRISSAILLE:<br> Madame de Chavigny. . .</p>
<p class="verse">SECOND MARQUIS:<br> Who sports with our poor hearts!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> Ha! so Monsieur de Corneille has come back from Rouen!</p>
<p class="verse">THE YOUNG MAN (to his father):<br> Is the Academy here?</p>
<p class="verse">THE BURGHER:<br> Oh, ay, I see several of them. There is Boudu, Boissat,<br> and Cureau de la Chambre, Porcheres, Colomby, Bourzeys,<br> Bourdon, Arbaud. . .all names that will live! ’Tis fine!</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST MARQUIS:<br> Attention! Here come our precieuses; Barthenoide, Urimedonte, Cassandace,<br> Felixerie. . .</p>
<p class="verse">SECOND MARQUIS:<br> Ah! How exquisite their fancy names are! Do you know them all, Marquis?</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST MARQUIS:<br> Ay, Marquis, I do, every one!</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE (drawing Christian aside):<br> Friend, I but came here to give you pleasure. The lady comes not. I will<br> betake me again to my pet vice.</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (persuasively):<br> No, no! You, who are ballad-maker to Court and City alike, can tell me<br> better than any who the lady is for whom I die of love. Stay yet awhile.</p>
<p class="verse">THE FIRST VIOLIN (striking his bow on the desk):<br> Gentlemen violinists!</p>
<p>(He raises his bow.)</p>
<p class="verse">THE BUFFET-GIRL:<br> Macaroons, lemon-drink. . .</p>
<p>(The violins begin to play.)</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Ah! I fear me she is coquettish, and over nice and fastidious!<br> I, who am so poor of wit, how dare I speak to her—how address her?<br> This language that they speak to-day—ay, and write—confounds me;<br> I am but an honest soldier, and timid withal. She has ever her place,<br> there, on the right—the empty box, see you!</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE (making as if to go):<br> I must go.</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (detaining him):<br> Nay, stay.</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> I cannot. D’Assoucy waits me at the tavern, and here one dies of thirst.</p>
<p class="verse">THE BUFFET-GIRL (passing before him with a tray):<br> Orange drink?</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> Ugh!</p>
<p class="verse">THE BUFFET-GIRL:<br> Milk?</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> Pah!</p>
<p class="verse">THE BUFFET-GIRL:<br> Rivesalte?</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> Stay.<br> (To Christian):<br> I will remain awhile.—Let me taste this rivesalte.</p>
<p>(He sits by the buffet; the girl pours some out for him.)</p>
<p class="verse">CRIES (from all the audience, at the entrance of a plump little man, joyously excited):<br> Ah! Ragueneau!</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE (to Christian):<br> ’Tis the famous tavern-keeper Ragueneau.</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU (dressed in the Sunday clothes of a pastry-cook, going up quickly to Lignière):<br> Sir, have you seen Monsieur de Cyrano?</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE (introducing him to Christian):<br> The pastry-cook of the actors and the poets!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU (overcome):<br> You do me too great honor. . .</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> Nay, hold your peace, Maecenas that you are!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> True, these gentlemen employ me. . .</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> On credit!<br> He is himself a poet of a pretty talent. . .</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> So they tell me.</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> —Mad after poetry!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> ’Tis true that, for a little ode. . .</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> You give a tart. . .</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Oh!—a tartlet!</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> Brave fellow! He would fain fain excuse himself!<br> —And for a triolet, now, did you not give in exchange. . .</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Some little rolls!</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE (severely):<br> They were milk-rolls! And as for the theater, which you love?</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Oh! to distraction!</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> How pay you your tickets, ha?—with cakes.<br> Your place, to-night, come tell me in my ear, what did it cost you?</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Four custards, and fifteen cream-puffs.<br> (He looks around on all sides):<br> Monsieur de Cyrano is not here? ’Tis strange.</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> Why so?</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Montfleury plays!</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> Ay, ’tis true that that old wine-barrel is to take Phedon’s part to-night;<br> but what matter is that to Cyrano?</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> How? Know you not? He has got a hot hate for Montfleury, and so!—has<br> forbid him strictly to show his face on the stage for one whole month.</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE (drinking his fourth glass):<br> Well?</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Montfleury will play!</p>
<p class="verse">CUIGY:<br> He can not hinder that.</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Oh! oh! that I have come to see!</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST MARQUIS:<br> Who is this Cyrano?</p>
<p class="verse">CUIGY:<br> A fellow well skilled in all tricks of fence.</p>
<p class="verse">SECOND MARQUIS:<br> Is he of noble birth?</p>
<p class="verse">CUIGY:<br> Ay, noble enough. He is a cadet in the Guards.<br> (Pointing to a gentleman who is going up and down the hall as if searching for some one):<br> But ’tis his friend Le Bret, yonder, who can best tell you.<br> (He calls him):<br> Le Bret!<br> (Le Bret comes towards them):<br> Seek you for De Bergerac?</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Ay, I am uneasy. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CUIGY:<br> Is it not true that he is the strangest of men?</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (tenderly):<br> True, that he is the choicest of earthly beings!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Poet!</p>
<p class="verse">CUIGY:<br> Soldier!</p>
<p class="verse">BRISSAILLE:<br> Philosopher!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Musician!</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> And of how fantastic a presence!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGENEAU:<br> Marry, ’twould puzzle even our grim painter Philippe de Champaigne to<br> portray him! Methinks, whimsical, wild, comical as he is, only Jacques<br> Callot, now dead and gone, had succeeded better, and had made of him the<br> maddest fighter of all his visored crew—with his triple-plumed beaver and<br> six-pointed doublet—the sword-point sticking up ’neath his mantle like an<br> insolent cocktail! He’s prouder than all the fierce Artabans of whom Gascony<br> has ever been and will ever be the prolific Alma Mater! Above his Toby ruff<br> he carries a nose!—ah, good my lords, what a nose is his! When one sees it<br> one is fain to cry aloud, ‘Nay! ’tis too much! He plays a joke on us!’ Then<br> one laughs, says ‘He will anon take it off.’ But no!—Monsieur de Bergerac<br> always keeps it on.</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (throwing back his head):<br> He keeps it on—and cleaves in two any man who dares remark on it!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU (proudly):<br> His sword—’tis one half of the Fates’ shears!</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST MARQUIS (shrugging his shoulders):<br> He will not come!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> I say he will! and I wager a fowl—a la Ragueneau.</p>
<p class="verse">THE MARQUIS (laughing):<br> Good!</p>
<p>(Murmurs of admiration in hall. Roxane has just appeared in her box. She seats herself in front, the duenna at the back. Christian, who is paying the buffet-girl, does not see her entrance.)</p>
<p class="verse">SECOND MARQUIS (with little cries of joy):<br> Ah, gentlemen! she is fearfully—terribly—ravishing!</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST MARQUIS:<br> When one looks at her one thinks of a peach smiling at a strawberry!</p>
<p class="verse">SECOND MARQUIS:<br> And what freshness! A man approaching her too near might chance to get a<br> bad chill at the heart!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (raising his head, sees Roxane, and catches Lignière by the arm):<br> ’Tis she!</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> Ah! is it she?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Ay, tell me quick—I am afraid.</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE (tasting his rivesalte in sips):<br> Magdaleine Robin—Roxane, so called! A subtle wit—a precieuse.</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Woe is me!</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> Free. An orphan. The cousin of Cyrano, of whom we were now speaking.</p>
<p>(At this moment an elegant nobleman, with blue ribbon across his breast, enters the box, and talks with Roxane, standing.)</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (starting):<br> Who is yonder man?</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE (who is becoming tipsy, winking at him):<br> Ha! ha! Count de Guiche. Enamored of her. But wedded to the niece of<br> Armand de Richelieu. Would fain marry Roxane to a certain sorry fellow, one<br> Monsieur de Valvert, a viscount—and—accommodating! She will none of that<br> bargain; but De Guiche is powerful, and can persecute the daughter of a plain<br> untitled gentleman. More by token, I myself have exposed this cunning plan of<br> his to the world, in a song which. . .Ho! he must rage at me! The end hit<br> home. . .Listen!</p>
<p>(He gets up staggering, and raises his glass, ready to sing.)</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> No. Good-night.</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> Where go you?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> To Monsieur de Valvert!</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> Have a care! It is he who will kill you<br> (showing him Roxane by a look):<br> Stay where you are—she is looking at you.</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> It is true!</p>
<p>(He stands looking at her. The group of pickpockets seeing him thus, head in air and open-mouthed, draw near to him.)</p>
<p class="verse">LIGNIÈRE:<br> ’Tis I who am going. I am athirst! And they expect me—in the taverns!</p>
<p>(He goes out, reeling.)</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (who has been all round the hall, coming back to Ragueneau reassured):<br> No sign of Cyrano.</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU (incredulously):<br> All the same. . .</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> A hope is left to me—that he has not seen the playbill!</p>
<p class="verse">THE AUDIENCE:<br> Begin, begin!</p>
