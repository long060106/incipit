<p>Roxane alone. Two sisters, for a moment.</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Ah! what a beauty in September’s close!<br> My sorrow’s eased. April’s joy dazzled it,<br> But autumn wins it with her dying calm.<br> (She seats herself at the embroidery frame. Two sisters come out of the<br> house, and bring a large armchair under the tree):<br> There comes the famous armchair where he sits,<br> Dear faithful friend!</p>
<p class="verse">SISTER MARTHA:<br> It is the parlor’s best!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Thanks, sister.<br> (The sisters go):<br> He’ll be here now.<br> (She seats herself. A clock strikes):<br> The hour strikes.<br> —My silks?—Why, now, the hour’s struck!<br> How strange<br> To be behind his time, at last, to-day!<br> Perhaps the portress—where’s my thimble?. . .<br> Here!—Is preaching to him.<br> (A pause):<br> Yes, she must be preaching!<br> Surely he must come soon!—Ah, a dead leaf!—<br> (She brushes off the leaf from her work):<br> Nothing, besides, could—scissors?—In my bag!<br> —Could hinder him. . .</p>
<p class="verse">A SISTER (coming to the steps):<br> Monsieur de Bergerac.</p>
