<p>Cyrano, Roxane, the duenna.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ah! if I see but the faint glimmer of hope, then I draw out my letter!<br> (Roxane, masked, followed by the duenna, appears at the glass pane of the door. He opens quickly):<br> Enter!. . .<br> (Walking up to the duenna):<br> Two words with you, Duenna.</p>
<p class="verse">THE DUENNA:<br> Four, Sir, an it like you.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Are you fond of sweet things?</p>
<p class="verse">THE DUENNA:<br> Ay, I could eat myself sick on them!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (catching up some of the paper bags from the counter):<br> Good. See you these two sonnets of Monsieur Beuserade. . .</p>
<p class="verse">THE DUENNA:<br> Hey?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> . . .Which I fill for you with cream cakes!</p>
<p class="verse">THE DUENNA (changing her expression):<br> Ha.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> What say you to the cake they call a little puff?</p>
<p class="verse">THE DUENNA:<br> If made with cream, Sir, I love them passing well.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Here I plunge six for your eating into the bosom of a poem by Saint Amant!<br> And in these verses of Chapelain I glide a lighter morsel. Stay, love you hot<br> cakes?</p>
<p class="verse">THE DUENNA:<br> Ay, to the core of my heart!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (filling her arms with the bags):<br> Pleasure me then; go eat them all in the street.</p>
<p class="verse">THE DUENNA:<br> But. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (pushing her out):<br> And come not back till the very last crumb be eaten!</p>
<p>(He shuts the door, comes down toward Roxane, and, uncovering, stands at a respectful distance from her.)</p>
