<p>Le Bret, Ragueneau.</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Since you are here, ’tis best she should not know!<br> I was going to your friend just now—was but<br> A few steps from the house, when I saw him<br> Go out. I hurried to him. Saw him turn<br> The corner. . .suddenly, from out a window<br> Where he was passing—was it chance?. . .may be!<br> A lackey let fall a large piece of wood.</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Cowards! O Cyrano!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> I ran—I saw. . .</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> ’Tis hideous!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Saw our poet, Sir—our friend—<br> Struck to the ground—a large wound in his head!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> He’s dead?</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> No—but—I bore him to his room. . .<br> Ah! his room! What a thing to see!—that garret!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> He suffers?</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> No, his consciousness has flown.</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> Saw you a doctor?</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> One was kind—he came.</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> My poor Cyrano!—We must not tell this<br> To Roxane suddenly.—What said this leech?—</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Said,—what, I know not—fever, meningitis!—<br> Ah! could you see him—all his head bound up!—<br> But let us haste!—There’s no one by his bed!—<br> And if he try to rise, Sir, he might die!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (dragging him toward the right):<br> Come! Through the chapel! ’Tis the quickest way!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (appearing on the steps, and seeing Le Bret go away by the colonnade leading to the chapel door):<br> Monsieur le Bret!<br> (Le Bret and Ragueneau disappear without answering):<br> Le Bret goes—when I call!<br> ’Tis some new trouble of good Ragueneau’s.</p>
<p>(She descends the steps.)</p>
