<p>Cyrano, Ragueneau, poets, Carbon de Castel-Jaloux, the cadets, a crowd, then De Guiche.</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Can we come in?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (without stirring):<br> Yes. . .</p>
<p>(Ragueneau signs to his friends, and they come in. At the same time, by door at back, enters Carbon de Castel-Jaloux, in Captain’s uniform. He makes gestures of surprise on seeing Cyrano.)</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON:<br> Here he is!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (raising his head):<br> Captain!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON (delightedly):<br> Our hero! We heard all! Thirty or more<br> Of my cadets are there!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (shrinking back):<br> But. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON (trying to draw him away):<br> Come with me!<br> They will not rest until they see you!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> No!</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON:<br> They’re drinking opposite, at The Bear’s Head.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> I. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON (going to the door and calling across the street in a voice of thunder):<br> He won’t come! The hero’s in the sulks!</p>
<p class="verse">A VOICE (outside):<br> Ah! Sandious!</p>
<p>(Tumult outside. Noise of boots and swords is heard approaching.)</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON (rubbing his hands):<br> They are running ’cross the street!</p>
<p class="verse">CADETS (entering):<br> Mille dious! Capdedious! Pocapdedious!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU (drawing back startled):<br> Gentlemen, are you all from Gascony?</p>
<p class="verse">THE CADETS:<br> All!</p>
<p class="verse">A CADET (to Cyrano):<br> Bravo!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Baron!</p>
<p class="verse">ANOTHER (shaking his hands):<br> Vivat!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Baron!</p>
<p class="verse">THIRD CADET:<br> Come!<br> I must embrace you!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Baron!</p>
<p class="verse">SEVERAL GASCONS:<br> We’ll embrace<br> Him, all in turn!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (not knowing whom to reply to):<br> Baron!. . .Baron!. . .I beg. . .</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Are you all Barons, Sirs?</p>
<p class="verse">THE CADETS:<br> Ay, every one!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Is it true?. . .</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST CADET:<br> Ay—why, you could build a tower<br> With nothing but our coronets, my friend!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (entering, and running up to Cyrano):<br> They’re looking for you! Here’s a crazy mob<br> Led by the men who followed you last night. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (alarmed):<br> What! Have you told them where to find me?</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (rubbing his hands):<br> Yes!</p>
<p class="verse">A BURGHER (entering, followed by a group of men):<br> Sir, all the Marais is a-coming here!</p>
<p>(Outside the street has filled with people. Chaises a porteurs and carriages have drawn up.)</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (in a low voice, smiling, to Cyrano):<br> And Roxane?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (quickly):<br> Hush!</p>
<p class="verse">THE CROWD (calling outside):<br> Cyrano!. . .</p>
<p>(A crowd rush into the shop, pushing one another. Acclamations.)</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU (standing on a table):<br> Lo! my shop<br> Invaded! They break all! Magnificent!</p>
<p class="verse">PEOPLE (crowding round Cyrano):<br> My friend!. . .my friend. . .</p>
<p class="verse">Cyrano:<br> Meseems that yesterday<br> I had not all these friends!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (delighted):<br> Success!</p>
<p class="verse">A YOUNG MARQUIS (hurrying up with his hands held out):<br> My friend,<br> Didst thou but know. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Thou!. . .Marry!. . .thou!. . .Pray when<br> Did we herd swine together, you and I!</p>
<p class="verse">ANOTHER:<br> I would present you, Sir, to some fair dames<br> Who in my carriage yonder. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (coldly):<br> Ah! and who<br> Will first present you, Sir, to me?</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (astonished):<br> What’s wrong?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Hush!</p>
<p class="verse">A MAN OF LETTERS (with writing-board):<br> A few details?. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> No.</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (nudging his elbow):<br> ’Tis Theophrast,<br> Renaudet,. . .of the ‘Court Gazette’!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Who cares?</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> This paper—but it is of great importance!. . .<br> They say it will be an immense success!</p>
<p class="verse">A POET (advancing):<br> Sir. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> What, another!</p>
<p class="verse">THE POET:<br> . . .Pray permit I make<br> A pentacrostic on your name. . .</p>
<p class="verse">SOME ONE (also advancing):<br> Pray, Sir. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Enough! Enough!</p>
<p>(A movement in the crowd. De Guiche appears, escorted by officers. Cuigy, Brissaille, the officers who went with Cyrano the night before. Cuigy comes rapidly up to Cyrano.)</p>
<p class="verse">CUIGY (to Cyrano):<br> Here is Monsieur de Guiche?<br> (A murmur—every one makes way):<br> He comes from the Marshal of Gassion!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (bowing to Cyrano):<br> . . .Who would express his admiration, Sir,<br> For your new exploit noised so loud abroad.</p>
<p class="verse">THE CROWD:<br> Bravo!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (bowing):<br> The Marshal is a judge of valor.</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> He could not have believed the thing, unless<br> These gentlemen had sworn they witnessed it.</p>
<p class="verse">CUIGY:<br> With our own eyes!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (aside to Cyrano, who has an absent air):<br> But. . .you. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Hush!</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET:<br> But. . .You suffer?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (starting):<br> Before this rabble?—I?. . .<br> (He draws himself up, twirls his mustache, and throws back his shoulders):<br> Wait!. . .You shall see!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (to whom Cuigy has spoken in a low voice):<br> In feats of arms, already your career<br> Abounded.—You serve with those crazy pates<br> Of Gascons?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ay, with the Cadets.</p>
<p class="verse">A CADET (in a terrible voice):<br> With us!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (looking at the cadets, ranged behind Cyrano):<br> Ah!. . .All these gentlemen of haughty mien,<br> Are they the famous?. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON:<br> Cyrano!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ay, Captain!</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON:<br> Since all my company’s assembled here,<br> Pray favor me,—present them to my lord!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (making two steps toward De Guiche):<br> My Lord de Guiche, permit that I present—<br> (pointing to the cadets):<br> The bold Cadets of Gascony,<br> Of Carbon of Castel-Jaloux!<br> Brawling and swaggering boastfully,<br> The bold Cadets of Gascony!<br> Spouting of Armory, Heraldry,<br> Their veins a-brimming with blood so blue,<br> The bold Cadets of Gascony,<br> Of Carbon of Castel-Jaloux:</p>
<p class="verse">Eagle-eye, and spindle-shanks,<br> Fierce mustache, and wolfish tooth!<br> Slash-the-rabble and scatter-their-ranks;<br> Eagle-eye and spindle-shanks,<br> With a flaming feather that gayly pranks,<br> Hiding the holes in their hats, forsooth!<br> Eagle-eye and spindle-shanks,<br> Fierce mustache, and wolfish tooth!</p>
<p class="verse">‘Pink-your-Doublet’ and ‘Slit-your-Trunk’<br> Are their gentlest sobriquets;<br> With Fame and Glory their soul is drunk!<br> ‘Pink-your-Doublet’ and ‘Slit-your-Trunk,’<br> In brawl and skirmish they show their spunk,<br> Give rendezvous in broil and fray;<br> ‘Pink-your-Doublet’ and ‘Slit-your-Trunk’<br> Are their gentlest sobriquets!</p>
<p class="verse">What, ho! Cadets of Gascony!<br> All jealous lovers are sport for you!<br> O Woman! dear divinity!<br> What, ho! Cadets of Gascony!<br> Whom scowling husbands quake to see.<br> Blow, ‘taratara,’ and cry ‘Cuckoo.’<br> What, ho! Cadets of Gascony!<br> Husbands and lovers are game for you!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (seated with haughty carelessness in an armchair brought quickly by Ragueneau):<br> A poet! ’Tis the fashion of the hour!<br> —Will you be mine?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> No, Sir,—no man’s!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Last night<br> Your fancy pleased my uncle Richelieu.<br> I’ll gladly say a word to him for you.</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (overjoyed):<br> Great Heavens!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> I imagine you have rhymed<br> Five acts, or so?</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (in Cyrano’s ear):<br> Your play!—your ‘Agrippine!’<br> You’ll see it staged at last!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Take them to him.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (beginning to be tempted and attracted):<br> In sooth,—I would. . .</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> He is a critic skilled:<br> He may correct a line or two, at most.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (whose face stiffens at once):<br> Impossible! My blood congeals to think<br> That other hand should change a comma’s dot.</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> But when a verse approves itself to him<br> He pays it dear, good friend.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> He pays less dear<br> Than I myself; when a verse pleases me<br> I pay myself, and sing it to myself!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> You are proud.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Really? You have noticed that?</p>
<p class="verse">A CADET (entering, with a string of old battered plumed beaver hats, full of holes, slung on his sword):<br> See, Cyrano,—this morning, on the quay<br> What strange bright-feathered game we caught!<br> The hats<br> O’ the fugitives. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON:<br> ‘Spolia opima!’</p>
<p class="verse">ALL (laughing):<br> Ah! ah! ah!</p>
<p class="verse">CUIGY:<br> He who laid that ambush, ’faith!<br> Must curse and swear!</p>
<p class="verse">BRISSAILLE:<br> Who was it?</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> I myself.<br> (The laughter stops):<br> I charged them—work too dirty for my sword,<br> To punish and chastise a rhymster sot.</p>
<p>(Constrained silence.)</p>
<p class="verse">THE CADET (in a low voice, to Cyrano, showing him the beavers):<br> What do with them? They’re full of grease!—a stew?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (taking the sword and, with a salute, dropping the hats at De Guiche’s feet):<br> Sir, pray be good enough to render them<br> Back to your friends.</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (rising, sharply):<br> My chair there—quick!—I go!<br> (To Cyrano passionately):<br> As to you, sirrah!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">VOICE (in the street):<br> Porters for my lord De Guiche!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (who has controlled himself—smiling):<br> Have you read ‘Don Quixote’?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> I have!<br> And doff my hat at th’ mad knight-errant’s name.</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> I counsel you to study. . .</p>
<p class="verse">A PORTER (appearing at back):<br> My lord’s chair!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> . . .The windmill chapter!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (bowing):<br> Chapter the Thirteenth.</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> For when one tilts ’gainst windmills—it may chance. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Tilt I ’gainst those who change with every breeze?</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> . . .That windmill sails may sweep you with their arm<br> Down—in the mire!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Or upward—to the stars!</p>
<p>(De Guiche goes out, and mounts into his chair. The other lords go away whispering together. Le Bret goes to the door with them. The crowd disperses.)</p>
