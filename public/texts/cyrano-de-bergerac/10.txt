<p>Ragueneau, pastry-cooks, then Lise. Ragueneau is writing, with an inspired air, at a small table, and counting on his fingers.</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST PASTRY-COOK (bringing in an elaborate fancy dish):<br> Fruits in nougat!</p>
<p class="verse">SECOND PASTRY-COOK (bringing another dish):<br> Custard!</p>
<p class="verse">THIRD PASTRY-COOK (bringing a roast, decorated with feathers):<br> Peacock!</p>
<p class="verse">FOURTH PASTRY-COOK (bringing a batch of cakes on a slab):<br> Rissoles!</p>
<p class="verse">FIFTH PASTRY-COOK (bringing a sort of pie-dish):<br> Beef jelly!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU (ceasing to write, and raising his head):<br> Aurora’s silver rays begin to glint e’en now on the copper pans, and thou, O<br> Ragueneau! must perforce stifle in thy breast the God of Song! Anon shall<br> come the hour of the lute!—now ’tis the hour of the oven!<br> (He rises. To a cook):<br> You, make that sauce longer, ’tis too short!</p>
<p class="verse">THE COOK:<br> How much too short?</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Three feet.</p>
<p>(He passes on farther.)</p>
<p class="verse">THE COOK:<br> What means he?</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST PASTRY-COOK (showing a dish to Ragueneau):<br> The tart!</p>
<p class="verse">SECOND PASTRY-COOK:<br> The pie!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU (before the fire):<br> My muse, retire, lest thy bright eyes be reddened by the fagot’s blaze!<br> (To a cook, showing him some loaves):<br> You have put the cleft o’ th’ loaves in the wrong place; know you not that<br> the coesura should be between the hemistiches?<br> (To another, showing him an unfinished pasty):<br> To this palace of paste you must add the roof. . .<br> (To a young apprentice, who, seated on the ground, is spitting the fowls):<br> And you, as you put on your lengthy spit the modest fowl and the superb<br> turkey, my son, alternate them, as the old Malherbe loved well to alternate<br> his long lines of verse with the short ones; thus shall your roasts, in<br> strophes, turn before the flame!</p>
<p class="verse">ANOTHER APPRENTICE (also coming up with a tray covered by a napkin):<br> Master, I bethought me erewhile of your tastes, and made this, which will<br> please you, I hope.</p>
<p>(He uncovers the tray, and shows a large lyre made of pastry.)</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU (enchanted):<br> A lyre!</p>
<p class="verse">THE APPRENTICE:<br> ’Tis of brioche pastry.</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU (touched):<br> With conserved fruits.</p>
<p class="verse">THE APPRENTICE:<br> The strings, see, are of sugar.</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU (giving him a coin):<br> Go, drink my health!<br> (Seeing Lise enter):<br> Hush! My wife. Bustle, pass on, and hide that money!<br> (To Lise, showing her the lyre, with a conscious look):<br> Is it not beautiful?</p>
<p class="verse">LISE:<br> ’Tis passing silly!</p>
<p>(She puts a pile of papers on the counter.)</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Bags? Good. I thank you.<br> (He looks at them):<br> Heavens! my cherished leaves! The poems of my friends! Torn, dismembered,<br> to make bags for holding biscuits and cakes!. . .Ah, ’tis the old tale again.<br> . .Orpheus and the Bacchantes!</p>
<p class="verse">LISE (dryly):<br> And am I not free to turn at last to some use the sole thing that your<br> wretched scribblers of halting lines leave behind them by way of payment?</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Groveling ant!. . .Insult not the divine grasshoppers, the sweet singers!</p>
<p class="verse">LISE:<br> Before you were the sworn comrade of all that crew, my friend, you did not<br> call your wife ant and Bacchante!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> To turn fair verse to such a use!</p>
<p class="verse">LISE:<br> ’Faith, ’tis all it’s good for.</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Pray then, madam, to what use would you degrade prose?</p>
