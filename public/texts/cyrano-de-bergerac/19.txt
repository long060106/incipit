<p>Cyrano, Christian.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Embrace me now!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Sir. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> You are brave.</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Oh! but. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Nay, I insist.</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Pray tell me. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Come, embrace! I am her brother.</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Whose brother?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Hers i’ faith! Roxane’s!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (rushing up to him):<br> O heavens!<br> Her brother. . .?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Cousin—brother!. . .the same thing!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> And she has told you. . .?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> All!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> She loves me? say!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Maybe!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (taking his hands):<br> How glad I am to meet you, Sir!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> That may be called a sudden sentiment!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> I ask your pardon. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (looking at him, with his hand on his shoulder):<br> True, he’s fair, the villain!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Ah, Sir! If you but knew my admiration!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> But all those noses?. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Oh! I take them back!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Roxane expects a letter.</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Woe the day!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> How?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> I am lost if I but ope my lips!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Why so?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> I am a fool—could die for shame!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> None is a fool who knows himself a fool.<br> And you did not attack me like a fool.</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Bah! One finds battle-cry to lead th’ assault!<br> I have a certain military wit,<br> But, before women, can but hold my tongue.<br> Their eyes! True, when I pass, their eyes are kind. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> And, when you stay, their hearts, methinks, are kinder?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> No! for I am one of those men—tongue-tied,<br> I know it—who can never tell their love.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> And I, meseems, had Nature been more kind,<br> More careful, when she fashioned me,—had been<br> One of those men who well could speak their love!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Oh, to express one’s thoughts with facile grace!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> . . .To be a musketeer, with handsome face!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Roxane is precieuse. I’m sure to prove<br> A disappointment to her!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (looking at him):<br> Had I but<br> Such an interpreter to speak my soul!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (with despair):<br> Eloquence! Where to find it?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (abruptly):<br> That I lend,<br> If you lend me your handsome victor-charms;<br> Blended, we make a hero of romance!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> How so?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Think you you can repeat what things<br> I daily teach your tongue?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> What do you mean?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Roxane shall never have a disillusion!<br> Say, wilt thou that we woo her, double-handed?<br> Wilt thou that we two woo her, both together?<br> Feel’st thou, passing from my leather doublet,<br> Through thy laced doublet, all my soul inspiring?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> But, Cyrano!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Will you, I say?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> I fear!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Since, by yourself, you fear to chill her heart,<br> Will you—to kindle all her heart to flame—<br> Wed into one my phrases and your lips?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Your eyes flash!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Will you?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Will it please you so?<br> —Give you such pleasure?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (madly):<br> It!. . .<br> (Then calmly, business-like):<br> It would amuse me!<br> It is an enterprise to tempt a poet.<br> Will you complete me, and let me complete you?<br> You march victorious,—I go in your shadow;<br> Let me be wit for you, be you my beauty!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> The letter, that she waits for even now!<br> I never can. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (taking out the letter he had written):<br> See! Here it is—your letter!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> What?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Take it! Look, it wants but the address.</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> But I. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Fear nothing. Send it. It will suit.</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> But have you. . .?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Oh! We have our pockets full,<br> We poets, of love-letters, writ to Chloes,<br> Daphnes—creations of our noddle-heads.<br> Our lady-loves,—phantasms of our brains,<br> —Dream-fancies blown into soap-bubbles! Come!<br> Take it, and change feigned love-words into true;<br> I breathed my sighs and moans haphazard-wise;<br> Call all these wandering love-birds home to nest.<br> You’ll see that I was in these lettered lines,<br> —Eloquent all the more, the less sincere!<br> —Take it, and make an end!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Were it not well<br> To change some words? Written haphazard-wise,<br> Will it fit Roxane?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> ’Twill fit like a glove!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> But. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ah, credulity of love! Roxane<br> Will think each word inspired by herself!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> My friend!</p>
<p>(He throws himself into Cyrano’s arms. They remain thus.)</p>
