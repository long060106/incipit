<p>Christian, Cyrano, two pages.</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Come to my aid!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Not I!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> But I shall die,<br> Unless at once I win back her fair favor.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> And how can I, at once, i’ th’ devil’s name,<br> Lesson you in. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (seizing his arm):<br> Oh, she is there!</p>
<p>(The window of the balcony is now lighted up.)</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (moved):<br> Her window!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Oh! I shall die!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Speak lower!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (in a whisper):<br> I shall die!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> The night is dark. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Well!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> All can be repaired.<br> Although you merit not. Stand there, poor wretch!<br> Fronting the balcony! I’ll go beneath<br> And prompt your words to you. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> But. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Hold your tongue!</p>
<p class="verse">THE PAGES (reappearing at back—to Cyrano):<br> Ho!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Hush!</p>
<p>(He signs to them to speak softly.)</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST PAGE (in a low voice):<br> We’ve played the serenade you bade<br> To Montfleury!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (quickly, in a low voice):<br> Go! lurk in ambush there,<br> One at this street corner, and one at that;<br> And if a passer-by should here intrude,<br> Play you a tune!</p>
<p class="verse">SECOND PAGE:<br> What tune, Sir Gassendist?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Gay, if a woman comes,—for a man, sad!<br> (The pages disappear, one at each street corner. To Christian):<br> Call her!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Roxane!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (picking up stones and throwing them at the window):<br> Some pebbles! wait awhile!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (half-opening the casement):<br> Who calls me?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> I!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Who’s that?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Christian!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (disdainfully):<br> Oh! you?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> I would speak with you.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (under the balcony—to Christian):<br> Good. Speak soft and low.</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> No, you speak stupidly!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Oh, pity me!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> No! you love me no more!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (prompted by Cyrano):<br> You say—Great Heaven!<br> I love no more?—when—I—love more and more!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (who was about to shut the casement, pausing):<br> Hold! ’tis a trifle better! ay, a trifle!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (same play):<br> Love grew apace, rocked by the anxious beating. . .<br> Of this poor heart, which the cruel wanton boy. . .<br> Took for a cradle!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (coming out on to the balcony):<br> That is better! But<br> An if you deem that Cupid be so cruel<br> You should have stifled baby-love in’s cradle!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (same play):<br> Ah, Madame, I assayed, but all in vain<br> This. . .new-born babe is a young. . .Hercules!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Still better!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (same play):<br> Thus he strangled in my heart<br> The. . .serpents twain, of. . .Pride. . .and Doubt!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (leaning over the balcony):<br> Well said!<br> —But why so faltering? Has mental palsy<br> Seized on your faculty imaginative?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (drawing Christian under the balcony, and slipping into his place):<br> Give place! This waxes critical!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> To-day. . .<br> Your words are hesitating.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (imitating Christian—in a whisper):<br> Night has come. . .<br> In the dusk they grope their way to find your ear.</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> But my words find no such impediment.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> They find their way at once? Small wonder that!<br> For ’tis within my heart they find their home;<br> Bethink how large my heart, how small your ear!<br> And,—from fair heights descending, words fall fast,<br> But mine must mount, Madame, and that takes time!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Meseems that your last words have learned to climb.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> With practice such gymnastic grows less hard!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> In truth, I seem to speak from distant heights!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> True, far above; at such a height ’twere death<br> If a hard word from you fell on my heart.</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (moving):<br> I will come down. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (hastily):<br> No!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (showing him the bench under the balcony):<br> Mount then on the bench!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (starting back alarmed):<br> No!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> How, you will not?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (more and more moved):<br> Stay awhile! ’Tis sweet,. . .<br> The rare occasion, when our hearts can speak<br> Our selves unseen, unseeing!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Why—unseen?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ay, it is sweet! Half hidden,—half revealed—<br> You see the dark folds of my shrouding cloak,<br> And I, the glimmering whiteness of your dress:<br> I but a shadow—you a radiance fair!<br> Know you what such a moment holds for me?<br> If ever I were eloquent. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> You were!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Yet never till to-night my speech has sprung<br> Straight from my heart as now it springs.</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Why not?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Till now I spoke haphazard. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> What?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Your eyes<br> Have beams that turn men dizzy!—But to-night<br> Methinks I shall find speech for the first time!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> ’Tis true, your voice rings with a tone that’s new.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (coming nearer, passionately):<br> Ay, a new tone! In the tender, sheltering dusk<br> I dare to be myself for once,—at last!<br> (He stops, falters):<br> What say I? I know not!—Oh, pardon me—<br> It thrills me,—’tis so sweet, so novel. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> How?<br> So novel?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (off his balance, trying to find the thread of his sentence):<br> Ay,—to be at last sincere;<br> Till now, my chilled heart, fearing to be mocked. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Mocked, and for what?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> For its mad beating!—Ay,<br> My heart has clothed itself with witty words,<br> To shroud itself from curious eyes:—impelled<br> At times to aim at a star, I stay my hand,<br> And, fearing ridicule,—cull a wild flower!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> A wild flower’s sweet.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ay, but to-night—the star!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Oh! never have you spoken thus before!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> If, leaving Cupid’s arrows, quivers, torches,<br> We turned to seek for sweeter—fresher things!<br> Instead of sipping in a pygmy glass<br> Dull fashionable waters,—did we try<br> How the soul slakes its thirst in fearless draught<br> By drinking from the river’s flooding brim!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> But wit?. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> If I have used it to arrest you<br> At the first starting,—now, ’twould be an outrage,<br> An insult—to the perfumed Night—to Nature—<br> To speak fine words that garnish vain love-letters!<br> Look up but at her stars! The quiet Heaven<br> Will ease our hearts of all things artificial;<br> I fear lest, ’midst the alchemy we’re skilled in<br> The truth of sentiment dissolve and vanish,—<br> The soul exhausted by these empty pastimes,<br> The gain of fine things be the loss of all things!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> But wit? I say. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> In love ’tis crime,—’tis hateful!<br> Turning frank loving into subtle fencing!<br> At last the moment comes, inevitable,—<br> —Oh, woe for those who never know that moment!<br> When feeling love exists in us, ennobling,<br> Each well-weighed word is futile and soul-saddening!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Well, if that moment’s come for us—suppose it!<br> What words would serve you?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> All, all, all, whatever<br> That came to me, e’en as they came, I’d fling them<br> In a wild cluster, not a careful bouquet.<br> I love thee! I am mad! I love, I stifle!<br> Thy name is in my heart as in a sheep-bell,<br> And as I ever tremble, thinking of thee,<br> Ever the bell shakes, ever thy name ringeth!<br> All things of thine I mind, for I love all things;<br> I know that last year on the twelfth of May-month,<br> To walk abroad, one day you changed your hair-plaits!<br> I am so used to take your hair for daylight<br> That,—like as when the eye stares on the sun’s disk,<br> One sees long after a red blot on all things—<br> So, when I quit thy beams, my dazzled vision<br> Sees upon all things a blonde stain imprinted.</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (agitated):<br> Why, this is love indeed!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ay, true, the feeling<br> Which fills me, terrible and jealous, truly<br> Love,—which is ever sad amid its transports!<br> Love,—and yet, strangely, not a selfish passion!<br> I for your joy would gladly lay mine own down,<br> —E’en though you never were to know it,—never!<br> —If but at times I might—far off and lonely,—<br> Hear some gay echo of the joy I bought you!<br> Each glance of thine awakes in me a virtue,—<br> A novel, unknown valor. Dost begin, sweet,<br> To understand? So late, dost understand me?<br> Feel’st thou my soul, here, through the darkness mounting?<br> Too fair the night! Too fair, too fair the moment!<br> That I should speak thus, and that you should hearken!<br> Too fair! In moments when my hopes rose proudest,<br> I never hoped such guerdon. Naught is left me<br> But to die now! Have words of mine the power<br> To make you tremble,—throned there in the branches?<br> Ay, like a leaf among the leaves, you tremble!<br> You tremble! For I feel,—an if you will it,<br> Or will it not,—your hand’s beloved trembling<br> Thrill through the branches, down your sprays of jasmine!</p>
<p>(He kisses passionately one of the hanging tendrils.)</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Ay! I am trembling, weeping!—I am thine!<br> Thou hast conquered all of me!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Then let death come!<br> ’Tis I, ’tis I myself, who conquered thee!<br> One thing, but one, I dare to ask—</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (under the balcony):<br> A kiss!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (drawing back):<br> What?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Oh!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> You ask. . .?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> I. . .<br> (To Christian, whispering):<br> Fool! you go too quick!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Since she is moved thus—I will profit by it!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (to Roxane):<br> My words sprang thoughtlessly, but now I see—<br> Shame on me!—I was too presumptuous.</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (a little chilled):<br> How quickly you withdraw.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Yes, I withdraw<br> Without withdrawing! Hurt I modesty?<br> If so—the kiss I asked—oh, grant it not.</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (to Cyrano, pulling him by his cloak):<br> Why?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Silence, Christian! Hush!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (leaning over):<br> What whisper you?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> I chid myself for my too bold advances;<br> Said, ‘Silence, Christian!’<br> (The lutes begin to play):<br> Hark! Wait awhile,. . .<br> Steps come!<br> (Roxane shuts the window. Cyrano listens to the lutes, one of which plays a merry, the other a melancholy, tune):<br> Why, they play sad—then gay—then sad! What? Neither man nor woman?—oh!<br> a monk!</p>
<p>(Enter a capuchin friar, with a lantern. He goes from house to house, looking at every door.)</p>
