<p>The same. De Guiche.</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (to Carbon):<br> Good-day!<br> (They examine each other. Aside, with satisfaction):<br> He’s green.</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON (aside):<br> He has nothing left but eyes.</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (looking at the cadets):<br> Here are the rebels! Ay, Sirs, on all sides<br> I hear that in your ranks you scoff at me;<br> That the Cadets, these loutish, mountain-bred,<br> Poor country squires, and barons of Perigord,<br> Scarce find for me—their Colonel—a disdain<br> Sufficient! call me plotter, wily courtier!<br> It does not please their mightiness to see<br> A point-lace collar on my steel cuirass,—<br> And they enrage, because a man, in sooth,<br> May be no ragged-robin, yet a Gascon!<br> (Silence. All smoke and play):<br> Shall I command your Captain punish you?<br> No.</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON:<br> I am free, moreover,—will not punish—</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Ah!</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON:<br> I have paid my company—’tis mine.<br> I bow but to headquarters.</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> So?—in faith!<br> That will suffice.<br> (Addressing himself to the cadets):<br> I can despise your taunts<br> ’Tis well known how I bear me in the war;<br> At Bapaume, yesterday, they saw the rage<br> With which I beat back the Count of Bucquoi;<br> Assembling my own men, I fell on his,<br> And charged three separate times!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (without lifting his eyes from his book):<br> And your white scarf?</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (surprised and gratified):<br> You know that detail?. . .Troth! It happened thus:<br> While caracoling to recall the troops<br> For the third charge, a band of fugitives<br> Bore me with them, close by the hostile ranks:<br> I was in peril—capture, sudden death!—<br> When I thought of the good expedient<br> To loosen and let fall the scarf which told<br> My military rank; thus I contrived<br> —Without attention waked—to leave the foes,<br> And suddenly returning, reinforced<br> With my own men, to scatter them! And now,<br> —What say you, Sir?</p>
<p>(The cadets pretend not to be listening, but the cards and the dice-boxes remain suspended in their hands, the smoke of their pipes in their cheeks. They wait.)</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> I say, that Henri Quatre<br> Had not, by any dangerous odds, been forced<br> To strip himself of his white helmet plume.</p>
<p>(Silent delight. The cards fall, the dice rattle. The smoke is puffed.)</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> The ruse succeeded, though!</p>
<p>(Same suspension of play, etc.)</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Oh, may be! But<br> One does not lightly abdicate the honor<br> To serve as target to the enemy<br> (Cards, dice, fall again, and the cadets smoke with evident delight):<br> Had I been present when your scarf fell low,<br> —Our courage, Sir, is of a different sort—<br> I would have picked it up and put it on.</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Oh, ay! Another Gascon boast!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> A boast?<br> Lend it to me. I pledge myself, to-night,<br> —With it across my breast,—to lead th’ assault.</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Another Gascon vaunt! You know the scarf<br> Lies with the enemy, upon the brink<br> Of the stream,. . .the place is riddled now with shot,—<br> No one can fetch it hither!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (drawing the scarf from his pocket, and holding it out to him):<br> Here it is.</p>
<p>(Silence. The cadets stifle their laughter in their cards and dice-boxes. De Guiche turns and looks at them; they instantly become grave, and set to play. One of them whistles indifferently the air just played by the fifer.)</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (taking the scarf):<br> I thank you. It will now enable me<br> To make a signal,—that I had forborne<br> To make—till now.</p>
<p>(He goes to the rampart, climbs it, and waves the scarf thrice.)</p>
<p class="verse">ALL:<br> What’s that?</p>
<p class="verse">THE SENTINEL (from the top of the rampart):<br> See you yon man<br> Down there, who runs?. . .</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (descending):<br> ’Tis a false Spanish spy<br> Who is extremely useful to my ends.<br> The news he carries to the enemy<br> Are those I prompt him with—so, in a word,<br> We have an influence on their decisions!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Scoundrel!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (carelessly knotting on his scarf):<br> ’Tis opportune. What were we saying?<br> Ah! I have news for you. Last evening<br> —To victual us—the Marshal did attempt<br> A final effort:—secretly he went<br> To Dourlens, where the King’s provisions be.<br> But—to return to camp more easily—<br> He took with him a goodly force of troops.<br> Those who attacked us now would have fine sport!<br> Half of the army’s absent from the camp!</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON:<br> Ay, if the Spaniards knew, ’twere ill for us,<br> But they know nothing of it?</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Oh! they know.<br> They will attack us.</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON:<br> Ah!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> For my false spy<br> Came to warn me of their attack. He said,<br> ‘I can decide the point for their assault;<br> Where would you have it? I will tell them ’tis<br> The least defended—they’ll attempt you there.’<br> I answered, ‘Good. Go out of camp, but watch<br> My signal. Choose the point from whence it comes.’</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON (to cadets):<br> Make ready!</p>
<p>(All rise; sounds of swords and belts being buckled.)</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> ’Twill be in an hour.</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST CADET:<br> Good!. . .</p>
<p>(They all sit down again and take up their games.)</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (to Carbon):<br> Time must be gained. The Marshal will return.</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON:<br> How gain it?</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> You will all be good enough<br> To let yourselves to be killed.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Vengeance! oho!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> I do not say that, if I loved you well,<br> I had chosen you and yours,—but, as things stand,—<br> Your courage yielding to no corps the palm—<br> I serve my King, and serve my grudge as well.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Permit that I express my gratitude. . .</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> I know you love to fight against five score;<br> You will not now complain of paltry odds.</p>
<p>(He goes up with Carbon.)</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (to the cadets):<br> We shall add to the Gascon coat of arms,<br> With its six bars of blue and gold, one more—<br> The blood-red bar that was a-missing there!</p>
<p>(De Guiche speaks in a low voice with Carbon at the back. Orders are given. Preparations go forward. Cyrano goes up to Christian, who stands with crossed arms.)</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (putting his hand on Christian’s shoulder):<br> Christian!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (shaking his head):<br> Roxane!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Alas!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> At least, I’d send<br> My heart’s farewell to her in a fair letter!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> I had suspicion it would be to-day,<br> (He draws a letter out of his doublet):<br> And had already writ. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Show!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Will you. . .?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (taking the letter):<br> Ay!<br> (He opens and reads it):<br> Hold!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> What?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> This little spot!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (taking the letter, with an innocent look):<br> A spot?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> A tear!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Poets, at last,—by dint of counterfeiting—<br> Take counterfeit for true—that is the charm!<br> This farewell letter,—it was passing sad,<br> I wept myself in writing it!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Wept? why?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Oh!. . .death itself is hardly terrible,. . .<br> —But, ne’er to see her more! That is death’s sting!<br> —For. . .I shall never. . .<br> (Christian looks at him):<br> We shall. . .<br> (Quickly):<br> I mean, you. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (snatching the letter from him):<br> Give me that letter!</p>
<p>(A rumor, far off in the camp.)</p>
<p class="verse">VOICE Of SENTINEL:<br> Who goes there? Halloo!</p>
<p>(Shots—voices—carriage-bells.)</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON:<br> What is it?</p>
<p class="verse">A SENTINEL (on the rampart):<br> ’Tis a carriage!</p>
<p>(All rush to see.)</p>
<p class="verse">CRIES:<br> In the camp?<br> It enters!—It comes from the enemy!<br> —Fire!—No!—The coachman cries!—What does he say?<br> —‘On the King’s service!’</p>
<p>(Everyone is on the rampart, staring. The bells come nearer.)</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> The King’s service? How?</p>
<p>(All descend and draw up in line.)</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON:<br> Uncover, all!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> The King’s! Draw up in line!<br> Let him describe his curve as it befits!</p>
<p>(The carriage enters at full speed covered with dust and mud. The curtains are drawn close. Two lackeys behind. It is pulled up suddenly.)</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON:<br> Beat a salute!</p>
<p>(A roll of drums. The cadets uncover.)</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Lower the carriage-steps!</p>
<p>(Two cadets rush forward. The door opens.)</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (jumping down from the carriage):<br> Good-day!</p>
<p>(All are bowing to the ground, but at the sound of a woman’s voice every head is instantly raised.)</p>
