<p>Cyrano, De Guiche.</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (who enters, masked, feeling his way in the dark):<br> What can that cursed Friar be about?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> The devil!. . .If he knows my voice!<br> (Letting go with one hand, he pretends to turn an invisible key. Solemnly):<br> Cric! Crac!<br> Assume thou, Cyrano, to serve the turn,<br> The accent of thy native Bergerac!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (looking at the house):<br> ’Tis there. I see dim,—this mask hinders me!<br> (He is about to enter, when Cyrano leaps from the balcony, holding on to the branch, which bends, dropping him between the door and De Guiche; he pretends to fall heavily, as from a great height, and lies flat on the ground, motionless, as if stunned. De Guiche starts back):<br> What’s this?<br> (When he looks up, the branch has sprung back into its place. He sees only the sky, and is lost in amazement):<br> Where fell that man from?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (sitting up, and speaking with a Gascon accent):<br> From the moon!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> From?. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (in a dreamy voice):<br> What’s o’clock?</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> He’s lost his mind, for sure!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> What hour? What country this? What month? What day?</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> But. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> I am stupefied!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Sir!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Like a bomb<br> I fell from the moon!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (impatiently):<br> Come now!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (rising, in a terrible voice):<br> I say,—the moon!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (recoiling):<br> Good, good! let it be so!. . .He’s raving mad!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (walking up to him):<br> I say from the moon! I mean no metaphor!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> But. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Was’t a hundred years—a minute, since?<br> —I cannot guess what time that fall embraced!—<br> That I was in that saffron-colored ball?</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (shrugging his shoulders):<br> Good! let me pass!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (intercepting him):<br> Where am I? Tell the truth!<br> Fear not to tell! Oh, spare me not! Where? where?<br> Have I fallen like a shooting star?</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Morbleu!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> The fall was lightning-quick! no time to choose<br> Where I should fall—I know not where it be!<br> Oh, tell me! Is it on a moon or earth,<br> that my posterior weight has landed me?</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> I tell you, Sir. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (with a screech of terror, which makes De Guiche start back):<br> No? Can it be? I’m on<br> A planet where men have black faces?</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (putting a hand to his face):<br> What?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (feigning great alarm):<br> Am I in Africa? A native you?</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (who has remembered his mask):<br> This mask of mine. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (pretending to be reassured):<br> In Venice? ha!—or Rome?<br> <br> DE GUICHE (trying to pass):<br> A lady waits. .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (quite reassured):<br> Oh-ho! I am in Paris!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (smiling in spite of himself):<br> The fool is comical!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> You laugh?</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> I laugh,<br> But would get by!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (beaming with joy):<br> I have shot back to Paris!<br> (Quite at ease, laughing, dusting himself, bowing):<br> Come—pardon me—by the last water-spout,<br> Covered with ether,—accident of travel!<br> My eyes still full of star-dust, and my spurs<br> Encumbered by the planets’ filaments!<br> (Picking something off his sleeve):<br> Ha! on my doublet?—ah, a comet’s hair!. . .</p>
<p>(He puffs as if to blow it away.)</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (beside himself):<br> Sir!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (just as he is about to pass, holds out his leg as if to show him something and stops him):<br> In my leg—the calf—there is a tooth<br> Of the Great Bear, and, passing Neptune close,<br> I would avoid his trident’s point, and fell,<br> Thus sitting, plump, right in the Scales! My weight<br> Is marked, still registered, up there in heaven!<br> (Hurriedly preventing De Guiche from passing, and detaining him by the button of his doublet):<br> I swear to you that if you squeezed my nose<br> It would spout milk!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Milk?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> From the Milky Way!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Oh, go to hell!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (crossing his arms):<br> I fall, Sir, out of heaven!<br> Now, would you credit it, that as I fell<br> I saw that Sirius wears a nightcap? True!<br> (Confidentially):<br> The other Bear is still too small to bite.<br> (Laughing):<br> I went through the Lyre, but I snapped a cord;<br> (Grandiloquent):<br> I mean to write the whole thing in a book;<br> The small gold stars, that, wrapped up in my cloak,<br> I carried safe away at no small risks,<br> Will serve for asterisks i’ the printed page!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Come, make an end! I want. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Oh-ho! You are sly!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Sir!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> You would worm all out of me!—the way<br> The moon is made, and if men breathe and live<br> In its rotund cucurbita?</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (angrily):<br> No, no!<br> I want. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ha, ha!—to know how I got up?<br> Hark, it was by a method all my own.</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (wearied):<br> He’s mad!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO(contemptuously):<br> No! not for me the stupid eagle<br> Of Regiomontanus, nor the timid<br> Pigeon of Archytas—neither of those!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Ay, ’tis a fool! But ’tis a learned fool!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> No imitator I of other men!<br> (De Guiche has succeeded in getting by, and goes toward Roxane’s door. Cyrano follows him, ready to stop him by force):<br> Six novel methods, all, this brain invented!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (turning round):<br> Six?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (volubly):<br> First, with body naked as your hand,<br> Festooned about with crystal flacons, full<br> O’ th’ tears the early morning dew distils;<br> My body to the sun’s fierce rays exposed<br> To let it suck me up, as ’t sucks the dew!<br> <br> DE GUICHE (surprised, making one step toward Cyrano):<br> Ah! that makes one!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (stepping back, and enticing him further away):<br> And then, the second way,<br> To generate wind—for my impetus—<br> To rarefy air, in a cedar case,<br> By mirrors placed icosahedron-wise.</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (making another step):<br> Two!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (still stepping backward):<br> Or—for I have some mechanic skill—<br> To make a grasshopper, with springs of steel,<br> And launch myself by quick succeeding fires<br> Saltpeter-fed to the stars’ pastures blue!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (unconsciously following him and counting on his fingers):<br> Three!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Or (since fumes have property to mount)—<br> To charge a globe with fumes, sufficiently<br> To carry me aloft!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (same play, more and more astonished):<br> Well, that makes four!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Or smear myself with marrow from a bull,<br> Since, at the lowest point of Zodiac,<br> Phoebus well loves to suck that marrow up!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (amazed):<br> Five!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (who, while speaking, had drawn him to the other side of the square near a bench):<br> Sitting on an iron platform—thence<br> To throw a magnet in the air. This is<br> A method well conceived—the magnet flown,<br> Infallibly the iron will pursue:<br> Then quick! relaunch your magnet, and you thus<br> Can mount and mount unmeasured distances!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Here are six excellent expedients!<br> Which of the six chose you?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Why, none!—a seventh!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Astonishing! What was it?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> I’ll recount.</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> This wild eccentric becomes interesting!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (making a noise like the waves, with weird gestures):<br> Houuh! Houuh!</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Well.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> You have guessed?</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Not I!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> The tide!<br> I’ th’ witching hour when the moon woos the wave,<br> I laid me, fresh from a sea-bath, on the shore—<br> And, failing not to put head foremost—for<br> The hair holds the sea-water in its mesh—<br> I rose in air, straight! straight! like angel’s flight,<br> And mounted, mounted, gently, effortless,. . .<br> When lo! a sudden shock! Then. . .</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (overcome by curiosity, sitting down on the bench):<br> Then?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Oh! then. . .<br> (Suddenly returning to his natural voice):<br> The quarter’s gone—I’ll hinder you no more:<br> The marriage-vows are made.</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE (springing up):<br> What? Am I mad?<br> That voice?<br> (The house-door opens. Lackeys appear carrying lighted candelabra. Light. Cyrano gracefully uncovers):<br> That nose—Cyrano?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (bowing):<br> Cyrano.<br> While we were chatting, they have plighted troth.</p>
<p class="verse">DE GUICHE:<br> Who?<br> (He turns round. Tableau. Behind the lackeys appear Roxane and Christian, holding each other by the hand. The friar follows them, smiling. Ragueneau also holds a candlestick. The duenna closes the rear, bewildered, having made a hasty toilet):<br> Heavens!</p>
