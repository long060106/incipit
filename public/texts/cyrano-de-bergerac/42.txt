<p>Roxane, Christian. In the distance cadets coming and going. Carbon and De Guiche give orders.</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (running up to Christian):<br> Ah, Christian, at last!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (taking her hands):<br> Now tell me why—<br> Why, by these fearful paths so perilous—<br> Across these ranks of ribald soldiery,<br> You have come?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Love, your letters brought me here!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> What say you?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> ’Tis your fault if I ran risks!<br> Your letters turned my head! Ah! all this month,<br> How many!—and the last one ever bettered<br> The one that went before!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> What!—for a few<br> Inconsequent love-letters!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Hold your peace!<br> Ah! you cannot conceive it! Ever since<br> That night, when, in a voice all new to me,<br> Under my window you revealed your soul—<br> Ah! ever since I have adored you! Now<br> Your letters all this whole month long!—meseemed<br> As if I heard that voice so tender, true,<br> Sheltering, close! Thy fault, I say! It drew me,<br> The voice o’ th’ night! Oh! wise Penelope<br> Would ne’er have stayed to broider on her hearthstone,<br> If her Ulysses could have writ such letters!<br> But would have cast away her silken bobbins,<br> And fled to join him, mad for love as Helen!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> But. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> I read, read again—grew faint for love;<br> I was thine utterly. Each separate page<br> Was like a fluttering flower-petal, loosed<br> From your own soul, and wafted thus to mine.<br> Imprinted in each burning word was love<br> Sincere, all-powerful. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> A love sincere!<br> Can that be felt, Roxane!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Ay, that it can!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> You come. . .?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> O, Christian, my true lord, I come—<br> (Were I to throw myself, here, at your knees,<br> You would raise me—but ’tis my soul I lay<br> At your feet—you can raise it nevermore!)<br> —I come to crave your pardon. (Ay, ’tis time<br> To sue for pardon, now that death may come!)<br> For the insult done to you when, frivolous,<br> At first I loved you only for your face!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (horror-stricken):<br> Roxane!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> And later, love—less frivolous—<br> Like a bird that spreads its wings, but can not fly—<br> Arrested by your beauty, by your soul<br> Drawn close—I loved for both at once!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> And now?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Ah! you yourself have triumphed o’er yourself,<br> And now, I love you only for your soul!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (stepping backward):<br> Roxane!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Be happy. To be loved for beauty—<br> A poor disguise that time so soon wears threadbare—<br> Must be to noble souls—to souls aspiring—<br> A torture. Your dear thoughts have now effaced<br> That beauty that so won me at the outset.<br> Now I see clearer—and I no more see it!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Oh!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> You are doubtful of such victory?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (pained):<br> Roxane!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> I see you cannot yet believe it.<br> Such love. . .?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> I do not ask such love as that!<br> I would be loved more simply; for. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> For that<br> Which they have all in turns loved in thee?—<br> Shame!<br> Oh! be loved henceforth in a better way!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> No! the first love was best!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Ah! how you err!<br> ’Tis now that I love best—love well! ’Tis that<br> Which is thy true self, see!—that I adore!<br> Were your brilliance dimmed. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Hush!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> I should love still!<br> Ay, if your beauty should to-day depart. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Say not so!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Ay, I say it!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Ugly? How?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Ugly! I swear I’d love you still!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> My God!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Are you content at last?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (in a choked voice):<br> Ay!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> What is wrong?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (gently pushing her away):<br> Nothing. . .I have two words to say:—one second. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> But?. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (pointing to the cadets):<br> Those poor fellows, shortly doomed to death,—<br> My love deprives them of the sight of you:<br> Go,—speak to them—smile on them ere they die!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (deeply affected):<br> Dear Christian!. . .</p>
<p>(She goes up to the cadets, who respectfully crowd round her.)</p>
