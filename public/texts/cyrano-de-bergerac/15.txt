<p>Cyrano, Roxane.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Blessed be the moment when you condescend—<br> Remembering that humbly I exist—<br> To come to meet me, and to say. . .to tell?. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (who has unmasked):<br> To thank you first of all. That dandy count,<br> Whom you checkmated in brave sword-play<br> Last night,. . .he is the man whom a great lord,<br> Desirous of my favor. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ha, De Guiche?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (casting down her eyes):<br> Sought to impose on me. . .for husband. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ay! Husband!—dupe-husband!. . .Husband a la mode!<br> (Bowing):<br> Then I fought, happy chance! sweet lady, not<br> For my ill favor—but your favors fair!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Confession next!. . .But, ere I make my shrift,<br> You must be once again that brother-friend<br> With whom I used to play by the lake-side!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ay, you would come each spring to Bergerac!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Mind you the reeds you cut to make your swords?. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> While you wove corn-straw plaits for your dolls’ hair!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Those were the days of games!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> And blackberries!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> In those days you did everything I bid!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Roxane, in her short frock, was Madeleine. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Was I fair then?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> You were not ill to see!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Ofttimes, with hands all bloody from a fall,<br> You’d run to me! Then—aping mother-ways—<br> I, in a voice would-be severe, would chide,—<br> (She takes his hand):<br> ‘What is this scratch, again, that I see here?’<br> (She starts, surprised):<br> Oh! ’Tis too much! What’s this?<br> (Cyrano tries to draw away his hand):<br> No, let me see!<br> At your age, fie! Where did you get that scratch?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> I got it—playing at the Porte de Nesle.</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (seating herself by the table, and dipping her handkerchief in a glass of water):<br> Give here!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (sitting by her):<br> So soft! so gay maternal-sweet!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> And tell me, while I wipe away the blood,<br> How many ’gainst you?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Oh! A hundred—near.</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Come, tell me!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> No, let be. But you, come tell<br> The thing, just now, you dared not. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (keeping his hand):<br> Now, I dare!<br> The scent of those old days emboldens me!<br> Yes, now I dare. Listen. I am in love.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ah!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> But with one who knows not.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ah!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Not yet.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ah!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> But who, if he knows not, soon shall learn.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ah!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> A poor youth who all this time has loved<br> Timidly, from afar, and dares not speak. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ah!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Leave your hand; why, it is fever-hot!—<br> But I have seen love trembling on his lips.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ah!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (bandaging his hand with her handkerchief):<br> And to think of it! that he by chance—<br> Yes, cousin, he is of your regiment!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ah!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (laughing):<br> —Is cadet in your own company!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ah!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> On his brow he bears the genius-stamp;<br> He is proud, noble, young, intrepid, fair. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (rising suddenly, very pale):<br> Fair!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Why, what ails you?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Nothing; ’tis. . .<br> (He shows his hand, smiling):<br> This scratch!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> I love him; all is said. But you must know<br> I have only seen him at the Comedy. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> How? You have never spoken?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Eyes can speak.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> How know you then that he. . .?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Oh! people talk<br> ’Neath the limes in the Place Royale. . .<br> Gossip’s chat<br> Has let me know. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> He is cadet?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> In the Guards.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> His name?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Baron Christian de Neuvillette.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> How now?. . .He is not of the Guards!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> To-day<br> He is not join your ranks, under Captain<br> Carbon de Castel-Jaloux.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ah, how quick,<br> How quick the heart has flown!. . .But, my poor child. . .</p>
<p class="verse">THE DUENNA (opening the door):<br> The cakes are eaten, Monsieur Bergerac!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Then read the verses printed on the bags!<br> (She goes out):<br> . . .My poor child, you who love but flowing words,<br> Bright wit,—what if he be a lout unskilled?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> No, his bright locks, like D’Urfe’s heroes. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ah!<br> A well-curled pate, and witless tongue, perchance!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Ah no! I guess—I feel—his words are fair!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> All words are fair that lurk ’neath fair mustache!<br> —Suppose he were a fool!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (stamping her foot):<br> Then bury me!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (after a pause):<br> Was it to tell me this you brought me here?<br> I fail to see what use this serves, Madame.</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Nay, but I felt a terror, here, in the heart,<br> On learning yesterday you were Gascons<br> All of your company. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> And we provoke<br> All beardless sprigs that favor dares admit<br> ’Midst us pure Gascons—(pure! Heaven save the mark!<br> They told you that as well?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Ah! Think how I<br> Trembled for him!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (between his teeth):<br> Not causelessly!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> But when<br> Last night I saw you,—brave, invincible,—<br> Punish that dandy, fearless hold your own<br> Against those brutes, I thought—I thought, if he<br> Whom all fear, all—if he would only. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Good.<br> I will befriend your little Baron.</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Ah!<br> You’ll promise me you will do this for me?<br> I’ve always held you as a tender friend.</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ay, ay.</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Then you will be his friend?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> I swear!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> And he shall fight no duels, promise!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> None.</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> You are kind, cousin! Now I must be gone.<br> (She puts on her mask and veil quickly; then, absently):<br> You have not told me of your last night’s fray.<br> Ah, but it must have been a hero-fight!. . .<br> —Bid him to write.<br> (She sends him a kiss with her fingers):<br> How good you are!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ay! Ay!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> A hundred men against you? Now, farewell.—<br> We are great friends?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Ay, ay!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Oh, bid him write!<br> You’ll tell me all one day—A hundred men!—<br> Ah, brave!. . .How brave!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (bowing to her):<br> I have fought better since.</p>
<p>(She goes out. Cyrano stands motionless, with eyes on the ground. A silence. The door (right) opens. Ragueneau looks in.)</p>
