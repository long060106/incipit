<p>The same, all but De Guiche.</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (entreatingly):<br> Roxane!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> No!</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST CADET (to the others):<br> She stays!</p>
<p class="verse">ALL (hurrying, hustling each other, tidying themselves):<br> A comb!—Soap!—My uniform is torn!—A needle!—A ribbon!—Lend your mirror!—My cuffs!—Your curling-iron!—A razor!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (to Cyrano, who still pleads with her):<br> No! Naught shall make me stir from this spot!</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON (who, like the others, has been buckling, dusting, brushing his hat, settling his plume, and drawing on his cuffs, advances to Roxane, and ceremoniously):<br> It is perchance more seemly, since things are thus, that I present to you some of these gentlemen who are about to have the honor of dying before your eyes.<br> (Roxane bows, and stands leaning on Christian’s arm, while Carbon introduces the cadets to her):<br> Baron de Peyrescous de Colignac!</p>
<p class="verse">THE CADET (with a low reverence):<br> Madame. . .</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON (continuing):<br> Baron de Casterac de Cahuzac,—Vidame de Malgouyre Estressac Lésbas d’Escarabiot, Chevalier d’Antignac-Juzet, Baron Hillot de Blagnac-Salechan de Castel Crabioules. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> But how many names have you each?</p>
<p class="verse">BARON HILLOT:<br> Scores!</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON (to Roxane):<br> Pray, open the hand that holds your kerchief.</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (opens her hand, and the handkerchief falls):<br> Why?</p>
<p>(The whole company start forward to pick it up.)</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON (quickly raising it):<br> My company had no flag. But now, by my faith, they will have the fairest in all the camp!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (smiling):<br> ’Tis somewhat small.</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON (tying the handkerchief on the staff of his lance):<br> But—’tis of lace!</p>
<p class="verse">A CADET (to the rest):<br> I could die happy, having seen so sweet a face, if I had something in my stomach—were it but a nut!</p>
<p class="verse">CARBON (who has overheard, indignantly):<br> Shame on you! What, talk of eating when a lovely woman!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> But your camp air is keen; I myself am famished. Pasties, cold <i>fricassée</i>, old wines—there is my bill of fare? Pray bring it all here.</p>
<p>(Consternation.)</p>
<p class="verse">A CADET:<br> All that?</p>
<p class="verse">ANOTHER:<br> But where on earth find it?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (quietly):<br> In my carriage.</p>
<p class="verse">ALL:<br> How?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Now serve up—carve! Look a little closer at my coachman, gentlemen, and you will recognize a man most welcome. All the sauces can be sent to table hot, if we will!</p>
<p class="verse">THE CADETS (rushing pellmell to the carriage):<br> ’Tis Ragueneau!<br> (Acclamations):<br> Oh, oh!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (looking after them):<br> Poor fellows!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (kissing her hand):<br> Kind fairy!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU (standing on the box like a quack doctor at a fair):<br> Gentlemen!. . .</p>
<p>(General delight.)</p>
<p class="verse">THE CADETS:<br> Bravo! bravo!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> . . .The Spaniards, gazing on a lady so dainty fair, overlooked the fare so dainty!. . .</p>
<p>(Applause.)</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (in a whisper to Christian):<br> Hark, Christian!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> . . .And, occupied with gallantry, perceived not—<br> (He draws a plate from under the seat, and holds it up):<br> —The galantine!. . .</p>
<p>(Applause. The galantine passes from hand to hand.)</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (still whispering to Christian):<br> Prythee, one word!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> And Venus so attracted their eyes that Diana could secretly pass by with—<br> (He holds up a shoulder of mutton):<br> —her fawn!</p>
<p>(Enthusiasm. Twenty hands are held out to seize the shoulder of mutton.)</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (in a low whisper to Christian):<br> I must speak to you!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (to the cadets, who come down, their arms laden with food):<br> Put it all on the ground!</p>
<p>(She lays all out on the grass, aided by the two imperturbable lackeys who were behind the carriage.)</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (to Christian, just as Cyrano is drawing him apart):<br> Come, make yourself of use!</p>
<p>(Christian comes to help her. Cyrano’s uneasiness increases.)</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> Truffled peacock!</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST CADET (radiant, coming down, cutting a big slice of ham):<br> By the mass! We shall not brave the last hazard without having had a gullet-full!—<br> (quickly correcting himself on seeing Roxane):<br> —Pardon! A Balthazar feast!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU (throwing down the carriage cushions):<br> The cushions are stuffed with ortolans!</p>
<p>(Hubbub. They tear open and turn out the contents of the cushions. Bursts of laughter—merriment.)</p>
<p class="verse">THIRD CADET:<br> Ah! Viedaze!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU (throwing down to the cadets bottles of red wine):<br> Flasks of rubies!—<br> (and white wine):<br> —Flasks of topaz!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (throwing a folded tablecloth at Cyrano’s head):<br> Unfold me that napkin!—Come, come! be nimble!</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU (waving a lantern):<br> Each of the carriage-lamps is a little larder!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (in a low voice to Christian, as they arrange the cloth together):<br> I must speak with you ere you speak to her.</p>
<p class="verse">RAGUENEAU:<br> My whip-handle is an Arles sausage!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (pouring out wine, helping):<br> Since we are to die, let the rest of the army shift for itself. All for the<br> Gascons! And mark! if De Guiche comes, let no one invite him!<br> (Going from one to the other):<br> There! there! You have time enough! Do not eat too fast!—Drink a little.—Why are you crying?</p>
<p class="verse">FIRST CADET:<br> It is all so good!. . .</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Tut!—Red or white?—Some bread for Monsieur de Carbon!—a knife! Pass your plate!—a little of the crust? Some more? Let me help you!—Some champagne?—A wing?</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO (who follows her, his arms laden with dishes, helping her to wait on everybody):<br> How I worship her!</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE (going up to Christian):<br> What will you?</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN:<br> Nothing.</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Nay, nay, take this biscuit, steeped in muscat; come!. . . but two drops!</p>
<p class="verse">CHRISTIAN (trying to detain her):<br> Oh! tell me why you came?</p>
<p class="verse">ROXANE:<br> Wait; my first duty is to these poor fellows.—Hush! In a few minutes. . .</p>
<p class="verse">LE BRET (who had gone up to pass a loaf on the end of a lance to the sentry on the rampart):<br> De Guiche!</p>
<p class="verse">CYRANO:<br> Quick! hide flasks, plates, pie-dishes, game-baskets! Hurry!—Let us all look unconscious!<br> (To Ragueneau):<br> Up on your seat!—Is everything covered up?</p>
<p>(In an instant all has been pushed into the tents, or hidden under doublets, cloaks, and beavers. De Guiche enters hurriedly—stops suddenly, sniffing the air. Silence.)</p>
