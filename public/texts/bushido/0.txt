<p class="verse">TO MY BELOVED UNCLE<br> TOKITOSHI OTA<br> WHO TAUGHT ME TO REVERE THE PAST<br> AND<br> TO ADMIRE THE DEEDS OF THE SAMURAI<br> I DEDICATE<br> THIS LITTLE BOOK</p>
<p>—“That way</p>
<p>Over the mountain, which who stands upon,</p>
<p>Is apt to doubt if it be indeed a road;</p>
<p>While if he views it from the waste itself,</p>
<p>Up goes the line there, plain from base to brow,</p>
<p>Not vague, mistakable! What’s a break or two</p>
<p>Seen from the unbroken desert either side?</p>
<p>And then (to bring in fresh philosophy)</p>
<p>What if the breaks themselves should prove at last</p>
<p>The most consummate of contrivances</p>
<p>To train a man’s eye, teach him what is faith?”</p>
<p>—Robert Browning,</p>
<p><i>Bishop Blougram’s Apology</i>.</p>
<p class="verse">“There are, if I may so say, three powerful spirits, which have from time to time, moved on the face of the waters, and given a predominant impulse to the moral sentiments and energies of mankind. These are the spirits of liberty, of religion, and of honor.”<br> —Hallam,<br> <i>Europe in the Middle Ages</i>.</p>
