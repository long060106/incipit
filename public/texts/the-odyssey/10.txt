<p class="lede">Arrival of Ulysses at the land of Aeolus, who gives him the winds in a bag⁠—Folly of the seamen, who untie the bag while Ulysses is asleep⁠—A tempest⁠—Disastrous encounter with the gigantic laestrigons⁠—Arrival at the island of Circè⁠—Transformation of the Greeks to swine, and recovery of their former shape⁠—Preparations for a journey to the region of the dead.</p>
<p class="verse">“We reached the Aeolian isle, where Aeolus, <br> Dear to the gods, a son of Hippotas, <br> Made his abode. It was a floating isle; <br> A wall of brass enclosed it, and smooth rocks <br> Edged it around. Twelve children in his halls <br> Were born, six daughters and six blooming sons; <br> He gave his daughters to his sons for wives. <br> And they with their dear father and his queen <br> Banquet from day to day, with endless change <br> Of meats before them. In his halls all day <br> The sound of pipes is in the perfumed air; <br> At night the youths beside their modest wives <br> Sleep on fair couches spread with tapestry. <br> So coming to his town and fair abode, <br> I found a friendly welcome. One full month <br> The monarch kept me with him, and inquired <br> Of all that might concern the fate of Troy, <br> The Argive fleet, and the return to Greece, <br> And just as it befell I told him all. <br> And when I spake to him of going thence, <br> And prayed him to dismiss me, he complied, <br> And helped to make us ready for the sea. <br> The bladder of a bullock nine years old <br> He gave, in which he had compressed and bound <br> The stormy winds of air; for Saturn’s son <br> Had given him empire o’er the winds, with power <br> To calm them or to rouse them at his will. <br> This in our roomy galley he made fast <br> With a bright chain of silver, that no breath <br> Of ruder air might blow. He only left <br> The west wind free to waft our ships and us <br> Upon our way. But that was not to be; <br> We perished by a folly of our own.</p>
<p class="verse">“Nine days we held our way, both day and night; <br> And now appeared in sight our native fields <br> On the tenth night, where on the shore we saw <br> Men kindling fires. Meantime a pleasant sleep <br> Had overcome my weary limbs, for long <br> Had I been guiding with incessant toil <br> The rudder, nor would trust it to the hand <br> Of any other, such was my desire <br> To reach our country by the shortest way. <br> Then talked my crew among themselves, and said <br> That I had brought with me from Aeolus, <br> The large-souled son of Hippotas, rich gifts <br> Of gold and silver. Standing side by side <br> And looking at each other, thus they said:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘How wonderfully is our chief revered <br> And loved by all men, wander where he will <br> Into what realm soever! From the coast <br> Of Troy he sailed with many precious things, <br> His share of spoil, while we, who with him went <br> And with him came, are empty-handed yet; <br> And now hath Aeolus, to show how much <br> He prizes him, bestowed the treasures here. <br> Come, let us see them; let us know how much <br> Of gold and silver is concealed in this.’</p>
<p class="verse">“Thus speaking to each other, they obeyed <br> The evil counsel. They untied the sack, <br> And straight the winds rushed forth and seized the ship, <br> And swept the crews, lamenting bitterly, <br> Far from their country out upon the deep; <br> And then I woke, and in my noble mind <br> Bethought me whether I should drop at once <br> Into the deep and perish, or remain <br> And silently endure and keep my place <br> Among the living. I remained, endured, <br> And covered with my mantle lay within <br> My galley, while the furious whirlwind bore <br> Back to the Aeolian isle our groaning crews.</p>
<p class="verse">“We landed on the coast, and to our barques <br> Brought water. Then my men prepared a meal <br> Beside the fleet; and having tasted food <br> And wine, I took a herald and a friend, <br> And, hastening to the sumptuous palace-halls <br> Of Aeolus, I found him with his wife <br> And children banqueting. We sat us down <br> Upon the threshold at the palace-doors, <br> And they were all astonished, and inquired:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘Why art thou here? What god thine enemy <br> Pursues thee, O Ulysses! whom we sent <br> So well prepared to reach thy native land, <br> Thy home, or any place that pleased thee most?’</p>
<p class="verse">“They spake, and sorrowfully I replied:⁠— <br> ‘The fault is all with my unthinking crew <br> And my own luckless slumber. Yet, my friends, <br> Repair the mischief, for ye have the power.’</p>
<p class="verse">“Thus with submissive words I spake, but they <br> Sat mute, the father only answered me:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘Hence with thee! Leave our island instantly, <br> Vilest of living men! It may not be <br> That I receive or aid as he departs <br> One who is hated by the blessed gods⁠— <br> And thou art hated by the gods. Away!’</p>
<p class="verse">“He spake, and sent us from the palace-door <br> Lamenting. Sorrowfully went we on. <br> And now with rowing hard and long⁠—the fruit <br> Of our own folly⁠—all our crews lost heart, <br> And every hope of safe return was gone.</p>
<p class="verse">“Six days and nights we sailed; the seventh we came <br> To lofty Laestrigoni with wide gates, <br> The city of Lamos, where, on going forth, <br> The shepherd calls to shepherd entering in. <br> There might a man who never yields to sleep <br> Earn double wages, first in pasturing herds, <br> And then in tending sheep; for there the fields <br> Grazed in the daytime are by others grazed <br> At night. We reached its noble haven, girt <br> By towering rocks that rise on every side, <br> And the bold shores run out to form its mouth⁠— <br> A narrow entrance. There the other crews <br> Stationed their barques, and moored them close beside <br> Each other, in that hill-encircled port. <br> No billow, even the smallest, rises there; <br> The water glimmers with perpetual calm. <br> I only kept my dark-hulled ship without, <br> And bound its cable to a jutting rock.</p>
<p class="verse">“I climbed a rugged headland, and looked forth. <br> No marks of tilth appeared, the work of men <br> Or oxen, only smokes that from below <br> Rose in the air. And then I sent forth scouts <br> To learn what race of men who live by bread <br> Inhabited the land. Two chosen men <br> I sent, a herald made the third; and these <br> Went inland by a level path, on which <br> The wains brought fuel from the woody heights <br> Into the city. On their way they met, <br> Before the town, a damsel with an ewer⁠— <br> The stately daughter of Antiphates, <br> The Laestrigonian, who was coming down <br> To where Artacia’s smoothly flowing fount <br> Gave water for the city. They drew near <br> And spake, and asked her who was sovereign there, <br> And who his people. Straight she pointed out <br> A lofty pile in which her father dwelt. <br> They entered that proud palace, and beheld, <br> Tall as a mountain peak, the monarch’s wife, <br> And shuddered at the sight. With eager haste <br> She called her husband, King Antiphates, <br> From council. With a murderous intent <br> He came, and, seizing one of my poor friends, <br> Devoured him, while the other two betook <br> Themselves to sudden flight and reached the ships. <br> And then he raised a fearful yell that rang <br> Through all the city. The strong Laestrigons <br> Rushed forth by thousands from all sides, more like <br> To giants than to common men. They hurled <br> Stones of enormous weight from cliffs above, <br> And cries of those who perished and the crash <br> Of shattered galleys rose. They speared our friends <br> Like fishes for their horrid feasts, and thus <br> Bore them away. While those within the port <br> Were slaughtered, drawing my good sword I cut <br> The hawsers fastened to my ship’s blue prow, <br> And cheered my men, and bade them fling themselves <br> Upon the oars, that so we might escape <br> Our threatened fate. They heard, and plied their oars <br> Like men who rowed for life. The galley shot <br> Forth from these beetling rocks into the sea <br> Full gladly; all the others perished there.</p>
<p class="verse">“Onward we sailed, with sorrow in our hearts <br> For our lost friends, though glad to be reprieved <br> From death. And now we landed at an isle⁠— <br> Aeaea, where the fair-haired Circè dwelt, <br> A goddess high in rank and skilled in song, <br> Own sister of the wise Aeaetes. Both <br> Were children of the source of light, the Sun, <br> And Persè, Ocean’s daughter, brought them forth. <br> We found a haven here, where ships might lie; <br> And guided by some deity we brought <br> Our galley silently against the shore, <br> And disembarked, and gave two days and nights <br> To rest, unmanned with hardship and with grief.</p>
<p class="verse">“When bright-haired Morning brought the third day round, <br> I took my spear and my good sword, and left <br> The ship, and climbed a height, in hope to spy <br> Some trace of human toil, or hear some voice. <br> On a steep precipice I stood, and saw <br> From the broad earth below a rising smoke, <br> Where midst the thickets and the forest-ground <br> Stood Circè’s palace. Seeing that dark smoke, <br> The thought arose within my mind that there <br> I should inquire. I pondered till at last <br> This seemed the wisest⁠—to return at once <br> To my good ship upon the ocean-side, <br> And give my crew their meal, and send them forth <br> To view the region. Coming to the spot <br> Where lay my well-oared barque, some pitying god <br> Beneath whose eye I wandered forth alone <br> Sent a huge stag into my very path, <br> High-horned, which from his pasture in the wood <br> Descended to the riverside to drink, <br> For grievously he felt the hot sun’s power. <br> Him as he ran I smote; the weapon pierced, <br> Just at the spine, the middle of his back. <br> The brazen blade passed through, and with a moon <br> He fell amid the dust, and yielded up <br> His life. I went to him, and set my foot <br> Against him, and plucked forth the brazen spear, <br> And left it leaning there. And then I broke <br> Lithe osiers from the shrubs, and twined of these <br> A rope, which, doubled, was an ell in length. <br> With that I tied the enormous creature’s feet, <br> And slung him on my neck, and brought him thus <br> To my black ship. I used the spear to prop <br> My steps, since he no longer could be borne <br> Upon the shoulder, aided by the hand, <br> Such was the animal’s bulk. I flung him down <br> Before the ship, encouraging my men <br> With cheerful words, and thus I said to each:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘My friends, we will not, wretched as we are, <br> Go down to Pluto’s realm before our time. <br> While food and wine are yet within the hold <br> Of our good galley, let us not forget <br> Our daily meals, and famine-stricken pine.’</p>
<p class="verse">“I spake; they all obeyed, and at my word <br> Came forth, and standing by the barren deep <br> Admired the stag, for he was huge of bulk; <br> And when their eyes were tired with wondering, <br> My people washed their hands, and soon had made <br> A noble banquet ready. All that day <br> Till set of sun we sat and feasted there <br> Upon the abundant meat and delicate wine; <br> And when the sun went down, and darkness came, <br> We slept upon the shore. But when the Morn, <br> The rosy-fingered child of Dawn, looked forth, <br> I called a council of my men and spake:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘Give ear, my friends, amid your sufferings, <br> To words that I shall say. We cannot here <br> Know which way lies the west, nor where the east, <br> Nor where the sun, that shines for all mankind, <br> Descends below the earth, nor where again <br> He rises from it. Yet will we consult, <br> If room there be for counsel⁠—which I doubt, <br> For when I climbed that height I overlooked <br> An isle surrounded by the boundless deep⁠— <br> An isle low lying. In the midst I saw <br> Smoke rising from a thicket of the wood.’</p>
<p class="verse">“I spake; their courage died within their hearts <br> As they remembered what Antiphates, <br> The Laestrigon, had done, and what foul deeds <br> The cannibal Cyclops, and they wept aloud. <br> Tears flowed abundantly, but tears were now <br> Of no avail to our unhappy band.</p>
<p class="verse">“Numbering my well-armed men, I made of them <br> Two equal parties, giving each its chief. <br> Myself commanded one; Eurylochus, <br> The hero, took the other in his charge.</p>
<p class="verse">“Then in a brazen helm we shook the lots; <br> The lot of brave Eurylochus leaped forth, <br> And he with two-and-twenty of our men <br> Went forward with quick steps, and yet in tears, <br> While we as sorrowful were left behind.</p>
<p class="verse">“They found the fair abode where Circè dwelt, <br> A palace of hewn stone within the vale, <br> Yet nobly seated. There were mountain wolves <br> And lions round it, which herself had tamed <br> With powerful drugs; yet these assaulted not <br> The visitors, but, wagging their long tails, <br> Stood on their hinder feet, and fawned on them, <br> Like mastiffs on their master when he comes <br> From banqueting and brings them food. So fawned <br> The strong-clawed wolves and lions on my men. <br> With fear my men beheld those beasts of prey, <br> Yet went, and, standing in the portico <br> Of the bright-haired divinity, they heard <br> Her sweet voice singing, as within she threw <br> The shuttle through the wide immortal web, <br> Such as is woven by the goddesses⁠— <br> Delicate, bright of hue, and beautiful.</p>
<p class="verse">“Polites then, a chief the most beloved <br> And most discreet of all my comrades, spake:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘Someone is here, my friends, who sweetly sings, <br> Weaving an ample web, and all the floor <br> Rings to her voice. Whoever she may be, <br> Woman or goddess, let us call to her.’</p>
<p class="verse">“He spake; aloud they called, and forth she came <br> And threw at once the shining doors apart, <br> And bade my comrades enter. Without thought <br> They followed her. Eurylochus alone <br> Remained without, for he suspected guile. <br> She led them in and seated them on thrones. <br> Then mingling for them Pramnian wine with cheese, <br> Meal, and fresh honey, and infusing drugs <br> Into the mixture⁠—drugs which made them lose <br> The memory of their home⁠—she handed them <br> The beverage and they drank. Then instantly <br> She touched them with a wand, and shut them up <br> In sties, transformed to swine in head and voice, <br> Bristles and shape, though still the human mind <br> Remained to them. Thus sorrowing they were driven <br> Into their cells, where Circè flung to them <br> Acorns of oak and ilex, and the fruit <br> Of cornel, such as nourish wallowing swine.</p>
<p class="verse">“Back came Eurylochus to our good ship <br> With news of our poor comrades and their fate, <br> He strove to speak, but could not; he was stunned <br> By that calamity; his eyes were filled <br> With tears, and his whole soul was given to grief. <br> We marvelled greatly; long we questioned him, <br> And thus he spake of our lost friends at last:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘Through yonder thickets, as thou gav’st command, <br> Illustrious chief! we went, until we reached <br> A stately palace of hewn stones, within <br> A vale, yet nobly seated. Someone there, <br> Goddess or woman, weaving busily <br> An ample web, sang sweetly as she wrought. <br> My comrades called aloud, and forth she came, <br> And threw at once the shining doors apart, <br> And bade us enter. Without thought the rest <br> Followed, while I alone, suspecting guile, <br> Remained without. My comrades, from that hour, <br> Were seen no more; not one of them again <br> Came forth, though long I sat and watched for them.’</p>
<p class="verse">“He spake; I slung my silver-studded sword <br> Upon my shoulders⁠—a huge blade of brass⁠— <br> And my bow with it, and commanded him <br> To lead the way. He seized and clasped my knees <br> With both his hands in attitude of prayer, <br> And sorrowfully said these winged words:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘Take me not thither; force me not to go, <br> O foster-child of Jove! but leave me here; <br> For thou wilt not return, I know, nor yet <br> Deliver one of our lost friends. Our part <br> Is to betake ourselves to instant flight <br> With these who yet remain, and so escape.’</p>
<p class="verse">“He spake, and I replied: ‘Eurylochus, <br> Remain thou here, beside our roomy ship, <br> Eating and drinking. I shall surely go. <br> A strong necessity is laid on me.’</p>
<p class="verse">“I spake, and from the ship and shore went up <br> Into the isle; and when I found myself <br> Within that awful valley, and not far <br> From the great palace in which Circè dwelt, <br> The sorceress, there met me on my way <br> A youth; he seemed in manhood’s early prime, <br> When youth has most of grace. He took my hand <br> And held it, and, accosting me, began:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘Rash mortal! whither art thou wandering thus <br> Alone among the hills, where every place <br> Is strange to thee? Thy comrades are shut up <br> In Circè’s palace in close cells like swine. <br> Com’st thou to set them free? Nay, thou like them <br> Wilt rather find thyself constrained to stay. <br> Let me bestow the means to make thee safe <br> Against that mischief. Take this potent herb, <br> And bear it with thee to the palace-halls <br> Of Circè, and it shall avert from thee <br> The threatened evil. I will now reveal <br> The treacherous arts of Circè. She will bring <br> A mingled draught to thee, and drug the bowl, <br> But will not harm thee thus; the virtuous plant <br> I gave thee will prevent it. Hear yet more: <br> When she shall smite thee with her wand, draw forth <br> Thy good sword from thy thigh and rush at her <br> As if to take her life, and she will crouch <br> In fear, and will solicit thine embrace. <br> Refuse her not, that so she may release <br> Thy comrades, and may send thee also back <br> To thine own land; but first exact of her <br> The solemn oath which binds the blessed gods, <br> That she will meditate no other harm <br> To thee, nor strip thee of thy manly strength.’</p>
<p class="verse">“The Argus-queller spake, and plucked from earth <br> The potent plant and handed it to me, <br> And taught me all its powers. The root is black, <br> The blossom white as milk. Among the gods <br> Its name is Moly; hard it is for men <br> To dig it up; the gods find nothing hard.</p>
<p class="verse">“Back through the woody island Hermes went <br> Toward high Olympus, while I took my way <br> To Circè’s halls, yet with a beating heart. <br> There, as I stood beneath the portico <br> Of that bright-haired divinity, I called <br> Aloud; the goddess heard my voice and came, <br> And threw at once the shining doors apart, <br> And prayed me to come in. I followed her, <br> Yet grieving still. She led me in and gave <br> A seat upon a silver-studded throne, <br> Beautiful, nobly wrought, and placed beneath <br> A footstool, and prepared a mingled draught <br> Within a golden chalice, and infused <br> A drug with mischievous intent. She gave <br> The cup; I drank it off; the charm wrought not, <br> And then she smote me with her wand and said:⁠— <br> ‘Go to the sty, and with thy fellows sprawl.’</p>
<p class="verse">“She spake; but drawing forth the trusty sword <br> Upon my thigh, I rushed at her as if <br> To take her life. She shrieked and, stooping low, <br> Ran underneath my arm and clasped my knees, <br> And uttered piteously these winged words:⁠— <br> “ ‘Who art thou? of what race and of what land, <br> And who thy parents? I am wonder-struck <br> To see that thou couldst drink that magic juice <br> And yield not to its power. No living man, <br> Whoever he might be, that tasted once <br> Those drugs, or passed them o’er his lips, has yet <br> Withstood them. In thy breast a spirit dwells <br> Not to be thus subdued. Art thou not then <br> Ulysses, master of wise stratagems, <br> Whose coming hither, on his way from Troy, <br> In his black galley, oft has been foretold <br> By Hermes of the golden wand? But sheathe <br> Thy sword and share my couch, that, joined in love, <br> Each may hereafter trust the other’s faith.’</p>
<p class="verse">“She spake, and I replied: ‘How canst thou ask, <br> O Circè, that I gently deal with thee, <br> Since thou, in thine own palace, hast transformed <br> My friends to swine, and plottest even now <br> To keep me with thee, luring me to pass <br> Into thy chamber and to share thy couch, <br> That thou mayst strip me of my manly strength <br> I come not to thy couch till thou engage, <br> O goddess, by a solemn oath, that thou <br> Wilt never seek to do me further harm.’</p>
<p class="verse">“I spake; she straightway took the oath required, <br> And, after it was uttered and confirmed, <br> Up to her sumptuous couch I went. Meanwhile <br> Four diligent maidens ministered within <br> The palace⁠—servants of the household they, <br> Who had their birth from fountains and from groves, <br> And sacred rivers flowing to the sea. <br> One spread the thrones with gorgeous coverings; <br> Above was purple arras, and beneath <br> Were linen webs; another, setting forth <br> The silver tables just before the thrones, <br> Placed on them canisters of gold; a third <br> Mingled the rich wines in a silver bowl, <br> And placed the golden cups; and, last, the fourth <br> Brought water from the fountain, and beneath <br> A massive tripod kindled a great fire <br> And warmed the water. When it boiled within <br> The shining brass, she led me to the bath, <br> And washed me from the tripod. On my head <br> And shoulders pleasantly she shed the streams <br> That from my members took away the sense <br> Of weariness, unmanning body and mind. <br> And when she thus had bathed me and with oil <br> Anointed me, she put a princely cloak <br> And tunic on me, led me in, and showed <br> My seat⁠—a stately silver-studded throne, <br> High-wrought⁠—and placed a footstool for my feet. <br> Then came a handmaid with a golden ewer, <br> And from it poured pure water for my hands <br> Into a silver laver. Next she placed <br> A polished table near to me, on which <br> The matron of the palace laid the feast, <br> With many delicacies from her store, <br> And bade me eat. The banquet pleased me not. <br> My thoughts were elsewhere; dark imaginings <br> Were in my mind. When Circè marked my mood, <br> As in a gloomy revery I sat, <br> And put not forth my hands to touch the feast, <br> She came to me and spake these winged words:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘Why sittest thou like one who has no power <br> Of speech, Ulysses, wrapt in thoughts that gnaw <br> Thy heart, and tasting neither food nor wine? <br> Still dost thou dream of fraud? It is not well <br> That thou shouldst fear it longer, since I pledged <br> Myself against it with a mighty oath.’</p>
<p class="verse">“She spake, and I replied: ‘What man whose heart <br> Is faithful could endure to taste of food <br> Or wine till he should see his captive friends <br> Once more at large? If with a kind intent <br> Thou bidst me eat and drink, let me behold <br> With mine own eyes my dear companions free.’</p>
<p class="verse">“I spake; and Circè took her wand and went <br> Forth from her halls, and, opening the gate <br> That closed the sty, drove forth what seemed a herd <br> Of swine in their ninth year. They ranged themselves <br> Before her, and she went from each to each <br> And shed on them another drug. Forthwith <br> Fell from their limbs the bristles which had grown <br> All over them, when mighty Circè gave <br> At first the baleful potion. Now again <br> My friends were men, and younger than before, <br> And of a nobler mien and statelier growth. <br> They knew me all; and each one pressed my hand <br> In his, and there were tears and sobs of joy <br> That sounded through the palace. Circè too <br> Was moved, the mighty goddess; she drew near <br> And stood by me, and spake these winged words:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘Son of Laertes, nobly born and wise, <br> Ulysses! go to thy good ship beside <br> The sea and draw it up the beach, and hide <br> The goods and weapons in the caverns there, <br> And come thou back and bring with thee thy friends.’</p>
<p class="verse">“She spake, and easily my generous mind <br> Was moved by what she said. Forthwith I went <br> To my good ship beside the sea, and found <br> My friends in tears, lamenting bitterly. <br> As in some grange the calves come leaping round <br> A herd of kine returning to the stall <br> From grassy fields where they have grazed their fill, <br> Nor can the stall contain the young which spring <br> Around their mothers with continual bleat; <br> So when my comrades saw me through their tears, <br> They sprang to meet me, and their joy was such <br> As if they were in their own native land <br> And their own city, on the rugged coast <br> Of Ithaca, where they were born and reared; <br> And as they wept they spake these winged words:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘O foster-child of Jove! we welcome thee <br> On thy return with a delight as great <br> As if we all had reached again the land <br> That gave us birth, our Ithaca. And now <br> Tell by what death our other friends have died.’</p>
<p class="verse">“They spake; I answered with consoling words:⁠— <br> ‘First draw our galley up the beach, and hide <br> Our goods and all our weapons in the caves, <br> And then let all make haste to follow me, <br> And see our friends in Circè’s sacred halls, <br> Eating and drinking at the plenteous board.’</p>
<p class="verse">“I spake; and cheerfully my men obeyed, <br> Save that Eurylochus alone essayed <br> To hold them back, and spake these winged words:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘Ah, whither are we going, wretched ones? <br> Are ye so eager for an evil fate, <br> That ye must go where Circè dwells, who waits <br> To turn us into lions, swine, or wolves, <br> Forced to remain and guard her spacious house? <br> So was it with the Cyclops, when our friends <br> Went with this daring chief to his abode, <br> And perished there through his foolhardiness.’</p>
<p class="verse">“He spake; and then I thought to draw my sword <br> From my stout thigh, and with the trenchant blade <br> Strike off his head and let it fall to earth, <br> Though he were my near kinsman; yet the rest <br> Restrained me, each one speaking kindly words:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘Nay, foster-child of Jove! if thou consent, <br> This man shall stay behind and with the ship, <br> And he shall guard the ship, but lead us thou <br> To where the sacred halls of Circè stand.’</p>
<p class="verse">“They spake, and from the ship and shore went up <br> Into the land, nor was Eurylochus <br> Left with the ship; he followed, for he feared <br> My terrible threat. Meantime had Circè bathed <br> My comrades at the palace, and with oil <br> Anointed them, and robed them in fair cloaks <br> And tunics. There we found them banqueting. <br> When they and those who came with me beheld <br> Each other, and the memory of the past <br> Came back to them, they wept abundantly, <br> And all the palace echoed with their sobs. <br> And then the mighty goddess came and said:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘Son of Laertes, nobly born and wise, <br> Prolong thou not these sorrows. Well I know <br> What ye have suffered on the fishy deep, <br> And all the evil that malignant men <br> Have done to you on land. Now take the food <br> Before you, drink the wine, till ye receive <br> Into your hearts the courage that was yours <br> When long ago ye left your fatherland, <br> The rugged Ithaca. Ye are unnerved <br> And spiritless with thinking constantly <br> On your long wanderings, and your minds allow <br> No space for mirth, for ye have suffered much.’</p>
<p class="verse">“She spake; her words persuaded easily <br> Our generous minds, and there from day to day <br> We lingered a full year, and banqueted <br> Nobly on plenteous meats and delicate wines. <br> But when the year was ended, and the hours <br> Renewed their circle, my beloved friends <br> From Circè’s palace called me forth and said:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘Good chief, do not forget thy native land, <br> If fate indeed permit that ever thou <br> Return in safety to that lofty pile <br> Thy palace in the country of thy birth.’</p>
<p class="verse">“So spake they, and my generous mind was moved. <br> All that day long until the set of sun <br> We sat and feasted on the abundant meats <br> And delicate wines; and when the sun went down <br> They took their rest within the darkened halls, <br> While I to Circè’s sumptuous couch went up, <br> A suppliant at her knees. The goddess heard <br> My prayer, as thus in winged words I said:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘O Circè! make, I pray, the promise good <br> Which thou hast given, to send me to my home. <br> My heart is pining for it, and the hearts <br> Of all my friends, who weary out my life <br> Lamenting round me when thou art not nigh.’</p>
<p class="verse">“I spake; the mighty goddess thus replied:⁠— <br> ‘Son of Laertes, nobly born and wise, <br> Ulysses! ye must not remain with me <br> Unwillingly; but ye have yet to make <br> Another voyage, and must visit first <br> The abode of Pluto, and of Proserpine <br> His dreaded queen, and there consult the soul <br> Of the blind seer Tiresias⁠—him of Thebes⁠— <br> Whose intellect was spared; for Proserpine <br> Gave back to him in death the power of mind, <br> That only he might know of things to come. <br> The rest are shades that flit from place to place.’</p>
<p class="verse">“Thus spake the goddess; and my heart was wrung <br> With sorrow, and I sat upon the couch <br> And wept, nor could I longer wish to live <br> And see the light of day. But when my grief, <br> With shedding tears and tossing where I sat, <br> Was somewhat spent, I spake to Circè thus:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘O Circè, who will guide me when I make <br> This voyage? for no galley built by man <br> Has ever yet arrived at Pluto’s realm.’</p>
<p class="verse">“I spake; the mighty goddess answered me:⁠— <br> ‘Son of Laertes, nobly born and wise, <br> Take thou no thought of who shall guide thy barque, <br> But raise the mast and spread the glimmering sail, <br> And seat thyself, and let the north-wind waft <br> Thy galley on. As soon as thou shalt cross <br> Océanus, and come to the low shore <br> And groves of Proserpine, the lofty groups <br> Of poplars, and the willows that let fall <br> Their withered fruit, moor thou thy galley there <br> In the deep eddies of Océanus, <br> And pass to Pluto’s comfortless abode. <br> There into Acheron are poured the streams <br> Of Pyriphlegethon, and of that arm <br> Of Styx, Cocytus. At the place where meet <br> The ever-roaring waters stands a rock; <br> Draw near to that, and there I bid thee scoop <br> In earth a trench, a cubit long and wide. <br> And round about it pour to all the dead <br> Libations⁠—milk and honey first, and next <br> Rich wine, and lastly water, scattering <br> White meal upon them. Offer there thy prayer <br> Fervently to that troop of airy forms, <br> And make the vow that thou wilt sacrifice, <br> When thou at last shalt come to Ithaca, <br> A heifer without blemish, barren yet, <br> In thine own courts, and heap the altar-pyre <br> With things of price; and to the seer alone, <br> Tiresias, by himself, a ram whose fleece <br> Is wholly black, the best of all thy flocks. <br> And after thou hast duly offered prayer <br> To all the illustrious nations of the dead, <br> Then sacrifice a ram and a black ewe, <br> Their faces turned toward Erebus, but thine <br> The other way and toward the river streams. <br> Thither the souls of those who died will flock <br> In multitudes. Then call thy friends, and give <br> Command to flay in haste the sheep that lie <br> Slain by the cruel brass, and, burning there <br> The carcasses, pay worship to the gods⁠— <br> The powerful Pluto and dread Proserpine. <br> Draw then the sword upon thy thigh, and sit, <br> And suffer none of all those airy forms <br> To touch the blood until thou first bespeak <br> Tiresias. He will come, and speedily⁠— <br> The leader of the people⁠—and will tell <br> What voyage thou must make, what length of way <br> Thou yet must measure, and will show thee how <br> Thou mayst return across the fishy deep.’</p>
<p class="verse">“She spake; and while she spake the Morn looked forth <br> Upon her golden throne. The Nymph bestowed <br> On me a cloak and tunic, and arrayed <br> Herself in a white robe with ample folds⁠— <br> A delicate web and graceful. Round her loins <br> She clasped a shining zone of gold, and hung <br> A veil upon her forehead. Forth I went <br> Throughout the palace and aroused my friends, <br> And thus I said in cheerful tones to each:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘No longer give yourselves to idle rest <br> And pleasant slumber; we are to depart. <br> The gracious Circè counsels us to go.’</p>
<p class="verse">“I spake, and easily their generous minds <br> Inclined to me. Yet brought I not away <br> All my companions safely from the isle. <br> Elpenor was the youngest of our band, <br> Not brave in war was he, nor wise in thought. <br> He, overcome with wine, and for the sake <br> Of coolness, had lain down to sleep, apart <br> From all the rest, in Circè’s sacred house; <br> And as my friends bestirred themselves, the noise <br> And tumult roused him; he forgot to come <br> By the long staircase; headlong from the roof <br> He plunged; his neck was broken at the spine, <br> And his soul went to the abode of death.</p>
<p class="verse">“My friends came round me, and I said to them:⁠— <br> ‘Haply your thought may be that you are bound <br> For the dear country of your birth; but know <br> That Circè sends us elsewhere, to consult <br> The Theban seer, Tiresias, in the abode <br> Of Pluto and the dreaded Proserpine.’</p>
<p class="verse">“I spake, and their hearts failed them as they heard; <br> They sat them down, and wept, and tore their hair, <br> But fruitless were their sorrow and their tears.</p>
<p class="verse">“Thus as we sadly moved to our good ship <br> Upon the seashore, weeping all the while, <br> Circè, meantime, had visited its deck, <br> And there had bound a ram and a black ewe <br> By means we saw not; for what eye discerns <br> The presence of a deity, who moves <br> From place to place, and wills not to be seen?”</p>
