<p class="lede">Antinoüs slain by an arrow from the bow of Ulysses, who now declares himself, and begins the slaughter⁠—Arms brought to him and to Eumaeus and Philoetius by Telemachus⁠—Arms brought to the suitors by Melanthius the goatherd⁠—Appearance of Pallas in the shape of Mentor⁠—Death of all the suitors⁠—Medon and Phemius spared⁠—The unfaithful serving-women hanged.</p>
<p class="verse">Then did Ulysses cast his rags aside, <br> And, leaping to the threshold, took his stand <br> On its broad space, with bow and quiver filled <br> With arrows. At his feet the hero poured <br> The winged shafts, and to the suitors called:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“That difficult strife is ended. Now I take <br> Another mark, which no man yet has hit. <br> Now shall I see if I attain my aim, <br> And, by the aid of Phoebus, win renown.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake; and, turning, at Antinoüs aimed <br> The bitter shaft⁠—Antinoüs, who just then <br> Had grasped a beautiful two-eared cup of gold, <br> About to drink the wine. He little thought <br> Of wounds and death; for who, when banqueting <br> Among his fellows, could suspect that one <br> Alone against so many men would dare, <br> However bold, to plan his death, and bring <br> On him the doom of fate? Ulysses struck <br> The suitor with the arrow at the throat. <br> The point came through the tender neck behind, <br> Sideways he sank to earth; his hand let fall <br> The cup; the dark blood in a thick warm stream <br> Gushed from the nostrils of the smitten man. <br> He spurned the table with his feet, and spilled <br> The viands; bread and roasted meats were flung <br> To lie polluted on the floor. Then rose <br> The suitors in a tumult, when they saw <br> The fallen man; from all their seats they rose <br> Throughout the hall, and to the massive walls <br> Looked eagerly; there hung no buckler there, <br> No sturdy lance for them to wield. They called <br> Thus to Ulysses with indignant words:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Stranger! in evil hour hast thou presumed <br> To aim at men; and thou shalt henceforth bear <br> Part in no other contest. Even now <br> Is thy destruction close to thee. Thy hand <br> Hath slain the noblest youth in Ithaca. <br> The vultures shall devour thy flesh for this.”</p>
<p class="verse">So each one said; they deemed he had not slain <br> The suitor wittingly; nor did they see, <br> Blind that they were, the doom which in that hour <br> Was closing round them all. Then with a frown <br> The wise Ulysses looked on them, and said:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Dogs! ye had thought I never would come back <br> From Ilium’s coast, and therefore ye devoured <br> My substance here, and offered violence <br> To my maidservants, and pursued my wife <br> As lovers, while I lived. Ye dreaded not <br> The gods who dwell in the great heaven, nor feared <br> Vengeance hereafter from the hands of men; <br> And now destruction overhangs you all.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake, and all were pale with fear, and each <br> Looked round for some escape from death. Alone <br> Eurymachus found voice, and answered thus:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“If thou indeed be he, the Ithacan <br> Ulysses, now returned to thine old home, <br> Well hast thou spoken of the many wrongs <br> Done to thee by the Achaians in thy house <br> And in thy fields. But there the man lies slain <br> Who was the cause of all. Antinoüs first <br> Began this course of wrong. Nor were his thoughts <br> So much of marriage as another aim⁠— <br> Which Saturn’s son denied him⁠—to bear rule <br> Himself o’er those who till the pleasant fields <br> Of Ithaca, first having slain thy son <br> In ambush. But he now has met his fate. <br> Spare, then, thy people. We will afterward <br> Make due amends in public for the waste <br> Here in thy palace of the food and wine. <br> For each of us shall bring thee twenty beeves, <br> And brass and gold, until thy heart shall be <br> Content. Till then we cannot blame thy wrath.”</p>
<p class="verse">Sternly the wise Ulysses frowned, and said: <br> “Eurymachus, if thou shouldst offer me <br> All that thou hast, thy father’s wealth entire, <br> And add yet other gifts, not even then <br> Would I refrain from bloodshed, ere my hand <br> Avenged my wrongs upon the suitor-crew. <br> Choose then to fight or flee, whoever hopes <br> Escape from death and fate; yet none of you <br> Will now, I think, avoid that bitter doom.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake. At once their knees and head grew faint, <br> And thus Eurymachus bespake the rest:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“This man, O friends, to his untamable arm <br> Will give no rest, but with that bow in hand, <br> And quiver, will send forth from where he stands <br> His shafts, till he has slain us all. Prepare <br> For combat then, and draw your swords, and hold <br> The tables up against his deadly shafts, <br> And rush together at him as one man, <br> And drive him from the threshold through the door. <br> Then, hurrying through the city, let us sound <br> The alarm, and soon he will have shot his last.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake, and, drawing his keen two-edged sword <br> Of brass, sprang toward him with a dreadful cry, <br> Just as the great Ulysses, sending forth <br> An arrow, smote the suitor on the breast, <br> Beside the nipple. The swift weapon stood <br> Fixed in his liver; to the ground he flung <br> The sword, and, reeling giddily around <br> The table, fell; he brought with him to earth <br> The viands and the double cup, and smote <br> The pavement with his forehead heavily, <br> And in great agony. With both his feet <br> He struck and shook his throne, and darkness came <br> Over his eyes. Then rushed Amphinomus <br> Against the glorious chief, and drew his sword <br> To thrust him from the door. Telemachus <br> O’ertook him, and between his shoulders drove <br> A brazen lance. Right through his breast it went, <br> And he fell headlong, with his forehead dashed <br> Against the floor. Telemachus drew back, <br> And left his long spear in Amphinomus, <br> Lest, while he drew it forth, someone among <br> The Achaians might attack him with the sword, <br> And thrust him through or hew him down. In haste <br> He reached his father’s side, and quickly said:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Now, father, will I bring to thee a shield, <br> Two javelins, and a helmet wrought of brass, <br> Well fitted to the temples. I will case <br> Myself in armor, and will also give <br> Arms to the swineherd, and to him who tends <br> The beeves; for men in armor combat best.”</p>
<p class="verse">And wise Ulysses answered: “Bring them then, <br> And quickly, while I yet have arrows here <br> For my defence, lest, when I am alone, <br> They drive me from my station at the door.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake. Obedient to his father’s word, <br> Telemachus was soon within the room <br> In which the glorious arms were laid. He took <br> Four bucklers thence, eight spears, and helmets four <br> Of brass, each darkened with its horsehair crest, <br> And bore them forth, and quickly stood again <br> Beside his father. But he first encased <br> His limbs in brass; his followers also put <br> Their shining armor on, and took their place <br> Beside the wise Ulysses, eminent <br> In shrewd devices. He, while arrows yet <br> Were ready to his hand, with every aim <br> Brought down a suitor; side by side they fell. <br> But when the shafts were spent, the archer-king <br> Leaned his good bow beside the shining wall, <br> Against a pillar of the massive pile, <br> And round his shoulders slung a fourfold shield, <br> And crowned his martial forehead with a helm <br> Wrought fairly, with a heavy horsehair crest <br> That nodded gallantly above, and took <br> In hand the two stout lances tipped with brass.</p>
<p class="verse">In the strong wall there was a postern door, <br> And, near the outer threshold of the pile, <br> A passage from it to a narrow lane, <br> Closed with well-fitting doors. Ulysses bade <br> The noble swineherd take his station there. <br> And guard it well, as now the only way <br> Of entrance. Agelaüs called aloud <br> To all his fellows, and bespake them thus:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Friends! will no one among you all go up <br> To yonder postern door, and make our plight <br> Known to the people? Then the alarm would spread, <br> And this man haply will have shot his last.”</p>
<p class="verse">Melanthius, keeper of the goats, replied: <br> “Nay, noble Agelaüs; ’tis too near <br> The palace gate; the entrance of the lane <br> Is narrow, and a single man, if brave, <br> Against us all might hold it. I will bring <br> Arms from the chamber to equip you all; <br> For there within, and nowhere else, I deem, <br> Ulysses and his son laid up their arms.”</p>
<p class="verse">Thus having said, the keeper of the goats, <br> Melanthius, climbed the palace stairs, and gained <br> The chamber of Ulysses. Taking thence <br> Twelve shields, as many spears, as many helms <br> Of brass, with each its heavy horsehair plume, <br> He came, and gave them to the suitors’ hands. <br> Then sank the hero’s heart, and his knees shook <br> As he beheld the suitors putting on <br> Their armor, and uplifting their long spears. <br> The mighty task appalled him, and he thus <br> Bespake Telemachus with winged words:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Telemachus, some woman here, or else <br> Melanthius, makes the battle hard for us.”</p>
<p class="verse">And thus discreet Telemachus replied: <br> “Father, I erred in this. I was the cause, <br> And no one else; I left the solid door <br> Ajar; the spy was shrewder far than I. <br> Now, good Eumaeus, shut the chamber door, <br> And see if any of the palace-maids <br> Have brought these arms, or if I rightly fix <br> The guilt upon Melanthius, Dolius’ son.”</p>
<p class="verse">So talked they with each other, while again <br> Melanthius, stealing toward the chamber, thought <br> To bring yet other shining weapons thence. <br> The noble swineherd marked him as he went, <br> And quickly drawing near Ulysses said:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Son of Laertes! nobly born and wise! <br> The knave whom we suspect is on his way <br> Up to thy chamber. Tell me now, I pray, <br> And plainly, shall I make an end of him, <br> If I may prove the stronger man, or bring <br> The wretch into thy presence, to endure <br> The vengeance due to all the iniquities <br> Plotted by him against thee in these halls?”</p>
<p class="verse">Ulysses, the sagacious, answered thus: <br> “Telemachus and I will keep at bay <br> The suitors in this place, however fierce <br> Their onset, while ye two bind fast his hands <br> And feet behind his back, and bringing him <br> Into the chamber, with the door made fast <br> Behind you, tie him with a double cord, <br> And draw him up a lofty pillar close <br> To the timbers of the roof, that, swinging there, <br> He may live long and suffer grievous pain.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake; they hearkened and obeyed, and went <br> Up to the chamber unperceived by him <br> Who stood within and searched a nook for arms. <br> On each side of the entrance, by its posts, <br> They waited for Melanthius. Soon appeared <br> The goatherd at the threshold of the room, <br> Bearing a beautiful helmet in one hand, <br> And in the other a broad ancient shield, <br> Defaced by age and mould. Laertes once, <br> The hero, bore it when a youth, but now <br> Long time it lay unused, with gaping seams.</p>
<p class="verse">They sprang and seized the goatherd, dragging him <br> Back to the chamber by the hair; and there <br> They cast him, in an agony of fear, <br> Upon the floor, and bound his hands and feet <br> With a stout cord behind his back, as bade <br> The great Ulysses, much-enduring son <br> Of old Laertes. Round him then they looped <br> A double cord, and swung him up beside <br> A lofty pillar, till they brought him near <br> The timbers of the roof. And then didst thou, <br> Eumaeus, say to him in jeering words:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Melanthius, there mayst thou keep watch all night <br> On a soft bed, a fitting place for thee; <br> And when the Mother of the Dawn shall come <br> Upon her golden seat from ocean’s streams, <br> Thou wilt not fail to see her. Thou mayst then <br> Drive thy goats hither for the suitors’ feast.”</p>
<p class="verse">They left him in that painful plight, and put <br> Their armor on, and closed the shining door, <br> And went, and by Ulysses, versed in wiles, <br> Stood breathing valor. Four were they who stood <br> Upon that threshold, while their foes within <br> Were many and brave. Then Pallas, child of Jove, <br> Drew near, like Mentor both in shape and voice. <br> Ulysses saw her, and rejoiced and said:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Come, Mentor, to the aid of one who loves <br> And has befriended thee, thy peer in age.”</p>
<p class="verse">Thus said Ulysses, but believed he spake <br> To Pallas, scatterer of hosts. Fierce shouts <br> Came from the suitors in the hall, and first, <br> Thus Agelaüs railed, Damastor’s son:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Mentor, let not Ulysses wheedle thee <br> To join him, and make war on us, for this <br> Our purpose is, and it will be fulfilled: <br> When by our hands the father and the son <br> Are slain, thou also shalt be put to death <br> For this attempt, and thy own head shall be <br> The forfeit. When we shall have taken thus <br> Thy life with our good weapons, we will seize <br> On all thou hast, on all thy wealth within <br> Thy dwelling or without, and, mingling it <br> With the possessions of Ulysses, leave <br> Within thy palaces no son of thine <br> Or daughter living, and no virtuous wife <br> Of thine, abiding here in Ithaca.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake, and woke new anger in the heart <br> Of Pallas, and she chid Ulysses thus:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Ulysses, thou art not, in might of arm <br> And courage, what thou wert when waging war <br> Nine years without a pause against the men <br> Of Troy for Helen’s sake, the child of Jove, <br> And many didst thou slay in deadly strife, <br> And Priam’s city, with its spacious streets, <br> Was taken through thy counsels. How is it <br> That, coming to thy own possessions here <br> And thy own palace, thou dost sadly find <br> Thy ancient valor fail thee in the strife <br> Against the suitors? Now draw near, my friend, <br> And stand by me, and see what I shall do, <br> And own that Mentor, son of Alcimus, <br> Amid a press of foes requites thy love.”</p>
<p class="verse">She spake, but gave not to Ulysses yet <br> The certain victory; for she meant to put <br> To further proof the courage and the might <br> Both of Ulysses and his emulous son. <br> To the broad palace roof she rose, and sat <br> In shape a swallow. Agelaüs now, <br> Damastor’s son, cheered on with gallant words <br> His friends; so also did Amphimedon, <br> Eurynomus, and Demoptolemus, <br> Polyctor’s son, Peisander, and with these <br> Sagacious Polybus. These six excelled <br> In valor all the suitors who survived, <br> And they were fighting for their lives. The bow <br> And the fleet shafts had smitten down their peers. <br> Thus to his fellows Agelaüs spake:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“O friends, this man will now be forced to stay <br> His fatal hand. See, Mentor leaves his side, <br> After much empty boasting, and those four <br> Are at the entrance gate alone. Now aim <br> At him with your long spears⁠—not all at once, <br> Let six first hurl their weapons, and may Jove <br> Grant that we strike Ulysses down, and win <br> Great glory! For the others at his side <br> We care but little, if their leader fail.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake; they hearkened. Eagerly they cast <br> Their lances. Pallas made their aim to err. <br> One struck a pillar of the massive pile; <br> One struck the panelled door; one ashen shaft, <br> Heavy with metal, rang against the wall.</p>
<p class="verse">And when they had escaped that flight of spears, <br> Hurled from the crowd, the much-enduring man, <br> Ulysses, thus to his companions said:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Now is the time, my friends, to send our spears <br> Into the suitor-crowd, who, not content <br> With wrongs already done us, seek our lives.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake, and, aiming opposite, they cast <br> Their spears. The weapon which Ulysses flung <br> Slew Demoptolemus; his son struck down <br> Euryades; the herdsman smote to death <br> Peisander, and the swineherd Elatus. <br> These at one moment fell, and bit the dust <br> Of the broad floor. Back flew the suitor-crowd <br> To a recess; and after them the four <br> Rushed on, and plucked their weapons from the dead.</p>
<p class="verse">Again the suitors threw their spears; again <br> Did Pallas cause their aim to err. One struck <br> A pillar of the massive pile, and one <br> The panelled door; another ashen shaft, <br> Heavy with metal, rang against the wall. <br> Yet did the weapon of Amphimedon <br> Strike lightly on the wrist Telemachus. <br> The brass just tore the skin. Ctesippus grazed <br> The shoulder of Eumaeus with his spear, <br> Above the shield; the spear flew over it <br> And fell to earth. Then they who stood beside <br> The sage Ulysses, versed in wiles, once more <br> Flung their keen spears. The spoiler of walled towns, <br> Ulysses, slew Eurydamas; his son <br> Struck down Amphimedon; the swineherd took <br> The life of Polybus; the herdsman smote <br> Ctesippus, driving through his breast the spear, <br> And called to him, and gloried o’er his fall:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“O son of Polytherses, prompt to rail! <br> Beware of uttering, in thy foolish pride, <br> Big words hereafter; leave it to the gods, <br> Mightier are they than we. See, I repay <br> The hospitable gift of a steer’s foot, <br> Which once the great Ulysses from thy hand <br> Received, as he was passing through this hall.”</p>
<p class="verse">Thus spake the keeper of the horned herd. <br> Meantime, Ulysses slew Damastor’s son <br> With his long spear, in combat hand to hand. <br> Telemachus next smote Evenor’s son, <br> Leiocritus. He sent the brazen spear <br> Into his bowels; through his body passed <br> The weapon, and he fell upon his face. <br> His forehead struck the floor. Then Pallas held <br> On high her fatal aegis. From the roof <br> She showed it, and their hearts grew wild with fear. <br> They fled along the hall as flees a herd <br> Of kine, when the swift gadfly suddenly <br> Has come among them, and has scattered them <br> In springtime, when the days are growing long. <br> Meantime, like falcons with curved claws and beaks, <br> That, coming from the mountain summits, pounce <br> Upon the smaller birds, and make them fly <br> Close to the fields among the snares they dread, <br> And seize and slay, nor can the birds resist <br> Or fly, and at the multitude of prey <br> The fowlers’ hearts are glad; so did the four <br> Smite right and left the suitors hurrying through <br> The palace-hall, and fearful moans arose <br> As heads were smitten by the sword, and all <br> The pavement swam with blood. Leiodes then <br> Sprang forward to Ulysses, clasped his knees, <br> And supplicated him with winged words:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“I come, Ulysses, to thy knees. Respect <br> And spare me. Never have I said or done, <br> Among the women of thy household, aught <br> That could be blamed, and I essayed to check <br> The wrongs of other suitors. Little heed <br> They gave my counsels, nor withheld their hands <br> From evil deeds, and therefore have they drawn <br> Upon themselves an evil fate. But I, <br> Who have done nothing⁠—I their soothsayer⁠— <br> Must I too die? Then is there no reward <br> Among the sons of men for worthy deeds.”</p>
<p class="verse">Ulysses, the sagacious, frowned and said: <br> “If then, in truth, thou wert as thou dost boast, <br> A soothsayer among these men, thy prayer <br> Within these palace-walls must oft have been <br> That far from me might be the blessed day <br> Of my return, and that my wife might take <br> With thee her lot, and bring forth sons to thee, <br> And therefore shalt thou not escape from death.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake, and seizing with his powerful hand <br> A falchion lying near, which from the grasp <br> Of Agelaüs fell when he was slain, <br> Just at the middle of the neck he smote <br> Leiodes, while the words were on his lips, <br> And the head fell, and lay amid the dust.</p>
<p class="verse">Phemius, the son of Terpius, skilled in song, <br> Alone escaped the bitter doom of death. <br> He by constraint had sung among the train <br> Of suitors, and was standing now beside <br> The postern door, and held his sweet-toned lyre, <br> And pondered whether he should leave the hall, <br> And sit before the altar of the great <br> Herceian Jove, where, with Laertes, once <br> Ulysses oft had burned the thighs of beeves, <br> Or whether he should fling himself before <br> Ulysses, as a suppliant, at his knees. <br> This to his thought seemed wisest⁠—to approach <br> Laertes’ son, and clasp his knees. He placed <br> His sweet harp on the floor, between the cup <br> And silver-studded seat, and went and clasped <br> The hero’s knees, and said in winged words:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“I come, Ulysses, to thy knees. Respect <br> And spare me. It will be a grief to thee, <br> Hereafter, shouldst thou slay a bard, who sings <br> For gods and men alike. I taught myself <br> This art; some god has breathed into my mind <br> Songs of all kinds, and I could sing to thee <br> As to a god. O, seek not then to take <br> My life! Thy own dear son Telemachus <br> Will bear me witness that not willingly <br> Nor for the sake of lucre did I come <br> To sing before the suitors at their feasts <br> And in thy palace, but was forced to come <br> By numbers and by mightier men than I.”</p>
<p class="verse">He ceased; Telemachus, the mighty, heard <br> And thus bespake his father at his side:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Refrain; smite not the guiltless with the sword; <br> And be the herald, Medon, also spared, <br> Who in our palace had the care of me <br> Through all my childhood; if he be not slain <br> Already by Philoetius, or by him <br> Who tends the swine, or if he have not met <br> Thyself, when thou wert ranging through the hail.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake, and the sagacious Medon heard, <br> As crouching underneath a throne he lay, <br> Wrapped in the skin just taken from a steer, <br> To hide from the black doom of death. He came <br> From where he lay, and quickly flung aside <br> The skin, and, springing forward, clasped the knees <br> Of the young prince, and said in winged words:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Dear youth, behold me here; be merciful; <br> Speak to thy father, that he put not forth <br> His sword to slay me, eager as he is <br> For vengeance, and incensed against the men <br> Who haunt these halls to make his wealth a spoil, <br> And in their folly hold thyself in scorn.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake; the sage Ulysses smiled and said: <br> “Be of good cheer, since this my son protects <br> And rescues thee. Now mayst thou well perceive, <br> And say to other men, how much more safe <br> Is doing good than evil. Go thou forth <br> Out of this slaughter to the open court, <br> Thou and the illustrious bard, and sit ye there, <br> While here within I do what yet I must.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake; they moved away and left the hall, <br> And by the altar of almighty Jove <br> Sat looking round them, still in fear of death.</p>
<p class="verse">Meantime, Ulysses passed with searching look <br> O’er all the place, to find if yet remained <br> A single one of all the suitor-crew <br> Alive, and skulking from his bitter doom. <br> He saw that all had fallen in blood and dust, <br> Many as fishes on the shelving beach <br> Drawn from the hoary deep by those who tend <br> The nets with myriad meshes. Poured abroad <br> Upon the sand, while panting to return <br> To the salt sea they lie, till the hot sun <br> Takes their life from them; so the suitors lay <br> Heaped on each other. Then Ulysses took <br> The word, and thus bespake Telemachus:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Go now, Telemachus, and hither call <br> The nurse, Dame Eurycleia. I would say <br> Somewhat to her that comes into my thought.”</p>
<p class="verse">So spake the chief. Telemachus obeyed <br> The word, and smote the door, and called the nurse:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Come hither, ancient dame, who hast in charge <br> To oversee the women in their tasks; <br> My father calls thee, and would speak with thee.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake; nor flew the word in vain; she flung <br> Apart the portals of those stately rooms, <br> And came in haste. Before her went the prince. <br> Among the corpses of the slain they found <br> Ulysses, stained with blood, and grimed with dust. <br> As when a lion, who has just devoured <br> A bullock of the pasture, moves away, <br> A terror to the sight, with breast and cheeks <br> All bathed in blood; so did Ulysses seem, <br> His feet and hands steeped in the blood of men. <br> She, when she saw the corpses and the pools <br> Of blood, and knew the mighty task complete, <br> Was moved to shout for joy. Ulysses checked <br> Her eager zeal, and said in winged words:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Rejoice in spirit, dame, but calm thyself, <br> And shout not. To exult aloud o’er those <br> Who lie in death is an unholy thing. <br> The pleasure of the gods, and their own guilt, <br> Brought death on these; for no respect had they <br> To any of their fellow-men⁠—the good <br> Or evil⁠—whosoever he might be <br> That came to them, and thus on their own heads <br> They drew this fearful fate. Now name to me <br> The women of the palace; let me know <br> Who is disloyal, and who innocent.”</p>
<p class="verse">Then thus the well-beloved nurse replied: <br> “My son, I will declare the truth. There dwell <br> Here in thy palace fifty serving-maids, <br> Whom we have taught to work, to comb the fleece <br> And serve the household. Twelve of these have walked <br> The way of shame. To me they give no heed, <br> Nor to Penelope herself. Thy son <br> Has just now grown to manhood, and the queen <br> Has never suffered him to rule the maids; <br> But let me now, ascending to her room⁠— <br> The royal bower⁠—apprise thy wife, to whom <br> Some deity has sent the gift of sleep.”</p>
<p class="verse">Ulysses, the sagacious, answered thus: <br> “Wake her not yet, but go and summon all <br> The women who have wrought these shameful deeds.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake; the matron through the palace went <br> To seek the women, and to bid them come. <br> Meanwhile, Ulysses called Telemachus, <br> The herdsman and the swineherd to his side, <br> And thus commanded them with winged words:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Begin to carry forth the dead, and call <br> The women to your aid; and next make clean, <br> With water and with thirsty sponges, all <br> The sumptuous thrones and tables. When ye thus <br> Have put the hall in order, lead away <br> The serving-maids, and in the space between <br> The kitchen vault and solid outer wall <br> Smite them with your long swords till they give up <br> The ghost, and lose the memory evermore <br> Of secret meetings with the suitor-train.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake; the women came, lamenting loud <br> With many tears, and carried forth the dead, <br> Leaning upon each other as they went, <br> And placed them underneath the portico <br> Of the walled court. Ulysses gave command, <br> Hastening their task, as all unwillingly <br> They bore the corpses forth. With water next, <br> And thirsty sponges in their hands, they cleansed <br> The sumptuous thrones and tables. Then the prince, <br> Telemachus, with shovels cleared the floor, <br> The herdsman and the swineherd aiding him, <br> And made the women bear the rubbish forth. <br> And now when all within was once again <br> In seemly order, they led forth the maids <br> From that fair pile into the space between <br> The kitchen vault and solid outer wall, <br> A narrow space from which was no escape, <br> And thus discreet Telemachus began:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“I will not take away these creatures’ lives <br> By a pure death⁠—these who so long have heaped <br> Reproaches on my mother’s head and mine, <br> And played the wanton with the suitor-crew.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake, and made the hawser of a ship <br> Fast to a lofty shaft; the other end <br> He wound about the kitchen vault. So high <br> He stretched it that the feet of none who hung <br> On it might touch the ground. As when a flock <br> Of broad-winged thrushes or wild pigeons strike <br> A net within a thicket, as they seek <br> Their perch, and find unwelcome durance there, <br> So hung the women, with their heads a-row, <br> And cords about their necks, that they might die <br> A miserable death. A little while, <br> And but a little, quivered their loose feet <br> In air. They led Melanthius from the hall <br> And through the porch, cut off his nose and ears, <br> Wrenched out the parts of shame, a bloody meal <br> For dogs, and in their anger from the trunk <br> Lopped hands and feet. Then having duly washed <br> Their feet and hands, they came into the hall, <br> And to Ulysses; they had done their work. <br> And then to the dear nurse Ulysses said:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Bring sulphur, dame, the cure of noxious air, <br> And fire, that I may purge the hall with smoke; <br> And go, and bid Penelope come down, <br> With her attendant women, and command <br> That all the handmaids of the household come.”</p>
<p class="verse">And thus in turn Dame Eurycleia spake: <br> “Well hast thou said, my son, but suffer me <br> To bring thee clothes, a tunic and a cloak, <br> Nor with those rags on thy broad shoulders stand <br> In thine own palace; it becomes thee not.”</p>
<p class="verse">Ulysses, the sagacious, answered thus: <br> “First let a fire be kindled in this hall.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake, and Eurycleia, the dear nurse, <br> Obeyed, and brought the sulphur and the fire. <br> Ulysses steeped in smoke the royal pile. <br> Both hall and court. The matron, passing through <br> The stately palace of Ulysses, climbed <br> The stair to find and summon all the maids. <br> And forth they issued, bearing in their hands <br> Torches, and, crowding round Ulysses, gave <br> Glad greeting, seized his hands, embraced him, kissed <br> His hands and brow and shoulders. The desire <br> To weep for joy o’ercame the chief; his eyes <br> O’erflowed with tears; he sobbed; he knew them all.</p>
