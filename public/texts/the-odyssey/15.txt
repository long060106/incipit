<p class="lede">Appearance of Pallas in a dream to Telemachus, warning him to return to Ithaca⁠—His departure from Lacedaemon, and arrival at Pylos⁠—Theoclymenus, an augur, taken on board by him at Pylos⁠—The story of Eumaeus related by him to Ulysses⁠—Arrival of Telemachus at the coast of Ithaca⁠—The ship sent forward to the city, while Telemachus, having committed Theoclymenus to the care of his friend Piraeus, goes to the lodge of Eumaeus.</p>
<p class="verse">Then Pallas, hastening to the mighty realm <br> Of Lacedaemon, sought the illustrious son <br> Of great Ulysses, to remind the youth <br> Of home, and bid him think of his return. <br> She found Telemachus and Nestor’s son <br> Upon their couches in the portico <br> Of Menelaus, the renowned. Deep sleep <br> Held Nestor’s son; but to Telemachus <br> The welcome slumber came not, for his thoughts <br> Uneasily through all the quiet night <br> Dwelt on his father. Now beside his bed <br> The blue-eyed Pallas took her stand and spake:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Telemachus, it is no longer well <br> That thou shouldst wander from thy home, and leave <br> All thy possessions, and those arrogant men <br> That crowd thy halls. Beware, lest they devour <br> Thy substance utterly, dividing all <br> Among them, and this journey be for naught. <br> Make suit to Menelaus, great in war, <br> Quickly to send thee home, that thou mayst join <br> Thy blameless mother in thy halls; for now <br> Her father and her brothers counsel her <br> To wed Eurymachus, whose gifts exceed <br> Those of the other suitors, and besides <br> He offers a yet richer bridal dower. <br> It were not hard without thy leave to take <br> Wealth from a palace. What a wife will do <br> Thou knowest. ’Tis her pleasure to increase <br> The riches of the man whom she has wed. <br> Care of her former children has she none, <br> Nor memory of the husband whom she took <br> While yet a maid, and who is in his grave; <br> Of these she never speaks. Return thou, then, <br> And give thy goods in charge to one among <br> The handmaids of thy household who shall seem <br> The fittest for the trust, until the gods <br> Bring thee a noble wife. Another word <br> Have I for thee, and bear thou it in mind: <br> The chief among the suitors in the strait <br> Between the rugged Samos and the isle <br> Of Ithaca are lurking, in the hope <br> To slay thee on thy voyage home; but this <br> I think they cannot do before the earth <br> Hold many of the suitor-crew who make <br> Thy wealth a spoil. Steer thou thy gallant barque <br> Far from the isles; sail only in the night. <br> Some god, whoever it may be that keeps <br> Watch over thee, will send a prosperous gale. <br> When to the nearest shore of Ithaca <br> Thou comest in thy ship, let it go on, <br> With all thy comrades, to the town, while thou <br> Repairest to the keeper of thy swine, <br> Whose heart is faithful to thee. There remain <br> With him that night, and send him to the town <br> With tidings to the sage Penelope <br> That thou art come from Pylos and art safe.”</p>
<p class="verse">So having said, the goddess took her way <br> Up to the Olympian height. Telemachus <br> Touched with his heel and wakened Nestor’s son <br> From a soft slumber and bespake him thus:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Rise, Nestor’s son, Peisistratus, and bring <br> The firm-paced steeds and yoke them to the car, <br> And we will now set forth upon our way.”</p>
<p class="verse">And Nestor’s son, Peisistratus, replied: <br> “Telemachus, whatever be our haste, <br> It were not well in darkness to begin <br> Our journey, and the morn will soon be here. <br> Remain till Menelaus, Atreus’ son, <br> The hero mighty with the spear, shall come, <br> And bring his gifts, and place them in our car, <br> And send us on our way with kindly words. <br> Well does a guest remember all his days <br> The generous host who shows himself his friend.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake, and quickly on her car of gold <br> Appeared the Morn. Then Menelaus came, <br> The great in battle, from his couch beside <br> The fair-haired Helen. When Telemachus <br> Knew of the king’s approach, the hero threw <br> In haste his tunic o’er his noble form, <br> And over his broad shoulders flung a cloak <br> Of ample folds. Then, going forth, the son <br> Of great Ulysses met the king and said:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Atrides Menelaus, loved of Jove <br> And sovereign of the people, send me hence, <br> I pray, to the dear country of my birth, <br> For earnestly I long to be at home.”</p>
<p class="verse">And Menelaus, great in war, replied: <br> “Telemachus, I will not keep thee long, <br> Since thou so much desirest to return. <br> I am displeased with him who as a host <br> Is lavish of his love, for he will hate <br> Beyond due measure; best it is to take <br> The middle way. It is alike a wrong <br> To thrust the unwilling stranger out of doors, <br> And to detain him when he longs to go. <br> While he is with us we should cherish him, <br> And, when he wishes, help him to depart. <br> Remain until I bring thee worthy gifts <br> And place them in thy chariot, that thine eyes <br> May look on them; and I will give command <br> That in the palace here the women spread <br> A liberal feast from stores that lie within. <br> But if, in turning from thy course, thou choose <br> To pass through Hellas and the midland tract <br> Of Argos, I will yoke my steeds and go <br> With thee, and show the cities thronged with men; <br> Nor will they send us empty-handed thence, <br> But bring us gifts which we may bear away⁠— <br> Tripod, perchance, or cauldron wrought of brass, <br> Perchance a pair of mules or golden cup.”</p>
<p class="verse">Then spake discreet Telemachus in turn: <br> “Atrides Menelaus, loved of Jove <br> And sovereign of the people, rather far <br> Would I return to my own home; for there <br> Is no man left in charge of what is mine, <br> And I must go, lest, while I vainly seek <br> My father, I may perish, or may lose <br> Some valued treasure from my palace rooms.”</p>
<p class="verse">The valiant Menelaus heard, and bade <br> His wife and maidens spread without delay <br> A ready banquet from the stores within. <br> Then Eteöneus from his morning sleep, <br> Son of Boëtheus, came, for very near <br> His dwelling was. The sovereign bade him light <br> A fire and roast the flesh, and he obeyed. <br> And then into the fragrant treasure-room <br> Descended Menelaus, not alone; <br> Helen and Megapenthes went with him. <br> And when they came to where the treasures lay, <br> Atrides took a double goblet up, <br> And bade his son, young Megapenthes, bear <br> A silver beaker thence, while Helen stood <br> Beside the coffers where the embroidered robes <br> Wrought by her hands were laid. The glorious dame <br> Took one and brought it forth, most beautiful <br> In needlework, and amplest of them all. <br> The garment glittered like a star, and lay <br> Below the other robes. Then, passing through <br> The palace halls, they found Telemachus, <br> And thus the fair-haired Menelaus spake:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Telemachus, may Jove the Thunderer, <br> Husband of Juno, grant thee to return <br> According to thy wish! I give thee here <br> Of all the treasures which my house contains <br> The fairest and most precious. I present <br> A goblet all of silver, save the lips, <br> And they are bound with gold; it is the work <br> Of Vulcan. Phaedimus the hero, king <br> Of the Sidonians, gave it me when once <br> His palace sheltered me. He gave it me <br> At parting, and I now would have it thine.”</p>
<p class="verse">Atrides spake, and gave into his hands <br> The double goblet. Megapenthes next <br> Before him set the shining beaker wrought <br> Of silver. Rosy Helen, holding up <br> The robe, drew near, and spake to him and said:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“I also bring to thee, dear son, a gift, <br> The work of Helen’s hands, which thou shalt keep, <br> In memory of her, until the day <br> Of thy desired espousals, when thy bride <br> Shall wear it. Let it in the meantime lie <br> Within thy halls, in thy dear mother’s care; <br> And mayst thou soon and happily arrive <br> At thy fair palace and thy native coast.”</p>
<p class="verse">So spake she, placing in his hands the robe. <br> He took it, and was glad. Peisistratus <br> Was moved with wonder as he saw, and laid <br> The presents in the car. The fair-haired king <br> Then led them to the hall, and seated them <br> On thrones and couches, where a maiden brought <br> Water in a fair golden ewer, and o’er <br> A silver basin poured it for their hands, <br> And near them set a table smoothly wrought. <br> The matron of the palace brought them bread <br> And many a delicate dish to please the taste <br> From stores within the house. Then to the board <br> Boëtheus’ son drew near and carved the meats, <br> And gave to each a portion, while the son <br> Of glorious Menelaus poured the wine. <br> The guests put forth their hands and shared the food <br> That lay prepared before them. When the calls <br> Of thirst and hunger ceased, Telemachus <br> And Nestor’s famous son brought forth and yoked <br> The steeds, and climbed into the sumptuous car, <br> And drove from out the echoing portico. <br> Atrides Menelaus, amber-haired, <br> Went forth with them, and, holding in his hand <br> A golden cup of generous wine, poured out <br> An offering for their voyage to the gods. <br> Before the steeds he took his stand, and first <br> Drank from the cup, and then bespake the guests:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Now fare ye well, young men, and when ye come <br> To Nestor, shepherd of the people, give <br> Greetings from me; for he was kind to me <br> As if he were a father, when the sons <br> Of Greece were warring in the realm of Troy.”</p>
<p class="verse">Then spake in turn discreet Telemachus: <br> “Assuredly I shall relate to him, <br> As soon as I am with him, all that thou, <br> foster-child of Jove, hast bid me say; <br> And would to heaven I might as surely tell <br> Ulysses in his palace, when again <br> I come to Ithaca, how welcome thou <br> Hast made me here, and how I came away <br> With treasures rich and many from thy court.”</p>
<p class="verse">As thus he spake, an eagle to the right <br> Appeared, that, flying, bore a large white goose, <br> Clutched from the tame flock in the palace court; <br> And men and women ran the way he flew, <br> And shouted after him. Before the steeds <br> Of the young men, and still on the right hand, <br> The bird went sweeping on. They saw well pleased, <br> And every heart was gladdened. To the rest <br> Peisistratus, the son of Nestor, said:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Now tell me, Menelaus, loved of Jove, <br> Prince of the people! does the god who sends <br> This portent mean the sign for us or thee?”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake; and Menelaus, dear to Mars, <br> Paused, thinking how to answer him aright, <br> When thus the long-robed Helen interposed:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Listen to me, and I will prophesy <br> As the gods prompt me, and as I believe <br> The event will be. Just as this eagle came <br> From the wild hills, his birthplace and his haunt, <br> And seized and bore away the waterfowl <br> Reared near our halls, so will Ulysses come, <br> After much hardship and long wanderings, <br> To his own home, to be avenged: perchance <br> Already is at home, and meditates <br> An evil end to all the suitor crew.”</p>
<p class="verse">Then spake discreet Telemachus in turn: <br> “May Juno’s husband, Jove the Thunderer, <br> So order the event, and I will there <br> Make vows to thee as to a deity.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake, and touched the coursers with the lash; <br> And through the city rapidly they went <br> And toward the plain, and all day long they shook <br> The yoke upon their necks. The sun went down: <br> The roads all lay in darkness as they came <br> To Pherae, and the house of Diodes, <br> Whose father was Orsilochus, and he <br> The offspring of Alpheius. There that night <br> They slept; their host was liberal of his cheer. <br> But when appeared the daughter of the Dawn, <br> The rosy-fingered Morn, they yoked the steeds <br> And climbed the sumptuous car, and drove afield <br> From underneath the echoing portico. <br> The son of Nestor plied the lash; the steeds <br> Flew not unwillingly, and quickly reached <br> The lofty citadel of Pylos. There <br> Telemachus bespake his comrade thus:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Wilt thou consent to do what I shall ask, <br> O son of Nestor? ’Tis our boast that we <br> Are friends because our fathers were; besides, <br> We are of equal age, and journeying thus <br> Has made our friendship firmer. Take me not, <br> O foster-child of Jove, beyond the spot <br> Where lies my galley, lest against my will <br> The aged Nestor should detain me here <br> Through kindness, when I needs must hasten home.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake, and then the son of Nestor mused <br> How what his friend desired might best be done. <br> And this seemed wisest after careful thought: <br> He turned the chariot to the ship and shore, <br> And taking out the garments and the gold⁠— <br> Beautiful gifts which Menelaus gave⁠— <br> He put them in the galley’s stern, and thus <br> Bespake Telemachus with winged words:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Embark in haste, and summon all thy crew <br> On board before I reach my home and tell <br> The aged king. I know how vehement <br> His temper is; he will not let thee go, <br> But hastening hither to enforce thy stay, <br> At Pylos, will not, I am sure, go back <br> Without thee; his displeasure will be great.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake, and toward the Pylian city turned <br> His steeds with flowing manes, and quickly reached <br> His home. Meantime Telemachus held forth <br> To his companions, thus exhorting them:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“My friends, make ready all things in our ship <br> And mount the deck, for we must now set sail.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake, they hearkened and obeyed, and leaped <br> On board and manned the benches. While he thus <br> Was hastening his departure, offering prayer <br> And pouring wine to Pallas at the stern, <br> A stranger came, a seer, a fugitive <br> From Argos, where his hand had slain a man. <br> Melampus was his ancestor, who dwelt <br> Some time in Pylos, mother of fair flocks⁠— <br> Rich, and inhabiting a sumptuous house <br> Among the Pylians. Afterward he joined <br> Another people, fleeing from his home <br> And from the mighty Neleus, haughtiest <br> Of living men, who, seizing his large wealth, <br> Held it a year by force. Melampus lay <br> Meantime within the house of Phylacus <br> Fast bound, and suffering greatly, both because <br> Of Neleus’ daughter, and of his own mind <br> Distempered by the unapproachable <br> Erinnys. Yet did he escape from death, <br> And drove the lowing herds to Phylace <br> And Pylos, and avenged his cruel wrong <br> On Neleus, carrying off his child to be <br> A consort for his brother. Then he came <br> Into the realm of Argos, famed for steeds; <br> For there it was decreed that he should dwell, <br> And rule o’er many of the Argive race. <br> And there he took a wife and built a house⁠— <br> A lofty pile; and there to him were born Antiphates and Mantius, valiant men. <br> Antiphates was father of a son, <br> The brave Oïcleus, and to him was born <br> Amphioraüs, one of those whose voice <br> Rouses the nations. Aegis-bearing Jove <br> And Phoebus loved him with exceeding love; <br> Yet reached he not the threshold of old age, <br> But, through the treachery of his bribed wife, <br> Perished too soon at Thebes. To him were born <br> Two sons, Alcmaeon and Amphilochus. <br> Clytus and Polyphides were the sons <br> Of Mantius; but Aurora, she who fills <br> A golden chariot, bore away to heaven <br> Clytus for his great beauty, there to dwell <br> Among the immortals, while Apollo gave <br> To Polyphides of the noble mind <br> To be a prophet, first of living men, <br> Since now Amphiaraüs was no more. <br> His father had displeased him, and he went <br> To Hyperesia, where he dwelt, and there <br> Revealed to all what yet should come to pass.</p>
<p class="verse">It was his son who now approached; his name <br> Was Theoclymenus; he saw the prince <br> Telemachus, who stood beside the swift <br> Black ship, and, pouring a libation, prayed; <br> And thus he said to him in winged words:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“My friend, whom here beside this barque I find <br> Making a pious offering, I entreat <br> Both by that offering and the deity, <br> And by thy life, and by the lives of these <br> Who follow thee, declare to me the truth, <br> And keep back naught of all that I inquire⁠— <br> Who art thou, from what race of men, and where <br> Thy city lies, and who thy parents are.”</p>
<p class="verse">Then spake in turn discreet Telemachus: <br> “Stranger, to every point I answer thee. <br> I am by race a son of Ithaca, <br> My father was Ulysses when alive, <br> But he has died a miserable death; <br> Long years has he been absent, and I came <br> With my companions here, and this black ship, <br> To gather tidings of my father’s fate.”</p>
<p class="verse">Then said the godlike Theoclymenus: <br> “I too, like thee, am far away from home; <br> For I have slain a man of my own tribe, <br> And he had many brothers, many friends, <br> In Argos famed for steeds. Great is the power <br> Of those Achaians, and I flee from them <br> And the black doom of death, to be henceforth <br> A wanderer among men. O, shelter me <br> On board thy galley! I, a fugitive, <br> Implore thy mercy, lest they overtake <br> And slay me; they are surely on my track.”</p>
<p class="verse">And thus discreet Telemachus replied: <br> “If thou desire to come on board my ship, <br> I shall not hinder thee. Come with us then, <br> And take a friendly share in what we have.”</p>
<p class="verse">So saying he received his brazen spear, <br> And laid it on the good ship’s deck, and went <br> Himself on board, and, taking at the stern <br> His place, he seated Theoclymenus <br> Beside him. Then the mariners cast loose <br> The hawsers, and Telemachus gave forth <br> The order to prepare for sea. They heard <br> And eagerly obeyed; they raised the mast, <br> A pine-tree stem⁠—and, bringing it to stand <br> In its deep socket, bound it there with cords, <br> And hoisted by their strongly twisted thongs <br> The ship’s white sails. The blue-eyed Pallas sent <br> A favorable and fresh-blowing wind, <br> That swept the sky to drive more speedily <br> The galley through the salt-sea waves. They came <br> To Cruni, and to Chalcis pleasantly <br> Watered by rivers. Now the sun went down; <br> Night closed around their way, but onward still <br> A favorable wind from Jupiter <br> Toward Pherae bore them, and the hallowed coast <br> Of Elis, where the Epeian race bear sway, <br> And then among the isles whose rocky peaks <br> Rise from the waters. Here Telemachus <br> Mused thoughtfully on what his fate might be⁠— <br> To perish by the ambush or escape.</p>
<p class="verse">Meantime Ulysses and the swineherd sat <br> At meat within the lodge; the other men <br> Were at the board, and when the calls of thirst <br> And hunger ceased, Ulysses spake to try <br> The swineherd, whether he were bent to show <br> Yet further kindness, and entreat his stay, <br> Or whether he would send him to the town.</p>
<p class="verse">“Eumaeus, hearken thou, and all the rest. <br> Tomorrow ’tis my wish to go to town, <br> That I may beg, and be no charge to thee <br> And thy companions. Give me thy advice, <br> And send a trusty guide to show the way. <br> There will I roam the streets, for so I must, <br> And haply someone there will give a cup <br> Of wine and cake of meal. And when I find <br> The house of great Ulysses, I will tell <br> The sage Penelope the news I bring. <br> Nay, I would even go among the crew <br> Of arrogant suitors, who perhaps might give <br> A meal, for there is plenty at their feasts, <br> And I would do whatever they require. <br> For let me tell thee, and do thou give heed, <br> There lives no man who can contend with me <br> In menial tasks⁠—to keep alive a fire <br> With fuel, cleave dry wood, and carve and roast <br> The meat and pour the wine⁠—whate’er is done <br> By poor men waiting on the better sort.”</p>
<p class="verse">And thou, Eumasus, keeper of the swine, <br> Didst answer in displeasure: “Woe is me! <br> How could thy bosom harbor such a thought? <br> O stranger! thou must surely be resolved <br> To perish if thy purpose be to go <br> Among the suitor crew, whose insolence <br> And riot reach the iron vault of heaven. <br> Not such attendants minister to them <br> As thou art, but fair youths arrayed in cloaks <br> And tunics, with sleek heads and smooth of face. <br> These wait at polished tables heavily <br> Loaded with bread and flesh and wine. Stay thou <br> Content among us, sure that no one here <br> Is wearied by thy presence, neither I <br> Nor any of my fellows. When he comes, <br> The dear son of Ulysses will provide <br> For thee the garments thou dost need⁠—a cloak <br> And tunic⁠—and will send thee where thou wilt.”</p>
<p class="verse">Ulysses, the great sufferer, answered thus: <br> “I pray that thou mayst be as dear to Jove, <br> The great All-Father, as thou art to me, <br> Since through thy kindness I enjoy a pause <br> Amid my weary wanderings. There is naught <br> Worse than a wandering life. Unseemly cares <br> A hungry stomach brings to homeless men; <br> Hardship and grief are theirs. But since thou wilt <br> That I remain and wait for thy young lord, <br> Speak to me of the mother of thy chief <br> Ulysses, and his father, whom he left <br> Just on the threshold of old age, if yet <br> They live, and still may look upon the sun; <br> Or have they died, and passed to Pluto’s realm?”</p>
<p class="verse">And then in turn the master swineherd spake: <br> “Rightly and truly will I answer thee, <br> stranger! still Laertes lives, but prays <br> Continually to Jove that he may die <br> In his own house; for sorely he laments <br> His son long absent, and his excellent wife, <br> Bride of his youth, whose death has brought on him <br> Sharp sorrow, and old age before its time. <br> By a sad death she died⁠—through wasting grief <br> For her lost, glorious son. May no one here, <br> No friend of mine, nor one who has bestowed <br> A kindness on me, die by such a death! <br> While yet she lived, great as her sorrow was, <br> I loved to speak with her and hear her words; <br> For she had reared me with her youngest-born⁠— <br> Her daughter, long-robed Ctimena. With her <br> Was I brought up, and scarcely less than her <br> Was held in honor. When at length we came <br> Into the pleasant years of youth, they sent <br> The princess hence to Samos, and received at <br> Large presents; but to me her mother gave <br> Garments of price, a tunic and a cloak, <br> And sandals for my feet, and sent me forth <br> Into the fields, and loved me more and more. <br> All this is over now, yet must I say <br> My calling has been prospered by the gods. <br> From this I have the means to eat and drink, <br> And wherewithal to feast a worthy guest; <br> But from the queen I never have a word <br> Or deed of kindness, since that evil came <br> Upon her house⁠—that crew of lawless men. <br> Greatly the servants would rejoice to speak <br> Before their mistress, and inquire her will, <br> And eat and drink, and carry to their homes <br> Some gift, for gifts delight a servant’s heart.”</p>
<p class="verse">Again Ulysses, the sagacious, spake: <br> “Swineherd Eumaeus, thou, while yet a child, <br> Wert doubtless strangely tossed about the world, <br> Far from thy kindred and thy native land. <br> Now tell me, was the spacious town wherein <br> Thy father and thy mother dwelt laid waste? <br> Or wert thou left among the flocks and herds <br> Untended, and borne off by hostile men, <br> Who came in ships and sold thee to the lord <br> Of these possessions for a worthy price?”</p>
<p class="verse">And then the master swineherd spake again: <br> “Since thou dost ask me, stranger, hear my words <br> In silence; sit at ease and drink thy wine. <br> These nights are very long; there’s time enough <br> For sleep, and time to entertain ourselves <br> With talk. It is not fitting to lie down <br> Ere the due hour arrive, and too much sleep <br> Is hurtful. Whosoever here shall feel <br> The strong desire, let him withdraw and sleep, <br> And rise with early morn and break his fast, <br> And tend my master’s swine. Let us remain <br> Within, and drink and feast, and pass the time <br> Gayly, relating what we have endured, <br> Each one of us; for in the after time <br> One who has suffered much and wandered far <br> May take a pleasure even in his griefs.</p>
<p class="verse">“But let me tell what thou hast asked of me: <br> Beyond Ortygia lies an island named <br> Syria; thou must have heard of it. The sun <br> Above it turns his course. It is not large, <br> But fruitful, fit for pasturage, and rich <br> In flocks, abounding both in wine and wheat. <br> There never famine comes, nor foul disease <br> Fastens on wretched mortals; but when men <br> Grow old, Apollo of the silver bow <br> Comes with Diana, aims his silent shafts, <br> And slays them. There two cities stand, and share <br> The isle between them. There my father reigned, <br> The godlike Ctesias, son of Ormenus, <br> And both the cities owned him as their king.</p>
<p class="verse">“There came a crew of that seafaring race, <br> The people of Phoenicia, to our isle. <br> Shrewd fellows they, and brought in their black ship <br> Large store of trinkets. In my father’s house <br> Was a Phoenician woman, large and fair, <br> And skilful in embroidery. As she came <br> A laundress to their ship, those cunning men <br> Seduced her. One of them obtained her love⁠— <br> For oft doth love mislead weak womankind, <br> Even of the more discreet. Her paramour <br> Asked who she was, and whence. She pointed out <br> The lofty pile in which my father dwelt.</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘At Sidon, rich in brass, I had my birth⁠— <br> A daughter of the opulent Arybas; <br> And once, as I was coming from the fields, <br> The Taphian pirates seized and bore me off, <br> And brought me to this isle and sold me here, <br> At that man’s house; much gold he paid for me.’</p>
<p class="verse">“Then said her paramour: ‘Wilt thou not then <br> Return with us, that thou mayst see again <br> Father and mother, and their fair abode? <br> For yet they live, and rumor says are rich.’</p>
<p class="verse">“To this the woman answered: ‘I consent <br> If first ye take an oath⁠—ye mariners⁠— <br> And pledge your faith to bear me safely home.’</p>
<p class="verse">“She spake, and they complied, and when the oath <br> Was duly taken, thus the woman said:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘Now hold your peace; let none of all the crew <br> Speak to me more, in meeting on the road <br> Or at the fountain, lest someone should tell <br> The old man at the house, and he suspect <br> Some fraud and bind me fast, and plot your death. <br> Lock up your words within your breast; make haste <br> To buy supplies, and when the ship is full <br> Of all things needful, let a messenger <br> Come to me at the palace with all speed; <br> And I will bring with me whatever gold <br> My hands may find, and something else to pay <br> My passage. I am nurse to the young heir <br> Of the good man who dwells in yonder halls⁠— <br> A shrewd boy for his years, who oft goes out <br> With me⁠—and I will lead him to the ship, <br> And he will bring, in any foreign land <br> To which ye carry him, a liberal price.’</p>
<p class="verse">“The woman spake, and to our fair abode <br> Departed. The Phoenician crew remained <br> Until the twelvemonth’s end, and filled their ship <br> With many things, and, when its roomy hull <br> Was fully laden, sent a messenger <br> To tell the woman. He, a cunning man, <br> Came to my father’s house, and brought with him <br> A golden necklace set with amber beads. <br> The palace maidens and the gracious queen, <br> My mother, took it in their hands, and gazed <br> Upon it, and debated of its price. <br> Meantime the bearer gave the sign, and soon <br> Departed to the ship. The woman took <br> My hand and led me forth. Within the hall <br> She found upon the tables ready placed <br> The goblets for my father’s guests, his peers; <br> But they were absent, and in council yet <br> Amid a great assembly. She concealed <br> Three goblets in her bosom, and bore off <br> The theft. I followed thoughtlessly. The sun <br> Went down, and darkness brooded o’er the ways. <br> Briskly we walked, and reached the famous port <br> And the fast-sailing ship. They took us both <br> On board, and sailed. Along its ocean path <br> The vessel ran, and Jupiter bestowed <br> A favorable wind. Six days we sailed, <br> Both night and day; but when Saturnian Jove <br> Brought the seventh day, Diana, archer-queen, <br> Struck down the woman, and with sudden noise <br> Headlong she plunged into the hold, as dives <br> A seagull. But the seamen cast her forth <br> To fishes and to sea-calves. I was left <br> Alone and sorrowful. The winds and waves <br> Carried our galley on to Ithaca; <br> And there Laertes purchased me, and thus <br> I first beheld the land in which I dwell.”</p>
<p class="verse">And then again the great Ulysses spake: <br> “Eumaeus, the sad story of thy wrongs <br> And sufferings moves me deeply; yet hath Jove <br> Among thy evil fortunes given this good, <br> That, after all thy sufferings, thou art lodged <br> With a good master, who abundantly <br> Provides thee meat and drink; thou leadest here <br> A pleasant life, while I am come to thee <br> From wandering long and over many lands.”</p>
<p class="verse">So talked they with each other. No long time <br> They passed in sleep, for soon the Morning came, <br> Throned on her car of gold. Beside the shore <br> The comrades of Telemachus cast loose <br> The sails, took down the mast, and with their oars <br> Brought in the vessel to its place. They threw <br> The anchors out and bound the hawsers fast, <br> And went upon the sea-beach, where they dressed <br> Their morning meal, and mingled purple wine. <br> Then, when the calls of thirst and hunger ceased, <br> Discreet Telemachus bespake the crew:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Take the black ship to town. I visit first <br> The fields, and see my herdsmen, and at eve <br> Will come to town. Tomorrow I will give <br> The parting feast, rich meats and generous wine.”</p>
<p class="verse">Then said the godlike Theoclymenus: <br> “Whither, my son, am I to go? What house <br> Of all the chiefs of rugged Ithaca <br> Shall I seek shelter in? with thee, perhaps, <br> In thine own palace where thy mother dwells.”</p>
<p class="verse">And thus discreet Telemachus replied: <br> “I would have asked thee at another time <br> To make our house thy home, for there would be <br> No lack of kindly welcome. ’Twere not well <br> To ask thee now, for I shall not be there, <br> Nor will my mother see thee⁠—since not oft <br> Doth she appear before the suitor-train, <br> But in an upper room, apart from them, <br> Weaves at her loom a web. Another man <br> I name, Eurymachus, the illustrious son <br> Of the sage Polybus, to be thy host. <br> The noblest of the suitors he, and seeks <br> Most earnestly to wed the queen, and take <br> The rank Ulysses held. Olympian Jove, <br> Who dwells in ether, knows the fatal day <br> That may o’ertake the suitors ere she wed.”</p>
<p class="verse">As thus he spake, a falcon on the right <br> Flew by, Apollo’s messenger. A dove <br> Was in his talons, which he tore, and poured <br> The feathers down between Telemachus <br> And where the galley lay. When this was seen <br> By Theoclymenus, he called the youth <br> Apart, alone, and took his hand and said:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“The bird that passed us, O Telemachus, <br> Upon the right, flew not without a god <br> To guide him. When I saw it, well I knew <br> The omen. Not in Ithaca exists <br> A house of a more kingly destiny <br> Than thine, and ever will its power prevail.”</p>
<p class="verse">And thus discreet Telemachus replied: <br> “O stranger, may thy saying come to pass; <br> Then shalt thou quickly know me for thy friend, <br> And be rewarded with such liberal gifts <br> That all who meet thee shall rejoice with thee.”</p>
<p class="verse">Then turning to Piraeus he bespake <br> That faithful follower thus: “Piraeus, son <br> Of Clytius, thou who ever wert the first <br> To move, at my command, of all the men <br> Who went with me to Pylos, take, I pray, <br> This stranger to thy house, and there provide <br> For him, and honor him until I come.”</p>
<p class="verse">Piraeus, mighty with the spear, replied: <br> “Telemachus, however long thy stay, <br> This man shall be my guest, nor ever lack <br> Beneath my roof for hospitable care.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake, and climbed the deck, and bade his men <br> Enter the ship and cast the fastenings loose. <br> Quickly they came together, went on board <br> And manned the benches, while Telemachus <br> Bound the fair sandals to his feet, and took <br> His massive spear with its sharp blade of brass <br> That lay upon the deck. The men unbound <br> The hawsers, shoved the galley forth, and sailed <br> Townward, as they were bidden by the son <br> Of great Ulysses. Meantime the quick feet <br> Of the young chieftain bore him on until <br> He reached the lodge where his great herds of swine <br> Were fed, and, careful of his master’s wealth, <br> Beside his charge the worthy swineherd slept.</p>
