<p class="lede">Nausicaä, daughter of Alcinoüs, king of the Phaeacians, directed by Pallas to go to the river and wash her marriage robes⁠—Sports of her maidens after the washing is performed⁠—Ulysses awakened by the noise, relieved and clothed by Nausicaä, and bidden to follow her into the city, and there make his suit to the queen, the wife of Alcinoüs.</p>
<p class="verse">Thus overcome with toil and weariness, <br> The noble sufferer Ulysses slept, <br> While Pallas hastened to the realm and town <br> Peopled by the Phaeacians, who of yore <br> Abode in spacious Hypereia, near <br> The insolent race of Cyclops, and endured <br> Wrong from their mightier hands. A godlike chief, <br> Nausithoüs, led them, to a new abode, <br> And planted them in Scheria, far away <br> From plotting neighbors. With a wall he fenced <br> Their city, built them dwellings there, and reared <br> Fanes to the gods, and changed the plain to fields. <br> But he had bowed to death, and had gone down <br> To Hades, and Alcinoüs, whom the gods <br> Endowed with wisdom, governed in his stead. <br> Now to his palace, planning the return <br> Of the magnanimous Ulysses, came <br> The blue-eyed goddess Pallas, entering <br> The gorgeous chamber where a damsel slept⁠— <br> Nausicaä, daughter of the large-souled king <br> Alcinoüs, beautiful in form and face <br> As one of the immortals. Near her lay, <br> And by the portal, one on either side, <br> Fair as the Graces, two attendant maids. <br> The shining doors were shut. But Pallas came <br> As comes a breath of air, and stood beside <br> The damsel’s head and spake. In look she seemed <br> The daughter of the famous mariner <br> Dymas, a maiden whom Nausicaä loved, <br> The playmate of her girlhood. In her shape <br> The blue-eyed goddess stood, and thus she said:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Nausicaä, has thy mother then brought forth <br> A careless housewife? Thy magnificent robes <br> Lie still neglected, though thy marriage day <br> Is near, when thou art to array thyself <br> In seemly garments, and bestow the like <br> On those who lead thee to the bridal rite; <br> For thus the praise of men is won, and thus <br> Thy father and thy gracious mother both <br> Will be rejoiced. Now with the early dawn <br> Let us all hasten to the washing-place. <br> I too would go with thee, and help thee there, <br> That thou mayst sooner end the task, for thou <br> Not long wilt be unwedded. Thou art wooed <br> Already by the noblest of the race <br> Of the Phaeacians, for thy birth, like theirs, <br> Is of the noblest. Make thy suit at morn <br> To thy illustrious father, that he bid <br> His mules and car be harnessed to convey <br> Thy girdles, robes, and mantles marvellous <br> In beauty. That were seemlier than to walk, <br> Since distant from the town the lavers lie.”</p>
<p class="verse">Thus having said, the blue-eyed Pallas went <br> Back to Olympus, where the gods have made, <br> So saith tradition, their eternal seat. <br> The tempest shakes it not, nor is it drenched <br> By showers, and there the snow doth never fall. <br> The calm clear ether is without a cloud; <br> And in the golden light, that lies on all, <br> Days after day the blessed gods rejoice. <br> Thither the blue-eyed goddess, having given <br> Her message to the sleeping maid, withdrew.</p>
<p class="verse">Soon the bright morning came. Nausicaä rose, <br> Clad royally, as marvelling at her dream <br> She hastened through the palace to declare <br> Her purpose to her father and the queen. <br> She found them both within. Her mother sat <br> Beside the hearth with her attendant maids, <br> And turned the distaff loaded with a fleece <br> Dyed in sea-purple. On the threshold stood <br> Her father, going forth to meet the chiefs <br> Of the Phaeacians in a council where <br> Their noblest asked his presence. Then the maid, <br> Approaching her beloved father, spake:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“I pray, dear father, give command to make <br> A chariot ready for me, with high sides <br> And sturdy wheels, to bear to the river-brink, <br> There to be cleansed, the costly robes that now <br> Lie soiled. Thee likewise it doth well beseem <br> At councils to appear in vestments fresh <br> And stainless. Thou hast also in these halls <br> Five sons, two wedded, three in boyhood’s bloom, <br> And ever in the dance they need attire <br> New from the wash. All this must I provide.”</p>
<p class="verse">She ended, for she shrank from saying aught <br> Of her own hopeful marriage. He perceived <br> Her thought and said: “Mules I deny thee not, <br> My daughter, nor aught else. Go then; my grooms <br> Shall make a carriage ready with high sides <br> And sturdy wheels, and a broad rack above.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake, and gave command. The grooms obeyed, <br> And, making ready in the outer court <br> The strong-wheeled chariot, led the harnessed mules <br> Under the yoke and made them fast; and then <br> Appeared the maiden, bringing from her bower <br> The shining garments. In the polished car <br> She piled them, while with many pleasant meats <br> And flavoring morsels for the day’s repast <br> Her mother filled a hamper, and poured wine <br> Into a goatskin. As her daughter climbed <br> The car, she gave into her hands a cruse <br> Of gold with smooth anointing oil for her <br> And her attendant maids. Nausicaä took <br> The scourge and showy reins, and struck the mules <br> To urge them onward. Onward with loud noise <br> They went, and with a speed that slackened not, <br> And bore the robes and her⁠—yet not alone, <br> For with her went the maidens of her train. <br> Now when they reached the river’s pleasant brink, <br> Where lavers had been hollowed out to last <br> Perpetually, and freely through them flowed <br> Pure water that might cleanse the foulest stains, <br> They loosed the mules, and drove them from the wain <br> To browse the sweet grass by the eddying stream; <br> And took the garments out, and flung them down <br> In the dark water, and with hasty feet <br> Trampled them there in frolic rivalry. <br> And when the task was done, and all the stains <br> Were cleansed away, they spread the garments out <br> Along the beach and where the stream had washed <br> The gravel cleanest. Then they bathed, and gave <br> Their limbs the delicate oil, and took their meal <br> Upon the river’s border⁠—while the robes <br> Beneath the sun’s warm rays were growing dry. <br> And now, when they were all refreshed by food, <br> Mistress and maidens laid their veils aside <br> And played at ball. Nausicaä the white-armed <br> Began a song. As when the archer-queen <br> Diana, going forth among the hills⁠— <br> The sides of high Taÿgetus or slopes <br> Of Erymanthus⁠—chases joyously <br> Boars and fleet stags, and round her in a throng <br> Frolic the rural nymphs, Latona’s heart <br> Is glad, for over all the rest are seen <br> Her daughter’s head and brow, and she at once <br> Is known among them, though they all are fair, <br> Such was this spotless virgin midst her maids.</p>
<p class="verse">Now when they were about to move for home <br> With harnessed mules and with the shining robes <br> Carefully folded, then the blue-eyed maid, <br> Pallas, bethought herself of this⁠—to rouse <br> Ulysses and to bring him to behold <br> The bright-eyed maiden, that she might direct <br> The stranger’s way to the Phaeacian town. <br> The royal damsel at a handmaid cast <br> The ball; it missed, and fell into the stream <br> Where a deep eddy whirled. All shrieked aloud. <br> The great Ulysses started from his sleep <br> And sat upright, discoursing to himself:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Ah me! upon what region am I thrown? <br> What men are here⁠—wild, savage, and unjust, <br> Or hospitable, and who hold the gods <br> In reverence? There are voices in the air, <br> Womanly voices, as of nymphs that haunt <br> The mountain summits, and the river-founts, <br> And the moist grassy meadows. Or perchance <br> Am I near men who have the power of speech? <br> Nay, let me then go forth at once and learn.”</p>
<p class="verse">Thus having said, the great Ulysses left <br> The thicket. From the close-grown wood he rent, <br> With his strong hand, a branch well set with leaves <br> And wound it as a covering round his waist. <br> Then like a mountain lion he went forth, <br> That walks abroad, confiding in his strength, <br> In rain and wind; his eyes shoot fire; he falls <br> On oxen, or on sheep, or forest-deer, <br> For hunger prompts him even to attack <br> The flock within its closely guarded fold. <br> Such seemed Ulysses when about to meet <br> Those fair-haired maidens, naked as he was, <br> But forced by strong necessity. To them <br> His look was frightful, for his limbs were foul <br> With sea-foam yet. To right and left they fled <br> Along the jutting riverbanks. Alone <br> The daughter of Alcinoüs kept her place, <br> For Pallas gave her courage and forbade <br> Her limbs to tremble. So she waited there. <br> Ulysses pondered whether to approach <br> The bright-eyed damsel and embrace her knees <br> And supplicate, or, keeping yet aloof, <br> Pray her with soothing words to show the way <br> Townward and give him garments. Musing thus, <br> It seemed the best to keep at distance still, <br> And use soft words, lest, should he clasp her knees, <br> The maid might be displeased. With gentle words <br> Skilfully ordered thus Ulysses spake:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“O queen, I am thy suppliant, whether thou <br> Be mortal or a goddess. If perchance <br> Thou art of that immortal race who dwell <br> In the broad heaven, thou art, I deem, most like <br> To Dian, daughter of imperial Jove, <br> In shape, in stature, and in noble air. <br> If mortal and a dweller of the earth, <br> Thrice happy are thy father and his queen, <br> Thrice happy are thy brothers; and their hearts <br> Must overflow with gladness for thy sake, <br> Beholding such a scion of their house <br> Enter the choral dance. But happiest he <br> Beyond them all, who, bringing princely gifts, <br> Shall bear thee to his home a bride; for sure <br> I never looked on one of mortal race, <br> Woman or man, like thee, and as I gaze <br> I wonder. Like to thee I saw of late, <br> In Delos, a young palm-tree growing up <br> Beside Apollo’s altar; for I sailed <br> To Delos, with much people following me, <br> On a disastrous voyage. Long I gazed <br> Upon it wonder-struck, as I am now⁠— <br> For never from the earth so fair a tree <br> Had sprung. So marvel I, and am amazed <br> At thee, O lady, and in awe forbear <br> To clasp thy knees. Yet much have I endured. <br> It was but yestereve that I escaped <br> From the black sea, upon the twentieth day, <br> So long the billows and the rushing gales <br> Farther and farther from Ogygia’s isle <br> Had borne me. Now upon this shore some god <br> Casts me, perchance to meet new sufferings here; <br> For yet the end is not, and many things <br> The gods must first accomplish. But do thou, <br> queen, have pity on me, since to thee <br> I come the first of all. I do not know <br> A single dweller of the land beside. <br> Show me, I pray, thy city; and bestow <br> Some poor old robe to wrap me⁠—if, indeed, <br> In coming hither, thou hast brought with thee <br> Aught poor or coarse. And may the gods vouchsafe <br> To thee whatever blessing thou canst wish, <br> Husband and home and wedded harmony. <br> There is no better, no more blessed state, <br> Than when the wife and husband in accord <br> Order their household lovingly. Then those <br> Repine who hate them, those who wish them well <br> Rejoice, and they themselves the most of all.” <br> And then the white-armed maid Nausicaä said:⁠— <br> “Since then, O stranger, thou art not malign <br> Of purpose nor weak-minded⁠—yet, in truth, <br> Olympian Jupiter bestows the goods <br> Of fortune on the noble and the base <br> To each one at his pleasure; and thy griefs <br> Are doubtless sent by him, and it is fit <br> That thou submit in patience⁠—now that thou <br> Hast reached our lands, and art within our realm, <br> Thou shalt not lack for garments nor for aught <br> Due to a suppliant stranger in his need. <br> The city I will show thee, and will name <br> Its dwellers⁠—the Phaeacians⁠—they possess <br> The city; all the region lying round <br> Is theirs, and I am daughter of the prince <br> Alcinoüs, large of soul, to whom are given <br> The rule of the Phaeacians and their power.”</p>
<p class="verse">So spake the damsel, and commanded thus <br> Her fair-haired maids: “Stay! whither do ye flee, <br> My handmaids, when a man appears in sight? <br> Ye think, perhaps, he is some enemy. <br> Nay, there is no man living now, nor yet <br> Will live, to enter, bringing war, the land <br> Of the Phaeacians. Very dear are they <br> To the great gods. We dwell apart, afar <br> Within the unmeasured deep, amid its waves <br> The most remote of men; no other race <br> Hath commerce with us. This man comes to us <br> A wanderer and unhappy, and to him <br> Our cares are due. The stranger and the poor <br> Are sent by Jove, and slight regards to them <br> Are grateful. Maidens, give the stranger food <br> And drink, and take him to the riverside <br> To bathe where there is shelter from the wind.”</p>
<p class="verse">So spake the mistress; and they stayed their flight <br> And bade each other stand, and led the chief <br> Under a shelter as the royal maid, <br> Daughter of stout Alcinoüs, gave command, <br> And laid a cloak and tunic near the spot <br> To be his raiment, and a golden cruse <br> Of limpid oil. Then, as they bade him bathe <br> In the fresh stream, the noble chieftain said:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Withdraw, ye maidens, hence, while I prepare <br> To cleanse my shoulders from the bitter brine, <br> And to anoint them; long have these my limbs <br> Been unrefreshed by oil. I will not bathe <br> Before you. I should be ashamed to stand <br> Unclothed in presence of these bright-haired maids.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake; they hearkened and withdrew, and told <br> The damsel what he said. Ulysses then <br> Washed the salt spray of ocean from his back <br> And his broad shoulders in the flowing stream, <br> And wiped away the sea-froth from his brows. <br> And when the bath was over, and his limbs <br> Had been anointed, and he had put on <br> The garments sent him by the spotless maid, <br> Jove’s daughter, Pallas, caused him to appear <br> Of statelier size and more majestic mien, <br> And bade the locks that crowned his head flow down, <br> Curling like blossoms of the hyacinth. <br> As when some skilful workman trained and taught <br> By Vulcan and Minerva in his art <br> Binds the bright silver with a verge of gold, <br> And graceful is his handiwork, such grace <br> Did Pallas shed upon the hero’s brow <br> And shoulders, as he passed along the beach, <br> And, glorious in his beauty and the pride <br> Of noble bearing, sat aloof. The maid <br> Admired, and to her bright-haired women spake:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Listen to me, my maidens, while I speak. <br> This man comes not among the godlike sons <br> Of the Phaeacian stock against the will <br> Of all the gods of heaven. I thought him late <br> Of an unseemly aspect; now he bears <br> A likeness to the immortal ones whose home <br> Is the broad heaven. I would that I might call <br> A man like him my husband, dwelling here, <br> And here content to dwell. Now hasten, maids, <br> And set before the stranger food and wine.”</p>
<p class="verse">She spake; they heard and cheerfully obeyed, <br> And set before Ulysses food and wine. <br> The patient chief Ulysses ate and drank <br> Full eagerly, for he had fasted long.</p>
<p class="verse">White-armed Nausicaä then had other cares. <br> She placed the smoothly folded robes within <br> The sumptuous chariot, yoked the firm-hoofed mules, <br> And mounted to her place, and from the seat <br> Spake kindly, counselling Ulysses thus:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Now, stranger, rise and follow to the town, <br> And to my royal father’s palace I <br> Will be thy guide, where, doubt not, thou wilt meet <br> The noblest men of our Phaeacian race. <br> But do as I advise⁠—for not inapt <br> I deem thee. While we traverse yet the fields <br> Among the tilth, keep thou among my train <br> Of maidens, following fast behind the mules <br> And chariot. I will lead thee in the way. <br> But when our train goes upward toward the town, <br> Fenced with its towery wall, and on each side <br> Embraced by a fair haven, with a strait <br> Of narrow entrance, where our well-oared barques <br> Have each a mooring-place along the road, <br> And there round Neptune’s glorious fane extends <br> A marketplace, surrounded by huge stones, <br> Dragged from the quarry hither, where is kept <br> The rigging of the barques⁠—sailcloth and ropes⁠— <br> And oars are polished there⁠—for little reck <br> Phaeacians of the quiver and the bow, <br> And give most heed to masts and shrouds and ships <br> Well poised, in which it is their pride to cross <br> The foamy deep⁠—when there I would not bring <br> Rude taunts upon myself, for in the crowd <br> Are brutal men. One of the baser sort <br> Perchance might say, on meeting us: ‘What man, <br> Handsome and lusty-limbed, is he who thus <br> Follows Nausicaä? where was it her luck <br> To find him? will he be her husband yet? <br> Perhaps she brings some wanderer from his ship, <br> A stranger from strange lands, for we have here <br> No neighbors; or, perhaps, it is a god <br> Called down by fervent prayer from heaven to dwell <br> Henceforth with her. ’Tis well if she have found <br> A husband elsewhere, since at home she meets <br> Her many noble wooers with disdain; <br> They are Phaeacians.’ Thus the crowd would say, <br> And it would bring reproach upon my name. <br> I too would blame another who should do <br> The like, and, while her parents were alive, <br> Without their knowledge should consort with men <br> Before her marriage. Stranger, now observe <br> My words, and thou shalt speedily obtain <br> Safe-conduct from my father, and be sent <br> Upon thy voyage homeward. We shall reach <br> A beautiful grove of poplars by the way, <br> Sacred to Pallas; from it flows a brook, <br> And round it lies a meadow. In this spot <br> My father has his country-grounds, and here <br> His garden flourishes, as far from town <br> As one could hear a shout. There sit thou down <br> And wait till we are in the city’s streets <br> And at my father’s house. When it shall seem <br> That we are there, arise and onward fare <br> To the Phaeacian city, and inquire <br> Where dwells Alcinoüs the large-souled king, <br> My father; ’tis not hard to find; a child <br> Might lead thee thither. Of the houses reared <br> By the Phaeacians there is none like that <br> In which Alcinoüs the hero dwells. <br> When thou art once within the court and hall, <br> Go quickly through the palace till thou find <br> My mother where she sits beside the hearth, <br> Leaning against a column in its blaze, <br> And twisting threads, a marvel to behold, <br> Of bright sea-purple, while her maidens sit <br> Behind her. Near her is my father’s throne, <br> On which he sits at feasts, and drinks the wine <br> Like one of the immortals. Pass it by <br> And clasp my mother’s knees; so mayst thou see <br> Soon and with joy the day of thy return, <br> Although thy home be far. For if her mood <br> Be kindly toward thee, thou mayst hope to greet <br> Thy friends once more, and enter yet again <br> Thy own fair palace in thy native land.”</p>
<p class="verse">Thus having said, she raised the shining scourge <br> And struck the mules, that quickly left behind <br> The river. On they went with easy pace <br> And even steps. The damsel wielded well <br> The reins, and used the lash with gentle hand, <br> So that Ulysses and her train of maids <br> On foot could follow close. And now the sun <br> Was sinking when they came to that fair grove <br> Sacred to Pallas. There the noble chief <br> Ulysses sat him down, and instantly <br> Prayed to the daughter of imperial Jove:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“O thou unconquerable child of Jove <br> The Aegis-bearer! hearken to me now, <br> Since late thou wouldst not listen to my prayer, <br> What time the mighty shaker of the shores <br> Pursued and wrecked me! Grant me to receive <br> Pity and kindness from Phaeacia’s sons.”</p>
<p class="verse">So prayed he, supplicating. Pallas heard <br> The prayer, but came not to him openly. <br> Awe of her father’s brother held her back; <br> For he would still pursue with violent hate <br> Ulysses, till he reached his native land.</p>
