<p class="lede">Hospitable reception by Eumaeus of Ulysses in the disguise of a beggar⁠—His feigned account of himself⁠—His promise that Ulysses shall return⁠—Supper in the lodge of Eumaeus⁠—Stratagem of Ulysses to procure a cloak for the night.</p>
<p class="verse">Then from the haven up the rugged path <br> Ulysses went among the woody heights. <br> He sought the spot where Pallas bade him meet <br> The noble swineherd, who of all that served <br> The great Ulysses chiefly had in charge <br> To bring the day’s supplies. He found him there <br> Seated beneath the portico, before <br> His airy lodge, that might be seen from far, <br> Well built and spacious, standing by itself. <br> Eumaeus, while his lord was far away, <br> Had built it, though not bidden by the queen <br> Nor old Laertes, with the stones he drew <br> From quarries thither. Round it he had set <br> A hedge of thorns, encircling these with stakes <br> Close set and many, cloven from the heart <br> Of oak. Within that circuit he had made <br> Twelve sties, beside each other, for the swine <br> To lie in. Fifty wallowed in each sty, <br> All females; there they littered. But the males <br> Were fewer, and were kept without; and these <br> The suitor train made fewer every day, <br> Feeding upon them, for Eumaeus sent <br> Always the best of all his fatling herd. <br> These numbered twice nine score. Beside them slept <br> Four mastiffs, which the master swineherd fed, <br> Savage as wolves. Eumaeus to his feet <br> Was fitting sandals, which he carved and shaped <br> From a stained ox-hide, while the other hinds <br> Were gone on different errands⁠—three to drive <br> The herds of swine⁠—a fourth was sent to take <br> A fatling to the city, that the crew <br> Of arrogant suitors, having offered him <br> In sacrifice, might feast upon his flesh.</p>
<p class="verse">The loud-mouthed dogs that saw Ulysses come <br> Ran toward him, fiercely baying. He sat down <br> At once, through caution, letting fall his staff <br> Upon the ground, and would have suffered there <br> Unseemly harm, within his own domain, <br> But then the swineherd, following with quick steps, <br> Rushed through the vestibule, and dropped the hide. <br> He chid the dogs and, pelting them with stones, <br> Drave them asunder, and addressed the king:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“O aged man, the mastiffs of the lodge <br> Had almost torn thee, and thou wouldst have cast <br> Bitter reproach upon me. Other griefs <br> And miseries the gods have made my lot. <br> Here sorrowfully sitting I lament <br> A godlike master, and for others tend <br> His fading swine; while, haply hungering <br> For bread, he wanders among alien men <br> In other kingdoms, if indeed he lives <br> And looks upon the sun. But follow me, <br> And come into the house, that there, refreshed <br> With food and wine, old man, thou mayst declare <br> Whence thou dost come and what thou hast endured.”</p>
<p class="verse">So the good swineherd spake, and led the way <br> Into the lodge, and bade his guest sit down, <br> And laid thick rushes for his seat, and spread <br> On these a wild goat’s shaggy hide to make <br> A soft and ample couch. Rejoiced to meet <br> So kind a welcome, thus Ulysses spake:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“May Jupiter and all the deathless gods <br> Bestow on thee, my host, in recompense <br> Of this kind welcome, all thy heart’s desire!”</p>
<p class="verse">And then, Eumaeus, thou didst answer thus: <br> “My guest, it were not right to treat with scorn <br> A stranger, though he were of humbler sort <br> Than thou, for strangers and the poor are sent <br> By Jove; our gifts are small, though gladly given, <br> As it must ever be with those who serve <br> Young masters, whom they fear. The gods themselves <br> Prevent, no doubt, the safe return of him <br> Who loved me much, and would ere this have given <br> What a kind lord is wont to give his hind⁠— <br> A house, a croft, the wife whom he has wooed, <br> Rewarding faithful services which God <br> Hath prospered, as he here hath prospered mine. <br> Thus would my master, had he here grown old, <br> Have recompensed my toils; but he is dead. <br> O that the house of Helen, for whose sake <br> So many fell, had perished utterly! <br> For he went forth at Agamemnon’s call, <br> Honoring the summons, and on Ilium’s coast, <br> Famed for its coursers, fought the sons of Troy.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake, and girt his tunic round his loins, <br> And hastened to the sties in which the herds <br> Of swine were lying. Thence he took out two <br> And slaughtered them, and singed them, sliced the flesh, <br> And fixed it upon spits, and, when the whole <br> Was roasted, brought and placed it reeking hot, <br> Still on the spits and sprinkled with white meal, <br> Before Ulysses. Then he mingled wine <br> Of delicate flavors in a wooden bowl, <br> And opposite Ulysses sat him down, <br> And thus with kindly words bespake his guest:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Feast, stranger, on these porkers. We who serve <br> May feed on them; it is the suitor train <br> That banquet on the fatted swine⁠—the men <br> Who neither fear heaven’s anger nor are moved <br> By pity. The great gods are never pleased <br> With violent deeds; they honor equity <br> And justice. Even those who land as foes <br> And spoilers upon foreign shores, and bear <br> Away much plunder by the will of Jove, <br> Returning homeward with their laden barques, <br> Feel, brooding heavily upon their minds, <br> The fear of vengeance. But these suitors know⁠— <br> For haply they have heard some god declare⁠— <br> That he, the king, is dead; they neither make <br> Their suit with decency, nor will withdraw <br> To their own homes, but at their ease devour <br> His substance with large waste, and never spare. <br> Of all the days and nights which Jupiter <br> Gives to mankind is none when they require <br> A single victim only, or but two, <br> For sacrifice, and lavishly they drain <br> His wine-jars. Once large revenues were his. <br> No hero on the dark-soiled continent <br> Nor in the isle of Ithaca possessed <br> Such wealth as he, nor even twenty men <br> Together. Hear me while I give the amount. <br> Twelve herds of kine that on the mainland graze <br> Are his, as many flocks of sheep, of swine <br> As many droves; as many flocks of goats <br> Are tended there by strangers, and by hinds, <br> His servants. Here moreover, in the fields <br> Beyond us, graze eleven numerous flocks <br> Of goats, attended by his trusty men, <br> Each one of whom brings daily home a goat, <br> The finest of the fatlings. I meantime <br> Am keeper of these swine, and from the drove <br> I choose and to the palace send the best.”</p>
<p class="verse">So spake the swineherd, while Ulysses ate <br> The flesh with eager appetite, and drank <br> The wine in silence, meditating woe <br> To all the suitors. When the meal was o’er, <br> And he was strengthened by the food, his host <br> Filled up with wine the cup from which he drank. <br> And gave it to Ulysses, who, well pleased, <br> Received it, and with winged words replied:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“What rich and mighty chief was he, my friend, <br> Of whom thou speakest, and who purchased thee? <br> Thou sayest that he died to swell the fame <br> Of Agamemnon. Tell his name, for I <br> Perchance know somewhat of him. Jupiter <br> And the great gods know whether I have seen <br> The man, and have some tidings for thy ear; <br> For I have wandered over many lands.”</p>
<p class="verse">And then again the noble swineherd spake: <br> “O aged man, no wanderer who should bring <br> News of Ulysses e’er would win his wife <br> And son to heed the tale. For roving men, <br> In need of hospitality, are prone <br> To falsehood, and will never speak the truth. <br> The vagabond who comes to Ithaca <br> Goes straightway to my mistress with his lies. <br> Kindly she welcomes him, and cherishes <br> And questions him, while tears abundantly <br> Fall from her lids⁠—such tears as women shed <br> Whose lords have perished in a distant land. <br> Thou too, old man, perchance, couldst readily <br> Frame a like fable, if someone would give <br> A change of raiment for thy news⁠—a cloak <br> And tunic. But the dogs and fowls of air <br> Have doubtless fed upon the frame from which <br> The life has passed, and torn from off his bones <br> The skin, or fishes of the deep have preyed <br> Upon it, and his bones upon the shore <br> Lie whelmed in sand. So is he lost to us, <br> And sorrow is the lot of all his friends, <br> Mine most of all; for nowhere shall I find <br> So kind a master, though I were to come <br> Into my father’s and my mother’s house, <br> Where I was born and reared. Nor do I pine <br> So much to look on them with my own eyes, <br> And in my place of birth, as I lament <br> Ulysses lost. Though he be far away, <br> Yet must I ever speak, O stranger guest, <br> His name with reverence, for exceedingly <br> He loved me and most kindly cared for me; <br> And though he is to be with us no more, <br> I hold him as an elder brother still.”</p>
<p class="verse">Ulysses, the great sufferer, thus replied: <br> “Since then, my friend, thou dost not say nor think <br> That he will come again, nor wilt believe <br> My words, I now repeat, but with an oath, <br> Ulysses will return. Let this reward <br> Be given for my good news: the very hour <br> When he once more is in his house, bestow <br> On me a comely change of raiment⁠—cloak <br> And tunic⁠—nor will I accept the gift, <br> Though great my need, until he comes again. <br> For as the gates of hell do I detest <br> The man who, tempted by his poverty, <br> Deceives with lying words. Now Jupiter <br> Bear witness, and this hospitable board <br> And hearth of good Ulysses where I sit, <br> That all which I foretell will come to pass. <br> This very year Ulysses will return. <br> He, when this month goes out, and as the next <br> Is entering, will be here in his domain, <br> To be avenged on those, whoe’er they be, <br> That dare insult his wife and noble son.”</p>
<p class="verse">And then, Eumaeus, thou didst answer thus: <br> “Old man, I shall not give thee that reward, <br> For never will Ulysses come again <br> To his own palace. Drink thy wine in peace, <br> And let us give our thoughts to other things. <br> Remind me not of this again; my heart <br> Grows heavy in my bosom when I hear <br> My honored master named. But leave the oath <br> Unsworn, and may Ulysses come, as we <br> Earnestly wish⁠—I and Penelope, <br> And old Laertes, and the godlike youth <br> Telemachus. And then, again, I bear <br> Perpetual sorrow for Telemachus, <br> My master’s son, to whom the gods had given <br> A generous growth like that of some young plant, <br> And who, I hoped, would prove no less in worth <br> Than his own father, and of eminent gifts <br> In form and mind. Some god, perchance some man, <br> Hath caused that mind to lose its equal poise, <br> And he is gone to Pylos the divine <br> For tidings of his father. Meanwhile here <br> The arrogant suitors plan to lie in wait <br> For him as he returns, that utterly <br> The stock of great Arcesius from our isle <br> May perish, and its name be heard no more. <br> Speak we no more of him, be it his fate <br> To fall or flee; but O, may Saturn’s son <br> Protect him with his arm! And now, old man, <br> Relate, I pray, thy fortunes; tell me true, <br> That I may know who thou mayst be, and whence <br> Thou earnest, where thy city lies, and who <br> Thy parents were, what galley landed thee <br> Upon our coast, and how the manners <br> Brought thee to Ithaca, and of what race <br> They claim to be; for I may well suppose <br> Thou hast not come to Ithaca on foot.”</p>
<p class="verse">Ulysses, the sagacious, answered him: <br> “I will tell all and truly. Yet if here <br> Were store of food, and wine for many days, <br> And we might feast at ease within thy lodge <br> While other labored, I should hardly end <br> In a whole year the history of the woes <br> Which I have borne, and of the many toils <br> Which it hath pleased the gods to lay on me.</p>
<p class="verse">“It is my boast that I am of the race <br> Who dwell in spacious Crete, a rich man’s son, <br> Within whose palace many other sons <br> Were born and reared, the offspring of his wife; <br> But me a purchased mother whom he made <br> His concubine brought forth to him. And yet <br> Castor Hylacides, from whom I sprang, <br> Held me in equal favor with the rest; <br> And he himself was honored like a god <br> Among the Cretan people, for his wealth <br> And for his prosperous life and gallant sons. <br> But fate and death o’ertook and bore him down <br> To Pluto’s realm, and his magnanimous sons <br> Divided his large riches, casting lots. <br> Small was the portion they assigned to me; <br> They gave a dwelling, but my valor won <br> A bride, the daughter of a wealthy house⁠— <br> For I was not an idler, nor in war <br> A coward; but all that is with the past. <br> And thou, who seest the stubble now, mayst guess <br> What was the harvest, ere calamities <br> Had come so thick upon me. Once did Mars <br> And Pallas lend me courage, and the power <br> To break through ranks of armed men. Whene’er <br> I formed an ambush of the bravest chiefs, <br> And planned destruction to the enemy, <br> My noble spirit never set the fear <br> Of death before me; I was ever first <br> To spring upon the foes, and with my spear <br> To smite them as they turned their steps to flee. <br> Such was I once in war; to till the fields <br> I never liked, nor yet the household cares <br> By which illustrious sons are reared. I loved <br> Ships well appointed, combats, polished spears <br> And arrows. Things that others hold in dread <br> Were my delight; some god inclined to them <br> My mind⁠—so true it is that different men <br> Rejoice in different labors. Ere the sons <br> Of Greece embarked for Troy, I served in war <br> Nine times as leader against foreign foes, <br> With troops and galleys under me, and then <br> I prospered; from the mass of spoil I chose <br> The things that pleased me, and obtained by lot <br> Still other treasures. Thus my household grew <br> In riches, and I was revered and great <br> Among the Cretans. When all-seeing Jove <br> Decreed the unhappy voyage to the coast <br> Of Troy, they made the great Idomeneus <br> And me commanders of the fleet. No power <br> Had we⁠—the public clamor was so fierce⁠— <br> To put the charge aside. Nine years we warred⁠— <br> We sons of Greece⁠—and in the tenth laid waste <br> The city of Priam, and embarked for home. <br> Our fleets were scattered by the gods. For me <br> Did all-disposing Jupiter ordain <br> A wretched lot. But one short month I dwelt <br> Happy among my children, with the wife <br> Wedded to me in youth, and my large wealth. <br> And then I planned a voyage to the coast <br> Of Egypt, with a gallant fleet, and men <br> Of godlike valor. I equipped nine ships, <br> And quickly came the people to embark. <br> Six days on shore my comrades banqueted, <br> And many a victim for the sacrifice <br> And for the feast I gave; the seventh we sailed <br> From Crete’s broad isle before a favoring wind <br> That blew from the clear north, and easily <br> We floated on as down a stream. No ship <br> Was harmed upon its way; in health and ease <br> We sat, the wind and helmsmen guiding us, <br> And came upon the fifth day to the land <br> Of Egypt, watered by its noble streams. <br> I bade my comrades keep beside our ships <br> Upon the strand, and watch them well. I placed <br> Sentries upon the Heights. Yet confident <br> In their own strength, and rashly giving way <br> To greed, my comrades ravaged the fair fields <br> Of the Egyptians, slew them, and bore off <br> Their wives and little ones. The rumor reached <br> The city soon; the people heard the alarm <br> And came together. With the early morn <br> All the great plain was thronged with horse and foot, <br> And gleamed with brass; while Jove, the Thunderer, sent <br> A deadly fear into our ranks, where none <br> Dared face the foe. On every side was death. <br> The Egyptians hewed down many with the sword, <br> And some they led away alive to toil <br> For them in slavery. To my mind there came <br> A thought, inspired by Jove; yet I could wish <br> That I had met my fate, and perished there <br> In Egypt, such have been my sorrows since. <br> I took the well-wrought helmet from my head, <br> And from my shoulders dropped the shield, and flung <br> The javelin from my hand, and went to meet <br> The monarch in his chariot, clasped his knees <br> And kissed them. He was moved to pity me, <br> And spared me. In his car he seated me, <br> And bore me weeping home. Though many rushed <br> At me with ashen spears, to thrust me through⁠— <br> For furious was their anger⁠—he forbade. <br> He feared the wrath of Jove, the stranger’s friend <br> And foe of wrong. Seven years I dwelt among <br> The Egyptians, and I gathered in their land <br> Large wealth, for all were liberal of their gifts. <br> But with the eighth revolving year there came <br> A shrewd Phoenician, deep in guile, whose craft <br> Had wrought much wrong to many. With smooth words <br> This man persuaded me to go with him <br> Into Phoenicia, where his dwelling lay <br> And his possessions. With him I abode <br> For one whole year; and when its months and days <br> Were ended, and another year began, <br> He put me in a ship to cross the sea <br> To Lybia. He had framed a treacherous plot, <br> By making half the vessel’s cargo mine, <br> To lure me thither, and to sell me there <br> For a large price. I went on board constrained, <br> But with misgivings. Under a clear sky, <br> With favoring breezes from the north, we ran <br> O’er the mid sea, beyond the isle of Crete. <br> When we had left the isle, and saw no land <br> But only sky and sea, Saturnius bade <br> A black cloud gather o’er our roomy ship. <br> The sea grew dark below. On high the God <br> Thundered again and yet again, and sent <br> A bolt into our ship, which, as it felt <br> The lightning, reeled and shuddered, and was filled <br> With sulphur-smoke. The seamen from the deck <br> Fell headlong, and were tossed upon the waves <br> Like seamews round our galley, which the God <br> Forbade them to regain. But Jupiter <br> Gave to my hands, bewildered as I was, <br> Our dark-prowed galley’s mast, unbroken yet, <br> That by its aid I might escape. I wound <br> My arms around it, and the raging winds <br> Swept me along. Nine days they bore me on, <br> And on the tenth dark night a mighty surge <br> Drifted me, as it rolled, upon the coast <br> Of the Thesprotians. There the hero-king <br> Of the Thesprotians freely sheltered me <br> And fed me; for his well-beloved son <br> Had found me overcome with cold and toil, <br> And took me by the hand and raised me up, <br> And led me to his father’s house, and gave <br> Seemly attire, a tunic and a cloak.</p>
<p class="verse">“There heard I of Ulysses. Pheidon told <br> How he received him as a guest and friend, <br> When on his homeward voyage. Then he showed <br> The wealth Ulysses gathered, brass and gold, <br> And steel divinely wrought. That store might serve <br> To feed, until ten generations pass, <br> Another household. But the chief himself, <br> So Pheidon said, was at Dodona then; <br> For he had gone to hear from the tall oak <br> Of Jupiter the counsel of the God, <br> Whether to land in opulent Ithaca, <br> After long years of absence, openly <br> Or in disguise. The monarch took an oath <br> In his own palace, pouring to the gods <br> Their wine, that even then the ship was launched, <br> And the crew ready to attend him home. <br> But me he first dismissed. There was a ship <br> Of the Thesprotians just about to make <br> A voyage to Dulichium, rich in fields <br> Of wheat. He bade them take me faithfully <br> To King Acastus; but another thought <br> Found favor with the crew, a wicked scheme <br> To plunge me deeper in calamity. <br> And when our ship had sailed away from land, <br> They hastened to prepare me for a life <br> Of slavery. They took my garments off, <br> Mantle and cloak, and clothed me in a vest <br> And cloak, the very rags which thou dost see. <br> The evening brought them to the pleasant fields <br> Of Ithaca. They bound me in the ship <br> With a strong cord, and disembarked, and took <br> A hasty meal upon the ocean-side; <br> Easily did the gods unbind my limbs. <br> I wrapped a tattered cloth about my head, <br> And, slipping from the polished rudder, brought <br> My bosom to the sea, and spread my hands, <br> And swam away. I soon had left the crew <br> At distance; then I turned and climbed the shore, <br> Where it was dark with forest, and lay close <br> Within its shelter, while they wandered round <br> And grumbled, but they ventured not to pass <br> Into the island farther on their search. <br> They turned, and went on board their roomy barque. <br> Thus mightily the gods delivered me, <br> And they have brought me to a wise man’s lodge, <br> And now I see it is my lot to live.”</p>
<p class="verse">Then thou, Eumaeus, thus didst make reply: <br> “Unhappy stranger, thou hast deeply moved <br> My heart in telling all that thou hast borne, <br> And all thy wanderings. Yet are some things wrong. <br> Thou hast not spoken of Ulysses well. <br> Why should a man like thee invent such tales, <br> So purposeless? Of one thing I am sure <br> Concerning his return⁠—the gods all hate <br> My master, since they neither caused his death <br> In the great war of Troy, nor, when the war <br> Was over, suffered him to die at home, <br> And in the arms of those who loved him most; <br> For then would all the Greeks have reared to him <br> A monument, and mighty would have been <br> The heritage of glory for his son; <br> But now ingloriously the harpy brood <br> Have torn him. I, apart among my swine, <br> Go never to the town, unless, perchance, <br> The sage Penelope requires me there, <br> When someone comes with tidings from abroad. <br> Then those who sorrow for their absent lord, <br> And those who waste his substance, both inquire <br> News of the king. For me, it suits me not <br> Ever to ask for tidings, since the day <br> When an Aetolian with a flattering tale <br> Deceived me. He had slain a man, and came <br> Wandering in many lands to my abode, <br> And kindly I received him. He had seen, <br> He said, my master with Idomeneus, <br> Among the Cretans, putting in repair <br> His galleys, shattered by a furious storm, <br> And in the summer time he would be here, <br> Or in the autumn, bringing ample wealth, <br> And his brave comrades with him. Seek not then, <br> O aged sufferer, whom some deity <br> Has guided hither, to amuse my grief <br> With fictions that may bring back pleasant thoughts, <br> Since not for them I minister to thee <br> And love thee, but through reverence for Jove⁠— <br> The stranger’s friend⁠—and pity for thyself.”</p>
<p class="verse">Ulysses, the sagacious, spake again: <br> “Within thy bosom thou dost bear a heart <br> Of slow belief, since not the oath I take <br> Persuades or even moves thee. Make we now <br> A covenant, and let the gods who dwell <br> Upon Olympus be our witnesses, <br> That when thy master comes to this abode <br> Thou wilt bestow a tunic and a cloak, <br> And wilt despatch me clothed in seemly garb <br> Hence to Dulichium, whither I would go. <br> But if he come not as I have foretold, <br> Then charge thy servants that they cast me down <br> From a tall rock, that never beggar more <br> May think to cozen thee with lying tales.”</p>
<p class="verse">The noble swineherd answered him and said: <br> “Great would my honor be, and I should gain <br> Great praise for worth among the sons of men, <br> If, having welcomed thee into my lodge <br> And spread the board for thee, I took thy life; <br> Then boldly might I pray to Saturn’s son. <br> But see, the supper hour is come, and soon <br> Will my companions be within, and they <br> Will make a liberal banquet ready here.”</p>
<p class="verse">Thus did the twain confer. Now came the swine, <br> And those who tended them. They penned the herd <br> In their enclosure, and a din of cries <br> Rose as they entered. Then the swineherd called <br> To his companions: “Bring the best of all, <br> And we will make an offering for the sake <br> Of one who comes from far and is my guest. <br> And we will also feast, for we have toiled <br> Long time in tendance of this white-toothed herd, <br> And others waste, unpunished, what we rear.”</p>
<p class="verse">So spake he, and began to cleave the wood <br> With the sharp steel; the others chose and brought <br> A fatted brawn, and placed him on the hearth. <br> Nor was the swineherd careless of the rites <br> Due to the gods⁠—such was his piety. <br> From off the white-toothed victim first he sheared <br> The bristles of the forehead, casting them <br> Into the flames, and prayed to all the gods <br> For wise Ulysses and his safe return. <br> Next, with a fragment of the oaken trunk <br> Which he had just then cleft, he smote the boar, <br> And the life left it. Then they cut its throat, <br> And, having singed it, quickly hewed the parts <br> Asunder, while the swineherd took and laid, <br> On the rich fat, raw portions from the limbs <br> For sacrifice, and other parts he cast, <br> Sprinkled with flour of meal, into the flames; <br> The rest they duly sliced and fixed on spits, <br> And roasted carefully, and drew it back, <br> And heaped it on the board. And now arose <br> The swineherd to divide the whole, for well <br> He knew the duty of a host. He made <br> Seven parts; and one he offered to the Nymphs, <br> To Hermes, son of Maia, one, and both <br> With prayer; the rest he set before the guests, <br> But, honoring Ulysses, gave to him <br> The white-toothed victim’s ample chine. The king, <br> The wise Ulysses, was well pleased, and said:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Eumaeus, be thou ever dear to Jove <br> As to myself, since with thy benefits <br> Thou freely honorest such a one as I.”</p>
<p class="verse">And thou, Eumaeus, madest answer thus: <br> “Eat, venerable stranger, and enjoy <br> What is before us. At his pleasure God <br> Gives or withholds; his power is over all.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake, and burned to the eternal gods <br> The firstlings, and poured out the dark red wine, <br> And to Ulysses, spoiler of walled towns, <br> Who sat beside the table, gave the cup. <br> Meantime to each Mesaulius brought the bread⁠— <br> A servant whom Eumaeus, while his lord <br> Was far away, had taken for himself, <br> Without the order of Penelope <br> Or old Laertes; from the Taphian tribe <br> With his own goods he bought him. Now the guests <br> Put forth their hands and shared the ready feast; <br> And when their thirst and hunger were appeased <br> Mesaulius took the bread away, and all, <br> Satiate with food and wine, lay down to rest.</p>
<p class="verse">Then came the darkness on, without a moon; <br> And Jupiter the whole night long sent down <br> The rain, and strong the showery west-wind blew. <br> And now to try the swineherd, if with all <br> His kindly ministrations to his guest <br> He yet would spare to him his cloak, or bid <br> Another do the like, Ulysses spake:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“Eumaeus, hearken thou, and all the rest, <br> Thy comrades, while I utter boastful words. <br> Wine makes me foolish, it can even cause <br> The wise to sing and laugh a silly laugh <br> And dance, and often to the lips it brings <br> Words that were better left unsaid. But since <br> I have begun to prattle, I will not <br> Keep back my thought. I would I were as young <br> And in the same full strength as when I formed <br> Part of an ambush near the walls of Troy. <br> The leaders were Ulysses, and the son <br> Of Atreus, Menelaus, with myself <br> The third, for they desired it. When we reached <br> The city and the lofty walls we lay <br> Couched in a marshy spot among the reeds <br> And thick-grown shrubs, with all our armor on. <br> ’Twas an inclement night, and the north-wind <br> Blew bitter chill, the cold snow fell and lay <br> White like hoar frost; ice gathered on our shields. <br> The rest had cloaks and tunics, and they slept <br> At ease, their shoulders covered with their shields. <br> I only, when I joined the squadron, left <br> My cloak unwisely, for I had not thought <br> Of such fierce cold. I went but with my shield <br> And my embroidered girdle. When the night <br> Was in its later watches, and the stars <br> Were turning toward their set, I thus bespake <br> Ulysses near me, thrusting in his side <br> My elbow, and he listened readily:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘Son of Laertes, nobly born and wise! <br> Ulysses, I shall not be long among <br> The living; for I perish with the cold. <br> I have no cloak; some god misled my thought, <br> So that I brought one garment and no more, <br> And now I see there is no help for me.’</p>
<p class="verse">“I spake, and instantly his mind conceived <br> This stratagem⁠—such was his readiness <br> In council and in battle⁠—and he said <br> To me in a low voice: ‘Be silent now, <br> And let no others of the Achaians hear!’ <br> And leaning on his elbow thus he spake:⁠—</p>
<p class="verse">“ ‘Hear me, my friends: a dream has come from heaven <br> Into my sleep. Far from our ships we lie; <br> And now let someone haste to bear from us <br> This word to Agamemnon, Atreus’ son, <br> The shepherd of the people, that he send <br> More warriors to this ambush from the fleet.’</p>
<p class="verse">“He spake, and Thoas instantly arose⁠— <br> Andraemon’s son⁠—and threw his purple cloak <br> Aside, and hastened toward the fleet. I took <br> Gladly the garment he had left, and lay <br> Till Morning in her golden chariot came. <br> And now I would that I were young again, <br> And in the vigor of my prime, for then <br> Someone among the swineherds in the stalls <br> Would find, I think, a cloak for me, through love <br> And reverence of such a man; but now <br> They hold me in slight favor, dressed in rags.”</p>
<p class="verse">And thus, Eumaeus, thou didst make reply: <br> “O aged man! we see no cause of blame <br> In thy recital, and of all thy words <br> Not one is unbecoming or inapt. <br> Thou shalt not lack for garments, nor aught else <br> That any suppliant in his poverty <br> Might hope for at our hands tonight. With morn <br> Gird thou thy tatters on again; for here <br> We have not many cloaks, nor many a change <br> Of raiment⁠—only one for each of us. <br> But when the son of our Ulysses comes <br> Again, he will provide thee with a cloak <br> And tunic, and will send thee where thou wilt.”</p>
<p class="verse">He spake and rose, and made his guest a bed <br> Close to the hearth, and threw on it the skins <br> Of sheep and goats, and there Ulysses lay, <br> O’er whom the swineherd spread a thick large cloak, <br> Which he had often worn for a defence <br> When a wild winter storm was in the air.</p>
<p class="verse">Thus slept Ulysses with the young men near. <br> A couch within, and distant from his charge, <br> Pleased not the swineherd, who first armed himself, <br> And then went forth. Ulysses gladly saw <br> That while he was in distant lands his goods <br> Were watched so faithfully. Eumaeus hung <br> About his sturdy shoulders a sharp sword, <br> And wrapped a thick cloak round him, tempest-proof, <br> And took the hide of a huge pampered goat, <br> And a well-pointed javelin for defence <br> Both against dogs and men. So went he forth <br> To take his rest where lay the white-toothed swine, <br> Herded and slumbering underneath a rock, <br> Whose hollow fenced them from the keen north-wind.</p>
