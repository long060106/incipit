<p>Man at his birth is supple and weak; at his death, firm and strong. (So it is with) all things. Trees and plants, in their early growth, are soft and brittle; at their death, dry and withered.</p>
<p>Thus it is that firmness and strength are the concomitants of death; softness and weakness, the concomitants of life.</p>
<p>Hence he who (relies on) the strength of his forces does not conquer; and a tree which is strong will fill the outstretched arms, (and thereby invites the feller.)</p>
<p>Therefore the place of what is firm and strong is below, and that of what is soft and weak is above.<sup>112</sup></p>
