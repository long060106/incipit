<p>When old Mr. Fox was dead, the wolf came as a wooer, and knocked at the door, and the cat who was servant to Mrs. Fox, opened it for him. The wolf greeted her, and said,</p>
<blockquote><p class="verse">“Good day, Mrs. Cat of Kehrewit, <br> How comes it that alone you sit? <br> What are you making good?”</p></blockquote>
<p>The cat replied,</p>
<blockquote><p class="verse">“In milk I’m breaking bread so sweet, <br> Will the gentleman please come in and eat?”</p></blockquote>
<p>“No, thank you, Mrs. Cat,” answered the wolf. “Is Mrs. Fox not at home?”</p>
<p>The cat said,</p>
<blockquote><p class="verse">“She sits upstairs in her room, <br> Bewailing her sorrowful doom, <br> Bewailing her trouble so sore, <br> For old Mr. Fox is no more.”</p></blockquote>
<p>The wolf answered,</p>
<blockquote><p class="verse">“If she’s in want of a husband now, <br> Then will it please her to step below?” <br> The cat runs quickly up the stair, <br> And lets her tail fly here and there, <br> Until she comes to the parlour door. <br> With her five gold rings at the door she knocks, <br> “Are you within, good Mistress Fox? <br> If you’re in want of a husband now, <br> Then will it please you to step below?”</p></blockquote>
<p>Mrs. Fox asked, “Has the gentleman red stockings on, and has he a pointed mouth?”</p>
<p>“No,” answered the cat.</p>
<p>“Then he won’t do for me.”</p>
<p>When the wolf was gone, came a dog, a stag, a hare, a bear, a lion, and all the beasts of the forest, one after the other. But one of the good points which old Mr. Fox had possessed, was always lacking, and the cat had continually to send the wooers away. At length came a young fox. Then Mrs. Fox said, “Has the gentleman red stockings on, and has he a little pointed mouth?”</p>
<p>“Yes,” said the cat, “he has.”</p>
<p>“Then let him come upstairs,” said Mrs. Fox, and ordered the servant to prepare the wedding-feast.</p>
<blockquote><p class="verse">“Sweep me the room as clean as you can, <br> Up with the window, fling out my old man! <br> For many a fine fat mouse he brought, <br> Yet of his wife he never thought, <br> But ate up every one he caught.”</p></blockquote>
<p>Then the wedding was solemnized with young Mr. Fox, and there was much rejoicing and dancing; and if they have not left off, they are dancing still.</p>
